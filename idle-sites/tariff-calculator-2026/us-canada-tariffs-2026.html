US-Canada Tariffs 2026: 50% Section 338 Duty on ~$20B + Canada's Sept 8 Retaliation

Published August 22, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC

🔴 IN EFFECT — 50% additional duty since Aug 22, 2026

The 50% U.S. tariff on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods took effect Saturday, August 22, 2026 at 12:01 a.m. EDT (04:01 GMT) after U.S.–Canada trade talks collapsed. It applies to covered goods regardless of USMCA/CUSMA origin. Canada will impose dollar-for-dollar retaliatory tariffs on U.S. imports starting Tuesday, September 8, 2026.

What happened

On July 20, 2026, President Trump signed three proclamations (11046, 11047, 11048) under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 (19 U.S.C. 1338) — the first-ever use of that authority — imposing an additional 50% ad valorem duty on Canadian motor vehicles, alcoholic beverages, and dairy. The original August 19 effective date was suspended for three days on August 18 to allow talks; the Aug 19–21 negotiations collapsed, and the duty took effect at the rescheduled time.

USTR Jamieson Greer said minutes before the deadline: "Tonight, Canada declined to finalize the trade deal under the terms agreed earlier this week." Prime Minister Mark Carney responded that Canada will match those tariffs dollar for dollar, with retaliatory duties on U.S. steel, electronics, dairy, household appliances, farming equipment, and pulp/paper starting September 8, 2026. Asked whether this was a trade war, Carney replied: "You're at war when you get attacked. We got attacked."

Affected categories and rates

Coverage spans roughly $20 billion of Canadian exports (~5% of Canada's annual exports to the U.S.) — products described in coverage as ranging "from hockey sticks to tongue depressors." The full line-level HTS list lives in the three proclamations; the categories below are the verified scope.

The 50% duty stacks on top of regular MFN/CUSMA rates and other applicable duties. Pre-existing steel, lumber, and auto tariffs remain in force. Excluded from the new duty: energy products, potash, fish, critical minerals, and goods already under Section 232. USMCA/CUSMA origin does not shield covered goods.

⚠️ THREATENED — NOT IN EFFECT: 50% tariff on Canadian cars, trucks & auto parts proposed for January 1, 2027

On Monday, August 24, 2026, President Trump threatened to raise U.S. tariffs on Canadian cars, trucks, and auto parts from the current ~25% to 50%, with an effective date of January 1, 2027. The threatened 50% auto rate is a proposal, not an enacted tariff — the current effective rate on Canadian autos remains ~25%.

Context: the auto threat is the latest escalation after the 50% tariffs on ~$20B of Canadian goods took effect August 22, 2026, and Canada announced dollar-for-dollar retaliation starting September 8, 2026. Trump framed the move as leverage: "We don't need Canada, they need us."

What the rejected deal would have meant — NOT enacted

The deal Canada declined on Aug 21, 2026 would have reduced U.S. tariffs — it is not in force and never took effect:

  • Steel & aluminum: would have been cut to 25% (rejected)
  • Automotive duties: would have been lowered to 15% (rejected)
  • Lumber 10% levy: would have been eliminated (rejected)

Instead, the 50% Section 338 duty took effect Aug 22, 2026. You can model the rejected offer as a what-if scenario in the Tariff Calculator 2026 ("Rejected US–Canada deal offer — what-if, NOT enacted"), but the live estimate uses the enacted 50% rate.

Why the talks failed

Three days of talks in Washington (Aug 19–21) followed the August 18 three-day suspension, which was premised on a preliminary deal "subject to finalization of documents." That finalization never happened — the talks collapsed on Friday, August 21, before the rescheduled deadline. Greer blamed "new demands and walk-backs of other commitments by Canada"; Carney said "last-minute changes in the U.S. proposed terms were unfair, uneconomic," adding "They asked too much and they offered too little." Negotiations are suspended, Canada's team has been recalled to Ottawa, and no new talks are scheduled. Asked whether this was a trade war, Carney replied: "You're at war when you get attacked. We got attacked."

Canada's retaliation: dollar-for-dollar tariffs start September 8, 2026

Starting Tuesday, September 8, 2026, Canada will impose dollar-for-dollar retaliatory tariffs on U.S. imports — matching the scale of the U.S. Section 338 duties on roughly $20B of Canadian goods. The announced targets are U.S. exports in six sectors: steel, electronics, dairy, household appliances, farming equipment, and pulp/paper.

  • Steel
  • Electronics
  • Dairy
  • Household appliances
  • Farming equipment
  • Pulp/paper

These are Canada-side duties on U.S. goods entering Canada — separate from the 50% U.S. duty on Canadian goods entering the United States. The retaliation is dollar-for-dollar against the U.S. package: Carney said Canada "will match those tariffs dollar for dollar to protect our workers and businesses." Because talks are suspended, the September 8 date and sector list are the figures to plan around; importers shipping U.S. goods north should model this as an additional Canada-side landed cost.

FAQ

Q: Are the US-Canada 2026 tariffs in effect right now?

A: Yes — the 50% additional duty on roughly $20B of Canadian goods took effect Saturday, August 22, 2026 at 12:01 a.m. EDT (04:01 GMT) under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930, after talks failed. The original Aug 19 date was pushed by the Aug 18 three-day suspension.

Q: Which products are covered?

A: Motor vehicles and auto parts, alcoholic beverages (beer, wine, liquor, cider), dairy including cheeses of all types, and Annex II goods — from hockey equipment and furniture to cement, honey, plywood, plastics, paper, candles, apparel, wigs, chandeliers, pools, and fishing rods.

Q: What about the 25% steel, 15% autos, and 10% lumber rates?

A: Those were the rejected deal offer — steel/aluminum would have been cut to 25%, autos to 15%, and the lumber levy eliminated. They were never enacted. The 50% Section 338 duty is what applies to covered goods.

Q: Does USMCA/CUSMA protect my goods?

A: No. The Section 338 duties apply to all covered goods regardless of whether they originate under USMCA/CUSMA.

Q: What is excluded?

A: Energy products, potash, fish, critical minerals, and goods already subject to Section 232 tariffs (steel, aluminum, copper, autos, lumber). Pre-existing steel/lumber/auto duties remain and stack where applicable.

Q: What is Canada's retaliation?

A: A dollar-for-dollar response beginning September 8, 2026 on U.S. steel, electronics, dairy, household appliances, farming equipment, and pulp/paper. Negotiations collapsed before the U.S. duties took effect; Carney said the situation felt like war — "You're at war when you get attacked. We got attacked." — and Canada's negotiating team has been recalled to Ottawa.
