Canada Tariff List 2026: Counter-Tariffs In Effect Sept 8 — Up to 50% on US Goods

Published August 31, 2026 · Updated September 8, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC

Canada's retaliatory tariffs on U.S. goods are in effect as of September 8, 2026. Duties of up to 50% now apply to roughly ~US$20 billion (C$27.6 billion) of U.S.-origin imports — spanning product categories from steel and aluminum, dairy, appliances and farm equipment to furniture and clothing — matching U.S. tariffs dollar for dollar, rate for rate.

🔴 IN EFFECT — CANADA'S COUNTER-TARIFFS ARE LIVE SINCE 12:01 a.m. ET, SEPT 8, 2026

Canada's retaliatory tariffs on US goods are IN EFFECT since Tuesday, September 8, 2026 at 12:01 a.m. ET — 15%, 25% and 50% tiers applied to C$27.6 billion (~US$20 billion) of US-origin imports (629 tariff items on the revised official list; more than 700 products by media and PMO counts), a dollar-for-dollar, rate-for-rate match of the US 50% Section 338/232 tariffs that took effect August 22, 2026. President Trump has threatened additional measures — this page will be updated if conditions change.

Q: What US products are hit by Canada's September 2026 counter-tariffs?

A: US-origin goods entering Canada now face counter-tariffs, in force since 12:01 a.m. ET, September 8, 2026, in three tiers: 50% on steel and aluminum, milk and cream powders and whey, furniture, clothing, pulp and paper, plywood, cosmetics, plastics, smartphones and networking gear, sporting goods, and more; 25% on cheese, major appliances, carpets, household paper products, sawn wood, power tools, cutlery, and more; 15% on fork-lift trucks, lifting machinery, agricultural machinery parts, machine tools, moulds, and certain air conditioners and heat pumps. In total: 629 tariff items across C$27.6 billion (~US$20 billion) of US imports. US goods already in transit to Canada on Sept 8 are exempt.

Escalation risk — September 7–8, 2026

The counter-tariffs are IN EFFECT as of 12:01 a.m. ET September 8, 2026 — and as of September 7 no US-Canada trade talks are scheduled. The Prime Minister's Office confirmed September 7 that there were no plans for last-minute calls before the levies took effect.
  • Trump threats (Sept 6–7). President Trump called Canada's exchange-rate position with the US "unacceptable" (Truth Social, Sept 6) and threatened to block Canadian planemaker Bombardier from US sales unless it manufactures in the US (Sept 7).

  • Fish and seafood (HS Ch. 03) — removed in the Aug 26 revision; no seafood items remain on the list.
  • Oil, gas and potash — not on the list.
  • Goods of non-US origin — even if shipped through the US, under the CUSMA marking test.

Escalation risk: what happens next

The counter-tariffs are in effect as of 12:01 a.m. ET on Tuesday, September 8, 2026 — and Canada is not waiting on a deal. The PMO confirmed September 7 that no new trade talks are scheduled and no calls with President Trump were planned before the levies took effect. Background: Canada suspended negotiations on August 22, 2026 after the final round collapsed and recalled its negotiating team from Washington; Carney said the US "asked too much and offered too little" and pledged retaliation "dollar for dollar, rate for rate."

Escalation risk is high. On September 6–7 President Trump called Canada's exchange-rate position "unacceptable" and threatened to block Bombardier from US sales unless it manufactures in the US; on August 24–25 he announced US tariffs on Canadian cars, trucks and auto parts will double from ~25% to 50% effective January 1, 2027. USTR Jamieson Greer has warned of possible outright US bans on Canadian products if Canada retaliates further and has said there are "no open channels" back to negotiations. PM Carney called the US tariffs "unjustified and unprovoked" (Labour Day statement, Sept 7) and said Canada is growing "stronger and less dependent on any single partner."

Bottom line for importers: the 15/25/50% Canadian counter-tariffs on US goods are in effect and apply at the border now, on entry dates on or after September 8, 2026. Rates are set per product and could still change through an agreement, a remission order, or further escalation — watch for CBSA guidance, and this page will be updated if conditions change. What importers should do now that the tariffs are in effect

  • Verify your HS code against the official 629-item list — category shorthand (e.g., "50% steel") is not enough; the tier varies at the 8-digit line level (cheese is 25% while milk powders are 50%, even though both are "dairy").
  • Check origin and timing. The surtax applies only to US-origin goods (CUSMA marking regulations), and US goods already in transit on Sept 8 are exempt.
  • Watch for the CBSA Customs Notice. As of Sept 7, 2026 the CBSA had not yet published an implementing Customs Notice number for the Sept 8 surtax — its Customs Notices page carries a "Starting September 8, 2026" banner and says border guidance is forthcoming, so confirm entry instructions with your broker.
  • Model both directions — the 50% US duty on Canadian-origin goods (in effect Aug 22) and the Canada-side tiers on US-origin goods (in effect Sept 8) are separate costs.
  • Plan for volatility. Remission relief (steel at 25%) is intended pending Governor in Council approval, Trump has threatened 50% US auto tariffs from Jan 1, 2027, Bombardier restrictions and possible product bans, and a deal could change the whole list.
