Canada Counter-Tariffs September 8, 2026: 15/25/50% Rates on C$27.6B of US Goods

Published August 31, 2026 · Updated September 3, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC

🔴 COUNTER-TARIFFS CONFIRMED — IN FORCE SEPT 8, 2026, 12:01 a.m. ET

Canada's retaliatory tariffs on US goods take effect Tuesday, September 8, 2026 at 12:01 a.m. ET — 15%, 25% and 50% tiers applied to C$27.6 billion of US-origin imports (629 tariff items on the revised official list), matching the US 50% Section 338 tariffs that took effect August 22, 2026. Rates may change if a US-Canada deal lands.

Q: What products get more expensive September 8?

A: US-origin goods entering Canada face new counter-tariffs from 12:01 a.m. ET, September 8, 2026 in three tiers: 50% on steel, aluminum, dairy powders and whey, furniture, clothing, pulp and paper, plywood, cosmetics, plastics, smartphones and networking gear, sporting goods, and more; 25% on cheese, major appliances, carpets, household paper products, sawn wood, power tools, cutlery, and more; 15% on fork-lift trucks, lifting machinery, agricultural machinery parts, machine tools, moulds, and certain air conditioners and heat pumps. In total: 629 tariff items across C$27.6 billion of US imports. US goods already in transit to Canada on Sept 8 are exempt.

Negotiation status — September 3, 2026

As of September 3, 2026, no US-Canada deal has been reached and the talks remain stalled. Unless a deal changes the timeline, Canada's tiered counter-tariffs — 15%, 25% and 50% on C$27.6 billion of US-origin goods — still take effect Tuesday, September 8, 2026 at 12:01 a.m. ET.

At a glance: what's happening

  • Effective: 12:01 a.m. ET, Tuesday, September 8, 2026 — in response to the US 50% Section 338 tariff on Canadian goods that took effect Aug 22, 2026.
  • Scope: C$27.6 billion of US-origin imports (≈US$19.9B), 629 tariff items on the revised official list — 413 at 50%, 195 at 25%, 21 at 15%.
  • Match: Rates are matched dollar for dollar, rate for rate, to the corresponding US Section 338 / Section 232 rate.
  • Origin + transit: US-origin goods only (CUSMA marking test); US goods already in transit on Sept 8 are NOT hit.
  • Not on the list: autos (separate existing 25% order), fish and seafood (removed in the Aug 26 revision), oil, gas and potash.
  • Talks: No deal as of Sept 3, 2026 — talks stalled because the US has not given assurances on tariff levels. Carney says Canada is ready to resume when the US is ready, but only on terms that keep auto and steel competitive. A deal could still change the rates.

What is NOT covered by the Sept 8 counter-tariffs

  • US automobiles and auto parts — NOT on the Sept 8 list. They remain under the separate existing 25% Canadian counter-tariff order.
  • Fish and seafood (HS Ch. 03) — removed in the Aug 26 revision; no seafood items remain on the list.
  • Oil, gas and potash — not on the list.
  • Goods of non-US origin — even if shipped through the US, under the CUSMA marking test.
  • US goods already in transit to Canada on September 8, 2026.

Why the US-Canada trade talks are stalled

As of September 3, 2026, no deal has been reached and the talks remain stalled — the US has not given assurances on tariff levels. PM Mark Carney said a US-Canada pact is possible only if the terms keep Canadian auto and steel operations competitive, pushed back on Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, and said Canada is ready to resume talks when the US is ready. (Source: Bloomberg, Sept 3, 2026.)

Background: Canada suspended negotiations on August 22, 2026 after the final round collapsed, and recalled its negotiating team from Washington. Carney said the US "asked too much and offered too little" and that Canada will match the US Section 338 tariffs "dollar for dollar, rate for rate." USTR Jamieson Greer said there were no plans to resume talks as of late August, and Canadian officials saw little chance of resumption before the November US midterms.

Bottom line for importers: as of Sept 3, 2026 the Sept 8 tariffs remain scheduled to take effect as announced, but both the date and the product list are conditional on the talks. Unless a deal changes the timeline, the tariffs take effect September 8. If a deal lands before then, rates could be reduced, suspended, or removed — and the calculator presets on this site are built to be updated without a risky deployment.

What importers should do before September 8

  • Verify your HS code against the official 629-item list — category shorthand (e.g., "50% steel") is not enough; the tier varies at the 8-digit line level.
  • Check origin and timing. The surtax applies only to US-origin goods (CUSMA marking regulations), and US goods already in transit on Sept 8 are exempt.
  • Watch for the CBSA Customs Notice. As of Aug 31, 2026 the CBSA had not yet published the implementing notice for the Sept 8 surtax — Finance Canada defers administration to CBSA, and a notice has accompanied every prior surtax order.
  • Model both directions — the 50% US duty on Canadian-origin goods (in effect Aug 22) and the new Canada-side tiers on US-origin goods (Sept 8) are separate costs.
  • Plan for volatility. Remission relief (steel at 25%) is intended pending Governor in Council approval, and a deal could change the whole list.
