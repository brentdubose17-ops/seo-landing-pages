⚠️ De Minimis Exemption Suspended — Duty Applies to All Shipments Over $0 Effective Now

The U.S. Court of International Trade upheld the suspension of the $800 de minimis duty exemption on August 13, 2026 (Axle of Dearborn / Detroit Axle v. Dept. of Commerce, Ct. No. 25-00091). The Section 321 low-value exemption remains suspended, so every package — including parcels under $800 — is now assessed duty and customs processing. Only bona fide gifts valued under $100 from individuals remain exempt. This calculator charges duty on all shipment values above $0.

No more duty-free packages. If you import low-value parcels, factor duty into your landed cost at every price point. Read the de minimis rule changes explainer.

⚠️ New: 50% US Tariff on Canadian Goods Effective Aug 19, 2026

President Trump signed three proclamations under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 on July 20, 2026, imposing an additional 50% duty on ~500–569 categories of Canadian goods — motor vehicles, alcoholic beverages, dairy, furniture, textiles & apparel, footwear, sporting goods, plywood & paper, cement, and more.

Applies even to USMCA/CUSMA-qualifying goods. Exempt: energy, potash, Section 232 goods (steel/aluminum), fish, and critical minerals. Select Canada and a covered product category to see the flag on your estimate. Read the full 60-country Section 301 expansion explainer.

What Changed: Section 301 Tariffs Now Cover 60 Countries

The across-the-board Section 122 tariff (10% global rate) expired July 24, 2026 at 12:01 a.m. ET and was replaced the same day by a new Section 301 forced-labor framework covering 60 economies. Goods from those countries now face an additional 10% or 12.5% duty on top of existing Column-1/MFN rates. USMCA-qualified goods (Canada, Mexico) and Section 232-covered articles are exempt. Read the full Section 301 60-country expansion explainer.

What Affects Your Tariff Rate?

  • HS Code / HTS Classification — The specific product category determines the base duty rate
  • Country of Origin — Trade agreements (USMCA, FTAs) and the new Section 301 matrix change the effective rate
  • Product Value (Customs Value) — Duties are calculated as a percentage of the customs value (typically the transaction price)
  • Additional Duties — Section 301 (China), Section 338 (Canada), Section 232 (steel/aluminum), AD/CVD orders may add extra duties
  • De Minimis Exemption Suspended — The $800 Section 321 low-value exemption is suspended (upheld Aug 13, 2026). Duty and customs processing apply to every package valued over $0; only gifts under $100 from individuals are exempt

Exemptions & Proposed Tariffs

  • USMCA: Canada and Mexico are exempt from Section 301 when goods qualify as originating — the calculator applies this by default. USMCA is now in annual review; interim arrangements are targeted by end of 2026.
  • Section 232: Steel, aluminum, copper, vehicles, wood, and semiconductors are exempt from Section 301 (no stacking).
  • Canada Section 338 (proposed): A separate 50% tariff on Canadian autos, alcohol, and dairy takes effect August 19, 2026 — it applies even to USMCA-qualifying goods. Not yet in effect. See the Canada 50% tariff explainer.
  • EU digital-services-tax response (threatened): A 25% Section 301 tariff on selected EU imports is threatened but not in effect.
  • In-transit exception: Goods loaded before July 24 and entered before July 28, 2026 are exempt from the new duty.
  • Pharmaceuticals (9903.05.89) and civil aircraft (9903.05.88) are exempt.

🆕 Section 232: Polysilicon & Solar Tariff (Effective Dec 4, 2026)

President Trump signed a 15% ad valorem Section 232 tariff on imported polysilicon ingots and derivatives (solar cells, modules, ingots, and wafers) on August 6, 2026. It takes effect December 4, 2026, 12:01 a.m. ET and includes Minimum Import Price (MIP) floors — importers must certify the first arm's-length U.S. sale is at or above the MIP. This replaces the Section 201 solar safeguard that expired in February 2026.

Select "Polysilicon & Solar (Section 232)" from the product category dropdown and set your import date to December 4, 2026 or later to see the new rate and MIP floors in the calculator.
