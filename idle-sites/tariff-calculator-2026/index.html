About US Import Tariffs in 2026

The United States applies tariffs on imported goods based on their Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS) classification. Rates vary significantly by product — from duty-free (pharmaceuticals, some electronics) to over 25% (certain textiles, footwear, and steel products). Country of origin also matters due to trade agreements, Section 301 tariffs on Chinese goods, and country-specific trade actions.

New: 50% Tariff on Canadian Goods (Effective August 19, 2026)

On July 20, 2026, the U.S. invoked Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 — its first use for tariffs — signing three proclamations that impose an additional 50% ad valorem duty on a wide range of Canadian goods: motor vehicles, alcoholic beverages, dairy, furniture, textiles & apparel, footwear, sporting goods (including hockey equipment), plywood & paper, cement, plastics, jewelry, and more. The duties cover roughly 500–569 product categories (about $24 billion, ~5.5% of annual Canadian exports to the U.S.).

Key rules: The 50% duty stacks on top of standard tariffs and applies to all covered goods whether or not they qualify under USMCA/CUSMA. Exempt categories include energy, potash, goods already subject to Section 232 tariffs (steel/aluminum), fish, and critical minerals. The duties take effect 12:01 a.m. ET on August 19, 2026 and carry no statutory expiration date — they remain until modified or revoked by presidential action.

Sources: White House fact sheet (July 20, 2026); Federal Register Proclamations 11046 (alcoholic beverages), 11047 (dairy), 11048 (motor vehicles); Chicago Fed Insights (July 2026).

What Affects Your Tariff Rate?

  • HS Code / HTS Classification — The specific product category determines the base duty rate
  • Country of Origin — Trade agreements (USMCA, etc.) and country-specific tariffs change the effective rate
  • Product Value (Customs Value) — Duties are calculated as a percentage of the customs value (typically the transaction price)
  • Additional Duties — Section 301 (China), Section 232 (steel/aluminum), AD/CVD orders, and the new Section 338 Canada 50% duty (effective Aug 19, 2026) may add extra duties
  • De Minimis Threshold — Shipments under $800 may enter duty-free under Section 321
