🔴 COUNTER-TARIFFS CONFIRMED — IN FORCE SEPT 8, 2026, 12:01 a.m. ET

Canada confirmed on August 25, 2026 it will impose counter-tariffs of 15%, 25% and 50% on C$27.6 billion of US imports, effective Tuesday, September 8, 2026 at 12:01 a.m. ET. The official list runs 874 tariff items, rates matched dollar for dollar to the US duty.

Q: What is the current US-Canada tariff rate?

A: The US applies a 50% Section 338 tariff on roughly C$27.6 billion (~US$20 billion) of Canadian goods since Aug 22, 2026. Canada's counter-tariffs of 15/25/50% on C$27.6 billion of US imports take effect September 8, 2026 at 12:01 a.m. ET. The Jan 1, 2027 US rise on autos, trucks, parts and steel to 50% is announced but not in force.

What changed: dollar-for-dollar, rate for rate

Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne confirmed Canada will match the new US tariffs "dollar for dollar, rate for rate", targeting the same product base the US hit under Section 338 and Section 232. The measures cover C$27.6 billion in US imports — about 8% of total US exports to Canada (2025 Census data). "When the United States asked too much and offered too little, we chose to stand up for Canadians," he said.

Two operational rules matter for importers and carriers: the counter-tariffs apply only to goods originating from the US (CUSMA marking rules), and they do not apply to US goods already in transit to Canada on the day they come into force. Existing Canadian counter-tariffs on US autos, steel and aluminum remain in place, and the tariff remission framework stays available for exceptional relief.

The official Finance Canada list contains 874 tariff items (press reports round to "more than 700 products"), each rate matching the corresponding US rate in three tiers:

Notably, US alcoholic beverages are absent — Canada did not repeat the first-term whiskey play. Industry Minister Mélanie Joly said some products were chosen "to put political pressure on particular U.S. states."

How tariffs affect trucking and freight

Trucks carry more than 55% of the value of US-Canada freight — roughly 5.5 million commercial trucks enter the US from Canada each year, about 15,000 per day, carrying an estimated $396 billion in goods. The busiest crossings: Detroit, Port Huron (MI), and Buffalo (NY). In H1 2026 the US exported $175.8 billion in goods to Canada (14% of all US exports) and imported $200 billion (11.5% of US imports).

The Canadian Trucking Alliance says the uncertainty "has direct implications for the movement of goods" as companies face "changing trade conditions, shifting freight volumes and increased costs" — and trucking is "a critical link" between producers and US markets. The signal is already visible: US steel imports into Canada have fallen 30% since Canada's 25% tariff, and the new 50% rate is expected to cut them further. For carriers, the operational fact to know is the in-transit exemption — US goods already moving to Canada on Sept 8 are not hit. UAW president Shawn Fain rejected any escalation on Canada, citing its strong unions and labor standards.

Q: When do Canada's retaliatory tariffs take effect?

A: At 12:01 a.m. ET on Tuesday, September 8, 2026.

Q: What is the 50% tariff Canada effective date?

A: September 8, 2026 at 12:01 a.m. ET — the 50% tier covers US-origin steel and aluminum, furniture, and clothing and apparel in the official 874-item list.

Q: What's on the US Canada tariff list for September 2026?

A: 874 tariff items: 404 at 50% (steel, aluminum, furniture, clothing), 449 at 25% (fish and seafood, dairy such as cheese, appliances, steel and aluminum derivatives), 21 at 15% (air conditioners, heat pumps, lift trucks, moulds, tool parts). Focus: steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp and paper, electronics.

Q: Which products face Canada's 50% retaliatory tariff?

A: Steel and aluminum previously subject to only a 25% counter-tariff, plus furniture and clothing and apparel — 404 tariff items including flat-rolled steel, aluminum sheet, metal furniture, t-shirts and motorcycles.

Q: Do Canada's counter-tariffs apply to goods already in transit?

A: No — US goods already in transit to Canada on September 8, 2026 are exempt, and the tariffs apply only to US-origin goods under CUSMA marking rules.

Q: How do tariffs affect trucking and freight between the US and Canada?

A: Trucks carry more than 55% of US-Canada freight value — about 15,000 trucks cross daily with roughly $396 billion in goods a year. The Canadian Trucking Alliance expects fewer loads, shifting volumes and higher costs, plus a structural equipment imbalance.

Q: What US tariff rate applies to Canadian goods right now?

A: The US 50% Section 338 tariff on ~C$27.6 billion (~US$20 billion) of Canadian goods has been in effect since August 22, 2026. Autos and parts are at 25% today; the announced 50% for Jan 1, 2027 is not yet in force.

Q: What support is Canada offering to businesses hit by the tariffs?

A: A C$7.5 billion package (Aug 25, 2026), on top of nearly C$25 billion cumulative — including C$2B for the Canada Strong Diversification Fund, C$3.5B Rapid Response Supports, C$1.5B more for the Regional Tariff Response Initiative, and C$500M BDC Pivot to Grow liquidity.
