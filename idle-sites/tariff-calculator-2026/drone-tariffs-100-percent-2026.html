Drone Tariffs Up to 100% Take Effect: What Importers Pay Now

Published September 3, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC

New US tariffs of up to 100% on imported drones and drone components took effect at 12:01 a.m. ET on September 3, 2026, under Proclamation 11055, a Section 232 action signed August 13, 2026. The duties apply to goods entered for consumption on or after that moment and land hardest on China-origin equipment — most heavy and thermal drones are made there.

Q: What is the new US drone tariff, and who is affected?

A: The US now applies Section 232 tariffs of up to 100% on imported drones and drone components. The main rates took effect 12:01 a.m. ET on September 3, 2026 for goods entered for consumption on or after that time; earlier entries are not subject. Importers and buyers of China-made drones are affected most, including police and fire departments flying the heavy and thermal models that carry the 100% rate.

Key takeaways

  • 100% tier: drones above 25 kg (~55 lb), thermal-imaging drones, docking stations, and critical components — from Sept 3, 2026.
  • 25% tier: smaller non-thermal drones now; more components at 25% from Feb 9, 2027.
  • China is the main target: DJI makes more than 70% of the world's commercial drones and is not Blue UAS-listed.
  • Only Blue UAS-listed suppliers are deferred to Feb 9, 2027; allied-origin products get capped rates of 15% (10% UK) — not exempt.

Drone tariff 100%: which drones pay the top rate

Under Proclamation 11055, the 100% rate applies from Sept 3, 2026 to drones with a maximum takeoff weight (MTOW) above 25 kg — about 55 lb, drones with thermal-imaging capability at any weight, UAS docking stations, and certain critical components (Annex I). AeroTime's report details the duties as they take effect.

The 25 kg boundary is authoritative in metric — "55 lb" is rounding (25 kg = 55.1 lb), so a 55-lb drone stays in the 25% tier. The duty applies in addition to other duties and fees; CBP hasn't yet defined the stacking with Section 301 or other China tariffs.

Chinese drone tariff 2026: DJI and the China-origin cost question

Quartz reports that DJI produces more than 70% of the world's commercial drones; the heavy and thermal models now carrying 100% duties are mostly made in China. DJI is not Blue UAS-listed, so its imports fall in the general China-origin bucket.

DJI tariff cost: the arithmetic on a drone order

At the 100% rate, a $10,000 invoice for a thermal or over-25-kg drone adds $10,000 in Section 232 duty before other duties and fees; at the 25% rate, the same invoice adds $2,500.

Blue UAS deferral vs. allied-nation caps: who gets a break

Companies on the Blue UAS Cleared List, the Blue UAS Framework, or the FCC's Conditional Approval List as of September 2, 2026 get their effective date moved to Feb 9, 2027 for covered products and components — a deferral, not an exemption; the full rate applies after.

Allied-nation products are not exempt. From Sept 3, products of the EU, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Switzerland, and Liechtenstein are capped at 15% total including the normal Column 1 duty (UK: 10%), if the importer certifies substantially all critical components and technology originate in the US, UK, or eligible allied economies. Quartz's summary: "Allied-nation products are not exempt but receive reduced rates."

Law enforcement and firefighting fleets feel it first

The New York Times reports the heavy and thermal drones in the 100% tier — the vast majority made in China — "have become essential for many law enforcement officials, as they're used to track wildfires and find lost people." More than 80% of surveyed state and local law-enforcement programs used DJI in 2024, per an FCC comment cited by Ars Technica. Scott Mlakar, head of the Lake County Drone Unit (combined police and fire) near Cleveland, told Quartz the tariffs would "put thermal drones out of reach for communities that needed them the most."

What importers and buyers should do now

  • Classify first: confirm MTOW in kilograms, thermal imaging, and whether the item is a docking station or Annex I component — these set the 100% vs 25% line.
  • Verify status: supplier Blue UAS/FCC-listed as of Sept 2, 2026 (deferral), or allied certification for a 15%/10% cap?
  • Budget the stack: duties compound, and Annex III components add a separate 25% line on Feb 9, 2027.

FAQ

Q: What is the drone tariff in 2026?

A: The US now applies Section 232 tariffs of up to 100% on imported drones and components (Proclamation 11055, signed Aug 13, 2026), effective 12:01 a.m. ET Sept 3, 2026: 100% on heavy, thermal, docking-station, and critical-component imports, 25% on smaller non-thermal drones.

Q: Is the drone tariff 100% on every drone?

A: No — up to 100%. The 100% tier covers drones above 25 kg (~55 lb), thermal-imaging drones at any weight, docking stations, and certain critical components; consumer and commercial camera drones under 25 kg without thermal imaging pay 25%.

Q: How much are drone import tariffs?

A: Small (25 kg or less) non-thermal drones from China pay 25% from Sept 3, 2026; heavy or thermal drones pay 100%. Allied-origin products are capped at 15% total (UK 10%) with certification; Blue UAS-listed suppliers are deferred to Feb 9, 2027. Duties stack on other US tariffs.

Q: Are DJI drones subject to the 100% tariff?

A: DJI is not Blue UAS-listed, so its China-origin imports fall in the general bucket: thermal or over-25-kg models pay 100%, smaller non-thermal models pay 25% (subject to CBP's product-level determination). DJI makes over 70% of the world's commercial drones.

Q: What is the Blue UAS exemption?

A: It is a deferral, not an exemption: companies on the Blue UAS Cleared List, Blue UAS Framework, or FCC Conditional Approval List as of Sept 2, 2026 pay no new Section 232 drone duty until Feb 9, 2027, when the full rate applies.

Q: Why are police and fire departments worried about the drone tariff?

A: Most heavy and thermal public-safety drones are made in China, and over 80% of surveyed state and local law-enforcement drone programs used DJI in 2024. The 100% rate hits the thermal models used for wildfires and search and rescue — one Ohio drone-unit chief said it would put thermal drones out of reach for communities that needed them most.
