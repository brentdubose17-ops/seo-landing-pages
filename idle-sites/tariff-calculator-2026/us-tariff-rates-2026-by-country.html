US Tariff Rates 2026 by Country: A Complete Guide

As of May 2026, the US tariff landscape has shifted significantly from previous years. New trade agreements, retaliatory measures, and updated trade policies have reshaped how much importers pay when bringing goods into the United States. Whether you are a small business owner, a supply chain manager, or an individual importer, understanding current US tariff rates by country is essential for accurate cost planning and compliance.

This guide provides a detailed breakdown of US tariff rates in 2026, organized by country and region, including specific data points and actionable advice for navigating these rates effectively.

Overview of US Tariff Policy in 2026

In 2026, the United States maintains a complex system of tariff rates that vary by product, country of origin, and trade agreement status. The average US applied MFN (Most Favored Nation) tariff rate remains around 3.4% for non-agricultural goods, but rates can exceed 20% for sensitive products such as textiles, footwear, and agricultural items. Key changes in 2026 include updated Section 301 tariffs on Chinese goods, new agreements with Southeast Asian nations, and adjustments to the USMCA rules for North American trade.

US Tariff Rates by Major Trading Partner

China

Tariffs on Chinese imports remain among the highest in 2026. The Section 301 tariffs, first imposed in 2018 and updated in 2024-2025, continue to affect over $350 billion worth of Chinese goods. As of May 2026, the average tariff rate on Chinese imports is approximately 19.3%, with specific rates ranging from 7.5% to 25% depending on the product category. Electronics, machinery, and automotive parts face the highest rates. For example, imported Chinese steel products carry a 25% tariff, while certain consumer electronics face a 7.5% rate. Importers should use a tariff calculator to verify the exact HTS code classification, as exclusions and product-specific rules apply.

European Union

Tariffs on EU goods in 2026 are generally lower than on Chinese imports but remain subject to sector-specific disputes. The average MFN tariff on EU goods is around 2.5%, but select products face higher rates due to ongoing Airbus-Boeing disputes and digital services tax retaliations. For instance, EU wine and cheese imports face a 25% tariff, while aircraft parts are taxed at 15%. However, most industrial goods from the EU enter at rates between 0% and 5%. Importers should check for product-specific exclusions that may apply.

Canada and Mexico (USMCA)

Under the USMCA, most goods from Canada and Mexico qualify for zero tariffs if they meet the rules of origin requirements. As of 2026, approximately 85% of US imports from Canada and 82% from Mexico enter duty-free. Key exceptions include dairy products (tariff-rate quotas apply), sugar, and certain textiles. For example, Canadian dairy imports above quota face tariffs as high as 200%, while Mexican auto parts must meet 75% regional value content to qualify for zero tariffs. Importers must maintain proper certification documentation to claim USMCA benefits.

Japan

The US-Japan Trade Agreement, in effect since 2020, continues to provide tariff reductions in 2026. Over 90% of US agricultural imports from Japan are now duty-free, while Japanese industrial goods face an average tariff of 2.1%. Notable exceptions include Japanese automobiles, which still face a 2.5% tariff, and automotive parts at 2.0%. These rates are lower than pre-agreement levels but remain subject to review under the agreement's built-in renegotiation clauses.

South Korea

The US-Korea Free Trade Agreement (KORUS) keeps most bilateral trade duty-free in 2026. Approximately 95% of US imports from South Korea enter without tariffs. Key exceptions include rice (tariff-rate quota), certain agricultural products, and used vehicles. South Korean electronics, machinery, and auto parts typically qualify for zero tariffs, but importers must provide valid KORUS certificates of origin.

Vietnam and Southeast Asia

Vietnam, Thailand, and Indonesia do not have free trade agreements with the US, so they face standard MFN rates. For Vietnam, the average tariff rate in 2026 is 4.8%, with higher rates on textiles (up to 20%) and footwear (up to 30%). Some Southeast Asian nations benefit from reduced tariffs under the Generalized System of Preferences (GSP) for eligible products, though GSP eligibility has been narrowed in 2026.

How Tariff Rates Are Determined in 2026

Several factors influence the tariff rate applied to a specific product in 2026. These include the product's Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS) code, the country of origin, any applicable free trade agreements, and temporary trade actions such as antidumping or countervailing duties. For example, solar panels from China face both Section 301 tariffs (25%) and antidumping duties (ranging from 15% to 30%), resulting in combined rates exceeding 50%. Importers must calculate the total landed cost using accurate HTS classification and current rate data.

Actionable Advice for Importers in 2026

  • Verify HTS codes annually. The US tariff schedule is updated each year. Use an official tariff lookup tool to confirm your product's classification and current rate.
  • Leverage free trade agreements. If you import from USMCA, KORUS, or Japan, ensure your supply chain meets the rules of origin to claim zero tariffs.
  • Monitor Section 301 exclusions. The USTR periodically reopens exclusion processes for Chinese goods. Subscribe to updates to avoid overpaying.
  • Consider duty drawback. If you re-export imported goods, you may qualify for a refund of up to 99% of duties paid under the duty drawback program.
  • Use a tariff calculator. Tools like Tariff Calculator 2026 allow you to input the HTS code and country of origin to get an instant, accurate rate estimate for your shipment.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I find the exact tariff rate for my product in 2026?

Use the USITC Tariff Database or a reliable tariff calculator tool. Enter the 10-digit HTS code for your product and the country of origin. The system will return the current MFN rate, any special rates under FTAs, and any additional duties such as Section 301 or antidumping duties.

Are there any new tariff changes in 2026 I should know about?

Yes. In early 2026, the US expanded Section 301 tariffs to cover additional Chinese goods, including certain medical supplies and semiconductors. Additionally, the Generalized System of Preferences (GSP) was not renewed for several countries, affecting duty-free access for products from Thailand, Indonesia, and other nations. Always check the latest Federal Register notices for updates.

Do tariff rates change if I import through a different port?

No. US tariff rates are set federally and do not vary by port of entry. However, port-specific fees such as harbor maintenance fees and merchandise processing fees may apply, and these can differ slightly by location. The tariff rate itself remains consistent regardless of which US port processes your shipment.
