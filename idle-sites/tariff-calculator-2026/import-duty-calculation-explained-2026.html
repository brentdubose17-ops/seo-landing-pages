The Three Pillars of Import Duty Calculation

Every import duty calculation in the United States rests on three fundamental components: the classification of goods, the valuation of goods, and the applicable duty rate. In 2026, each of these pillars has seen significant updates that directly impact your bottom line.

1. Classification: The Harmonized Tariff Schedule (HTS) in 2026

The Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS) is the foundation of duty calculation. Every product imported into the U.S. must be assigned a 10-digit HTS code. In 2026, the HTSUS has undergone its annual revision, with over 1,200 individual tariff lines modified as of January 1, 2026. Key changes include new subheadings for advanced technology components, lithium-ion batteries, and certain medical devices.

Real-world example: A company importing electric vehicle (EV) batteries in 2025 used HTS code 8507.60.00. In 2026, due to a new breakout for high-capacity batteries (over 50 kWh), the correct code is now 8507.60.0010. Using the old code could result in a duty rate difference of 3.2% and potential penalties for misclassification.

2. Valuation: The Transaction Value Method

The transaction value is the price actually paid or payable for the merchandise. Under U.S. customs law (19 U.S.C. §1401a), this must include certain additions:

  • Packing costs incurred by the buyer
  • Any selling commissions
  • Royalties or license fees related to the imported goods
  • Proceeds from any subsequent resale that accrue to the seller
  • Assists (materials, tools, dies, etc. provided by the buyer to the seller)

In 2026, CBP has issued new guidance (Cargo Systems Messaging No. 26-0012) clarifying that transportation costs from the place of shipment to the U.S. port of entry must be included in the transaction value. This reverses a previous ambiguity that allowed some importers to exclude these costs. For a shipment valued at $100,000 with $5,000 in freight costs, this change alone increases the dutiable value by 5%.

Step-by-Step: How to Calculate Import Duty in 2026

Let’s walk through a complete calculation using a real-world scenario. Assume you are importing 2,000 units of lithium-ion power tools (HTS 8467.29.0030) from China, with a unit price of $45 FOB (Free on Board) Shanghai.

Step 1: Determine the Transaction Value

  • Total FOB value: 2,000 units × $45 = $90,000
  • Ocean freight (per unit): $3.50, total $7,000
  • Insurance: $500
  • Packing costs: $1,200
  • Total dutiable value: $98,700

Step 2: Identify the Applicable Duty Rate

  • MFN rate for HTS 8467.29.0030: 2.5%
  • Section 301 tariff (China): 25%
  • Total duty rate: 27.5%

Step 3: Calculate the Basic Duty

  • Duty = $98,700 × 27.5% = $27,142.50

Step 4: Consider Additional Fees

  • Merchandise Processing Fee (MPF): 0.3464% of value, capped at $538.40 per entry (for formal entries). Since $98,700 × 0.3464% = $341.88, this is under the cap, so MPF = $341.88.
  • Harbor Maintenance Fee (HMF): 0.125% of value for imports arriving by vessel. $98,700 × 0.125% = $123.38.
  • Total duties and fees: $27,142.50 + $341.88 + $123.38 = $27,607.76

Step 5: Check for Trade Agreements or Exclusions

In 2026, certain Chinese-made power tools are eligible for a Section 301 exclusion if they are used in renewable energy projects. If your tools qualify, the 25% tariff is waived, reducing duty to $2,467.50 (2.5% of $98,700) — a savings of $24,675.
