Client Advisory: Ground Beef Out-of-Quota Tariff Waiver — Up to 300,000 Metric Tons for 90 Days

Published August 21, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC

What changed: On Friday morning, Aug 21, 2026, President Trump announced that for the next 90 days the United States will allow up to 300,000 metric tons (~661 million lbs) of product for ground beef (lean beef trimmings) to be imported with no out-of-quota tariff — normally 26.4% above the tariff-rate quota. In exchange, foreign beef exporters committed to sell at 25% below current market prices, with the stated intent of lowering retail prices from the July 2026 average of $6.89/lb (vs. $5.55/lb in Jan 2025).

What importers should know now:

  • The window is temporary and capped. The waiver runs 90 days (modeled Aug 21 – Nov 19, 2026) or until the 300,000 MT cap is filled, whichever comes first.
  • EO pending. The executive order is to be signed within two weeks; no HTS scope, country list, or claiming mechanics are published yet. The 90-day clock start (announcement vs. EO signature) is not specified — dates may shift when the EO lands.
  • Baseline duty unresolved. Whether the 4.4¢/kg in-quota tariff still applies to waiver imports was not answered by officials.
  • Model it now. Run the scenario in the Tariff Calculator 2026 — select "Ground Beef — 90-Day Out-of-Quota Waiver (Aug 21, 2026)" and an import date inside the window to see the duty-free result; outside the window the 26.4% out-of-quota rate applies.

Client Advisory: Canada Section 338 50% Tariffs — IN EFFECT Since Aug 22, 2026

Published August 19, 2026Updated August 22, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC

What changed: The 50% Section 338 tariffs on Canadian goods took effect Saturday, August 22, 2026 at 12:01 a.m. EDT (04:01 GMT) after three days of talks in Washington failed to finalize a deal. The duties apply to roughly $20 billion of Canadian exports (~5% of Canada's annual exports to the US) — products ranging from hockey sticks to tongue depressors — under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 (signed July 20, 2026; first-ever use of the authority). USTR Jamieson Greer, minutes before the deadline: "Tonight, Canada declined to finalize the trade deal under the terms agreed earlier this week." There is no USMCA/CUSMA exemption.

What happened: The three proclamations were signed July 20, 2026 with an original August 19 effective date. On August 18, 2026 — hours before the duty was due — President Trump announced a three-day suspension after a preliminary deal, setting a new effective date of August 22. The August 19–21 talks collapsed; PM Carney recalled negotiators to Ottawa and announced dollar-for-dollar retaliation beginning September 8, 2026 (US steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural machinery, paper, electronics).

What importers should do now

  • Book the 50% additional duty for Canadian-origin covered goods entered on or after August 22, 2026, 12:01 a.m. EDT, on top of existing MFN and other duties. No in-transit grace period has been announced.
  • Verify product coverage. The covered list spans dairy (milk, cream, whey, lactose, sugars), alcohol (beer, wine, liquor, cider), wood & hockey equipment, honey, seeds, tulips and orchids, cement, candles, wigs, clothing, chandeliers, ice skates, swimming pools, fishing rods, and more. Official line-level HTS numbers are in the three July 20 proclamations / USTR statement — confirm your HTS against that list, not the shorthand.
  • No USMCA exemption. The duty applies even to CUSMA/USMCA-qualifying goods — do not assume origin qualification protects you.
  • Confirm exemptions: energy products, potash, fish, critical minerals, and items already under Section 232 are outside the duty. Pre-existing steel/lumber/auto tariffs remain in force and stack.
  • Watch Canadian retaliation from September 8, 2026 if you import US steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural machinery, paper, or electronics into Canada.

Key dates

  • Jul 20, 2026 — Three Section 338 proclamations signed: 50% additional duty on ~$20B of Canadian goods; original effective date Aug 19.
  • Aug 18, 2026 (Tue, evening) — Trump announces 3-day suspension; new effective date Aug 22.
  • Aug 19, 2026 (Wed, 12:01 a.m. ET) — Original effective date passed without the duty taking effect (suspension active).
  • Aug 19–21, 2026 — Three days of talks fail; no final agreement.
  • Aug 22, 2026 (Sat, 12:01 a.m. EDT / 04:01 GMT) — 50% duty takes effect. Carney announces dollar-for-dollar retaliation; negotiators recalled to Ottawa.
  • Sep 8, 2026 — Canadian retaliatory tariffs begin (US steel, dairy, appliances, ag machinery, paper, electronics).
