Client Advisory: H.R. 5334 Signed Into Law — Up to 500% Duty Authority on Russian Goods, Due Within 30 Days of Enactment

Published September 19, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC

What changed: On Friday, September 18, 2026 the President signed H.R. 5334 into law — the "Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026" (White House statement, published 2026-09-18). The Act switches on two new tariff authorities at enactment, on top of duties that already apply. §112 requires a duty on all goods from Russia at up to 500 percent ad valorem, imposed within 30 days of enactment — expressly including oil, natural gas, LNG, petroleum and petroleum products, petrochemical products, coal and coal products, in addition to any other duty, fee, tax or charge. §113 allows up to 100 percent ad valorem on goods from up to five described countries drawn from data — no country is named in the statute. Every rate is a ceiling, not a rate: §112 sets no minimum, and the rate within the ceiling, any §113(b) adjustment and any §115 waiver are left to the President and USTR. No duty changed on the signature date itself. Read the source-by-source comparison in the Presidential Tariff Authority 2026 hub →

What importers should know now:

  • Authority, not an imposed duty. The Act requires the Russia duty to be imposed within 30 days of enactment. Before booking against a rate, check the instrument that imposes it — a proclamation or a Federal Register document — not the ceiling in the bill.
  • The §112 rate is a ceiling. Up to 500 percent ad valorem on all goods from Russia, in addition to any other duty, fee, tax or charge, with no minimum rate set — the operative rate is whatever the imposition instrument states.
  • The §113 list is described by data, not named. Up to five countries that knowingly make new purchases of Russian-origin crude oil or natural gas on or after day 30 and ranked among the five largest importers by volume in the year before enactment, plus a separate facilitator list of up to five. Any named list beyond the statute's text is inference — including the defeated amendment that would have named ten.
  • Not Section 232, 301, 338 or IEEPA — and it stacks. A fifth, separate source of tariff authority: §112 duties apply "in addition to any other duty, fee, tax, exaction or charge". Duties already in force — metals, drones, China, Canada — are unaffected by the enactment itself.
  • Exit ramps are written in. A §115 national-interest waiver, §117 termination on a Russia–Ukraine peace-agreement certification, and a 5-year sunset on the division (§203).
  • The federal trackers lag the signature — as of September 19, 2026. No public-law number had been published: govinfo's Public Laws collection showed no entry, congress.gov still read "To President" (latest action 09/17/2026, "Presented to President"), and a name-restricted Federal Register API query returned zero documents. Do not cite a PL number until one appears. The Federal Register index carries rules, notices and proclamations — not enacted statutes — so a zero count there never tested whether the bill became law; the September 18 signature is on the White House record.

  • Sept 15, 2026 — House adopts the procedural rule H. Res. 1530, 214–211 (Clerk roll call 300), making one motion to concur in the Senate amendments in order.
  • Sept 16, 2026 — House concurs in the Senate amendments, 262–159 (Clerk roll 308: Republican 203–7, Democratic 58–152, Independent 1–0; 12 not voting).

TariffsSTATUS: CONFIRMED — counter-tariffs of 15/25/50% effective Sept 8, 2026 at 12:01 a.m. ET

Client Advisory: Canada Confirms 15/25/50% Retaliatory Tariffs on C$27.6B of US Imports — Effective September 8, 2026

Published August 25, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC

What changed: On August 25, 2026, Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne confirmed Canada will impose counter-tariffs of 15%, 25% and 50% on C$27.6 billion of US imports, effective 12:01 a.m. ET, Tuesday, September 8, 2026. Each product rate matches the corresponding US rate (products drawn from those targeted by US Section 338 and Section 232 tariffs). The official Finance Canada list was revised on August 26 from 874 to 629 tariff items: 413 at 50% (steel & aluminum, furniture, clothing and apparel, pulp and paper), 195 at 25% (appliances, cheese and dairy, carpets, sawn wood, steel/aluminum derivatives), and 21 at 15% (air conditioners, heat pumps, lift trucks, mowers, moulds, tool parts). Fish and seafood were removed in the Aug 26 revision; autos are not on the list (separate existing 25% order). See the full verified tier table →

What importers should know now:

  • Origin + in-transit rules. The tariffs apply only to goods originating from the US (CUSMA marking regulations); US goods already in transit to Canada on Sept 8 are not covered.
  • Tier varies by HTS line. The category-level shorthand is 50% for steel/aluminum, 25% for electronics/appliances/dairy, 15% for farm equipment — but confirm your exact 8-digit HS code against the official 629-item list (revised Aug 26) before booking.
  • Scope is wider than the six focus sectors. The list also covers furniture, apparel/clothing, cosmetics, tools, sporting goods, motorcycles/trailers, and more.
  • Support package. A C$7.5 billion package (C$1.5B Regional Tariff Response Initiative, C$500M BDC Pivot to Grow, C$2B Canada Strong Diversification Fund, C$3.5B Rapid Response Supports) accompanies the measures, on top of nearly C$25B in cumulative supports. Existing Canadian counter-tariffs on US autos remain in place.
  • Watch the US side. On Aug 24, 2026 President Trump announced US tariffs on Canadian cars, trucks, auto parts and steel will rise to 50% on January 1, 2027 — announcement only, not yet signed.

Key dates

  • Aug 21, 2026 — US–Canada trade talks collapse; negotiators recalled to Ottawa.
  • Aug 22, 2026 (12:01 a.m. EDT) — US 50% Section 338 duty on C$27.6B (~US$20B) of Canadian goods takes effect.
  • Aug 24, 2026 — Trump announces (Truth Social): US tariffs on Canadian cars, trucks, auto parts and steel to rise to 50% on Jan 1, 2027 (announcement only).
  • Aug 25, 2026 — Canada confirms 15/25/50% counter-tariffs on C$27.6B of US imports; C$7.5B support package.
  • Sep 8, 2026 (12:01 a.m. ET) — Canadian counter-tariffs come into force (629 items, revised Aug 26; US-origin only; in-transit exemption).

TariffsSTATUS: IN FORCE — Proclamation 11059 signed Aug 26, published Aug 31, 2026

Client Advisory: Beef Quota Increase — 300,000 Metric Tons Added for Sept–Nov 2026 (Proclamation 11059)

Published August 21, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC

What changed: The Aug 21, 2026 announcement is now an instrument of record. Proclamation 11059 of August 26, 2026 — "Further Ensuring Affordable Beef for the American Consumer" — was published in the Federal Register on August 31, 2026 (91 FR 55989; FR Doc. 2026-17842). It temporarily increases the 2026 in-quota quantity of the beef tariff-rate quota by 300,000 metric tons (~661 million lbs) for lean beef trimmings under four HTSUS lines, released first come, first served in three 100,000 MT tranches: Sept 1–30, Oct 1–30, and Oct 31 until filled or Nov 30, 2026. Covered entries pay the 4.4¢/kg in-quota duty instead of the 26.4% out-of-quota rate — the relief is real, but it is not duty-free. Retail context is unchanged: ground beef averaged $6.89/lb in July 2026 (vs. $5.55/lb in Jan 2025).

What importers should know now:

  • It is a proclamation, not an executive order — and it is in force. The August announcement described an executive order to be signed within two weeks; what issued was Proclamation 11059, signed Aug 26 and published Aug 31, 2026. The relief runs through the beef TRQ, not as a stand-alone tariff waiver.
  • Three fixed tranches, first come, first served. 100,000 MT Sept 1–30, 2026 · 100,000 MT Oct 1–30, 2026 · 100,000 MT Oct 31 – Nov 30, 2026 or until the added quantity is filled, whichever is earlier. CBP administers the added quantity.
  • Scope is published. Lean beef trimmings under HTSUS statistical reporting numbers 0201.30.5091, 0201.30.5097, 0202.30.5091, 0202.30.5097. The entire additional quantity is allocated to "other countries or areas"; Argentina's 80,000 MT allocation from Proclamation 11010 is unchanged.
  • Duty treatment is settled — and it is not duty-free. Entries under the added quota are subject to the in-quota rate of duty (4.4¢/kg). What they avoid is the 26.4% out-of-quota rate. The Aug 21 open question about the 4.4¢/kg baseline is therefore answered.
  • The 25% discount is a monitored condition. USDA and USTR must monitor whether the imports are sold at 25% below the market price for lean beef trimmings; if not, they must notify the President, who may eliminate what remains of the increase. It is not an exporter price guarantee, and the benchmark is trimmings — not retail.
  • Model it now. Run the scenario in the Tariff Calculator 2026 — select "Ground Beef — Proclamation 11059 Beef TRQ Increase (Sept–Nov 2026)" and an import date inside the tranche window (Sept 1 – Nov 30, 2026); outside the window the 26.4% out-of-quota rate applies.

TariffsSTATUS: IN EFFECT — 50% additional duty applies as of Aug 22, 2026

Client Advisory: Canada Section 338 50% Tariffs — IN EFFECT Since Aug 22, 2026

Published August 19, 2026Updated August 22, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC

What changed: The 50% Section 338 tariffs on Canadian goods took effect Saturday, August 22, 2026 at 12:01 a.m. EDT (04:01 GMT) after three days of talks in Washington failed to finalize a deal. The duties apply to roughly $20 billion of Canadian exports (~5% of Canada's annual exports to the US) — products ranging from hockey sticks to tongue depressors — under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 (signed July 20, 2026; first-ever use of the authority). USTR Jamieson Greer, minutes before the deadline: "Tonight, Canada declined to finalize the trade deal under the terms agreed earlier this week." There is no USMCA/CUSMA exemption.

What happened: The three proclamations were signed July 20, 2026 with an original August 19 effective date. On August 18, 2026 — hours before the duty was due — President Trump announced a three-day suspension after a preliminary deal, setting a new effective date of August 22. The August 19–21 talks collapsed; PM Carney recalled negotiators to Ottawa and announced dollar-for-dollar retaliation beginning September 8, 2026 (US steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural machinery, paper, electronics).

What importers should do now

  • Book the 50% additional duty for Canadian-origin covered goods entered on or after August 22, 2026, 12:01 a.m. EDT, on top of existing MFN and other duties. No in-transit grace period has been announced.
  • Verify product coverage. The covered list spans dairy (milk, cream, whey, lactose, sugars), alcohol (beer, wine, liquor, cider), wood & hockey equipment, honey, seeds, tulips and orchids, cement, candles, wigs, clothing, chandeliers, ice skates, swimming pools, fishing rods, and more. Official line-level HTS numbers are in the three July 20 proclamations / USTR statement — confirm your HTS against that list, not the shorthand.
  • No USMCA exemption. The duty applies even to CUSMA/USMCA-qualifying goods — do not assume origin qualification protects you.
  • Confirm exemptions: energy products, potash, fish, critical minerals, and items already under Section 232 are outside the duty. Pre-existing steel/lumber/auto tariffs remain in force and stack.
  • Watch Canadian retaliation from September 8, 2026 if you import US steel, dairy, appliances, agricultural machinery, paper, or electronics into Canada.

Key dates

  • Jul 20, 2026 — Three Section 338 proclamations signed: 50% additional duty on ~$20B of Canadian goods; original effective date Aug 19.
  • Aug 18, 2026 (Tue, evening) — Trump announces 3-day suspension; new effective date Aug 22.
  • Aug 19, 2026 (Wed, 12:01 a.m. ET) — Original effective date passed without the duty taking effect (suspension active).
  • Aug 19–21, 2026 — Three days of talks fail; no final agreement.
  • Aug 22, 2026 (Sat, 12:01 a.m. EDT / 04:01 GMT) — 50% duty takes effect. Carney announces dollar-for-dollar retaliation; negotiators recalled to Ottawa.
  • Sep 8, 2026 — Canadian retaliatory tariffs begin (US steel, dairy, appliances, ag machinery, paper, electronics).
