Canada 50% Section 338 Tariff SUSPENDED until Aug 22, 2026 — What Importers Need to Know

Published August 7, 2026 · Updated August 19, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC

⚠️ STATUS: SUSPENDED — Paused until Aug 22, 2026 (NOT in effect, NOT cancelled)

President Trump announced Tuesday evening, Aug 18, 2026 — hours before the 50% duties were due to take effect — that the tariffs are suspended for a three-day period after the U.S. and Canada reached a preliminary deal. The new effective date is Saturday, August 22, 2026 at 12:01 a.m. ET (Carney: "until August 22"; the three-day suspension covers Aug 19–21). U.S.–Canada deal talks are ongoing — if no deal is finalized, the 50% duty takes effect Aug 22.

No proclamation has been withdrawn or amended. The three Section 338 proclamations remain legally in force, so the 50% duty takes effect on Saturday, Aug 22 unless a deal or another extension is announced before then. Deal contents are not yet public — both sides say it is "subject to the finalization of documents."

What happened: On July 20, 2026, President Trump signed three proclamations under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 — the first time that rarely-used authority has been deployed in the modern tariff era — imposing an additional 50% ad valorem duty on Canadian motor vehicles, alcoholic beverages, and dairy products, covering roughly $20 billion of Canadian exports. The duties were scheduled to take effect at 12:01 a.m. ET on August 19, 2026 (30 days after signing) — but on the evening of Aug 18, Trump suspended them for three days, pushing the effective date to Aug 22, saying "based on the fact that Canada and the USA, subject to the finalization of documents, have a DEAL!"

Here's what the pause means, which products are in scope, and how to estimate what the duty will cost you if it takes effect.

Status: what the Aug 18 suspension changes

  • The 50% duty is SUSPENDED until Aug 22, not in effect and not cancelled. The new effective date is 12:01 a.m. ET Saturday, August 22, 2026. Any page or post claiming the tariff "took effect Aug 19" or "hit Aug 19" is stale as of the Aug 18 announcement.
  • Legal authority intact. No proclamation has been withdrawn or amended under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 (19 U.S.C. 1338) — the first-ever modern use of the authority. If talks collapse, the 50% duty takes effect Aug 22.
  • Deal is not final. USTR Greer's office describes the deal as "comprehensive market access for all American goods, economic security commitments, digital trade alignment" — no specifics public. Carney: "Substantial progress has been made, although there is important work still to be done."
  • Keystone XL is NOT a deal term. Trump's same-night post said the pipeline "may be awoken from the grave!" — a statement of intent/speculation, not a confirmed commitment linked to the tariffs.

Which products are affected

The three proclamations cover three baskets of goods (per the Chicago Fed and the NYT):

  • Motor vehicles (456 HTS subheadings): the widest basket, reaching far beyond cars — it sweeps in agricultural products, cotton apparel, leather goods, fine art, antiques, diamonds, jewelry, furniture, and sporting goods. This is retaliation for Canada's 25% surtax on non-USMCA U.S. vehicles.
  • Alcoholic beverages (61 HTS subheadings): beer, wine, whiskies, rum, vodka, gin, liqueurs, and cider — a response to the provincial liquor bans that cut U.S. alcohol exports to Canada by roughly 81%.
  • Dairy (52 HTS subheadings): milk powders, concentrated whey, caseinates, and lactose, tied to Canada's cheese TRQ rules that the U.S. says discriminate against American dairy.

As the Global News full product list shows, the practical impact is even wider than the three headlines suggest: the annexes run from honey, flower bulbs, and live plants to cement, candles, plastics, rubber, hides, plywood, paper, and even hockey sticks — coverage the White House itself described as ranging "from wine to hockey sticks to cement."

For a business importing any Canadian-origin good, the question is no longer whether you're affected — it's which of the ~569 subheadings your product falls under, and whether the pause ends in a deal or a tariff.

Key rules if the duty takes effect

  • USMCA goods are NOT exempt. Unlike the Section 301 framework, the 50% duty applies to covered goods even if they qualify as originating under the USMCA/CUSMA trade agreement.
  • There is no expiration date. Unlike the temporary Section 122 duty that expired July 24, Section 338 duties stay in place until a future presidential action modifies or revokes them.
  • Exemptions are narrow: energy, potash, goods already subject to Section 232 tariffs (steel, aluminum, copper, vehicles, wood, semiconductors), fish, and critical minerals are excluded from the new duties.
  • No in-transit grace period was announced for the original effective date — and none has been announced for the Aug 22 effective date either.

What happens during the suspension

Canada's Trade Minister Dominic LeBlanc and chief negotiator Janice Charette met USTR Greer in Washington through mid-August, with Carney telling reporters in Saguenay, Quebec: "Will we get all of that by the 19th of August? We'll see. But we want to have pathways in order to get that."

The binding question is now Saturday, August 22 at 12:01 a.m. ET — the new effective date after the 3-day suspension. Three outcomes are possible: the deal is finalized and the suspension becomes a cancellation or extension; the suspension is extended; or talks collapse and the 50% duty takes effect Aug 22. Carney has said Ottawa wants a "comprehensive," "win-win" deal covering steel, aluminum, forestry, autos, and "all strategic sectors," has not ruled out retaliation, and has ruled out using energy exports as leverage. Every day that passes without a signed deal keeps the 50% rate on the table for your next entry.

FAQ: Is the 50% tariff in effect? Does it apply to steel?

Q: Are the 50% Section 338 tariffs on Canadian goods in effect right now?

A: No. They were suspended Aug 18, 2026 for three days — the new effective date is 12:01 a.m. ET Saturday, August 22, 2026 — while the U.S. and Canada continue deal talks. Not in effect and not cancelled — the proclamations remain legally in force and the duty takes effect Aug 22 if talks collapse. The original effective date was 12:01 a.m. ET Aug 19, 2026 (30 days after the July 20 signing), suspended by the Aug 18 proclamation.

Q: Does the suspended Section 338 tariff apply to steel?

A: No. Steel is exempt from the Section 338 50% duty because it is already covered by Section 232 tariffs — Section 232 goods (steel, aluminum, copper, vehicles, wood, semiconductors) are excluded from the new duties. Steel remains subject to US Section 232 steel tariffs, which the calculator does not currently model. On the Canadian side, the August 10 steel transport rebate (CSSP) cuts interprovincial rail/marine steel freight by 50% — trucking excluded. See the CSSP rebate section on the Tariff Calculator.
