The short answer. Claude API pricing in 2026 ranges from $2 per 1M input tokens (Sonnet 5) to $10 per 1M input tokens (Fable 5). Anthropic's flagship Claude Fable 5 costs $10 per 1M input and $50 per 1M output ($1.00 cache read); the value-tier Claude Opus 5 costs exactly half — $5/$25; and Claude Sonnet 5 is $2/$10, made permanent on Aug 10, 2026. But list prices are not what businesses actually run: Ramp's August AI Index shows Fable 5 — Anthropic's most expensive model by far — drew only ~6% of Anthropic business tokens and 11.4% of model-attributed Anthropic spend in July 2026, while cheaper Opus 5 has already passed it in enterprise spending. For most business workloads, the best-value Claude is Opus 5 or Sonnet 5, not the flagship.

Is Claude too expensive? What the Ramp data says

The honest answer: the flagship is, which is why businesses route around it. Ramp's data shows Fable 5 — at $10/$50 per 1M, roughly 2x the price of GPT-5.6 Sol and 5x Sonnet 5 on input — captured just 6% of Anthropic business tokens and 11.4% of model-attributed spend in July. The cheaper Opus 5 moving ahead of the flagship in enterprise spending reinforces the point: businesses do not necessarily want maximum capability all the time, and price wins volume.

That said, "Claude is too expensive" is not the same as "Claude is losing." In July, more US businesses paid Anthropic (43.5%) than OpenAI (39.7%) — Claude's platform-level adoption is the strongest of any vendor. The expensive part is the flagship tier, and the market is voting with tokens for Opus 5 and Sonnet 5 instead.

Agency takeaway: don't default client quotes to Fable 5 list rates. Price retainers against a realistic model mix — Opus 5 / Sonnet 5 / GPT-5.6 Sol capture the volume, and Fable 5 is a premium line for quality-critical work. Best Claude model for business

  • Claude Opus 5 ($5/$25) — the enterprise value frontier. Near-flagship quality at exactly half of Fable 5's price, and per FT-cited Ramp data the model enterprise spend is moving to. Published per-task reference ≈ $2.34 per task (Artificial Analysis, Aug 2026) — a ~6x spread vs cheap small models on the same class of work.
  • Claude Sonnet 5 ($2/$10) — the cost-efficient production workhorse. Input at open-weight parity (Qwen 3.8 Max $2), permanent pricing with no scheduled increase, cache reads at $0.20/1M. The default for high-volume production workloads.
  • Claude Fable 5 ($10/$50) — maximum capability, for quality-critical work where the premium (roughly 2x GPT-5.6 Sol) is justified. Watch the price, the 30-day retention requirement, and the fact that most businesses don't run it.

Rule of thumb: route by task, benchmark on your own workload, and re-run quarterly — 2026 pricing moves weekly (GPT-5.6 Sol Aug 21, DeepSeek Aug 16, Gemini 3.7 Flash Aug 13, Grok 4.6 Aug 12, Claude Sonnet 5 Aug 10).

Fable 5's 30-day data-retention requirement

There's a governance catch on the flagship: under Anthropic's June 9, 2026 policy, a mandatory 30-day data-retention requirement applies to every user's Fable 5 traffic — not just enterprise or government accounts — and Anthropic says the same standard extends to Mythos 5 and future Mythos-class models released through Bedrock and comparable platforms.

  • Zero-data-retention (ZDR) agreements do not extend to Fable 5 or any Mythos-class model; organizations must explicitly enable retention in the Claude Console to route any traffic to Fable 5.
  • This is a real business-adoption barrier for enterprises with strict data-handling requirements — DigitalApplied documents Microsoft limiting internal Fable 5 use over retention, and the policy is a factor alongside price in the adoption numbers above.
  • Pricing is unchanged by the policy: $10 per 1M input / $50 per 1M output.

Frequently asked questions

What is Claude Fable 5 API pricing?

Claude Fable 5, Anthropic's flagship, costs $10 per 1M input tokens and $50 per 1M output tokens, with cache reads at $1.00 per 1M (verified Aug 16, 2026 against Anthropic official pricing). It launched June 9, 2026 and is Anthropic's most expensive model by far — roughly twice the price of OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol.

Is Claude too expensive for businesses?

The flagship is, but most businesses don't run the flagship. Ramp's August 2026 AI Index measured July 2026 business purchases: Claude Fable 5 drew only ~6% of Anthropic business tokens and 11.4% of model-attributed Anthropic spend despite being the most expensive model by far, while cheaper Claude Opus 5 ($5/$25 per 1M) has already passed it in enterprise spend per FT-cited Ramp data. At the same time, 43.5% of US businesses on Ramp's tracker paid Anthropic in July vs 39.7% for OpenAI — Claude overall is the most widely adopted vendor, but price-sensitive businesses are choosing Opus 5 and Sonnet 5 over Fable 5.

What is the best Claude model for business?

For most business workloads, Claude Opus 5 ($5/$25 per 1M, exactly half of Fable 5, launched late July 2026) is the best value — near-flagship quality at half the price, and the model enterprise spend is moving to per FT-cited Ramp data. Claude Sonnet 5 ($2/$10 per 1M, permanent since Aug 10, 2026) is the cost-efficient production workhorse. Claude Fable 5 ($10/$50) is worth its premium only for quality-critical work where maximum capability justifies roughly 2x the price of GPT-5.6 Sol.

How does Claude pricing compare to GPT-5.6 Sol?

Claude Fable 5 is roughly twice as expensive as OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol — $10/$50 per 1M vs Sol's $4/$20 promotional rate (base $5/$30) through Nov 21, 2026. Ramp's August 2026 AI Index shows GPT-5.6 Sol holds ~25% of OpenAI tokens and 23% of OpenAI spend, while Fable 5 generated only ~75% as much model-attributed spend as Sol in July 2026 — the cheaper model wins the volume.

Does Claude Fable 5 require 30-day data retention?

Yes. Under Anthropic's June 9, 2026 policy, a mandatory 30-day data-retention requirement applies to all Fable 5 traffic (Mythos-class models) across every surface — consumer, enterprise, and government. Zero-data-retention (ZDR) agreements do not extend to Fable 5 or Mythos-class models, and organizations must explicitly enable retention in the Claude Console to route any traffic to Fable 5. This is a governance barrier for enterprises with strict data-handling requirements (Microsoft, for example, has limited internal Fable 5 use over retention, per DigitalApplied).
