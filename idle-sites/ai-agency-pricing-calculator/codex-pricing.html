Status: Codex just crossed 20 million active users — and every paid user got a banked reset

Codex and ChatGPT Work crossed 20 million active users during the week of August 21, 2026, per Tibo Sottiaux, OpenAI's Codex & ChatGPT engineering lead — up from 15 million just over a week earlier (Aug 13), and roughly 10 million on July 21.[1][2][5] To celebrate, OpenAI credited every Codex and ChatGPT Work user with a BANKED reset they could use at their leisure.[1]

Rollout status (as of Aug 23, 2026): The reset was promised "during the day" on Aug 21, then given a firmer 8pm PST same-evening deadline for all paid ChatGPT Work and Codex users.[15] 8pm PST came and went with many users reporting no reset; some users did receive it.[2][3] On Aug 23, OpenAI announced it found usage inefficiencies (image-heavy long sessions, Computer History p95+ usage, a title-generation feature) plus a worse cache hit rate, and posted a new reset for 2026-08-24T21:00:00Z (2pm PST).[3]

What a banked reset is (official definition)

Per OpenAI's Help Center, a banked reset is a saved usage-limit reset credit: "Using a full banked reset resets both your 5-hour and weekly usage windows. As a result, your weekly reset date will move to roughly seven days after you redeem it."[4] Banked resets are separate from automatic resets: they appear as available reset credits on the ChatGPT Codex usage page (chatgpt.com/codex/settings/usage) and can be redeemed after you hit a limit, rather than applying immediately.[4][11] Codex is included across ChatGPT plans, including Free and Go; usage limits vary by plan.[4]

What this means for agency pricing and usage-based billing

For agencies billing AI work, the 20M milestone and the banked-reset saga are a capacity and billing-clarity story, not a price story:

  1. No list price changed. Plus $20, Pro 5x $100, Pro 20x $200, Business per seat, and API usage-based rates all stand. Do not raise client quotes because Codex "got more users" — the milestone did not touch the price sheet.[14]
  2. Limits are the real capacity risk. Codex runs on 5-hour and weekly usage windows that vary by plan, and OpenAI does not publish one fixed weekly allowance that applies to every user and workload.[10] Agencies quoting fixed-fee retainers should verify the allowance before pricing, and plan headcount against standard limits — the Aug 21–23 drain complaints (GitHub issues #36468, #36488) show how fast a heavy agent session can consume a week's quota.[10][1]
  3. Banked resets are a scheduling tool, not a billing line. A banked reset lets a blocked migration or a long agent loop keep running without waiting for the 5-hour window — but it resets windows, it does not change token math. Budget it as capacity insurance, never as a cost line in a client quote.[4][11]
  4. Keep subscription and token lines separate in client quotes. This calculator models API/token and project pricing, not seat-based subscriptions — Codex/ChatGPT Work seats are a budget line of their own, and mixing them into a per-task price hides the real consumption profile.[14]
Watch item: OpenAI has acknowledged usage-limit inefficiencies and worse cache-hit rates this week (Aug 23 update) and is fixing them; the Aug 24 reset announcement includes the exact delivery time for the first time.[3] If limits keep draining abnormally fast after the fix, agencies should re-verify the assumptions behind fixed-fee AI retainers. No deliberate "quiet cut" has been confirmed — treat that as critique, not fact.[2]
