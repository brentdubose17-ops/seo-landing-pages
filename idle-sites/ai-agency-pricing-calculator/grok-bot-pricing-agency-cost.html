Published August 16, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC
Grok Bot pricing AI agent cost Bundled AI agent access

The short answer: Grok Bot has no standalone price. It ships inside three existing subscriptions — SuperGrok Heavy (~$300/mo), Cursor Ultra ($200/mo), and Cursor Teams Premium ($120 per seat/mo) — so the real question for an agency is not "what does Grok Bot cost" but "which subscription do I already pay, and does it unlock Grok Bot?"

Why the SERP is confused about Grok Bot pricing

Search results for "Grok Bot pricing" conflict because most posts quote a single plan, or they repeat "$200/mo Cursor Ultra" without mentioning the $120 Teams seat or the ~$300 SuperGrok Heavy route. The three numbers are not contradictory — they are three different entrances to the same bundled product. The verdict:

  • Cheapest solo entry: Cursor Ultra at $200/mo (individual).
  • Cheapest team entry: Cursor Teams Premium at $120/seat/mo — notably cheaper per seat than the solo plan.
  • No-Cursor entry: SuperGrok Heavy at ~$300/mo (individual; not listed on xAI's public pricing page).
  • Effectively free: if your agency already pays Cursor Ultra/Teams Premium or SuperGrok Heavy, Grok Bot is a $0 marginal add.

What's included (and what's not disclosed)

  • Included: always-on agents with a cloud computer, access to the user's tools and apps (including platforms with no clean API), scheduled routines, desktop (macOS) + iOS operation, bot-to-bot coordination with a chief-of-staff bot.
  • Not usage-metered: there is no per-token or per-task price, so there is no surprise-bill risk — but also no published usage caps, so "unlimited" is not a claim any source makes.
  • Authentication mechanics undisclosed: SpaceXAI has not said how bots authenticate to third-party tools.
  • Beta + availability: beta on desktop (macOS) and iOS; enterprise access is on a waitlist.

How an agency should budget for Grok Bot

Because Grok Bot is bundled, the budget line is the eligible subscription, not the agent. For cost-modeling purposes (this is the one place our calculator does not treat Grok Bot as an input):

  1. Benchmark against the retainer it replaces. A Grok Bot doing inbox triage, expense processing, or ops tickets competes with a managed-service retainer, not with per-token APIs. Compare $120–$300/mo of subscription against the $1,500–$5,000+ monthly retainer a human-staffed equivalent would run.
  2. For teams, price per seat. At $120/seat/mo, five seats = $600/mo — under one junior hire in most US markets.
  3. Keep the bundled-vs-metered tradeoff in client conversations. Grok Bot's flat bundled price is the counterpoint to metered blowups; it also means no per-task cost attribution for client billing, so agencies that bill usage-based need a different accounting line.

Frequently asked questions

How much does Grok Bot cost in 2026?

Grok Bot has no standalone price. It is bundled with three subscriptions: SuperGrok Heavy (~$300/mo individual), Cursor Ultra ($200/mo individual), or Cursor Teams Premium ($120 per seat per month). Free, SuperGrok ($30/mo), and SuperGrok Plus ($100/mo) plans do not include Grok Bot as of Aug 12, 2026.

Is Grok Bot included with Cursor Ultra?

Yes. Per SpaceXAI's product page, Grok Bot is included with Cursor Ultra at $200/month for individuals — the plan includes a cloud computer for Grok Bot, access to the user's tools, scheduled routines, desktop and mobile operation, and extended features.

What does Grok Bot cost for a team?

For teams, Grok Bot comes with Cursor Teams Premium at $120 per seat per month — which undercuts the $200 solo Cursor Ultra route on a per-seat basis. SuperGrok Heavy (~$300/mo) is the individual route for access without Cursor.

How should an agency budget for Grok Bot?

Treat the eligible subscription as the line item, because Grok Bot is not usage-metered and has no published usage caps. The cheapest realistic entry for a solo operator is Cursor Ultra at $200/mo; for a team, Cursor Teams Premium at $120/seat/mo. If the agency already pays Cursor or SuperGrok Heavy, Grok Bot is effectively a $0 marginal add.
