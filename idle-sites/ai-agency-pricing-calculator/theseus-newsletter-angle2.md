## EMAIL BODY

**Theseus, explained for agencies: power is now the AI pricing story**

If you price client AI spend, the most important AI deal of August wasn't a model
release — it was a data center venture.

On August 10, Anthropic, Macquarie Asset Management, and Singapore's GIC announced
**Theseus Infrastructure**: a platform to develop, operate, and lease purpose-built
U.S. data centers to Anthropic under long-term agreements. Anthropic is the anchor
tenant. Macquarie and GIC own the platform and fund the majority of each project's
equity. Anthropic separately pledged to pay **100% of grid-upgrade costs** for its
own data centers — through increases to its own electricity charges, not yours.

Three things matter for your agency:

**1. This is not Anthropic leaving AWS.** AWS remains Anthropic's primary training
and cloud provider, and the company has committed more than $100 billion over ten
years for up to 5GW of Trainium capacity. Theseus is dedicated capacity financed by
institutional capital — a hedge against scarcity and overcommitment, not a divorce.

**2. Power, not chips, is the binding constraint.** Gartner forecasts global data
center electricity up 26% in 2026 to 565 TWh — and past 1,200 TWh by 2030, where
the grid may be insufficient. Anthropic's grid-upgrade pledge is the tell: power
access has become the real scarcity, and whoever pays for the grid will build that
cost into serving tokens.

**3. Supply is being locked in years ahead.** Up to 5GW Trainium + ~3.5GW Google
TPU via Broadcom from 2027 means a big share of frontier capacity is already sold
to one customer. Locked-in capacity can hold token prices steady — or even push
them down — while the power cost underneath pushes them up. The per-token outlook
for 2026–2027 is a tug of war between those two forces.

Anthropic's run-rate revenue has now surpassed **$30 billion** (up from ~$9B at the
end of 2025). That scale is exactly why it can sign these deals — and why the rest
of us watch the per-token price line instead.
