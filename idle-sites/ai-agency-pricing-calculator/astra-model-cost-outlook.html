Astra is OpenAI's next major model — first officially named on August 1, 2026 — and it is not released yet. That creates a problem for agencies that price client work on token math: there is no model card, no API rate, and no availability to test against. The only cost signal is a counterfactual (a $2,000 estimate for a research run, not a price). This outlook separates what is confirmed from what is speculation, gives you a comparison table with shipped models, and gives you three scenarios for budgeting before OpenAI publishes real rates.

Quick answer: how much does OpenAI Astra cost?

No official OpenAI Astra pricing exists. The $2,000 figure widely repeated online is a token-cost estimate for OpenAI's August 1 research runs at GPT-5.6 Sol API rates — not a price for the model. Expect a model card and pricing page only when OpenAI ships it; until then any "Astra API pricing" is a reseller claim.

What we know about Astra

The confirmed facts are narrow, and they come from OpenAI itself: Astra exists and is OpenAI's next major model (named Aug 1, referenced Aug 18 and Aug 28). On August 7, OpenAI disclosed it could not rule out Astra meeting the "Critical" cybersecurity capability threshold under its Preparedness Framework — the highest classification, which no prior OpenAI model reached — and paused some Astra workloads until stricter safeguards were in place. On August 18, OpenAI announced a two-week reinforcement-learning pause on deployment-bound models and put its largest planned frontier RL run on hold, with roughly 20% inference-compute monitoring overhead. On August 28, OpenAI notified SpaceX that it will wind down the contract supplying OpenAI models to Cursor (proposed cutoff November 12, 2026) and will not provide future models — explicitly including Astra — under that contract.

What is not confirmed: the leaked "mozaik-alpha-fdm" checkpoint codename, the leaked game/website outputs, a release date (the Sept 3-10 window is an unverified rumor), whether it ships as "GPT-6," and all pricing. For the full leak explainer — what Astra is, what the demos allegedly showed, and the counter-claim that they may be Claude Opus 5 output — see what Astra is and what it means for agencies on Find AI Agency.

The $2,000 math run is not a price

The single most repeated "Astra cost" figure online is a misread. On August 1, OpenAI published Ten advances in mathematics and theoretical computer science and credited "an internal version of Astra, our next major model" with producing ten results, each with a Lean 4 certificate. Then came the line that gets quoted as a price: "The total number of tokens needed to find solutions to these problems would cost roughly $2,000 at Sol API rates."

That is a token-cost estimate for the research runs, computed at GPT-5.6 Sol's API rates — not a price for Astra, not a per-month cost, not a promise about what OpenAI will charge. It is useful for exactly one thing: it tells you the research team burned enough tokens on ten hard math problems to equal $2,000 of Sol usage. It tells you nothing about Astra's per-token rate.

Why cost matters for agencies

Three forces make Astra a real budgeting question even before a price exists:

  • Token burn on "Max effort." The leaked demos were reportedly generated zero-shot (some 1-shot) on a "Max effort" reasoning mode, and the poster claimed "on Max the model tends to think ALOT more than Sol." That claim is unverified — but if a next-gen reasoning model spends substantially more tokens per task than Sol, per-task cost rises even at identical rates. Modeling token volume, not just rate, is the whole game.
  • Monitoring overhead. OpenAI's own August 18 post estimates monitoring overhead at roughly 20% of the inference compute being monitored for Astra-with-tools. OpenAI has said this overhead is not billed to customers, so its effect on your invoice is indirect — but it is a real constraint on how OpenAI can price the model and how much capacity it can sell.
  • Price-war context. OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Sol API pricing over 20% on August 21, 2026 — promo rates $4/$20 per 1M input/output, through at least November 21, 2026 — in a competitive cycle against Claude Opus 5. A flagship replacement launching into that environment has pressure to land at or below Sol's promo, not above it. See GPT-5.6 Sol API pricing for the full window and OpenAI inference monitoring overhead for the ~20% context.

Scenario planning for Astra pricing

Since no price exists, agencies should budget three ways. Each scenario states its assumption explicitly, so when OpenAI ships pricing you know which one broke.

Scenario A — premium flagship (Astra prices above Sol). Assumption: OpenAI prices Astra above GPT-5.6 Sol list ($5/$30), similar to how Fable 5.1 ($10/$50) sits above Opus 5. Planning move: model per-task cost increases on agentic work — especially if Max-effort reasoning multiplies tokens — and re-baseline client retainers that assume Sol economics.
Scenario B — mid (Astra ≈ Sol price points). Assumption: Astra lands at or near Sol's promo ($4/$20) or list ($5/$30). Planning move: swap-in is cost-neutral on rate, but watch token burn — a model that thinks "a lot more than Sol" can raise per-task cost at the same rate. Keep token-volume instrumentation on before and after the swap.
Scenario C — aggressive (Astra at or below Sol). Assumption: the price-war cycle continues — OpenAI cut Sol over 20% in August, and Astra launches into that market. Planning move: treat it as a capacity and margin opportunity; re-price deliverables that were constrained by model cost, and watch whether OpenAI uses Astra pricing to pull agencies off Claude.

Whichever scenario you plan for, the contract language matters: keep model-swap clauses (client agrees the agency may switch underlying models as availability changes) and token-burn clauses (client pays for actual usage above a baseline). The Cursor shift is a live example of why model portability is a cost control: see Cursor's OpenAI model cost shift.

Scenario planning has a capacity half too: a cheaper per-token rate buys nothing if the deployment is capacity-limited before it is budget-limited. The GPT-Live-1 concurrency planner applies the concurrency formula to calls per day and call length against the 25 / 50 / 200 / 300 / 500 session ceilings, applies the busy-hour peak factor, and returns a capacity-limited-before-budget-limited verdict — the check that tells you whether an Astra price cut actually reaches your invoice.

Frequently asked questions

How much does OpenAI Astra cost?

No official pricing as of August 31, 2026. OpenAI has not published a model card, pricing page, or API id, and Astra is not in the public model catalog. Any "Astra API pricing" claim online is a reseller fabrication until OpenAI ships the model.

Will Astra be more expensive than GPT-5.6 Sol?

Unknown. Max-effort reasoning could raise cost per call, but OpenAI's price-war cuts (Sol promo at $4/$20 through at least Nov 21, 2026) suggest competitive pressure to land near or below Sol. Model both directions; don't assume.

What was the $2,000 Astra math cost?

A token estimate for the August 1 research runs at Sol API rates — a counterfactual, not a price. It equals about $2,000 of Sol usage for the ten math problems, and says nothing about Astra's future rate.

How should agencies plan for Astra pricing?

Use the three-scenario framework above (premium / mid / aggressive), keep client contracts with model-swap and token-burn clauses, instrument token volumes before any swap, and re-price when OpenAI publishes real rates.

When will Astra pricing be announced?

Unknown. OpenAI has given no release date or pricing as of August 31, 2026; the Sept 3-10 window is an unverified rumor. Monitor OpenAI's pricing page and the update triggers in the Astra explainer.
