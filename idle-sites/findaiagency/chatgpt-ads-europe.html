ChatGPT Ads Europe: What AI Agencies & SMB Advertisers Need to Know

Published August 22, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC
Paid media / ChatGPT Ads / Europe

On August 24, 2026, ChatGPT Ads expands to 31 European markets — its largest rollout since the U.S. pilot in February 2026, and the moment the channel stops being a U.S.-only conversation. OpenAI announced the expansion on August 18; at launch the platform reaches 40 markets total.

This matters to two audiences on this site: AI agencies that now have a new, sellable managed-service line in Europe, and SMB advertisers who finally have a ChatGPT ad surface to buy — with real caveats about who sees ads, who can buy them, and what targeting is possible under GDPR.

What's changing: EU availability from August 24

The 31 European markets include Germany, France, Spain, Italy, Sweden, Norway, Denmark, the Netherlands, and Austria, plus Poland and Ireland, with Switzerland, Benelux, and Scandinavia also named. That covers most of Western and Northern Europe at day one, with the total footprint reaching 40 markets including the earlier U.S. pilot and the August 11 launch in the UK, Mexico, Brazil, Japan, and South Korea.

The timing matters: OpenAI says the European rollout took longer because GDPR requires explicit consent for personalized targeting. That's a structural difference from the U.S. product — not a temporary gap. EU campaigns will lean on conversation context and brand safety rather than audience graphs.

Who sees the ads: Free and Go tiers only

Ads appear only on the Free and Go plans. Plus, Pro, and Enterprise remain ad-free. Go is OpenAI's budget tier at about €8 per month in Europe (versus roughly €23 for Plus), which means the ad surface is dominated by price-sensitive users doing exactly the kind of discovery conversations advertisers want: trip planning, software selection, furnishing, and hobbies.

How advertisers buy: agency-led first

At launch, buying is agency-led. Advertisers purchase through the OpenAI Ads Solutions team, agency partners, and technology partners — there is no open self-serve Ads Manager yet. A self-serve Ads Manager is expected "later this summer."

For agencies, the agency-led window is the opportunity: partners who get onboarded early own the channel before direct access opens. For SMB advertisers, it means the practical route to ChatGPT Ads in Europe runs through an agency or tech partner today.

What's excluded: premium and sensitive topics

Three exclusions are worth quoting to clients before anyone promises inventory:

  • Premium tiers are ad-free: Plus, Pro, and Enterprise users never see ads, so the surface is Free + Go only.
  • Sensitive topics excluded: ads are not eligible near health, mental-health, or political content.
  • Under-18 accounts excluded from the ad surface entirely.

Health, legal, and finance-adjacent SMBs should verify category eligibility before committing budget — the same manual-review caution that applies to the U.S. product applies in Europe.

Platform maturity: more than a test

The EU launch is not an early prototype. OpenAI says bidding now goes beyond CPM/CPC to conversion optimization, with geo-targeting, custom audiences, the OpenAI Pixel, Conversions API, and third-party measurement live, and "tens of thousands of marketers" already advertising. Measurement is still young — there are no independent EU ROI benchmarks yet — but the plumbing is real.

How agencies should price ChatGPT Ads setup and management

There is no market standard for ChatGPT Ads management yet. Google/Meta agencies typically charge 10–20% of ad spend plus a setup fee. For ChatGPT Ads, a defensible starting point is:

Setup fee (per campaign)€1,000–€2,500Manual, agency-led onboarding; consent-compliant targeting setup; brand-safety checks; conversation-format creative; Pixel/Conversions API wiring.
Management fee15–20% of ad spendAt or slightly above the Google/Meta range — the channel is unproven and manually operated at launch, so it earns a premium until tooling matures.

Two pricing principles to hold:

  1. Structure it as a pilot, not a promise. Quote a test budget and a learning period. Independent ROI benchmarks don't exist yet — promising Google-scale volume is how agencies lose trust.
  2. Anticipate margin compression. The self-serve Ads Manager arrives "later this summer," which will open the channel to direct advertisers and put downward pressure on management fees. Price the manual window now and have a lower-friction tier ready for when self-serve opens.
