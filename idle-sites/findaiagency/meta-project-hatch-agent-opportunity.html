Quick answer: What is a personal AI agent?

A personal AI agent is software that does tasks for you online — filling forms, buying things, booking, researching — using its own computer, holding memory across sessions, and often running in the background. Meta's Project Hatch was the internal codename for the highest-profile consumer version yet, and it is no longer a rumour: Meta shipped it publicly as Muse on September 8, 2026, with its own secure virtual machine, persistent memory, and an approval gate before it sends an email or makes a purchase. [3][4]

What is Meta Project Hatch?

Project Hatch was the internal codename for the consumer personal AI agent Meta shipped publicly as Muse on September 8, 2026. TestingCatalog — the outlet that first published Hatch's leaked pre-release materials — reported on September 2, 2026 that "Meta is moving closer to launching its planned agent super app, previously known internally as Project Hatch," and that the product was being prepared under the launch name Muse, with an internal-testing iOS app replaced by a Muse waitlist experience. [3] Meta itself has never used the name "Hatch" publicly; it announced the product as Muse. [4]

Before launch, the only public picture came from an internal memo covered by Business Insider on August 27, 2026, which described the agent as "Meta's answer to OpenClaw" — one that "can do anything you can do online, like filling out forms, buying something, or performing deep research," because it has its own computer. It has memory that remembers "more than most AIs," works in the background "even when the app is closed," and is designed so that "no two are alike." [1] India Today's August 28 report independently confirmed the same core: online tasks performed on a user's behalf, connectors to email, calendars, Spotify, Instagram and OpenTable, and memory of user details. [2]

The September 8 launch resolves all of it into a documented product, and the resolution is close to the memo: own dedicated machine, own browser, persistent memory, background work that continues after the app closes, and a human approval gate before sensitive actions. [4]

What Meta actually shipped on September 8: Muse

Meta's announcement describes Muse as a personal agent that "doesn't just answer questions, it actually does the work," running on Muse Secure VM — "a dedicated, virtual machine (VM) that houses both the agent and a person's data" — with its own browser. It is powered by Muse Spark, Meta's most capable model to date, and it is reached "in the Muse app or directly in WhatsApp." [4]

The security and payment architecture agencies need to know

Meta built Muse around a threat model rather than a demo, and the details are the citable part for client work: [4]

  • Muse Secure VM — the agent and the user's data live on a dedicated cloud computer, "contained so no one else's agent can reach it." [4]
  • Sentinel — a separate agent runs on the same machine, "kept apart from Muse at the system level." Nothing Muse does reaches the internet unless Sentinel approves it. [4]
  • Credentials are hidden from the agent — "Muse has no visibility into people's passwords or payment methods"; shared credentials go into secure storage so Muse can use them without seeing them. [4]
  • Approval + audit trail — Muse checks before sensitive actions and "shows people a complete audit trail of everything it has done and plans to do." [4]
  • Scoped, revocable access — people choose which apps Muse connects to and how much access it gets, and can disconnect a service at any time; they can opt out of their interactions training Meta's models, and Meta says Muse does not share conversations or VM data with its ad systems. [4]
  • Muse Confidential VM — coming later in 2026, encrypting the whole VM, including conversations, with a key only the user holds. [4]

The payments layer is the part that changes agency deliverables. Muse checks out with Link, built by Stripe, and Meta says Muse is the first AI agent covered by Link's purchase protections — free coverage for damaged or lost items, price drops, no-fee returns and a return guarantee on eligible purchases. Link's wallet for agents "generates a one-time-use card so your real card details stay hidden." Shop Pay and 1Password support are both coming. [4][8][9]

Muse Spark: the model behind Project Hatch

At the core is Muse Spark, Meta's purpose-built multimodal reasoning model from Superintelligence Labs. [4] TestingCatalog's leaked materials described the stack as "built around tool use, computer use, and agentic tasks," which is precisely what a personal agent needs to browse, fill forms and execute multi-step workflows rather than just generate text. [11] That is now Meta's own framing: the launch states Muse is "powered by Muse Spark, Meta's most capable model to date, built for real-world agentic work like this." [4]

It is the same model family we covered in Muse Spark for small agent tasks, and it matters for agencies because the model is built for action: multimodal input, multi-agent orchestration, and agentic reasoning that plans and executes multi-step workflows autonomously. [4] TestingCatalog separately reports Meta is testing a computer-use variant called Ava, unshipped and not yet accessible. [3]

The Instagram shopping question: what is confirmed and what is not

Confirmed. Muse connects to Instagram. Meta's own launch example is Muse "turn[ing] a recipe reel the person saved on Instagram into a grocery list," and launch coverage lists Instagram among the apps Muse can work across. [4][5][10]

Confirmed. Muse can shop and pay for a consumer: it "can open a browser, fill out forms, and negotiate on their behalf," and it checks with the person before making a purchase, paying through Stripe's Link with agent purchase protections and a one-time-use card. [4][9]

Still not confirmed, and not shipped. A dedicated in-feed Instagram shopping agent — discovery from Reels and posts, price comparison, and checkout completed inside Instagram without a click-through — is a separate product. It was reported as "weeks from launch" in early September 2026 and did not ship with Muse. [14][15][16] Treat it as reported-only, targeted before Q4 2026. Any agency content that states it has launched is wrong today.

Project Hatch (Muse) vs OpenClaw: what changed for consumers

OpenClaw lives in the developer world — GitHub repos, API keys, terminal configuration — and "personal AI agent 2026" search results are still dominated by open-source comparisons between OpenClaw, Manus and Lindy. Hatch was Meta's answer to exactly that, and it shipped: Muse is the consumer version, onboarding, connectors and approvals included, for people who have never heard of GitHub. [1][4]

What changed is distribution. A Muse-class agent reaches people who already live in Meta's apps, and it is reachable directly in WhatsApp on day one. [4] When "AI agents that do tasks for you" becomes a consumer app with a free tier, the agency conversation shifts from "can we build this" to "how do we make this useful and safe for clients."

Why autonomous agents with memory + commerce are a new agency build surface

Three capabilities make Muse-class agents a client-viable build surface rather than a demo — and all three are now shipped rather than forecast: [4]

  • Memory — an agent that remembers the user can personalize, and personalization justifies recurring agency work. Muse memory is user-visible and user-deletable, which is a governance hook an agency can own. [4]
  • Commerce — the agent can buy, with an approval gate and a Stripe-issued one-time-use card: payment infrastructure that is already live, not a roadmap item. The still-unshipped Instagram in-feed shopping agent is the clearest next step. [4][14]
  • Social distribution — an agent reachable in WhatsApp and connected to Instagram and Facebook inherits the largest consumer surfaces in the world. [4][10]

For agencies, this mirrors agentic commerce on the Shopify side (Shopify UCP and agentic commerce): storefront, catalog and permission layer become agent-facing infrastructure. The catalog build that follows from it is specified in the Shopify Catalog API agentic commerce playbook. Agencies that treat client catalogs as agent-ready data — clean inventory, structured product info, defined approval rules — build the moat. The flip side is governance: autonomous agents can loop, spend and surprise, so agent cost blowups and agent risk checklists are real client deliverables. How agencies can pitch agent-building to ecommerce clients

Five concrete plays, now written against a shipped product rather than a forecast: [4][8]

  1. Audit the Meta commerce catalog. Is the client's Instagram Shop structured, priced, and discoverable from Reels? A Muse-class agent acting on saved Reels content needs a clean product graph. [4]
  2. Design approval and purchase flows. Muse ships an approval gate before purchases and an audit trail of what it did and plans to do — design the client-side threshold policy (order limits, confirmation steps, human-in-the-loop for high-value orders) that sits alongside it. [4]
  3. Set up agent memory governance. Muse lets users see what it remembers and tell it to forget. Define what the agent may remember about a client's customers, who can read it, and how deletion is evidenced — a deliverable agencies can own and bill. [4]
  4. Prepare for agent checkout rails. Link's agent wallet issues a one-time-use card and Shop Pay is coming; make sure the client's product data, pricing and return policy are legible to an agent that compares and buys on a person's behalf. [4][8][9]
  5. Monitor the two open items. The in-feed Instagram shopping agent (before Q4 2026, reported) and Muse Shared Agents (reported, unreleased). Set client re-engagement dates at Meta Connect, September 23–24, 2026, and before the Q4 window. [16][20][22]

What Meta sells to businesses: the customer-facing agent and the Platform

Meta sells two products to businesses. The customer-facing Meta Business Agent answers customers on WhatsApp and Messenger, with Instagram added in Meta’s June 3, 2026 announcement; the Meta Business Agent Platform is infrastructure to build, customize and deploy agents at scale, live to partners and integrated clients from July 1, 2026. [17]

Meta’s own documentation publishes the meter: $2.00 per 1M tokens, about 20,000–25,000 tokens per message, roughly 4–5 cents. Per-token charging began August 1, 2026, service messages are charged from October 1, 2026, and the June 3 “getting started is free” line is superseded. [18]

Meta’s business agents: which client workflows qualify — and what a message costs

Meta sells a customer-facing agent it prices per token — about 4–5 cents per message — so the question is which client conversations qualify and what the meter is allowed to reach. [18]

The number must run on the WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API) — not the WhatsApp Business app — in authorized countries, with business verification and one agent per number; every vertical except Finance, Government, Health, Alcohol, Gambling, over-the-counter drugs and matrimony services is supported. [18]

For the implementation side of that number — adding and verifying it, registering for Cloud API access, the Terms-of-Service and template checks — see who does the WhatsApp Business setup, and what Meta’s WhatsApp Business Tools MCP server changes about it.

The control an agency can govern is the Agent Budget Cap — token usage or AI agent turns over 1, 7, 14 or 30-day windows that roll rather than reset, in the account’s timezone. Billing gates delivery: no payment method, no delivered messages, though an allowlisted-only agent can be tested. [18]

The agency service line: which clients to pitch and what to say no to

The mapping worth selling is fit, not build: high-volume, structured, repetitive conversations clear the bar; open-ended and compliance-sensitive ones do not. The deliverable is the handoff boundary — what the agent owns, what it escalates. [18]

Meta’s own page states that more than one million businesses were already using a Meta Business Agent (WhatsApp and Messenger, June 3, 2026, no cadence qualifier). The Q2 2026 earnings call restated that figure on a different basis — weekly use, attributed to the quarter’s global rollout. It is a floor, not a demand signal: fit is the differentiator. [17][19]

It also points inward: Stilla announced on September 9, 2026 that it is joining Meta (no terms), built for team- and company-facing work, with the transfer reported as pending the deal’s close — see Meta’s search index push. [21]

What is Meta Project Hatch?

TestingCatalog reported on September 2, 2026 that the product would launch under the name Muse, and Meta announced Muse on September 8, 2026. [1][3][4]

Is Meta Project Hatch launched?

Yes. It launched on September 8, 2026 under the public name Muse, rolled out in the US on iOS, Android and muse.ai with WhatsApp access, initially for users 18 and over. It is free for most uses, with paid plans at $20/month (Power) and $100/month (Maximum); the pre-launch $199.99/month figure never landed. Will Meta Project Hatch let AI agents shop on Instagram?

Partly, and not the way agencies expected. Confirmed: Muse connects to Instagram — Meta's own example is Muse turning a saved Instagram recipe reel into a grocery list — and Muse can shop and pay, checking out with Link built by Stripe using a one-time-use card, with Meta saying it is the first AI agent covered by Link's purchase protections. Still unconfirmed and unshipped: a dedicated in-feed Instagram shopping agent that completes checkout inside the feed. That separate product was reported as weeks from launch on September 4, 2026 and did not ship with Muse. [4][9][14][16]

What does Meta Project Hatch mean for AI agencies?

It shipped, so the agency question moved from speculation to implementation. Muse makes autonomous agents with memory, payments and social distribution a mainstream consumer product, which turns agent-readiness into a client-ready agency service: auditing ecommerce catalogs, designing purchase approval flows, governing what an agent remembers, and preparing storefronts for agent checkout. The confirmed payment rail is Stripe's Link with agent purchase protections, so agent-legible catalog and checkout data is now a concrete deliverable rather than a forecast. [4][8][9]

Have Meta’s Muse “Shared Agents” launched?

No. As of September 15, 2026, the only source for Muse’s hidden Shared Agents section reports Meta has not publicly released it, and calls the business-suite expansion an assumption. Meta’s Muse launch page, the Business Agent changelog and a 61-post newsroom sweep carry zero “shared agent” or “sub-agent” hits. Reported, not released. [20][17][18]

What would Meta Connect on 2026-09-23/24 change for an agency?

Only the dates are confirmed from Meta (September 23–24, Menlo Park); the agenda is not verifiable from any Meta-served page: meta.com/connect, its agenda and sessions paths and developers.meta.com/connect serve JavaScript shells carrying none of the reported session strings, so a session list is a reported possibility, not an agenda fact.
