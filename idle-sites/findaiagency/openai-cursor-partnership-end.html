Quick answer: Does Cursor still use OpenAI?

Yes, for now. On August 28, 2026, OpenAI notified SpaceX that it will wind down the contract providing OpenAI models to Cursor, with a proposed shutoff date of November 12, 2026. Until that date, Cursor users can still access OpenAI models inside the editor. After the cutoff, Cursor will lose direct access to OpenAI models and will not receive OpenAI's upcoming Astra model. Cursor says OpenAI models served about 5% of its user traffic, with the rest coming from Anthropic, Google, and xAI models.

What happened: the Aug 28 notice and the Nov 12 cutoff

On August 28, 2026, OpenAI notified SpaceX that it intends to wind down the contract supplying OpenAI models to Cursor — the AI coding agent SpaceX acquired for $60 billion — with a proposed shutoff date of November 12, 2026. OpenAI also said it will not provide future models to Cursor, including its upcoming Astra model.

"Today, we notified SpaceX that we intend to wind down our contract providing OpenAI models to Cursor, with a proposed shutoff date of November 12, 2026. To maximize the time that developers can retain access to our models through Cursor, we are giving the maximum notice provided by our contract. This decision was incredibly tough, as we care deeply about our models being broadly available for developers. We are making this choice because we cannot be confident that SpaceX will use our technology within our terms of service, based on our experience with Elon Musk's companies violating contracts." — OpenAI, August 28, 2026

OpenAI did not accuse Cursor of violating terms — it said it has worked with Cursor for nearly four years and has "enormous respect for their team, their product, and what they've built for the developer community." The termination targets Cursor's new owner: Bloomberg broke the story at 10:16 PM EDT on August 28.

Why OpenAI is ending the contract

OpenAI's stated reasoning: after the change of ownership it cannot be confident that SpaceX will use its technology within OpenAI's terms, based on experience with Musk's companies violating contracts. OpenAI pointed to two precedents — Twitter (which it describes as "now part of SpaceX") broke the terms of its contract "alongside many others," and Musk admitted under oath earlier this year that xAI, "now also part of SpaceX," had violated OpenAI's terms.

The custom agreement gave OpenAI "a limited time window to cancel after a change of control" — the mechanism it is using now. On Astra, OpenAI cited "a new level of accountability to ensure our upcoming model, Astra, is being used in accordance with our terms" — future models, Astra included, are off the table for Cursor.

Musk dismissed the move on X early on August 29: "I couldn't care less."

The $60B SpaceX acquisition timeline

  • April 2026: SpaceX secures the rights to buy Cursor, or pay $10 billion to "work together" with the company.
  • June 16, 2026: SpaceX announces it will move forward with the $60 billion acquisition; Cursor becomes a wholly owned subsidiary when the deal closes in Q3.
  • August 14, 2026: SpaceX completes the $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Anysphere, Cursor's parent company.

Cursor is a large prize — $3B+ annual recurring revenue, 1M+ daily active users — and the ownership change triggered OpenAI's cancellation window.

Cursor's ~5% claim: OpenAI models are already the minority

Cursor co-founder and CEO Michael Truell responded on August 29:

"We're sorry to see that OpenAI put out a note saying they plan to block Cursor users from accessing OpenAI models in three months. OpenAI models serve about 5% of Cursor user traffic, and we're speaking with the OpenAI team to resolve this." — Michael Truell, Cursor CEO

The 5% figure is Cursor's claim, not an independently audited number, but it matches the multi-model platform: Anthropic, Google, and xAI models already served 95% of traffic before any of this. Cursor's model list still includes GPT-5.6 Luna/Sol/Terra alongside Claude Fable 5, Opus 5, and Sonnet 5, Gemini 3.1 Pro and 3.7 Flash, and Grok 4.5 and 4.6 — with Grok 4.5, shipped with SpaceXAI in July, on every plan. So "OpenAI models in Cursor" is a slice of the pie, not the pie — most daily work runs on Anthropic, Google, or xAI models, and those aren't going anywhere.

Anthropic's counter-move: more Claude compute for Cursor

Hours after the announcement, Anthropic moved to lock in the displaced traffic. Tom Brown, Anthropic co-founder and Chief Compute Officer:

"Cursor has been a trusted partner of Anthropic since Sonnet 3.5. We'll continue to increase compute to support Claude models in Cursor and are excited for what comes next with them at SpaceX." — Tom Brown, Anthropic

The pledge builds on an existing partnership: in May 2026, Anthropic agreed to use all of the compute capacity at SpaceX's Colossus 1 data center — more than 300 megawatts, over 220,000 NVIDIA GPUs. The practical read for agencies: Claude capacity inside Cursor is expanding, not shrinking, in the near term.

What agencies should do now

  1. Audit which Cursor workflows actually depend on OpenAI models. Cursor says ~5% of traffic, but per-team mixes vary — a GPT-5.6-centric team will feel the cutoff far more than a Claude/Grok team.
  2. Re-price per-developer model costs. As of August 2026 list prices, Claude Opus 5 ($5/$25 per million tokens) undercuts GPT-5.6 Sol ($5/$30) by about 20% on output, and Anthropic bills long context at standard rates where OpenAI charges a long-prompt premium ($10/$45 for Sol) — though Claude's newer tokenizer emits ~30% more tokens for the same text, so per-token deltas don't map 1:1 to workloads. Our AI coding agent pricing guide has the full math, and our Cursor model-cost shift analysis runs the coding-workload numbers for the OpenAI-to-Claude re-route.
  3. Test before November 12. Run Claude Code, Copilot, and Cursor-with-Grok on your own repos before the cutoff — the worst time to find a dependency is the day after it disappears.
  4. Watch the Anthropic–SpaceX compute partnership. Claude capacity in Cursor is expanding, which makes the "switch everything" panic overkill for most agencies.

Bottom line: keep your stack portable. Model access inside any tool can change on a contract's notice period — agencies that can swap models per client and price per developer on real usage won't feel the cutoff.
