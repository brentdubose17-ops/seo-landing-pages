Microsoft Retires Copilot Consumer Features by Aug 18: What Agencies Should Do

Published August 15, 2026Updated August 15, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC
AI tools

What's happening. On August 13, 2026, Microsoft announced it is merging its consumer Copilot app and its business-oriented Microsoft 365 Copilot app into a single updated Copilot experience — and retiring a set of consumer Copilot features by August 18, 2026.

Features going away by Aug 18

  • Group Chat — retired. Group chats migrate to individual 1:1 chats with Copilot; content shared by other participants (including images) will not be accessible afterward. Download media before the update.
  • AI-generated Podcasts — no longer available after August 18; download individual podcasts first.
  • Deep Research (consumer) — retired in the consumer Copilot app. Saved research remains, but no new reports. Paid replacement: Researcher, for Microsoft 365 Premium subscribers.
  • Copilot Labs experiments — retired. Content created in Labs (3D assets, audio clips) does not carry forward; save before updating.

Nuance: Aug 18 is firm for Podcasts and Deep Research; Group Chat and Labs vary by account and rollout wave. (Mico, the animated voice character, relocates to Microsoft's Learn Live — a move, not a retirement.)

Why the app consolidation matters

The apps themselves are merging: users of both apps with a personal account get chats and content merged into one; files move to OneDrive; personal and work/school accounts stay separate. Copilot chat becomes available to personal Microsoft 365 Copilot app users at no extra cost. Mobile and web change mid-August; Windows and Mac follow mid-September.

Why this is a concrete example of AI product churn

The announcement-to-effective window is five days. Agencies that recommended Group Chat, Podcasts, Deep Research, or Labs now have a feature that disappears before the invoice is due. It's not isolated: Claude is merging Cowork into Chat, OpenAI is merging Operator into ChatGPT, and Google is folding deep research into Gemini. Feature retirement and app consolidation are now normal in AI tools. Tool-comparison tables that treat "free" capabilities as permanent are a liability.

What to do before Aug 18

  1. Re-audit your recommended AI tool stack. Verify feature availability at the moment of every recommendation — and on a cadence, not just at contract time. Flag experimental, free, or paywalled features: those are the ones at risk.
  2. Identify alternatives now. Deep Research users can move to Researcher (Microsoft 365 Premium) or another research tool. Test replacements before the deadline, not after clients hit a broken workflow.
  3. Communicate changes to clients before the retirement date. If a client's workflow touches Group Chat, Podcasts, Deep Research, or Labs, tell them now: export reports, download podcasts, save Labs content, and download group-chat media before Aug 18. A five-day window means the email cannot wait.

Bottom line

Microsoft turned a five-day warning into a hard cutoff on features agencies actively recommend. Agencies that keep tool stacks under continuous review — and tell clients about changes before they break — turn churn into trust. The rest will be explaining it on August 19.
