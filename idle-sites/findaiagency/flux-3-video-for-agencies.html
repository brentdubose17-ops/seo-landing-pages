Quick answer: Can agencies build client work on FLUX 3 Video?

Yes, and the math is unusually clean. FLUX 3 Video generates up to 20-second clips with native audio from $0.17/second at HD (drafts at $0.06/s), through one API with three modes — text-to-video, image-to-video, and video continuation. For an agency, draft iteration costs cents while the delivered spot bills at creative rates. The honest caveats: FLUX 3 Image is not available yet, and the headline quality numbers are BFL's own preliminary evaluations, not independent results.

What is FLUX 3?

FLUX 3 is Black Forest Labs' multimodal foundation model, trained jointly on images, video, and audio, with action-prediction work (FLUX-mimic) on top. The company announced FLUX 3 in early access on July 23, 2026. The Decoder reports FLUX 3 Video became generally available through the BFL API and select partners on August 5, 2026 — note that bfl.ai's own blog post still says "early access," so the GA date is a press-attributed fact.

Two things matter for an agency decision. First, FLUX 3 Image is not available — BFL says it enters early access "in the following weeks." Don't build a pitch that implies it ships. Second, this is the first major video model with a published per-second price an agency can bill around, which changes how you scope and quote video work.

FLUX 3 Video capabilities agencies can sell

One request shape, three modes, per BFL's docs:

  • Text-to-video (t2v): prompt to clip, up to 20 seconds.
  • Image-to-video (i2v): 1–10 reference images, including start- and end-frame pairs — useful for product shots and brand consistency.
  • Video continuation (v2v): extend or continue from a reference clip, 5–15 seconds per generation.

Keyframe pairs [seconds, image] pin scene transitions — the storyboard hook for agencies that want directed, not random, output. Generations run up to FHD (1920×1088 for 16:9) at 24 fps, with aspect ratios including 9:16, 1:1, 3:4, 4:3, 21:9, and 2:1 — so vertical social is first-class, not a crop.

The differentiator is native synchronized audio: dialogue, sound effects, and ambience on every output, with multilingual speech and lipsync. The Decoder puts the language count at more than 14 languages (BFL's own pages just say "multilingual," so quote 14+ with that attribution). Draft mode renders previews at roughly one-third cost, and a draft_cache bundle reproduces the exact shot at full quality via draft_enhance — iterate cheap, commit once. BFL also claims agentic chaining of clips into multi-minute sequences with character consistency; that claim is not proven in production yet, so treat it as a pilot item, not a promise.

Five client offerings you can package on FLUX 3

  1. Ad creative (paid + social): 9:16 t2v spots up to 20s with native VO and SFX. Generate five draft variations (~$0.60 each), pick winners, commit at full quality ($5.80 for a 20s FHD spot). Sell a "3 concepts, 1 delivered spot" package with a defined price.
  2. Social video (TikTok/Reels/Shorts): vertical native, audio included, typography on model — a weekly content drop for local businesses that can't afford editors.
  3. Localization: one master shot, no asset translation. Multilingual speech plus lipsync per market from a single clip — a premium add-on for brands selling across regions.
  4. Keyframe-controlled transitions: [seconds, image] pairs pin scene changes. Package as "animated storyboards" or product renders built from the client's own stills.
  5. Multi-shot chaining / character continuity: v2v carries a character into a new scene from a reference clip. This is BFL's claim, unproven at scale — run a paid pilot before you promise a series.

FLUX 3 vs Runway, Kling, Seedance, and other video models

BFL's own evaluations put FLUX 3 against Runway Gen-4.5, Luma Ray 3.2, Kling v3 Pro, Seedance 2.0, Gemini Omni Flash, Grok Imagine Video, and Happy Horse v1/1.1, with Minimax H3 in the Elo comparisons. BFL reports its model was preferred over Runway Gen-4.5 in 77% of comparisons, Luma Ray 3.2 in 93%, Grok Imagine Video in up to 69%, Kling v3 Pro in 60%, and Happy Horse 1.1 in 57%. The Decoder's Elo table puts FLUX 3 top of text-to-video (1,135) and image-to-video (1,051).

Caveat before you repeat any of that to a client: these are BFL's own numbers on an early checkpoint, with no published methodology, sample sizes, or rater counts, and no independent benchmark exists yet. Treat them as directional, not proof.

What agencies can verify themselves today: per-second pricing with a draft tier, native audio on every output, and keyframe control. Those are workflow facts, not marketing claims. How agencies should start: API vs Playground vs licensing

API and Playground are the same price, so the choice is workflow: API for scalable client work and automation, Playground for demos and quick pilots. The draft-to-final loop (draft → draft_cache → draft_enhance) is the repeatable agency pattern: cheap exploration, one committed render.

For licensing, BFL's pricing page lists a Professional tier explicitly "for agencies and service providers" — fine-tuning and LoRA rights, multiple domains and licensed users — worth a sales conversation if you're packaging video as a productized line. For enterprise objections, BFL advertises SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001:2022 compliance, which covers the security question most larger clients will ask.

What to verify before you pitch clients

  • Quality numbers are BFL's own, preliminary. Quote them as self-reported and offer a pilot instead of guarantees.
  • FLUX 3 Image is still weeks out. Scope image work on other tools until it ships.
  • No public SLA/uptime, and no early-access approval-rate data. Don't promise 99.9% availability.
  • Continuity and multi-minute chaining are claims, not production reality. Run a paid pilot on a real client asset before promising a series.
  • Pricing can change during early access. BFL says evaluations and performance will improve; prices may move — build a re-quote clause into retainers.
