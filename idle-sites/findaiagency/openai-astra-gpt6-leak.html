Between August 29 and 31, 2026, the first claimed outputs from OpenAI's unreleased next-generation model circulated on X and Discord under the checkpoint codename mozaik-alpha-fdm: a Grand Theft Auto 2-style game, complete websites, 3D objects, and voxel environments, reportedly generated in a single pass on "Max effort."

For agency owners, this is the story to watch: Astra is OpenAI's next model, the model behind a cyber-safety pause, and the model OpenAI barred from Cursor.

One caveat: none of the leaked outputs is confirmed by OpenAI, and the leak's authenticity is already disputed. Treat everything else as reported, not established.

What is OpenAI Astra?

Astra is OpenAI's next major model, first officially named on August 1, 2026 in a mathematics research post: "The results were achieved by an internal version of Astra, our next major model." OpenAI has called Astra its upcoming model twice since: an August 18 safety-pacing post and an August 28 decision about Cursor.

As of August 31, 2026, Astra is not released: no release date, no model card, no pricing, and no listing in OpenAI's public model catalog.

Confirmed by OpenAI

  • August 1 — Named "our next major model"; an internal version produced ten math results with Lean 4 certificates; the runs "would cost roughly $2,000 at Sol API rates."
  • August 7 — Disclosed it could not rule out Astra meeting the "Critical" cybersecurity capability threshold under its Preparedness Framework — the highest classification, which no prior OpenAI model reached. Non-compliant workloads were paused; testing moved to isolated environments with government agencies and selected AI-safety organizations.
  • August 18 — Two-week reinforcement-learning (RL) pause on deployment-bound models; largest planned frontier RL run on hold; ~20% inference-compute monitoring overhead.
  • August 28 — Notified SpaceX it will wind down the contract providing OpenAI models to Cursor; Astra named as the upcoming model it will not provide under that contract.

Not confirmed

  • The codename mozaik-alpha-fdm — "OpenAI has not announced a release date or confirmed that 'mozaik-alpha-fdm' is an official codename."
  • That the leaked outputs are actually Astra's — disputed on August 31.
  • The zero-shot / one-shot "Max effort" claims — "one shot" is a claim by the posters, not a verified property of the demonstrations.
  • Any release window (the Sept 3–10 rumor), pricing, availability, or the "GPT-6" branding.

The leak: what mozaik-alpha-fdm allegedly produced

The earliest public demo claim surfaced at 08:28:36 UTC on August 29 from X account @XIVIX_134: "OpenAI has just dropped its first internal Astra checkpoint: mozaik-alpha-fdm," with two outputs "generated 1-shot on Max effort." Sixty-eight seconds later, @Lentils80 amplified it — "we have the FIRST EVER public outputs of it" — and repeated that "Both outputs are zero-shot on Max effort." TestingCatalog published the first article the same day — its X account announcing "First outputs of the upcoming unreleased model from OpenAI have arrived, and these results are stunning!" — attributing the outputs to the zAI Discord community: "a GTA 2-style game in one attempt, alongside detailed websites, 3D objects, and voxel environments," generated on Max effort.

The counter-claim. On August 31, pseudonymous developer teortaxesTex claimed the leaked samples are actually Claude Opus 5 output, not Astra's. Kingy's fact-check found none of the media shows an OpenAI model selector, an Astra model ID, the original prompt, or a complete generation session. For planning, the demos are directional — not proof of what Astra ships.

Is Astra the same as GPT-6?

Not officially. Per The Information, OpenAI still hasn't decided whether Astra ships as GPT-6, a GPT-5 point release (like GPT-5.7), or a separate tier alongside Sol, Terra, and Luna. TestingCatalog: "GPT-6 remains a plausible public name, but OpenAI has given no indication that it has chosen this branding." Russian outlet modelora.ru likewise reported OpenAI did not confirm the GPT-6 rumors.

"GPT-6" dominates headlines because it is the public's shorthand — but OpenAI has never used the name.

When is GPT-6 / Astra coming?

OpenAI has not announced a release date, model card, or public name for its next model, Astra. A Sept 3–10 window circulating online is an unverified leak rumor. As of August 31, 2026, Astra is not in OpenAI's model catalog, and the company paused parts of its training in August over cyber-safety review.

The Sept 3–10 window traces to X posts and aggregators, not OpenAI — Kingy's tracker calls it "still a rumor." Polymarket's "GPT-6 released by" event priced an Astra release within August at roughly 13%. A federal voluntary 30-day pre-release review framework took effect on August 1, and Astra is expected to be the first model through it.

The honest answer today: no confirmed date.

Why OpenAI paused parts of Astra

Astra is the first OpenAI model that may cross the Critical cyber capability threshold — the top of the Preparedness Framework ladder. On August 7, OpenAI said internal evaluations showed "significant advancements in agentic coding and cybersecurity," and it could not rule out critical cyber capabilities. Non-compliant work was paused; the model moved to isolated, sandboxed environments; and OpenAI confirmed "relevant government agencies and selected AI safety organizations will participate in testing."

On August 18, OpenAI published Pacing model development in an era of cyber-critical capabilities: a two-week pause in RL training on deployment-bound models, its largest planned frontier RL run on hold, and an extra monitoring requirement on all Astra inference with tools — "monitoring overhead at roughly 20% of the inference compute being monitored." See OpenAI paused Astra training in August.

For agencies, the takeaway is timing risk: a model under this level of review can be delayed, gated, or changed before launch. Build client commitments around shipped, stable models.

Astra and the Cursor breakup

On August 14, 2026, Cursor (Anysphere) announced it had officially become part of SpaceX after a reported $60 billion acquisition. On August 28, OpenAI notified SpaceX that it intends to wind down the contract providing OpenAI models to Cursor, with a proposed shutoff date of November 12, 2026. OpenAI's rationale: "we cannot be confident that SpaceX will use our technology within our terms of service, based on our experience with Elon Musk's companies violating contracts." Astra is explicit: "we also have a new level of accountability to ensure our upcoming model, Astra, is being used in accordance with our terms."

Practical effects: current OpenAI models continue through the proposed transition, but no future OpenAI models — Astra included — will be provided under the contract; workarounds (BYO API keys, compatible gateways, the Codex IDE extension) cover only supported local Chat+Agent requests — not Cursor Tab, Auto, Cloud Agents, Background Agents, Automations, CLI, API, or SDK. If you standardized on Cursor's OpenAI integration, you now have a hard date to re-plan your model stack — see the OpenAI–Cursor split and what still works.

Is Astra better than Claude or Gemini?

There are no public head-to-head benchmarks between OpenAI's unreleased Astra and Claude or Gemini — it is unmeasured, not proven. OpenAI's only public evidence is the August 1 math results (10 open problems with Lean 4 certificates). For shipping models, compare GPT-5.6 Sol, Claude Fable 5/Opus 5, and Gemini 3.x directly.

There is no public data behind this question yet. Kingy's verdict is the framing: "Astra itself is real... but the viral demo clips do not prove they were made by Astra." Until OpenAI publishes a benchmark — a SWE-bench Verified run, an agentic-coding eval, or a model card — any "Astra beats X" claim is rumor or marketing. Not to be confused with Google's Project Astra

"Astra" is overloaded. OpenAI Astra is an unreleased OpenAI model family. Google's Project Astra is a DeepMind multimodal assistant prototype — "a universal assistant that sees, hears, remembers, and acts across devices and Google products" — and the two are "unrelated projects" that "happen to share a name." Add Astral (a Python tooling company) and Astra Platform (a Google Cloud product): four different products inside one keyword. Before trusting a claim about "Astra" coding, pricing, or availability, check which product the source means.

What Astra means for agency workflows

Coding and agent work. OpenAI's internal evaluations reportedly show "significant advancements in agentic coding and cybersecurity" — the capability axis the demos dramatize. If a shipped Astra delivers long-horizon, tool-using generation at that level, the agency differentiator shifts from "we prompt well" to "we supervise agents that build" — start the staffing and pricing conversation now.

One-shot generation (unverified). The leak claims a full website or a playable game in a single pass on Max effort. If real, that compresses scoping-to-prototype timelines dramatically — but provenance is disputed, so don't re-plan delivery on it yet.

Cost planning. Max effort "spends substantially longer reasoning than GPT-5.6 Sol," which risks pushing per-call cost up — and there is no Astra pricing to model against yet. Model mix. If Astra ships and stays barred from Cursor, expect pressure to consolidate around OpenAI's own surfaces (ChatGPT, Codex) or rebalance toward Claude and Gemini in the IDE — the choice Cursor users are already making. Keep client contracts flexible: model-swap and token-burn clauses protect you when the frontier shifts mid-engagement.

How much will Astra cost?

No official OpenAI Astra pricing exists. The $2,000 figure widely repeated is a token-cost estimate for the August 1 research runs at GPT-5.6 Sol API rates — not a price for the model. Expect a model card/pricing page only when OpenAI ships it; until then any "Astra API pricing" is a reseller claim.

The only official cost figure is the August 1 counterfactual: the math runs "would cost roughly $2,000 at Sol API rates." That describes research runs, not a product price. For context, OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Sol's API price on August 24 — 20% on input, roughly 33% on output — a temporary promotion through at least November 21, 2026, at a promotional rate of $4/$20 per 1M tokens. If Astra ships with a Max-effort mode that reasons longer, per-call cost could rise even in a price-cutting market.
