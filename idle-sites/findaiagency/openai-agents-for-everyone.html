OpenAI Agents for Everyone: What ChatGPT Can Do for Business

Published August 24, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC
AI agents ChatGPT Work OpenAI automation

On August 24, 2026, TechCrunch reported that OpenAI is building AI agents for everyone: ChatGPT Work, released last month on the company's lowest subscription tier at $20 a month, is the clearest signal yet that autonomous agents are leaving the developer world for everyday business software. For small businesses and the agencies that serve them, the question is no longer whether AI agents for small business are coming — it's who deploys them, what they cost, and what can ChatGPT agents do once they're pointed at real workflows. Here's what it means for agencies.

What happened: OpenAI is shipping agents to everyone

ChatGPT Work launched July 9, 2026, alongside the GPT-5.6 family (Sol, Terra, Luna) — TechCrunch called it "a workplace companion for enterprise teams, running on desktop, web, and mobile" for drafting documents, spreadsheets, and presentations. It's a modified version of Codex, OpenAI's coding agent, "meant to give non-engineers a version of the same functionality that software engineers already get from agents: an AI tool that doesn't just answer questions, but completes multistep projects on its own."

The most telling detail is internal. Andrew Ambrosino, lead engineer for OpenAI's desktop app, has given it "access to, and control over, his inbox, his Slack account, his phone, apps like Notion and Figma, and more." OpenAI is dogfooding the exact integrations its customers will ask for — email, Slack, phone, Notion, Figma. Thibault Sottiaux, who leads OpenAI's core product work including Work, put the ambition plainly:

"In this new factor, ChatGPT can actually do entire, very complicated tasks for you all autonomously in a way that is delightful and safe. It's the very mission of OpenAI — to bring everyone along."

The numbers: adoption is inside-out

The adoption gap is the opportunity: in June 2026, 98% of OpenAI employees used Codex, versus just 17% of organizational subscribers and under 1% of individual subscribers. The Register corroborates: 97.9% of employees, 17.3% of organizations, 0.7% of individuals.

  • Active agent users grew more than fivefold in H1 2026, fastest outside the developer audience.
  • Non-developer usage of Codex has risen 137x for individuals, 189x for organizations, and 12x within OpenAI since August 2025.
  • The joint ChatGPT Work + Codex app has roughly 20 million users — versus more than a billion prompting ChatGPT online.
  • Codex accounts for 99.8% of OpenAI's weekly output tokens; every department, including Legal and Recruiting, uses it as the primary work tool.
  • By May 2026, 80.6% of individual users made requests estimated to exceed 30 minutes of human work, 70.2% over an hour, and 25.6% over eight hours.

OpenAI measures readiness with GDPval, a benchmark drawn from 44 occupations and hundreds of knowledge-work tests; frontier models are "approaching industry experts in deliverable quality."

What can ChatGPT agents do?

ChatGPT agents plan and execute multi-step tasks on their own: they browse, use apps and plugins, write and run code, and operate files without step-by-step prompting — a different category from chat that answers questions.

In practice: weekly metrics reports, spreadsheets turned into planning tools, VC investment memos, ops dashboards — and Sam Altman planning vacations. A TechCrunch reporter used Work to pull a preschool calendar out of email into Google Calendar and build an auto-updating financial dashboard.

The plugin model covers the everyday stack: Slack and Microsoft Teams, Google Drive and SharePoint, email, calendars, CRMs, and project trackers, plus Scheduled Tasks that run unattended. On August 20, ChatGPT gained an Apple Messages plug-in that drafts, sends, and deletes messages — persistent approval "removes your final chance to review a message before ChatGPT sends it as you."

The friction is real, and it's the agency opportunity. TechCrunch found permission setup "confusing and circular" (read-only access failed; only full access worked), many settings are web-app-only, and the model can create calendar events but not new calendars. Harness lead Joe Gershenson concedes effort settings aren't intuitive: "there are things that we can do better to help them get the right level of reasoning... Watch this space."

How much do ChatGPT agents cost?

ChatGPT agents are bundled into ChatGPT plans — there is no separate Work SKU. The $20-a-month Plus plan is the entry point that unlocks the Work agent, on a ladder from free up to a $200 Pro plan. Business runs $20 per user per month billed annually ($25 billed monthly, 2-seat minimum); Enterprise is custom quote-only.

The hidden cost is token burn. One TechCrunch reporter "used more than 80 million tokens in four days, which cost $65" — over 3x the $20 subscription price, on casual use. Why this is OpenAI's biggest business bet

Agents that run longer burn more tokens, making them "more lucrative for OpenAI on a per-user basis" — and reaching new professions is how labs justify massive investment in training and computation. Sottiaux on the economics: "You sit there and you're like, 'of course I want to pay $20 bucks a month for this,' because the value that you get is so much more." Efficiency is the counterweight: OpenAI cut Luna pricing by 80%.

The strategic stakes are what agencies should watch. a16z's Christian Catalini warns that "if the labs cannot rapidly get ahold of the key complementary assets needed to scale AI in the market, value will accrue elsewhere." If OpenAI can't own integration, permissions, and workflows itself, that value accrues to the service layer — agencies included. Competition is real: Codex passed Claude Code in download interest around April 2026, and Ramp data shows Anthropic at ~44% versus OpenAI's ~40% among business spenders in July 2026, with OpenAI growing faster in Q3; the share of Ramp customers paying for AI reached nearly 56% by July, up from 50% in March.

What this means for your clients

The buyer is no longer the developer. Non-engineers are the fastest-growing user group, so the person asking about AI is now the owner, the ops lead, or the marketer. Client expectations will flip from "chatbot on our website" to "an agent that does the work" — and many clients will have tried ChatGPT Work on their own $20 plan first.

What agencies should prepare for

Client demand for agent deployments. Integration, plugin setup, permissions, and Scheduled Tasks governance for client tool stacks — the setup friction TechCrunch documented is exactly the implementation work agencies sell.

Evaluation before outcomes. Digital Applied's 250-agency survey (Q1 2026) found evaluation/testing complexity is the #1 blocker to agent rollout (49% of agencies); shops that build eval harnesses first report 11x ROI instead of 0.7x.

Cost governance. Median agency spend is $1,800/month per active agent (top quartile $4,200, bottom quartile $420); median total agency AI spend is $7,400/month, top decile $48,000.

Pricing model shift. AI-native agencies (12% of the sample) bill against agent throughput rather than retainer hours and report ROI medians above 6x; retrofit agencies (38%) report 2.4x; legacy agencies (50%) are still piloting. SEO audit agents lead the ROI ranking at 11.4x, code generation 8.3x, lead qualification 5.8x.

Security and trust. Agents with inbox and phone access raise real data-exposure questions — OpenAI's own engineer concedes the private-DM risk — so agent-security review becomes a sellable service; OpenAI's enterprise layer adds auto-review and admin spend controls.

Talent restructure. Agencies report +22% AI/ML engineers and +14% senior content strategists — but -15% junior content writers, -11% junior SEO specialists, and -7% manual QA.

The adoption clock. 41% of agencies already have at least one agent in production, and 56% of Ramp-tracked businesses now pay for AI — the window to differentiate is the next 12–18 months.

Action items for agencies

  • Offer a "ChatGPT Work readiness" audit: which client workflows are agent-safe, which tools to connect, what the permission model should be.
  • Stand up a deployment practice: plugins, Scheduled Tasks, and per-client approval gates.
  • Put token budgets and spend caps in every agent retainer; model cost per task, not per seat — remember the $65-in-four-days burn on a $20 subscription.
  • Build a client evaluation harness (golden outputs, scoring rubrics) before promising agent outcomes; it's the biggest ROI determinant.
  • Publish an agent-security checklist covering sensitive-data access (email, DMs, phone) and "approval required" defaults.
  • Reskill the bench: hire or contract AI engineers; shift junior writers and SEOs into agent-editing and evaluation roles.
  • Rethink pricing toward throughput and outcomes; position agent implementation as a scoped project with a managed-service tier.

Frequently asked questions

What is ChatGPT Work?

OpenAI's agent workspace, launched July 9, 2026 on GPT-5.6: an agent in ChatGPT that turns a goal into finished documents, decks, and spreadsheets, pulling context from connected apps like email, Slack, Notion, and Figma. It ships inside paid plans — there is no separate Work SKU.

Are ChatGPT agents free?

No — agent mode and ChatGPT Work are paid-tier features. The $20-a-month Plus plan is the first tier that unlocks the Work agent; free and lower tiers don't get it.

Can ChatGPT agents use my email, Slack, or Notion?

Yes, via plugins. OpenAI's connector list covers Slack, Microsoft Teams, Google Drive, SharePoint, email, calendars, CRMs, and project trackers; internally, OpenAI's desktop lead runs Work across his inbox, Slack, phone, Notion, and Figma.

Does ChatGPT agent mode require coding?

No — the entire point is giving non-engineers the same capability developers get, through plain-language prompts instead of step-by-step instruction.

Are ChatGPT agents worth it?

For repeatable multi-step office work, yes — with guardrails. Agency data shows median ROI from 11.4x for SEO audit agents down to 1.6x for client-report drafting; the risks are permissions and trust, output quality variance, and token burn on long tasks.

ChatGPT Work vs Claude Cowork: which is better?

They're direct rivals in the same "agent workspace" category — TechCrunch notes Work even asked its reporter to port over Claude Cowork data on first run. Work rides OpenAI's 1B+ ChatGPT base and $20 entry tier; Cowork rides Anthropic's enterprise seat. Both are in flux — compare features, not vendor claims.
