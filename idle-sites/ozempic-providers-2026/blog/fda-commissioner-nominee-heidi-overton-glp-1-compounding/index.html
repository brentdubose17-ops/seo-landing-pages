Heidi Overton Nominated to Lead the FDA: What It Means for GLP-1 Providers and Compounded Access

Published August 19, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC

President Donald Trump announced Wednesday (Aug 19, 2026) that he is nominating Dr. Heidi Overton, deputy director of the White House Domestic Policy Council, to be FDA commissioner. "Dr. Heidi has been a ROCKSTAR in my Administration," Trump posted. "We need her leadership at the FDA now to ensure that the U.S. remains the WORLD LEADER for Scientific Discovery and CURES."

The announcement matters for anyone prescribing or taking GLP-1 medications because one of the biggest pending decisions in the category — the FDA's proposed exclusion of semaglutide, tirzepatide, and liraglutide from the 503B compounding bulks list — has no final determination yet. That call now sits on the next commissioner's desk.

Who is Heidi Overton?

Overton is a physician with a medical degree from the University of New Mexico School of Medicine, a doctorate from Johns Hopkins, and board certification in public health. Before joining the Domestic Policy Council, she served as chief policy officer of the America First Policy Institute.

Her public record is mostly outside drug regulation. She stood beside Trump at the Aug 10 vaccine order signing, which cut the recommended childhood vaccination schedule from 18 shots to 11 and split the MMR vaccine into three doses. She has described mifepristone as "teleabortion" and urged Congress to strictly regulate it "to protect women and children."

Important for GLP-1 prescribers: no public statements from Overton on GLP-1 compounding, the 503A/503B lists, or peptides have been found. Until she states a position in hearings or testimony, anyone claiming she will "ban" or "expand" compounding is speculating.

A leaderless agency, a third choice

The FDA has had no Senate-confirmed leader since Marty Makary resigned in May. Makary's tenure, per Politico, was "marked by mass layoffs, persistent churn among senior leaders and policy fights with lawmakers, drugmakers and Trump." Senior departures continued through 2026: vaccine and biotech chief Vinay Prasad stepped down in April, and acting drug-center director Tracy Beth Hoeg was replaced.

Overton is Trump's third choice for the vacancy. Acting commissioner Kyle Diamantas declined the job, as did former Rep. Brad Wenstrup (R-Ohio); Diamantas continues to lead the agency in an acting capacity.

Confirmation is far from assured. No hearing date has been set, and the announcement may not yet be a formal Senate submission. Senate HELP Committee chair Bill Cassidy has written that Overton's record "alone is almost disqualifying," and Republicans hold a one-member majority on the committee — a single GOP defection could sink the nomination.

The decision on her desk: the 503B GLP-1 exclusion

On April 30, 2026, the FDA proposed excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide, and liraglutide from the 503B bulks list, "finding no clinical need for outsourcing facilities to compound these drugs from bulk substances." 503B facilities are outsourcing pharmacies that compound drugs in bulk without patient-specific prescriptions — the main channel clinics used for large-scale compounded GLP-1 supply during the shortage era.

"When FDA-approved drugs are available, outsourcing facilities cannot lawfully compound using bulk drug substances unless there is a clear clinical need," said then-Commissioner Makary at the time. "This action reflects our responsibility to protect patients and preserve the integrity of the drug approval process."

The comment period was extended to July 30, 2026 and has closed — but the FDA has issued no final determination. The decision lands on the next commissioner, meaning Overton would inherit it if confirmed. What this means for GLP-1 availability

Two separate tracks are in play, and it's worth keeping them distinct:

  • Branded GLP-1s (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound). Supply has stabilized. Neither semaglutide nor tirzepatide currently appears on the FDA's drug shortage list — the shortage-era compounding pathway for these drugs is closed. The nomination changes nothing about branded availability; if anything, access keeps expanding through the administration's "most favored nation" price deals with Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk (announced Nov 2025), which cut prices for blockbuster weight-loss drugs in exchange for tariff relief and wider Medicare access.
  • Compounded copies. Compounding access has been narrowing since the FDA declared the semaglutide shortage resolved in February 2025 and removed tirzepatide from the shortage list in October 2024. The pending 503B exclusion would formalize the end of bulk-substance compounding for all three drugs if finalized. An Overton-led FDA would decide whether to finalize the proposal, modify it, or leave it unresolved — and on what timeline. Enforcement posture

The enforcement trend lines are worth watching. The FDA under Makary issued 12 warning letters and 5 cease-and-desist letters to compounders in March 2026, and roughly 38% of compounding pharmacies reported stopping semaglutide production by April (per our shortage and supply update). A commissioner who accelerates that posture would tighten the market further.

Industry analysts expect more of the same. "Industry will view this pick as extremely troublesome and FDA career staff will continue to flee the agency," Raymond James analyst Chris Meekins told Axios. "Any progress made since Makary's departure is likely to be immediately reversed if she is confirmed."

At the same time, the compounding-policy machinery is moving in a more permissive direction on other fronts: in late July, the FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted 8–6 to recommend six peptides — including BPC-157 and TB-500 — for the compounding-safety (503A) list. Formal placement still requires rulemaking that could take months or years, with an enforcement-discretion policy the likely near-term path. Whether an Overton-led FDA follows those recommendations is open.

Bottom line for providers

  1. Nothing changes today. The nomination does not alter current compounding rules, branded supply, or enforcement. Semaglutide and tirzepatide are not on the shortage list, and bulk compounding of them already rests on thin legal footing.
  2. The 503B decision is pending and now politicized. Watch three signals: (a) confirmation hearings and any statements Overton makes on compounding, (b) FDA action on the 503B exclusion proposal, and (c) whether the nomination survives the one-seat GOP committee majority.
  3. Keep compliance conservative. With warning letters already flowing and an agency that may get more aggressive, don't build clinical workflow on compounded GLP-1 supply that could end with a final rule. Our Ozempic vs Wegovy vs Mounjaro comparison covers the branded alternatives that remain the stable path.
  4. Patients should watch cost, not availability. The Lilly and Novo price deals and Medicare coverage changes keep expanding branded access — the pressure point is cost and coverage, not whether drugs are in stock.

The one-line takeaway: The Overton nomination puts the FDA commissioner role in play just as the FDA's GLP-1 compounding policy decision reaches its final stage — but with no public position from Overton on compounding, no hearing date, and a razor-thin confirmation margin, providers should treat every concrete policy outcome as speculation until the Senate actually acts.
