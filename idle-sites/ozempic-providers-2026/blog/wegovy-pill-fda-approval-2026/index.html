FDA Approves Oral Wegovy (Semaglutide 25 mg): The First GLP-1 Pill for Weight Management

Published August 22, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC

The News

On December 22, 2025, the FDA approved Wegovy pill (once-daily oral semaglutide 25 mg), making it the first oral GLP-1 receptor agonist approved for weight management in the United States. The indication covers adults with obesity or overweight plus at least one weight-related comorbidity, used together with diet and exercise. Novo Nordisk launched the tablet in pharmacies and select telehealth providers in early January 2026. Pharmacy Times called the decision a major shift in the obesity treatment landscape, and AJMC noted the pill was cleared for weight loss and for reducing major adverse cardiovascular events.

Dosing: One Pill a Day

Oral Wegovy is taken once daily with water. Treatment starts at a low 1.5 mg starting dose and is titrated up over the first months to the 25 mg once-daily maintenance dose studied in the pivotal trial. At launch, the starting dose was offered at $149 per month with savings offers, with additional coverage and cost-reduction programs announced by Novo Nordisk at the same time.

The Expanded Cardiovascular (MACE) Indication

The pill approval is not just about weight. Oral Wegovy is also indicated to reduce the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) — cardiovascular death, heart attack, and stroke — in adults with overweight or obesity and established cardiovascular disease. That makes the Wegovy brand the only GLP-1 indicated for weight loss proven to help prevent life-threatening cardiovascular events in that population, as Pharmaceutical Commerce reported in its approval coverage.

What the Trials Showed

The approval rests on the phase 3 OASIS 4 trial (NCT05564117). Adults taking oral semaglutide 25 mg lost about 17% of body weight over 64 weeks when treatment was followed (about 14% regardless of adherence), versus roughly 2% with placebo, and about one in three patients lost at least 20%. Pharmacy Times reported the results were comparable to the injectable Wegovy 2.4 mg formulation, with gastrointestinal side effects — nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea — the most common, mostly during dose escalation.

Pill vs Injection: What Changes for Patients

The biggest practical change is delivery: no needles. For patients who have avoided GLP-1 therapy because of injections, an oral option makes starting or continuing treatment more feasible — the same adherence story that has driven the broader oral GLP-1 push. Bottom Line

The approval matters because it is the first FDA-approved GLP-1 pill: the efficacy of injectable semaglutide in a once-daily tablet, with a cardiovascular benefit on top.
