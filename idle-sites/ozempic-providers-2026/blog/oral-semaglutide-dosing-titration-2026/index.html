Oral Semaglutide Dosing at a Glance

Oral semaglutide is the tablet form of semaglutide, the GLP-1 receptor agonist also sold as the Ozempic and Wegovy injections. There are currently two approved oral semaglutide products in the United States: the Wegovy pill (oral semaglutide 25 mg, approved for weight management and cardiovascular risk reduction) and the Ozempic pill (oral semaglutide 1.5/4/9 mg, approved for type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular risk reduction). Both are taken once daily, and both require the same strict empty-stomach routine because of the absorption enhancer SNAC.

The Wegovy Pill Titration Schedule: 1.5 → 4 → 9 → 25 mg

The FDA-approved Wegovy pill schedule is a four-step escalation, with each dose taken for 30 days. This gradual ramp exists to reduce gastrointestinal side effects — nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea are most likely during dose increases, and slow titration gives your body time to adjust. The schedule below is taken directly from Table 2 of the FDA-approved Wegovy prescribing information.

Most patients reach the 25 mg maintenance dose after about 3 months (90 days of titration). Two label details matter: if you do not tolerate a dose during escalation, your clinician may delay the escalation rather than push forward. And if you cannot tolerate 25 mg, the label directs consideration of a switch to Wegovy injection 1.7 mg once weekly — a decision for your prescriber, not something to manage on your own. Do not take more than one tablet per day.

The 25 mg dose is the semaglutide 25 mg people search for when they look up the oral Wegovy dose — it is the maintenance strength studied in the pivotal trial, not a starter dose. The Ozempic Pill (Oral Semaglutide for Type 2 Diabetes): 1.5/4/9 mg

The Ozempic pill is Novo Nordisk's rebranded oral semaglutide for type 2 diabetes — a reformulation of Rybelsus at updated strengths of 1.5 mg, 4 mg, and 9 mg, available in the US since May 4, 2026. It follows the same titration logic: start at the lowest strength and increase gradually as needed and as tolerated, with the 9 mg tablet as the highest approved once-daily dose.

The Fasting Rule: SNAC and the 30-Minute Window

The single most important practical detail about oral semaglutide is that it must be taken on an empty stomach. The tablet contains an absorption enhancer called SNAC (salcaprozate sodium) that helps semaglutide cross the stomach lining — and it only works when the stomach is empty. Take it wrong and absorption drops sharply, which means a weaker effect and more stomach upset. The FDA label instructions are strict:

  • Take the tablet once daily, first thing in the morning, on an empty stomach
  • Swallow it with no more than 4 ounces (about a half cup) of plain water — no other liquids
  • Wait at least 30 minutes before eating food, drinking beverages, or taking any other oral medications
  • Swallow the tablet whole — do not split, crush, chew, or dissolve it

Coffee counts as "drinking" — that morning cup has to wait until the 30 minutes are up. This fasting routine applies to both the Wegovy pill and the Ozempic pill, and it is the main practical difference from injectable GLP-1s, which have no food or timing restrictions. It is also the key differentiator from Eli Lilly's Foundayo (orforglipron), the other approved GLP-1 pill, which can be taken any time of day with no fasting requirement — our GLP-1 pill category guide compares all four approved oral GLP-1s side by side.

Missed Dose Rules for Oral Semaglutide

The missed-dose rule for oral semaglutide is simple and worth knowing before you start: if you miss a dose, skip it and take the next dose the following day. Do not double up to "catch up," and never take more than one tablet per day. If you find yourself missing doses repeatedly because of the morning routine, talk to your clinician — adherence is a real consideration in choosing between the pill and the injection, and our pill vs injection guide covers how the routines compare.

Switching Between the Pill and the Injection

The FDA label includes specific switching guidance, which exists because oral and injectable semaglutide are not interchangeable and the transition must be timed correctly:

All switching should happen under a clinician's direction — the timing matters, and dose mapping between forms is not something to guess at. If you are weighing a switch, bring the table above to your prescriber.

What to Expect: Side Effects by Dose

The most common adverse reactions with Wegovy tablets — the same class effects seen across GLP-1s — are nausea, diarrhea, vomiting, constipation, and abdominal pain, along with headache, fatigue, dyspepsia, and others. These are most likely during dose escalation, which is exactly why titration is gradual. In the Wegovy tablet safety trial, severe gastrointestinal adverse reactions were reported in 2% of tablet-treated patients versus 0% of placebo, and 6.9% of tablet patients discontinued treatment due to adverse reactions (5.9% on placebo).

Because absorption depends on the fasting routine, taking the tablet incorrectly can make GI side effects worse or make the dose less effective. And because oral semaglutide has lower bioavailability than injected semaglutide, side effects are not directly comparable between the forms at equivalent doses — another reason transitions should be clinician-managed. What the 25 mg Dose Studied Shows

The 25 mg maintenance dose is the one tested in the pivotal weight-management trials. In the Wegovy tablet study (Study 7, 64 weeks), patients treated with oral semaglutide lost an average of 13.6% of body weight versus 2.4% with placebo, and 76.3% of tablet patients lost at least 5% of their body weight (31.3% on placebo). The label also carries a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodents and contraindicates Wegovy in anyone with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2 — the standard GLP-1 safety conversation to have with your clinician before starting.

Bottom Line

Oral semaglutide dosing is a slow, deliberate climb: the Wegovy pill starts at 1.5 mg and steps up every 30 days to the 25 mg maintenance dose by roughly day 91, while the Ozempic pill tops out at 9 mg for type 2 diabetes. The non-negotiable is the morning routine — empty stomach, ≤4 ounces of water, 30-minute wait — because SNAC only works when the stomach is empty. Missed a dose? Skip it and take the next one the next day. Want to switch forms? That is a clinician conversation with label-specific timing. FAQ: Oral Semaglutide Dosing & Titration

Q: What is the starting dose of the Wegovy pill?

A: The starting dose of the Wegovy pill (oral semaglutide) is 1.5 mg taken once daily for the first 30 days. The dose is then increased in 30-day steps to 4 mg, then 9 mg, then the 25 mg maintenance dose.

Q: What is the Wegovy pill titration schedule?

A: Per the FDA label, take 1.5 mg once daily on days 1–30, then 4 mg once daily on days 31–60, then 9 mg once daily on days 61–90, then the 25 mg maintenance dose once daily from day 91 onward. Each step lasts 30 days, so patients typically reach 25 mg after about 3 months.

Q: What is the maintenance dose of oral Wegovy?

A: The recommended maintenance dosage of Wegovy tablets is 25 mg taken orally once daily, beginning on day 91 of treatment. Patients who do not tolerate 25 mg may be switched by their clinician to Wegovy injection 1.7 mg once weekly.

Q: Why must oral semaglutide be taken on an empty stomach?

A: Oral semaglutide tablets contain an absorption enhancer called SNAC (salcaprozate sodium) that helps the medicine cross the stomach lining. SNAC only works when the stomach is empty: take the tablet first thing in the morning with no more than 4 ounces of plain water, and wait at least 30 minutes before eating, drinking, or taking other oral medications.

Q: What if I miss a dose of the Wegovy pill?

A: If a dose of Wegovy tablets is missed, skip the missed dose and take the next dose the following day. Do not take two tablets in one day. Do not take more than one tablet per day.

Q: What are the Ozempic pill doses?

A: The Ozempic pill (oral semaglutide for type 2 diabetes) comes in 1.5 mg, 4 mg, and 9 mg strengths. Treatment starts at 1.5 mg once daily and increases gradually to 4 mg and then 9 mg as needed and as tolerated, following the same empty-stomach administration rules.

Q: Can I switch between the Wegovy pill and the Wegovy injection?

A: Yes, but switching must be clinician-managed. Per the FDA label, patients taking Wegovy 2.4 mg injection may switch to 25 mg tablets one week after their last injection. Patients on 25 mg tablets may switch to 2.4 mg injection the day after their last tablet. Do not switch on your own — the forms are not interchangeable without guidance.
