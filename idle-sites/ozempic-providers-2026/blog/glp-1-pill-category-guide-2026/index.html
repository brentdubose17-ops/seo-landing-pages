GLP-1 Pills in 2026: All 4 FDA-Approved Oral GLP-1s Compared

Published August 22, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC

For most of the GLP-1 era, "GLP-1" meant an injection. That changed in December 2025, when the FDA approved the first oral GLP-1 for weight management, and by mid-2026 the US market had four approved GLP-1 pills — three semaglutide tablets from Novo Nordisk and one small-molecule pill from Eli Lilly. This is the category guide: what each pill is, who it's approved for, how they compare on dosing, fasting rules, cost, and coverage, and what the arrival of the pill changed for patients.

What the Wegovy Pill Approval Changed

On December 22, 2025, the FDA approved Wegovy pill (oral semaglutide 25 mg) — the first oral GLP-1 receptor agonist approved for weight management in the US. Pharmacy Times called the decision a major shift in obesity treatment, and AJMC noted the pill was cleared both for weight loss and for reducing major adverse cardiovascular events.

The approval changed the category in three ways:

  • It made needle-free GLP-1 therapy real for weight loss. More than 80% of new Wegovy pill prescriptions go to patients new to GLP-1 therapy — people who had avoided injections entirely.
  • It attached a heart indication to a pill. The Wegovy pill is the first oral GLP-1 indicated to reduce MACE (cardiovascular death, heart attack, stroke) in adults with overweight or obesity and established cardiovascular disease.
  • It set the price benchmark. The $149-per-month starting dose became the reference price that Foundayo matched at launch.

Wegovy Pill (Oral Semaglutide 25 mg)

The Wegovy pill is once-daily oral semaglutide — the same molecule as injectable Wegovy — delivered as a tablet. It is FDA-approved for chronic weight management in adults with obesity, or overweight plus at least one weight-related comorbidity, used with diet and exercise. It also carries the MACE-reduction indication described above.

Dosing: Treatment starts at a low 1.5 mg dose and is titrated up through 4 mg and 9 mg to the 25 mg once-daily maintenance dose studied in the pivotal trial. It must be taken first thing in the morning on an empty stomach with a small sip of water, with at least 30 minutes before eating or drinking.

Evidence: In the phase 3 OASIS 4 trial, adults on oral semaglutide 25 mg lost about 17% of body weight over 64 weeks when treatment was followed (~16.6% mean on-treatment; ~14% regardless of adherence), versus roughly 2% with placebo, with about one in three patients losing at least 20%. Gastrointestinal side effects — nausea, vomiting, diarrhea — were the most common, mostly during dose escalation.

Market reality: Novo Nordisk reported 3 million US prescriptions by June 2026 (just over five months from the January 5 launch — "one of the strongest US pharmaceutical launches by volume on record"), passing 5 million by August 2026. Weekly scripts have run in the 130K–172K range through the summer. See our approval brief and the pill vs injection guide for more.

Foundayo (Orforglipron): The No-Fasting Pill

Foundayo is Eli Lilly's once-daily oral GLP-1, approved April 1, 2026 — the second oral GLP-1 for obesity (the Wegovy pill was first). What makes it different is the molecule: orforglipron is a small-molecule (non-peptide) GLP-1 receptor agonist, the first of its kind, and it was the first new molecular entity approved under the FDA's National Priority Voucher program — the fastest NME approval since 2002.

The practical difference: Foundayo has no food or water restrictions. You take it any time of day, no empty stomach required. For patients who struggle with the semaglutide fasting ritual, that is the single biggest lifestyle difference in the category.

Dosing: Starts at 0.8 mg, increases to 2.5 mg after at least 30 days, then 5.5 mg after another 30 days, with optional higher maintenance doses (9/14.5/17.2 mg).

Evidence: In ATTAIN-1, adults at the highest dose who stayed on treatment lost about 27 pounds (~12.4%) versus about 2 pounds with placebo; across all randomized patients, about 25 pounds (~11.1%). Common side effects include nausea, constipation, diarrhea, vomiting, dyspepsia, abdominal pain, headache, burping, GERD, gas, and hair loss. Foundayo carries the thyroid C-cell tumor boxed warning shared across the class and has no cardiovascular indication.

Market reality: Foundayo launched April 9, 2026 through retail pharmacies, telehealth platforms, and LillyDirect, and has been building steadily — roughly 39,000 weekly scripts by mid-August 2026. It reached full coverage across the three major PBMs by June 1, 2026. Our Foundayo UK approval explainer covers the molecule's global rollout.

Ozempic Pill (Oral Semaglutide 1.5/4/9 mg)

The Ozempic pill is Novo Nordisk's oral semaglutide for type 2 diabetes, available in the US since May 4, 2026. It is a reformulated version of Rybelsus sold under the Ozempic name, with an indication to reduce cardiovascular events in adults with T2D and established cardiovascular disease — the SOUL trial evidence. It is not approved for weight loss; the Wegovy pill is the obesity version of the same molecule.

Dosing: 1.5 mg, 4 mg, or 9 mg once daily, with the same fasting rule as the other semaglutide pills (empty stomach, small sip of water, wait 30 minutes). The Ozempic pill tablets are not mg-for-mg interchangeable with Rybelsus, even though both are oral semaglutide. Self-pay pricing starts at $149 per month.

Rybelsus: The Original Oral Semaglutide

Rybelsus (oral semaglutide 3/7/14 mg) is the original oral GLP-1 for type 2 diabetes, approved in 2019. It remains on the market but is being transitioned to the Ozempic pill brand as Novo Nordisk consolidates its oral semaglutide franchise. Rybelsus carries a glycemic indication for T2D with the same fasting requirement, and it does not carry a weight-loss indication. For patients established on Rybelsus, the practical questions are about the transition: same molecule, same fasting rules, different brand and tablet strengths — talk to your prescriber before switching products.

How the Pills Compare: Efficacy, Tolerability, Convenience

There is no head-to-head trial between the two weight-loss pills. The closest comparison is Novo's ORION analysis (April 2026), a population-adjusted indirect comparison of trial data, which suggested 25 mg oral semaglutide produced about 3.2 percentage points more weight loss than 36 mg orforglipron, with orforglipron associated with roughly 14x the odds of stopping for gastrointestinal side effects and 4x the odds of discontinuing for any adverse event. ORION was not a randomized head-to-head and Novo flagged its limitations.

What patients can weigh directly:

  • Convenience: Foundayo wins on the fasting rule — no morning ritual, any time of day.
  • Heart indication: The Wegovy pill (obesity) and Ozempic pill (T2D) carry MACE/CV indications; Foundayo does not. See the MACE guide.
  • Track record: Oral semaglutide has years of real-world data (Rybelsus since 2019, the Wegovy pill since January 2026, 3M+ prescriptions); orforglipron is newer but launching fast.
  • GI tolerability: Both classes cause nausea and GI effects; indirect analysis favors semaglutide on discontinuation rates, with the usual caveat that head-to-head data don't exist.

GLP-1 Pill vs Injection

The pill versions bring the efficacy of semaglutide into a once-daily tablet and remove the needle — the adherence story that has driven the oral push. The trade-offs are daily dosing (versus weekly injections), the fasting rule for semaglutide pills, and coverage differences. Injectable Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound still dominate the market, but oral Wegovy now accounts for roughly a third of all Wegovy prescriptions. Cost and Coverage in 2026

Pricing for the weight-loss pills is anchored at the entry level: $149 per month for the lowest dose of both the Wegovy pill and Foundayo for cash-pay patients, part of the White House drug-price deal that exempted both companies from certain taxes. Commercially insured patients can pay as little as $25 per month through manufacturer savings programs (Novo for Wegovy, Lilly's savings card for Foundayo).

On Medicare, the GLP-1 Bridge Program went live July 1, 2026: eligible Part D beneficiaries pay $50 per month for covered GLP-1s including Wegovy (injection and tablets) and Foundayo, through December 31, 2027. Wegovy pill had full PBM coverage from its first launch week; Foundayo reached coverage at all three major PBMs by June 1, 2026. Real Pills vs Supplement Imposters

Because "GLP-1 pill" is one of the most searched terms in the category, the SERP also fills with supplements and "natural GLP-1" products that contain no approved medication. A genuine GLP-1 pill is a prescription drug — oral semaglutide or orforglipron — dispensed by a licensed pharmacy after a clinician determines it's appropriate. Over-the-counter capsules that claim to mimic GLP-1 are supplements, not FDA-approved treatments, and they have not demonstrated the outcomes of the approved drugs. If a product doesn't require a prescription and a pharmacy, it is not one of the four pills in this guide.

Who Should Ask Their Doctor About a GLP-1 Pill

A GLP-1 pill may be worth discussing if you:

  • Have obesity (BMI 30+) or overweight (BMI 27+) with a weight-related condition and want weight management — the Wegovy pill or Foundayo.
  • Prefer to avoid injections, or have avoided GLP-1 therapy entirely because of needles.
  • Have overweight or obesity and established cardiovascular disease — the Wegovy pill is the oral option with the MACE indication.
  • Have type 2 diabetes — the Ozempic pill (with CV risk reduction) or Rybelsus, and note the Ozempic pill is not for weight loss.
  • Want the flexibility of no fasting rules — Foundayo is the only pill that allows any-time-of-day dosing.

Starting, stopping, or switching any GLP-1 requires a prescription and medical supervision. A licensed clinician can review your BMI, comorbidities, coverage, and preferences. Bottom Line

The GLP-1 pill category is now four approved products deep: Wegovy pill and Foundayo for weight management, Ozempic pill and Rybelsus for type 2 diabetes. The Wegovy pill broke the category open in December 2025 with the first oral GLP-1 for obesity and the first oral MACE indication; Foundayo added a no-fasting small-molecule option in April 2026; the Ozempic pill rebranded oral semaglutide for diabetes in May 2026. The choice among them comes down to indication, the fasting rule, heart benefits, and coverage — not to a meaningful efficacy gap, since no head-to-head trial exists. Ask your doctor which profile fits your situation.

What GLP-1 pills are FDA-approved in 2026?

Four oral GLP-1 receptor agonists are on the US market: Wegovy pill (oral semaglutide 25 mg, approved December 22, 2025 for weight management), Foundayo (orforglipron, approved April 1, 2026 for weight management), Ozempic pill (oral semaglutide 1.5/4/9 mg, available in the US since May 4, 2026 for type 2 diabetes), and Rybelsus (the original oral semaglutide 3/7/14 mg for type 2 diabetes, now being transitioned to the Ozempic pill brand).

Is the Wegovy pill the first GLP-1 pill?

Yes for weight management: the FDA approved the Wegovy pill (oral semaglutide 25 mg) on December 22, 2025, making it the first oral GLP-1 approved for weight management in the US. Foundayo (orforglipron), approved April 1, 2026, is the second oral GLP-1 for obesity — not the first.

What is the Ozempic pill?

The Ozempic pill is Novo Nordisk's reformulated oral semaglutide (1.5/4/9 mg) for type 2 diabetes, available in the US starting May 4, 2026. It rebrands Rybelsus under the Ozempic name. The tablets are not mg-for-mg interchangeable with Rybelsus, and the Ozempic pill is not approved for weight loss — the Wegovy pill is the obesity version.

Which GLP-1 pill has no fasting requirement?

Foundayo (orforglipron) is the only GLP-1 pill that can be taken any time of day without food or water restrictions. The semaglutide pills (Wegovy pill, Ozempic pill, and Rybelsus) must be taken first thing in the morning on an empty stomach with a small sip of water, and you must wait at least 30 minutes before eating or drinking.

How much do GLP-1 pills cost in 2026?

Both the Wegovy pill and Foundayo launched with $149 per month cash-pay pricing for the lowest dose. Commercially insured patients may pay as little as $25 per month through manufacturer savings programs. Medicare Part D beneficiaries eligible for the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge Program pay $50 per month for covered GLP-1s including Wegovy (injection and tablets) and Foundayo, through December 31, 2027.

Do GLP-1 pills work as well as injections?

In clinical trials, the Wegovy pill produced about 14-17% mean weight loss in OASIS 4 (on-treatment ~16.6%; ~14% regardless of adherence), broadly comparable to injectable Wegovy. Foundayo produced about 11-12% weight loss at the highest dose in ATTAIN-1. No head-to-head pill-vs-injection trial exists for the weight-loss pills; both are approved based on placebo-controlled trials.

Which GLP-1 pill has a heart (MACE) indication?

The semaglutide pills carry cardiovascular indications: the Wegovy pill is approved to reduce the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) — cardiovascular death, heart attack, and stroke — in adults with overweight or obesity and established cardiovascular disease, and the Ozempic pill is indicated for CV risk reduction in type 2 diabetes. Foundayo (orforglipron) has no cardiovascular indication.
