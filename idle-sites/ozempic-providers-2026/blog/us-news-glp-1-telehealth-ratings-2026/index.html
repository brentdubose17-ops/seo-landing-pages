U.S. News GLP-1 Telehealth Ratings 2026: The 11 Top-Ranked Platforms, Scored

Published August 14, 2026 · Last checked August 13, 2026By ABD Legacy LLC

On August 12, 2026, U.S. News & World Report released its debut ratings for online GLP-1 telehealth platforms — the first time the publisher has formally scored the companies that prescribe weight-loss medications like Wegovy and Zepbound over the internet.[1][3]

The inaugural "Best GLP-1 Telehealth Platforms 2026-2027" list evaluates 20 platforms offering FDA-approved, brand-name GLP-1s, and 11 of them earned a "Best" designation on a 5-point scale.[2]

The ratings arrive as GLP-1 telehealth use is exploding: about 11% of Americans now take a GLP-1 for weight loss, according to Gallup, and U.S. News itself found 52% of health and nutrition experts called GLP-1 expansion the single biggest public health trend of 2026.[1][3][5] For anyone shopping for a provider online, here's what the new ratings actually say — and what they don't.

How U.S. News scored the platforms

The methodology runs deep for a consumer-health list: U.S. News says it collected 14,161 data points and combined them with a PureSpectrum survey of 1,251 U.S. adults who had used a GLP-1 from an online telehealth platform in the past 12 months (May–July 2026).[1][2]

Platform data came from company websites and, where possible, direct communication with representatives.[2]

Each platform was scored across six weighted pillars:

  • Clinical support — 25% of the rating (video check-ins, lab work, anti-nausea medication, customer-rated medical support)[2]
  • Health screening process — 20% (medical documentation, photo/weight verification, video consults, customer-rated thoroughness)[2]
  • Transparency — 17% (published medical and cost information)[2]
  • Insurance support — 15% (insurance acceptance, FSA/HSA, concierge services)[2]
  • Supplemental support — 13% (nutrition/diet support, online community, progress tracking)[2]
  • Customer satisfaction — 10% (reported experience and likelihood to recommend)[2]

The threshold for "Best" was set at the largest natural drop-off in the scoring distribution, which separated the top 11 from the rest of the field.[2][4]

The brand-name vs. compounded rule — and why it matters

One exclusion matters more than any pillar weight: companies that only offer compounded GLP-1 medications were not included in the analysis at all.[1][2] For platforms that sell both brand-name and compounded versions, U.S. News rated only the FDA-approved, brand-name side of their business.[1][2][4]

MedPage Today summarized the stakes bluntly: compounded GLP-1s "are not FDA-approved and may carry greater risks."[3] Compounded semaglutide and tirzepatide exploded in popularity during the 2024-2025 shortage, but the copycat market has been contracting since — Hims exited compounding in February 2026 amid regulatory pressure and its settlement with Novo Nordisk.[6][7]

What this means for you: the 4.4 scores earned by Mochi Health and the 4.0s for Fridays and Ivim Health reflect only their brand-name programs — even though all three market compounded options.[2] If you're considering a compounded GLP-1 for cost reasons, these ratings won't tell you how well those programs are run. What the ratings don't tell you

Three gaps are worth knowing before you treat this list as gospel:

  1. Nine platforms are missing — by name. U.S. News evaluated 20 platforms but named only the 11 top-rated ones. The other nine appear on the rankings page only as anonymous gray bars in the score-distribution chart — "gray indicates all other platforms" — with no names and no scores.[2] No outlet has published who they are, and U.S. News has not disclosed the remaining nine.
  2. No numeric ranks. The output is a 5-point score per named platform, not a ranked list, and ties are display artifacts, not actual ties.[2][4]
  3. Affiliate links sit on the page. U.S. News states companies do not pay to be included and that it may earn commissions on purchases through its links — a tension worth keeping in mind when you click "Get Started."[2]

Our take: use it as one input, not the verdict

The debut ratings are the most rigorous independent comparison of GLP-1 telehealth platforms published to date — 14,161 data points and a 1,251-person user survey dwarf the typical affiliate roundup.[1][2]

The category winners map cleanly to real use cases: Amazon One Medical if insurance support matters most, Found for clinical oversight, Noom if you want coaching baked in, Ro if customer experience tops your list.[2]

But the brand-only lens is a real limitation. Millions of patients buy compounded GLP-1s for affordability, and this list is silent on that market by design.[1][2] And a 4.8 vs. 3.9 gap reflects the pillars U.S. News chose to weight, not a quality verdict on any individual clinician.[2] Gretel Schueller, U.S. News managing editor of health, frames the ratings as a starting point: consumers should pick a platform "in consultation with their doctor" that "aligns with their budget and health goals."[1]
