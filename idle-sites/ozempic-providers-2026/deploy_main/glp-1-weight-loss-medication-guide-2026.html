How GLP-1 Medications Work for Weight Loss

GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) receptor agonists mimic a natural hormone that regulates appetite and blood sugar. By activating GLP-1 receptors in the brain, these medications slow gastric emptying and increase feelings of fullness. Clinical data consistently shows average weight loss of 15-20% of total body weight over 68 weeks with medications like semaglutide and tirzepatide. For a person weighing 220 pounds, this translates to a loss of 33 to 44 pounds.

Approved GLP-1 Medications for Weight Loss in 2026

As of mid-2026, the FDA has approved several GLP-1 medications specifically for chronic weight management. Here are the leading options:

  • Semaglutide (Wegovy): Approved since 2021, remains a first-line option. Studies show 15% average weight loss at 68 weeks. Administered as a weekly injection.
  • Tirzepatide (Zepbound): A dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist. Clinical trials in 2025-2026 report 22.5% average weight loss at 72 weeks, making it the most potent option currently available.
  • Liraglutide (Saxenda): An older, daily injection option. Less potent, with 8% average weight loss, but still effective for some patients and often more affordable.
  • Oral Semaglutide (Rybelsus): The first oral GLP-1 for diabetes; a higher-dose version for weight loss received FDA approval in late 2025. Studies show 12% average weight loss at 68 weeks with the 50mg daily dose.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly will I see results on a GLP-1 medication?

Most patients notice reduced appetite within the first week. Measurable weight loss typically begins in weeks 2-4. Clinical trials show average loss of 5-8% of body weight by week 12. Individual results vary based on dosage, adherence, and lifestyle factors.

Can I take GLP-1 medications if I have a history of thyroid cancer?

No. GLP-1 receptor agonists carry a boxed warning for medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC). They are contraindicated if you or a family member has a history of MTC or Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2. Always disclose your full medical history to your provider.

What happens if I miss a dose of my weekly injection?

If you miss a dose and it has been less than 5 days, take it as soon as possible. If more than 5 days have passed, skip that dose and resume your regular schedule. Never double up doses. Contact your provider for specific guidance based on your medication and dosing schedule.

Are GLP-1 medications safe for long-term use beyond two years?

Emerging data from ongoing extension studies suggests good safety profiles for up to 4 years of continuous use. The SELECT trial (2025) followed patients for an average of 3.3 years and found no new safety signals. Long-term risks include potential gallbladder issues and rare cases of pancreatitis, but overall, the cardiovascular benefits outweigh these risks for most patients.
