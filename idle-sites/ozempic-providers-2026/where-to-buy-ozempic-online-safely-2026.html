How to Verify a Legitimate Online Pharmacy: The 7-Point Checklist

In a market where 97% of online pharmacies fail verification, you need a rigorous screening process. The BBB reported a 250% increase in fake pharmacy websites targeting GLP-1 patients between 2023 and 2025. Here is the checklist we recommend to every patient.

1. LegitScript Certification is Non-Negotiable

LegitScript is the gold standard for online pharmacy verification. They review the pharmacy's licensure, compliance with state and federal laws, and their dispensing practices. If a website displays a LegitScript certification seal, click on it. It should link directly to the LegitScript profile page. If the seal is not clickable or the profile does not exist, it is a fake.

2. NABP VIPPS Accreditation

The National Association of Boards of Pharmacy (NABP) runs the Verified Internet Pharmacy Practice Sites (VIPPS) program. This is a more stringent accreditation than LegitScript. Only about 1.5% of online pharmacies achieve VIPPS status. It verifies that the pharmacy is licensed in all states where it operates and adheres to strict patient safety protocols.

3. State Board of Pharmacy License Check

Every legitimate pharmacy must be licensed with the state board of pharmacy in the state where it is physically located. Look for the pharmacy's license number on their website, then verify it on the state board's website. For example, if the pharmacy claims to be in Florida, check the Florida Department of Health's license verification portal.

4. Physical Address and Pharmacist Availability

A legitimate pharmacy will prominently display a physical street address (not just a PO box) and a phone number. Call the number. Ask to speak to a licensed pharmacist. If you cannot reach a pharmacist directly, or if the call goes to a generic call center in another country, walk away.

5. They Require a Prescription

This seems obvious, but it bears repeating. A legitimate pharmacy will require a valid prescription from a licensed practitioner. They will not offer an "online consultation" that results in an automatic prescription without a real patient-provider relationship. If they are willing to ship Ozempic without a script, they are also willing to ship counterfeit product.

6. Secure Payment and HTTPS

Check the URL. It must start with https:// and have a valid SSL certificate. Look at the payment methods. Legitimate pharmacies accept major credit cards and often process insurance. Scam sites often require wire transfers, cryptocurrency, or Zelle payments because these are untraceable and non-refundable.

7. Domain Country and Contact Information

Be wary of domains ending in .ru, .cn, .top, or .xyz. Most legitimate US pharmacies use .com, .health, or .pharmacy. Also, check the "Contact Us" page. If the phone number is international or the address is a residential home, it is a red flag.
