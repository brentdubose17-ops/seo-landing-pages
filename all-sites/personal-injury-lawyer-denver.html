What Does a Denver Personal Injury Lawyer Cost?

Denver personal injury lawyers work on a contingency fee — you pay nothing upfront, and the attorney takes a percentage of your recovery, typically 33% if the case settles before trial and 40% if it goes to court. If there's no recovery, there's no fee. Most firms also advance the costs of filing, experts, and investigation, recovering those from the settlement.

That structure means a legitimate firm will never ask you for money upfront to "get started." Be wary of any lawyer who demands a retainer for a personal injury claim — that's not how the practice works in Colorado. A free consultation is standard, and it should give you an honest read on your case's value and chances, not a hard sell.

Colorado's Comparative Negligence Rule — Why It Matters

Colorado follows a "modified comparative negligence" rule with a 50% bar. That means you can recover damages even if you were partly at fault, so long as your share of fault is less than 50%. Your recovery is reduced by your percentage of fault — if you were 20% at fault in a $100,000 case, you recover $80,000. But if you're found 50% or more at fault, you recover nothing.

This is exactly where insurers attack: they'll argue you were more at fault than the evidence shows, trying to push you past that 50% bar or chip away at your percentage. A Denver attorney who investigates promptly — gathering dashcam footage, witness statements, and police reports — protects you from a blame-shifting defense that quietly destroys the value of your claim.

Colorado Deadlines: Don't Miss the Statute of Limitations

In Colorado, you generally have three years from the date of the injury to file a personal injury lawsuit, under the state's statute of limitations. For claims against a government entity — like an accident involving a city bus or a state highway — the rules are far stricter: you must file a notice of claim within 182 days. Miss either deadline and your case is gone, no matter how strong it is.

This is the single biggest reason to call a lawyer early. Evidence disappears, witnesses move, and surveillance footage gets overwritten — often within 30 days. A lawyer who starts investigating in the first week is doing work that's far harder to do six months later, and an early call ensures you don't sleep through a filing deadline.

Denver's Accident Hotspots and What They Mean for Claims

Denver's growth has made I-25, I-70, and the I-225 junction among the busiest and most accident-prone corridors in the region, and the mix of mountain traffic, winter conditions, and commuters creates frequent multi-vehicle collisions. Colorado also sits in the top tier for uninsured motorists — roughly one in seven drivers lacks insurance, so uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage is critical.

If you're hit by an uninsured driver, your own UM/UIM coverage becomes the source of recovery, and an attorney negotiates that claim against your own insurer. Colorado requires only the minimum liability limits of $25,000 per person and $50,000 per accident, which is often far below the cost of a serious injury. A lawyer can identify every available source of compensation — at-fault driver, your UM/UIM, and umbrella policies.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to file a Denver personal injury claim?

Generally three years from the date of injury in Colorado. But claims against government entities require notice within 182 days, and evidence preservation is urgent, so contact an attorney as soon as possible after any serious accident.

What if I was partly at fault for the accident?

You can still recover under Colorado's modified comparative negligence rule as long as you're less than 50% at fault. Your recovery is reduced by your fault percentage. An attorney can challenge inflated fault assessments from insurers.

How much is my Denver injury case worth?

It depends on your medical bills, lost wages, and pain and suffering, plus the available insurance coverage. A free consultation gives you a realistic range, not a guarantee. Beware any lawyer who promises a specific dollar figure before reviewing the evidence.

What should I do right after a Denver car accident?

Get medical attention, call the police, photograph the scene and vehicles, collect witness contact info, and don't admit fault or post about it on social media. Then contact a lawyer before speaking to the other driver's insurance company.

Why does the insurance company want a recorded statement?

Insurers use recorded statements to lock you into a version of events they can use against you later. You are generally not required to give one to the other party's insurer. Let your attorney handle communications so a casual remark doesn't reduce your claim.
