HVAC Repair Costs in Seattle

Seattle homeowners typically pay $150–$650 for common HVAC repairs. Diagnostic fees run $89–$129 and are credited toward the repair. Typical fixes: capacitor replacement ($150–$320), refrigerant recharge for heat pumps ($250–$550), blower motor ($400–$750), and thermostat replacement ($130–$380). Emergency after-hours service adds $95–$160 but gets a technician to your door within 3 hours — critical during Seattle's rare but real heat waves, like the 108°F June 2021 event that pushed AC demand through the roof.

Why Heat Pumps Dominate in Seattle

Washington's mild winters (average January low of 37°F) make heat pumps the most economical choice for most Seattle homes — a ducted heat pump costs $7,500–$14,000 installed and cuts heating costs 30–50% versus a 15-year-old gas furnace. Seattle City Light and Puget Sound Energy offer rebates of $500–$1,500 for heat pump installations, and the WA State Clean Energy Fund adds up to $3,000 for income-qualified households. Ductless mini-splits ($3,500–$6,000 per zone) are the go-to for the city's older homes and ADUs, which often lack ductwork.

Seattle's Moisture Problem: Coil Leaks and Drain Clogs

The Pacific Northwest's humidity creates specific failure patterns: evaporator coil leaks from condensation stress, clogged condensate drains (moss and algae grow fast in damp climates), and heat pump defrost board failures after wet freezes. Seattle's 155+ days of measurable rain per year mean outdoor units sit in standing water if not elevated — installers should mount condenser pads at least 4 inches above grade. Annual maintenance ($120–$180) catches these before they become $800+ repairs.

Washington HVAC Licensing — What to Look For

Washington requires HVAC contractors to hold a specialty license from the Department of Labor & Industries (L&I) — the 01 (HVAC) specialty. Contractors must carry $50,000+ general liability and $25,000+ bond coverage, and all work over $1,000 requires a permit with an L&I inspection. Seattle also requires a city business license for work within city limits. Always verify the L&I license number online before authorizing work over $500 — unlicensed "handyman" HVAC work invalidates manufacturer warranties and permits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to replace a furnace in Seattle?

Gas furnace replacement runs $4,500–$9,000 installed including removal and permit. High-efficiency 96% AFUE units qualify for PSE rebates of $300–$600.

Do Seattle homes need air conditioning?

Increasingly yes — 3 of the 10 hottest days in Seattle's history occurred after 2021. Heat pumps provide both heating and cooling for $150–$300/month in combined energy costs for a typical 1,800 sq ft home.

How often should a heat pump be serviced in Seattle?

Twice a year: once in spring before cooling season, once in fall before heating season. Seattle's wet climate makes coil cleaning essential — dirty coils cut efficiency 20–30%.

Do I need a permit for HVAC work in Seattle?

Yes. Any HVAC work over $1,000 requires a mechanical permit through the Seattle Department of Construction and Inspections, with L&I inspection after installation.

Are ductless mini-splits worth it for Seattle homes?

For homes without ductwork — common in Seattle's older housing stock — yes. Mini-splits cost $3,500–$6,000 per zone and qualify for City Light rebates, saving 25–40% vs electric baseboard heat.
