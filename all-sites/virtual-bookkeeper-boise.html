Idaho made small business accounting simpler when it moved to a flat 5.3% individual income tax rate. What did not get simpler is running the books: sales tax returns are due the 20th of every month, job costs on construction work need to be tracked before the job ends, and a growing Boise company that waits until March to look at its numbers has already lost the year. We close Boise companies' books remotely, every month.

Idaho Tax Basics Every Boise Owner Should Know

Idaho taxes individual income at a flat 5.3% rate under HB 40 of 2025, and the corporate rate also moved to 5.3%. Because Idaho starts from federal taxable income, most federal deductions and the qualified business income deduction flow through, which means good federal bookkeeping is most of Idaho tax compliance. There is no Idaho franchise tax and no local income tax, including in Boise.

Idaho's sales tax is 6% statewide with no county or city option, so there is one rate to program and one rate to reconcile. Sales tax returns are due on the 20th of the month following the reporting period, with the filing frequency assigned by the Tax Commission based on volume, and Idaho generally does not tax professional services. Idaho requires a seller's permit for businesses making taxable sales, and the City of Boise requires a business license for companies operating inside city limits.

Two Idaho habits trip up new owners. First, sales tax collected is not revenue: it is a liability sitting in the operating account until it is remitted, and companies that spend it end up borrowing from the state. Second, the 20th comes fast when a month ends on a weekend, and Idaho assesses penalties and interest on late returns even when the tax due is small.

What Monthly Bookkeeping Looks Like for a Boise Company

Every monthly engagement starts with reconciliation: bank accounts, credit cards, merchant accounts and loans, so that the numbers in the ledger match the money in the accounts. From there we categorize transactions, code job costs, invoice customers and age receivables, record bills, and produce a profit and loss statement, balance sheet and cash flow statement with a short written summary of what changed and why.

Boise's economy is construction, technology and professional services, and each needs a different chart of accounts. A contractor needs job costing by project, retainage tracking and change order documentation, because a job that looks profitable on a monthly P&L can be underwater once labor burden and materials are allocated correctly. A software or consulting shop needs deferred revenue, subscription metrics and contractor 1099 tracking. A retail or restaurant business needs sales tax collected and remitted by category and a clean inventory count.

Payroll adds Idaho withholding and unemployment insurance on top of federal requirements. We run payroll alongside the books, keep quarterly returns on schedule, and issue 1099-NECs by January 31 for every contractor paid $600 or more. Doing payroll outside the books is how a company ends up with unreconciled wage expense and a payroll tax notice six months later.

Catch-Up Bookkeeping and Audit-Ready Records

Most Boise companies that come to us for catch-up work have the same root problem: commingled personal and business spending. A business account that also pays for groceries, family travel and a personal vehicle takes hours to reconstruct, and it is the first thing an auditor or a lender questions. Separating accounts is free, and it typically cuts cleanup cost in half.

The Idaho State Tax Commission can assess back sales tax, penalties and interest going back several years, and it audits by comparing gross receipts on the income tax return against sales tax returns. When those two numbers disagree and nobody can explain why, the burden lands on the business. Monthly reconciliation is what makes that reconciliation explainable, and it is also what makes a bank loan or a business sale go smoothly.

Record retention follows the federal pattern: keep supporting documents for at least three years, and seven years for employment tax records and anything tied to a loss or an asset. QuickBooks Online holds the ledger indefinitely, but the receipts, contracts and bank statements that support the entries are yours to keep. We file them by month in a shared folder so the record is complete before anyone asks for it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Idaho's income tax rate for small business owners?

Idaho taxes individual income at a flat 5.3% under HB 40 of 2025, and the corporate rate also moved to 5.3%. Idaho starts from federal taxable income, so federal deductions and the qualified business income deduction flow through.

When are Idaho sales tax returns due?

The 20th of the month following the reporting period. The Tax Commission assigns monthly, quarterly, semi-annual or annual filing based on your sales volume, and Idaho has no local sales tax, so 6% is the only rate to program.

Do I need a license to do bookkeeping in Idaho?

No. Idaho does not license bookkeepers, so anyone can provide the service. What matters is whether the person keeps clean, reconciled records and can hand a CPA a defensible file.

How much does virtual bookkeeping cost in Boise?

Can you clean up a year of missed bookkeeping?

Yes. Catch-up projects typically run $1,000 to $5,500 per year of neglected books depending on transaction volume and how much personal spending is mixed into the business accounts.
