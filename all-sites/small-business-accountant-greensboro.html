How Much Does a Small Business Accountant Cost in Greensboro?

Monthly bookkeeping for a typical Greensboro small business runs $250–$800, covering reconciliations, invoicing, and monthly reports. Tax preparation runs $400–$1,500 depending on entity type and complexity — an S-corp with payroll runs at the top of that range. Payroll adds $50–$150 per month plus $5–$15 per employee per run. Annual tax planning engagements run $1,000–$3,000 and usually pay for themselves in the first quarter.

What you're really buying is the trade-off: owners who DIY their books with shoebox receipts pay an average of $2,000–$5,000 in catch-up fees and missed deductions at tax time. A Greensboro accountant who sees your books monthly spots cash-flow problems and tax issues while they're still fixable — not in April.

North Carolina Business Taxes You Need to Know

North Carolina's individual income tax rate is a flat 4.75% for 2025 and drops to 4.5% in 2026 — that's what most LLC and S-corp owners pay on pass-through profits. The corporate income tax rate is 2.5% in 2025 and falls to 2.25% in 2026, one of the lowest in the country for C-corporations. On top of income tax, NC charges a franchise tax on the net worth of corporations and LLCs taxed as corporations.

Two things trip up Greensboro owners: NC generally does not tax services at the sales tax counter, but tangible goods sales do — and the state's marketplace-facilitator rules mean online sellers have collection obligations even when they never touch the state. The other is the privilege license: North Carolina municipalities, including Greensboro, require an annual privilege license to do business within city limits, and the fee depends on gross receipts. A good accountant files it for you — and knows when you don't owe it.

The Piedmont Triad Business Mix

Greensboro's economy runs on manufacturing, logistics, and distribution — the Piedmont Triad International Airport's FedEx hub and the region's position as an East Coast distribution crossroads mean thousands of small freight, warehousing, and light-manufacturing businesses. Those businesses have specific tax angles: bonus depreciation on equipment, vehicle deductions for delivery fleets, and sales tax exemptions for manufacturing machinery that owners routinely miss.

Entity structure matters more in NC than owners expect: an LLC taxed as a sole proprietorship saves on filing fees but self-employment tax hits the full profit, while an S-corp election can save $3,000–$8,000 annually in self-employment tax once profits pass $50,000 — at the cost of payroll administration. The right structure depends on your actual revenue trajectory, and changing it later is expensive. That decision is the first thing a Greensboro accountant should walk you through.

Deductions Greensboro Owners Miss Every Year

The most-missed deductions in the Triad: the home office deduction (2025 IRS standard rate is $5 per square foot, up to 300 sq ft), the business standard mileage rate (70¢ per mile in 2025 — worth $7,000 per year to a 10,000-mile driver), Section 179 expensing on equipment, the 20% qualified business income (QBI) deduction, and the 50% meals deduction. Retirement plans are the sleeper: a SEP IRA lets a solo owner contribute up to 25% of compensation, and the 2025 contribution cap is $70,000 — a dollar-for-dollar tax reduction.

Quarterly estimated taxes are where most NC owners get hurt: the IRS and NC Department of Revenue both require quarterly payments, and underpayment penalties apply even if you pay the full amount at filing. An accountant sets the right quarterly numbers so you never face the penalty — and never face the April surprise of a $15,000 bill you didn't see coming.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a small business accountant cost in Greensboro?

Monthly bookkeeping runs $250–$800, tax preparation $400–$1,500, and payroll $50–$150 per month plus per-employee fees. Most firms offer fixed monthly bundles.

Should my business be an LLC or S-corp?

For most profitable NC businesses, an S-corp election saves $3,000–$8,000 per year in self-employment tax once profits exceed $50,000. An accountant models both structures on your actual numbers before you file the election.

Do I need to file quarterly estimated taxes?

Yes — both the IRS and NC Department of Revenue require quarterly estimates when you owe more than $1,000. Missing them triggers underpayment penalties on top of the tax itself.

What records do I need to hand over?

Bank and credit card statements, receipts over $75, mileage logs, and payroll records. If your books are behind, most accountants offer catch-up cleanup for $500–$2,000 before taking over monthly work.

Can an accountant fix past tax mistakes?

Yes. Amended returns can recover overpaid taxes for up to 3 years (NC) and the IRS allows amended federal returns within 3 years. Unfiled returns can also be brought current — the longer you wait, the more expensive it gets.
