Common Personal Injury Cases in Durham

Car accidents are the most common personal injury case. A driver with a clear green light hit by a red-light runner has an easy liability case. But if both drivers have partial fault, North Carolina's strict rule makes recovery difficult.

Slip-and-fall cases are harder. Property owners are only liable if they knew (or should have known) about the hazard and failed to warn or fix it. Proving 'should have known' requires evidence.

Medical malpractice cases require expert testimony proving the doctor's care fell below the standard of care. These are complex and expensive to litigate.

Settlement vs. Trial in a Durham Injury Case

Most personal injury cases settle before trial. Insurance companies assess the strength of your claim and make an offer to avoid trial risk.

A good settlement should cover medical bills, lost wages, and reasonable pain & suffering. Don't accept an offer that leaves you short on future medical care.

If the insurer lowballs you or denies liability, trial may be necessary. A jury can award more than a settlement, but there's risk — juries in NC are conservative.

No Upfront Cost — How Contingency Fees Work

Personal injury attorneys work on contingency: you pay no upfront fees. The attorney takes 25–40% of the final settlement or judgment.

If your case loses, the attorney gets nothing. This aligns the attorney's interest with yours.

Be transparent with your attorney about the facts. Exaggerating injuries or misrepresenting liability hurts your credibility and weakens your case.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is North Carolina's statute of limitations for personal injury?

Three years from the date of injury. Miss this deadline and you lose the right to sue, even if you have a strong case.

How does North Carolina's contributory negligence rule affect my case?

If you're found even 1% at fault, you recover $0. It's a strict rule — NC bars any recovery if the plaintiff shares any fault, unlike comparative negligence states.

How much can I recover in a personal injury case in Durham?

Medical bills, lost wages, pain & suffering, and future care costs. NC has no cap on pain & suffering damages, but recovery depends on proving defendant liability.

How much does a personal injury lawyer charge in Durham?

Attorneys work on contingency: 25–40% of the settlement or judgment if you win, $0 if you lose. No upfront cost.

What should I do immediately after a car accident in Durham?

Document the scene with photos, get witness contact information, file a police report, seek medical attention, and contact an attorney before giving a statement to the insurer.
