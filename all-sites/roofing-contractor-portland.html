%Portland Roofing Repair & Replacement%

%Portland gets rain on roughly half the days of the year — more than 150 days with measurable precipitation. Why Does Moss Grow So Fast on Portland Roofs?

Portland's combination of frequent rain, mild temperatures, and shaded neighborhoods is a moss factory. Moss holds moisture against the shingles, works under the edges, and slowly lifts them — turning a $400 cleaning into a $12,000 replacement if ignored for years.

The cheapest prevention is zinc or copper strips installed along the ridge; rainwater washes trace minerals down the slope and suppresses growth. A moss treatment and preventive strips together cost $500–$1,100 and typically hold for 3–5 years. If you already have thick moss, have it chemically treated and gently raked — never pressure-washed by an uninsured handyman, which voids most shingle warranties.

Do You Need a CCB-Licensed Roofing Contractor in Oregon?

Oregon requires any contractor performing work over $1,000 to be licensed with the Oregon Construction Contractors Board (CCB). That covers virtually every roof replacement, and most repairs. Before hiring, verify three things:

  • CCB license is active and in the roofing classification.
  • The contractor carries workers' compensation and general liability insurance.
  • They pull the required building permit through the City of Portland (or your local jurisdiction).

Also check for storm-damage 'storm chasers' — out-of-state crews who show up after wind events, demand a deposit, and disappear. A CCB-licensed local roofer with a physical office in the metro area is your safest bet.

Flat vs. Sloped Roofing for Portland's Wet Climate

Portland's older commercial buildings and many mid-century homes have low-slope or flat roofs, which need a completely different system than sloped asphalt. EPDM rubber and TPO membranes are the workhorses here; built-up gravel roofs are still around but are being replaced as they age.

Flat roofs in Portland fail most often at the seams and penetrations. A good contractor will inspect parapet flashing, roof drains, and HVAC curbs every year — the $150–$300 inspection cost is cheap insurance against a $3,000+ interior water damage claim.

Frequently Asked Questions

%

How long does a roof last in Portland?

Architectural asphalt shingles last 20–30 years in Portland's mild climate. Metal and tile last 40–60. Moss is the main factor that shortens asphalt life.

Does homeowners insurance cover storm damage to my roof?

Yes, for sudden wind and hail damage. Oregon policies typically cover the repair or replacement minus your deductible. Moss-related wear is considered maintenance and is not covered.

How do I know if my roof is leaking?

Ceiling stains, damp attic insulation, peeling paint, and a musty smell are the usual signs. Catching it early matters — rot spreads fast in Portland's humidity.

Should I repair or replace my roof?

If more than 30% of the roof is damaged, or the shingles are curling and brittle, replacement is usually cheaper in the long run. A one-off leak on an otherwise healthy roof is a repair.

How long does a roof replacement take in Portland?

A typical 1,800 sq ft home takes 2–4 days of dry weather. Repairs are usually same-day or next-day.
