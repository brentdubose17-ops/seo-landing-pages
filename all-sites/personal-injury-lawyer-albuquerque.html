How Much Is an Albuquerque Personal Injury Case Worth?

Settlement values depend on medical expenses, lost income, pain and suffering, and liability clarity. Minor injury claims (soft tissue, no hospitalization) settle for $5,000–$30,000. Moderate injury claims (fractures, surgery required) range $35,000–$120,000. Serious injury claims (permanent disability, traumatic brain injury) reach $150,000–$2M+. New Mexico has no cap on damages in most personal injury cases, which means serious injury claims can be significantly higher than in states with damage caps. The average Albuquerque auto accident settlement is approximately 3–4x the medical specials.

New Mexico's Statute of Limitations — Act Fast

New Mexico gives you 3 years from the accident date to file a personal injury lawsuit. While that's longer than many states, don't wait — evidence disappears, witnesses move, and insurance companies use delay tactics. If a government entity is involved (city bus, school zone, traffic light malfunction), the claim against the government must be filed within 90 days under the New Mexico Tort Claims Act. An experienced attorney identifies these deadlines immediately and ensures nothing is missed.

New Mexico's Pure Comparative Fault Rule

New Mexico follows a pure comparative fault rule: even if you're 99% at fault, you can still recover 1% of your damages from the other party. This is different from most states that bar recovery once you're 50% or 51% at fault. The practical effect: if you're rear-ended at a stoplight in Albuquerque but had a burned-out taillight, the other driver's insurer will argue partial fault. Your attorney needs to counter with accident reconstruction, police reports, and witness statements to minimize your assigned percentage — every percent costs you real money.
