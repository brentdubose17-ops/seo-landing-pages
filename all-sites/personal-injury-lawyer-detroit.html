How Much Does a Detroit Personal Injury Lawyer Cost?

Nothing upfront. Michigan personal injury attorneys work on contingency: typically 33% of the recovery if the case settles before a lawsuit is filed, and 40% if a lawsuit is filed and litigated. If the case doesn't win, you owe no attorney fees — though you may still owe out-of-pocket costs like medical record retrieval and filing fees.

That structure means a lawyer only takes your case if it's worth taking. The free consultation is also your due-diligence moment: ask about their trial experience, case volume in Wayne County, and who specifically will handle your file — at many Detroit firms, the partner you meet isn't the associate who does the work.

What's the Statute of Limitations in Michigan?

Michigan gives you 3 years from the date of injury to file a personal injury lawsuit (MCL 600.5805). Medical malpractice is shorter: 2 years. There are also notice requirements for claims against government entities — a crash with a city bus, pothole damage claim, or road-defect case may require notice within 120 days.

Missing any of these deadlines is fatal to the case, no matter how strong the merits. If you were injured more than 12 months ago, stop researching and call today — the investigation phase (medical records, accident reconstruction, expert review) eats months off that 3-year window.

Michigan No-Fault: What Does It Actually Cover?

Michigan is a no-fault state: your own PIP coverage pays your medical bills, rehab, and up to 85% of lost wages regardless of who caused the crash. It also covers attendant care and survivors' benefits. That's why the immediate move after a Detroit crash is filing a no-fault application with your own carrier — not waiting to see who's "at fault."

You can pursue the at-fault driver for pain and suffering only if your injury meets Michigan's "serious impairment of body function" threshold — a permanent, objectively documented impact on your life. That threshold is case-law driven, which is exactly why an experienced Wayne County attorney adds value: proving serious impairment is a legal fight, not a paperwork exercise.

How Much Is My Car Accident Case Worth?

Case value stacks three buckets: economic damages (medical bills, lost wages, future care), non-economic damages (pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life), and — in cases involving trucking companies or drunk drivers — potentially punitive damages. Detroit-area settlements commonly range from $25,000 for soft-tissue injuries to seven figures for catastrophic injuries.

Michigan applies comparative fault: your recovery is reduced by your percentage of fault, and you're barred entirely if you're more than 50% at fault. Insurers aggressively argue comparative fault in Wayne County cases — a passenger, a rear-end victim, or a pedestrian usually has the strongest position, while a disputed-lane-change case is weaker. A free case review with an attorney who knows the local courts is the only realistic way to value your claim.
