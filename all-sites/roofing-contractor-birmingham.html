Hail Damage Signs Every Birmingham Homeowner Should Know

Hail damage is not always obvious from the ground. Shingles may have bruises or soft spots rather than obvious cracks. Look for missing granules (bald spots), cracked shingles with rounded edges, or indents in gutters.

The National Storm Damage Center says that 1-inch hail can damage most roofs. Spring hail in Alabama often exceeds that, and many homeowners don't realize their roof is damaged until water leaks appear weeks later.

Get on the roof after a hail storm (safely) and document damage with photos. Hire a contractor to assess before filing a claim. Many insurance adjusters have 30-day claim windows, so fast action matters.

Asphalt vs. Metal Roofing in Birmingham's Climate

Asphalt shingles are the standard in Birmingham (cheaper upfront) but last only 20–25 years in a hot, humid climate with UV exposure. Birmingham's spring hail also ages shingles faster.

Metal roofing (standing seam or metal shingles) costs 30–50% more but lasts 40–50 years, withstands hail better, and reflects heat (saving 10–20% on cooling costs). Some insurers offer discounts on metal roofs in hail zones.

If your roof is nearing end of life and you've had hail claims, metal may be worth the upfront cost.

Insurance Claims for Hail & Storm Damage

When you file an insurance claim for roof damage, the adjuster inspects and estimates repair costs. The insurer pays the actual cash value (older roof) or replacement cost value (newer roof) minus your deductible.

Many roofing contractors handle the claim process by providing detailed damage assessments and repair quotes. They submit the paperwork to the insurer and work with the adjuster.

Common mistakes: waiting more than 30 days to file (claim denial), not photographing damage, or underestimating the extent of damage. Get a professional inspection and quote before filing.
