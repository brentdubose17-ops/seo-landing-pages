Personal Injury Lawyer in Reno, Nevada

Reno's I-80 corridor has one of Nevada's highest accident rates. Speeding, aggressive lane changes, and truck traffic create multi-vehicle pileups regularly. If you've been hit by another driver, you need a personal injury lawyer who knows Nevada negligence law, knows how Reno juries handle cases, and can negotiate with insurance companies that lowball injury claims.

Nevada Negligence Law and Comparative Fault in Reno

Nevada uses pure comparative negligence: if you're 80% at fault and the other driver is 20% at fault, you can recover 20% of your damages. This means even if you share some blame for the accident, you may still have a claim. The threshold is not "did you cause 50% of the accident"—it's "does the other party have ANY liability." Understanding Reno courts' interpretation of "pure negligence" is critical.

Common negligence factors in Reno I-80 accidents: speeding, texting, failing to yield, aggressive lane changes, tailgating, and DUI. If the other driver had a traffic citation (speeding ticket issued at the scene), that citation is evidence of negligence and strengthens your claim significantly.

Reno I-80 Truck Accidents and Liability

Semi-truck accidents on I-80 between Reno and Sacramento involve a different party: the trucking company, not just the driver. Trucking companies carry liability insurance (required by federal law) and have deep pockets compared to individual drivers. If a semi hits you, your injury lawyer will file a claim against the trucking company's insurer, which often settles cases faster than personal auto policies.

Truck accident cases are more complex: were the brakes maintained? Was the driver fatigued (DOT hours-of-service violations)? Was cargo properly secured? These factors increase your case value and are worth investigating.

How Reno Insurance Adjusters Lowball Claims

Insurance adjusters are trained to minimize payouts. They'll ask leading questions ("You were going how fast?"), request a recorded statement (which they use against you), and offer a low settlement within days, hoping you'll accept before consulting a lawyer. Don't sign anything or accept an offer without legal counsel.

A settlement offer of $2,000 for a car accident with $5,000 in medical bills is a red flag. Your attorney should demand the full amount of special damages (medical bills, lost wages, car repair) plus pain and suffering. Reno juries typically award pain and suffering at 1.5–3x the medical expenses for moderate injuries.

Nevada Statute of Limitations and Filing Deadlines

Nevada has a 2-year statute of limitations for personal injury claims (NRS 11.190). If you don't file a lawsuit within 2 years of the accident date, your claim is barred forever. Start consulting with an attorney within weeks, not months. Many attorneys work on contingency (they take a percentage of the settlement, not a retainer), so there's no financial barrier to seeking legal advice.

Your insurance claim and a lawsuit are separate. Filing an insurance claim doesn't extend the 2-year deadline for suing—the clock starts the day of the accident. Don't delay.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do immediately after a car accident in Reno?

Call 911 if injuries or significant damage. Get the other driver's name, phone, insurance info, and license plate. Take photos of the scene and vehicle damage. Don't admit fault. Contact a personal injury attorney within days, before talking to the insurance adjuster.

How much is my Reno car accident worth?

It depends on medical expenses, lost wages, injury severity, and the other driver's fault. A minor accident (soft-tissue injury, $3,000 medical bills) is worth $5,000–$15,000. Serious injuries (surgery, ongoing treatment) can be worth $50,000–$200,000+.

Should I accept the insurance company's first settlement offer?

Almost never. Insurance adjusters lowball initial offers, hoping you'll accept quickly. Consult an attorney before accepting anything. Many personal injury lawyers work on contingency and offer free consultations.

What's Nevada's statute of limitations for personal injury claims?

2 years from the date of the accident. If you don't file a lawsuit within 2 years, your claim is permanently barred. Start consulting with an attorney within weeks of the accident, not months.
