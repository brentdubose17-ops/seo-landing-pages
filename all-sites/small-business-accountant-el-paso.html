Texas Taxes — What El Paso Business Owners Need to Know

Texas has no state income tax — a major advantage for El Paso business owners. However, Texas does have a franchise tax (0.375% for most businesses, 0.75% for retail/wholesale) on revenue over $1.23 million. If your El Paso business has gross receipts under the threshold, you file a no-tax-due report annually. If you're over, proper deductions and cost-of-goods-sold calculations can dramatically reduce your taxable margin. An El Paso CPA who specializes in Texas franchise tax knows exactly which deductions maximize your savings and which triggers audit flags.

Borderplex Accounting — The Cross-Border Advantage

El Paso's unique position on the US-Mexico border creates distinct accounting considerations. Businesses with cross-border suppliers or customers need to understand maquiladora regulations, Section 482 transfer pricing rules, and customs valuation requirements. The USMCA treaty provides preferential tariff treatment for qualifying goods — but only if your accountant prepares the correct NAFTA/USMCA certification documentation. If you import raw materials from Juarez manufacturing partners for finishing in El Paso, proper cost segregation between US and Mexican operations can save 15–25% on combined tax liability. This is specialized expertise — not every accountant handles cross-border work.
