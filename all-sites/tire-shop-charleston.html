Charleston Roads, Potholes and Tire Damage

Charleston's streets suffer from flooding and heavy rain damage, which creates potholes and ruts that damage tires, bent rims and suspension misalignment. A single large pothole can puncture a tire, bend a rim or destroy a shock absorber. Tires rated for 5–7 years of normal use can fail immediately after a pothole hit. After hitting a pothole in Charleston, you should have the tire, rim and suspension checked even if the vehicle seems to be driving normally.

High humidity also affects tire pressure. Tire air is affected by temperature and humidity, and Charleston's hot humid air causes tire pressure to increase by 2–5 psi compared to cooler/drier areas. Overinflated tires wear faster in the center tread and are more prone to blowouts on hot days. Check tire pressure monthly, especially in the summer.

The salt-laden air from the Atlantic Ocean accelerates corrosion of wheel rims and lug bolts. A steel rim in Charleston will show rust within 6–12 months if not painted or treated. Aluminum rims are less prone to corrosion but can still oxidize. Regularly wash rims and tires to remove salt buildup.

Tire Brands, Warranty and Replacement Cycles

We stock tires from all major manufacturers: Michelin, Goodyear, Bridgestone, Continental, Dunlop, Kumho and others. Premium tires last 40,000–60,000 miles; budget tires last 25,000–40,000 miles. Most tires come with a 3–5 year manufacturer warranty covering defects, and some offer mileage warranties (e.g., 40,000-mile warranty means Michelin will replace the tire if tread depth reaches 2/32", the legal minimum, before 40,000 miles).

South Carolina's legal tire tread depth is 2/32 of an inch. At that depth, a tire is no longer safe for highway driving, especially in wet conditions. Check your tread with a penny test: insert a penny into the tread with Lincoln's head facing down. If the top of Lincoln's head is visible, the tread is less than 2/32" and the tire must be replaced.

A new tire in Charleston costs $80–$200 per tire depending on size and brand. Most vehicles need 4 tires replaced at a time, so a set of 4 tires costs $320–$800 plus installation, balancing and alignment. Winter tires are not required in Charleston but are recommended for occasional cold weather driving.

Tire Repair, Patching and Alignment Services

A puncture in the tread area (not on the sidewall) can often be repaired with a plug or patch. Plug repair costs $10–$30 per tire; patch repair (more permanent) costs $20–$50 per tire. A puncture on the sidewall cannot be safely repaired and the tire must be replaced. Run-flat tires (designed to be driven on after a puncture) cost 15–20% more but are convenient if you live or work far from a tire shop.

Wheel alignment is critical after a pothole hit or suspension work. Misaligned wheels cause uneven tire wear (the inside or outside edges wear faster), pulling to one side and poor handling. We use a computerized alignment rack to measure and adjust all four wheels. Alignment costs $75–$150 depending on whether it is a 2-wheel or 4-wheel alignment.

Tire balancing (static and dynamic) ensures even weight distribution around the tire and wheel. Unbalanced tires cause vibration at highway speeds and accelerate tread wear. Balancing costs $15–$35 per tire and is recommended every 6,000–10,000 miles or when a new tire is installed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the legal tire tread depth in South Carolina?

2/32 of an inch. Use a penny test to check: if you see the top of Lincoln's head, the tire must be replaced.

How much does a new tire cost in Charleston?

Tires cost $80–$200 per tire depending on size and brand. A set of 4 tires with installation and balancing runs $320–$800.

Can a tire sidewall puncture be repaired?

No. Sidewall punctures cannot be safely repaired; the tire must be replaced. Only tread punctures can be patched.

How often should I rotate my tires?

Every 5,000–7,000 miles to ensure even wear. Front tires wear faster due to braking and turning.

Do I need winter tires in Charleston?

Not required, but recommended for occasional cold weather driving. All-season tires are adequate for most Charleston winters.
