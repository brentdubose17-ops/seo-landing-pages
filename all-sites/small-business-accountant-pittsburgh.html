How Much Does a Small Business Accountant Cost in Pittsburgh?

Pittsburgh accounting fees run below the national metro average. Monthly bookkeeping runs $200–$600 for most small businesses; tax preparation runs $400–$1,500 for a business return and $150–$400 for a personal return. Full-service engagements — bookkeeping plus tax planning plus payroll — run $500–$2,500 per month depending on transaction volume and complexity.

Most Pittsburgh CPAs quote flat monthly fees for bookkeeping (based on transaction count) and flat fees for tax prep, not hourly rates. A 3-person contracting company in the Strip District recently paid $450/month for bookkeeping and $1,100 for its S-corp tax return — typical. The cheapest option isn't usually the best: a bookkeeper who misses the quarterly estimated tax dates costs more in penalties than their entire annual fee.

Pennsylvania Business Taxes in 2026: What Your Accountant Handles

Pennsylvania taxes businesses harder than most states. The Corporate Net Income Tax rate is 6.49% in 2026, on a phased path down to 4.99% by 2031 — still one of the highest CIT rates in the country, and Pennsylvania is one of only two states that taxes LLCs' gross receipts through the PA-20S/PA-65 reporting requirements. There's no state gross receipts tax for most small businesses, but there is a personal income tax flat rate of 3.07% on pass-through income — among the lowest flat rates, but it applies regardless of federal brackets.

Pittsburgh adds its own layers: the city's Payroll Preparation Tax ($52 per employee per year, withheld by employers), the Business Privilege Tax on gross receipts for businesses operating in the city, and local services tax ($52 per employee). Allegheny County adds a 1% sales tax on top of Pennsylvania's 6% state rate — 7% total in the city. A Pittsburgh accountant who doesn't register you for the city payroll and BPT filings will cost you in late fees before your first tax season ends.

LLC vs S-Corp in Pennsylvania: The Decision That Saves the Most

Pennsylvania LLCs default to tax treatment as a sole proprietorship (single-member) or partnership (multi-member), with all profit flowing through at the 3.07% state rate. Electing S-corp status changes the game: you can pay yourself a "reasonable salary" (subject to payroll taxes) and take the rest as distributions that avoid self-employment tax — a strategy that saves a typical Pittsburgh service business $3,000–$8,000 per year once profit passes $50,000–$60,000.

The tradeoff: S-corps add payroll administration (quarterly 941s, unemployment filings, payroll tax deposits) and Pennsylvania requires the S-corp election at the state level within 2.5 months of the federal election. Your accountant should run the numbers both ways — the S-corp election is usually worth it above roughly $60,000 in net profit, but the payroll compliance burden is real, and a missed Pennsylvania payroll filing wipes out the savings fast.

When to Hire an Accountant vs DIY in Pittsburgh

If you're a freelancer or single-member LLC with no employees and under $100,000 in revenue, quality software plus a once-a-year CPA review can work — expect to pay $250–$500 for a CPA to review your return. The moment you have employees, sell in multiple states, carry inventory, or pass $100,000 in revenue, the DIY math breaks: a missed quarterly estimated payment triggers a 4.5% annualized penalty in PA, and sales tax misclassification in Allegheny County's 7% combined rate adds up fast.

Watch for the audit triggers: Pennsylvania actively cross-checks 1099 filings against business returns, and home-office and vehicle deductions are a top audit item here. A Pittsburgh accountant who sets up your chart of accounts correctly in month one prevents the $1,500–$5,000 cleanup-and-reconstruct mess that DIY books produce by tax season. If your books are already messy, ask about catch-up bookkeeping — most firms charge $300–$2,500 to clean up a year of backlog.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an accountant cost for a small business in Pittsburgh?

Monthly bookkeeping runs $200–$600, business tax prep $400–$1,500, and full-service (bookkeeping + tax + payroll) $500–$2,500 per month. Most Pittsburgh firms quote flat monthly fees based on transaction volume.

Should I form an LLC or S-corp in Pennsylvania?

Start as an LLC for simplicity; consider the S-corp election once net profit passes roughly $60,000, where the self-employment tax savings typically exceed the payroll compliance costs. Your accountant should model both.

What taxes does a Pittsburgh business pay?

Federal income tax, Pennsylvania's 3.07% flat personal income tax on pass-through income (or 6.49% corporate rate), Allegheny County's 1% local sales tax on top of the 6% state rate, and city-level payroll and business privilege taxes for businesses operating in Pittsburgh.

How far back can I file back taxes in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania generally allows 3 years for a refund claim and can go back further for unfiled returns. If you haven't filed in years, a CPA can negotiate a filing plan — but penalties accrue, so the sooner you fix it the cheaper it is.

Do I need a CPA or is a bookkeeper enough?

A bookkeeper keeps the records clean; a CPA or enrolled agent signs tax returns and gives legal-signature tax advice. Most Pittsburgh small businesses need both — a bookkeeper monthly and a CPA for tax strategy and filings.
