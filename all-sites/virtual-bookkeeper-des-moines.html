QuickBooks Online is the dominant platform for Des Moines virtual bookkeepers, used by approximately 72% of Iowa-based remote bookkeeping firms. Monthly reconciliation should include all bank accounts, credit cards, merchant accounts, and loan accounts, with a variance report explaining any discrepancies over $50.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a virtual bookkeeper different from an in-person one?

Virtual bookkeepers work remotely through cloud-based accounting software. The service quality is identical — often better because virtual bookkeepers specialize in specific software and industries rather than general practice. Monthly meetings are via Zoom or phone.

Do virtual bookkeepers need to be licensed in Iowa?

No state license is required for bookkeeping (vs. CPA which requires a license). However, any bookkeeper offering tax preparation or financial statement assurance must be a licensed CPA. For basic bookkeeping, a certification in QuickBooks or Xero plus 2+ years of experience is the standard benchmark.

Can a virtual bookkeeper handle Iowa sales tax?

Iowa does not have a state sales tax on services (only on tangible goods at 6%), so most service-based Des Moines businesses don't file sales tax. Product-based businesses do. A virtual bookkeeper familiar with Iowa's tax code will know which rules apply to your specific business type.

How do I share documents with a virtual bookkeeper securely?

Encrypted document portals (ShareFile, SmartVault, or QBO's built-in document center) are standard. Never email bank statements or tax documents. Your bookkeeper should provide a secure upload link on onboarding.

What's included in a standard monthly bookkeeping package in Des Moines?

Bank and credit card reconciliation, accounts payable/receivable management, monthly financial statements (P&L, balance sheet, cash flow), sales tax filing (if applicable), payroll data integration, and a monthly review call. Some packages include 1099-NEC preparation for independent contractors.
