Rhode Island's Winter Windshield Hazards

Rhode Island's winters bring ice storms, heavy snow and rapid temperature changes that damage windshields. A windshield exposed to 20°F air suddenly heated to 70°F by the defroster can crack along pre-existing stress points. Road salt and sand scatter across I-95 and local streets, and frozen particles kicked up by plows create stone chips. A chip that starts small can develop into a crack overnight in cold weather.

Many Rhode Island drivers do not replace windshields until they are 100% necessary, but a cracked windshield fails MOT inspection and is illegal to drive on. A chip smaller than a dime in the driver's line of sight violates RI law and is a traffic violation. A crack longer than 12 inches in any direction requires replacement.

Thermal shock damage is unique to cold climates. In winter, avoid directing the defroster heat directly at the windshield for more than 5 minutes at a time. Instead, use the windshield wipers and exterior defroster to clear ice before applying cabin heat. A gradual temperature increase is less likely to crack the glass.

Insurance Coverage for Windshield Replacement in Rhode Island

Rhode Island does not mandate comprehensive coverage or require a zero-deductible glass policy. Many policies include comprehensive coverage with a deductible (usually $500–$1,000), and the policyholder pays the deductible for glass replacement. Some insurers offer optional glass coverage with a $0 deductible, which is popular in New England. Check your policy or contact your agent to see what glass coverage you have.

If your insurance covers the windshield replacement, we process the claim directly with the insurer. We obtain an approval before starting work, and the insurance company pays us. You only pay your deductible. If you choose to pay out of pocket, windshield replacement costs $300–$700 depending on the vehicle and glass type.

Some newer vehicles have advanced features in the windshield: heads-up display (HUD) projectors, camera sensors for adaptive cruise control or automatic emergency braking, or rain-sensing wipers. Replacing the windshield on these vehicles requires recalibration of the camera and HUD system, which adds $300–$600 to the cost.

Windshield Chip Repair vs. Replacement Decision

A small chip (smaller than a quarter) in the non-impact zone (outside the driver's direct line of sight) can often be repaired with resin injection. The repair fills the chip and prevents water infiltration and crack development. Chip repair costs $60–$150 per chip and takes 30–60 minutes. The repair is cosmetic and does not restore full strength, so the vehicle is still roadworthy.

A chip in the driver's line of sight, or a chip larger than a quarter, requires windshield replacement. A crack of any length requires replacement. Replacement takes 1–2 hours for most vehicles because the windshield must be removed, the frame cleaned and dried, and a new windshield bonded in place with automotive urethane adhesive. The adhesive must cure for 1 hour before the vehicle is safe to drive.

Aftermarket windshields cost $250–$400 (basic quality). OEM windshields (manufacturer original) cost $400–$600 and are preferred for high-end vehicles. The choice of aftermarket vs. OEM should be discussed with your insurance company, as some policies specify which type is covered.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a windshield chip be repaired or does it need replacement?

Chips smaller than a quarter can be repaired with resin injection ($60–$150). Larger chips or cracks require full windshield replacement.

Does Rhode Island insurance cover windshield replacement?

Coverage depends on your policy. Most comprehensive plans have a deductible ($500–$1,000). Some policies offer optional $0 deductible glass coverage.

How long does windshield replacement take?

Replacement takes 1–2 hours, and the adhesive needs 1 hour to cure before driving. Mobile service is available at your home or work.

What is the difference between OEM and aftermarket windshields?

OEM glass is from the original manufacturer; aftermarket is from a third party. OEM is higher quality but more expensive. Most insurance covers aftermarket.

Do new vehicles with cameras in the windshield need special replacement?

Yes. The camera sensor must be recalibrated after windshield replacement, which adds $300–$600 to the cost. Some insurance policies cover this.
