How Much Does a Greensboro Personal Injury Lawyer Cost?

Greensboro personal injury lawyers work on contingency — you pay nothing upfront, and the attorney takes a percentage of the settlement only if you win. Standard North Carolina contingency fees run 33.3% of the settlement if the case settles before filing suit, and 40% if a lawsuit is filed or goes to trial. Free consultations are the norm; most Triad firms offer them and only get paid out of the recovery.

The math matters: a $50,000 settlement with a 33.3% fee nets $33,350 after the attorney's cut, minus case costs (medical records, expert fees) which are typically deducted before or after the fee depending on the contract. Ask upfront how costs are handled — some firms deduct costs before calculating their percentage, which can cost you thousands on a big case.

North Carolina's Contributory Negligence Rule — Critical for Greensboro

North Carolina is one of only a handful of states that still follows pure contributory negligence: if you are found even 1% at fault for an accident, you can recover nothing. This makes North Carolina one of the hardest states to win a personal injury case in, and it's exactly why you need a lawyer who knows how to protect your claim from the first phone call.

What that means in practice: don't admit fault at the scene, don't give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurance company, and get the police report corrected if it's inaccurate. A Greensboro attorney will investigate fault aggressively — witness statements, dashcam footage, traffic camera video, and accident reconstruction are the tools that keep a case from being killed by the contributory negligence bar.

What's a Greensboro Injury Claim Worth?

Greensboro car accident settlements vary wildly: minor soft-tissue claims settle for $5,000–$25,000, moderate injuries with medical bills under $50,000 settle for $25,000–$75,000, and serious injuries (fractures, surgeries, permanent impairment) routinely settle for $100,000–$500,000+. Wrongful death and catastrophic injury cases can go far higher. The key drivers are medical bills, lost wages, permanent disability ratings, and policy limits of the at-fault driver.

North Carolina requires minimum auto coverage of $30,000/$60,000 bodily injury — which is low. That means many Greensboro claims are capped by the at-fault driver's policy limits unless there are additional policies to stack: your own underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage, umbrella policies, or the at-fault driver's umbrella. A good attorney checks every available policy; that's often the difference between a $30,000 offer and a $250,000 recovery.

Statute of Limitations and What to Do After a Greensboro Accident

North Carolina's statute of limitations for personal injury is 3 years from the date of the accident, and 2 years for wrongful death. Miss the deadline and your claim is dead — courts strictly enforce it. Medical bills and treatment take time, but the legal clock starts ticking immediately, so meet with a lawyer early even if you're still treating.

In the first days after a Greensboro accident: seek medical care (even if you feel fine — whiplash symptoms can take 48 hours to appear), document everything with photos, save all bills and records, don't post about the accident on social media, and don't accept a quick settlement from the insurance adjuster. Insurers often make low early offers hoping you'll settle before you know the full extent of your injuries.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in Greensboro?

Three years from the accident date for personal injury, two years for wrongful death. Courts enforce these strictly, so don't wait — especially if you're still in treatment.

What if I was partially at fault for the accident?

North Carolina's contributory negligence rule means even 1% fault can bar recovery entirely. A Greensboro attorney can fight fault findings with evidence, but never admit fault at the scene and never give a recorded statement without counsel.

How much is my injury case worth?

It depends on medical bills, lost wages, permanency, and available insurance. Soft-tissue cases often settle in the $5,000–$25,000 range; serious injuries with surgery or permanent impairment routinely reach $100,000+. A free consultation will give you a realistic range.

Do I have to pay anything upfront?

No. Greensboro personal injury lawyers work on contingency — you pay nothing unless the case settles or wins, and the fee comes out of the recovery. Free consultations are standard.

Should I talk to the other driver's insurance company?

No. The adjuster's job is to minimize payout, and anything you say can be used against you. Refer them to your attorney — most firms handle all insurance communications once retained.
