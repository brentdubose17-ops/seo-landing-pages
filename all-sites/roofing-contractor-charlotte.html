Roofing Contractor in Charlotte, NC

Last updated: August 2026 · Serving Charlotte and surrounding areas

Charlotte's spring hailstorms and hurricane-season rain make roof damage a fact of life in Mecklenburg County. A licensed roofing contractor in Charlotte handles hail inspections, storm tarping, insurance claims, and full replacements — with documented damage photos, workmanship warranties, and pricing that won't move after the estimate.

How Much Does a New Roof Cost in Charlotte?

A typical asphalt shingle roof on a 1,500–2,000 sq ft Charlotte home runs $8,500–$18,000 installed — including tear-off, underlayment, new flashing, and disposal. Architectural shingles add $2,000–$4,000 over 3-tab, and standing-seam metal runs $15,000–$28,000. Emergency tarping after a storm costs $250–$800 and is usually refundable against the replacement.

Repairs are far cheaper when caught early: a missing shingle patch runs $300–$600, flashing repair $400–$1,200, and a leak around a chimney or skylight $500–$2,000. The longer you wait after hail damage, the more likely water intrusion turns a $1,500 repair into an $18,000 replacement plus interior damage.

Does Charlotte Get Hail Damage? How Do Claims Work?

Yes — the Charlotte metro sits in the Carolinas' hail corridor, with damaging storms arriving most springs and summer evenings. Golf-ball-size hail (1.75") easily dents shingles, knocks granules loose, and exposes the fiberglass mat. A free hail inspection after any storm with 1"+ hail is the single best habit a Charlotte homeowner can adopt.

If hail damaged your roof, document it with photos, call a licensed roofer for a written damage report, then file with your insurer within your policy's reporting window. Your payout is either actual cash value (ACV — depreciated) or replacement cost value (RCV — full current cost). Charlotte adjusters see hundreds of these claims each spring, so the process moves quickly when you have professional documentation.

Do Roofers in North Carolina Need a License?

Yes. Any roofing project over $30,000 requires a Class I or Class II General Contractor license from the North Carolina Licensing Board for General Contractors, plus liability insurance and workers' comp. Many established Charlotte roofers hold Class I (unlimited) licenses, meaning they're legally qualified for any size job.

Verify the license number on the NC Licensing Board website before signing. Storm-chasing out-of-state crews regularly work Charlotte after hail events without NC licenses — their work is frequently uninsured and uninspected, and homeowners end up paying twice.

How Fast Can I Get Storm Repairs in Charlotte?

Emergency tarping typically happens within 24–48 hours of a storm so water stops entering immediately. Once materials are secured, a full replacement takes 1–3 days for an average home — but after major hail events, demand can push material delivery 2–4 weeks out, so schedule your inspection early to lock in a slot.

For non-emergency work, the best windows are March–May and September–October. Charlotte's 90°F+ summers make shingle sealing less reliable, and cold-weather adhesive struggles below 50°F — timing the install season saves callbacks and warranty disputes.
