How Much Does a New Roof Cost in San Antonio?

Architectural shingle replacement on a typical 2,000 sq ft San Antonio home runs $9,000–$16,000 installed; metal roofs run $18,000–$32,000 and are increasingly common given hail. Roof repair (spot shingle replacement, flashing, leak repair) runs $350–$2,500 depending on access.

San Antonio's hail exposure changes the math: most homeowners file through insurance rather than paying cash, and a standard homeowner's policy covers hail and wind damage with a 1% deductible (about $2,500–$4,000 on a $250K–$400K home). Expect $6,500–$25,000 for full replacement after a major storm event — and expect wait times of 6–10 weeks after a big hailstorm, because every roofer in town books out simultaneously.

What License Does a Roofer Need in Texas?

Texas requires all roofing contractors to hold a Roofing Contractor License (RCL) issued by the Texas Department of Insurance. Since the requirement took effect in 2015, any contractor offering roof work without an RCL is operating illegally — and your insurance company can refuse to pay if work is done by an unlicensed roofer.

Ask for the RCL number, verify it on TDI's lookup page, and confirm the company carries general liability ($1M+) and workers' comp. Texas is also a strict state on insurance fraud: after major hail events, the TDI runs enforcement sweeps against "storm chasers" who knock on doors post-hail — a legitimate San Antonio roofer will never pressure you into a same-day contract.

San Antonio Hail Season: What Your Roof Faces

San Antonio sits in "Hail Alley" — the metro averages 1–3 significant hail events per year, with the peak season running March through May. Hailstones of 1–2 inches are common; stones over 2 inches hit the area every few years and can punch straight through asphalt shingles.

Hail damage isn't always visible from the ground. Granule loss, bruising, and cracked shingles show up on a careful roofline inspection, and insurance adjusters use the "coin test" plus satellite imagery. If your roof is 15+ years old, even minor hail can justify a full replacement claim because the shingles are nearing end-of-life anyway — a knowledgeable roofer will document this properly for your adjuster.

Insurance Claims: How the Process Works in Texas

After a hailstorm, the process is: (1) get a free roof inspection and documentation, (2) file a claim with your homeowner's insurer, (3) the adjuster inspects and writes an estimate, (4) your roofer negotiates supplements for items the adjuster missed (decking, underlayment, flashing). Legitimate San Antonio roofers handle the supplement process — it's routine and expected in hail country.

Know your deductible before you sign anything: Texas allows a separate wind/hail deductible (often 1% of dwelling coverage) that's higher than your all-peril deductible. Also, Texas law requires insurers to pay for matching shingles — if half your roof is damaged, the whole slope must match. A good roofer will fight for the full roof when the damage warrants it, not just patch the visible hit.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my San Antonio roof has hail damage?

Look for missing granules in gutters, soft spots or bruising on shingles, cracked or split tabs, and dented metal flashing or AC fins. A licensed roofer can give a free inspection and document findings with photos for your insurance claim.

How long do I have to file a hail claim in Texas?

Texas homeowners policies typically require claims within 1 year of the loss, though the Texas Department of Insurance recommends filing as soon as possible after a storm. Check your specific policy for the deadline.

Will my insurance drop me after a hail claim?

Possibly, if you file multiple claims in a short window. One hail claim on an old roof is normal in San Antonio; two or more within 3 years may trigger non-renewal — weigh that before filing for minor damage.

How long does a roof replacement take in San Antonio?

Most residential tear-offs and replacements take 1–3 days of active work, but scheduling can stretch to 6–10 weeks after a major hailstorm because demand spikes across the city.

Are metal roofs worth it in San Antonio?

For hail resistance, yes — metal roofs shrug off hail that destroys asphalt and last 40–60 years. At $18,000–$32,000 they cost roughly double upfront, but many insurers offer premium discounts of 10–25% for Class 4 impact-rated roofing.
