How Much Does a New Roof Cost in Orlando?

Orlando roof replacements run $8,000–$18,000 for a typical 2,000-square-foot home with architectural asphalt shingles, $15,000–$30,000 for standing-seam metal, and $20,000–$40,000 for concrete tile — the classic central Florida look. Repairs are cheaper: missing shingles $300–$800, leak repairs $400–$1,500, and flashing replacement $500–$2,000. Labor and material costs have climbed with every hurricane season, so 2026 prices run roughly 15–25% above 2020 levels.

Orlando's hot, humid climate shortens roof life: an asphalt shingle roof that lasts 25 years in a cool climate lasts 15–20 years here. The UV exposure cooks the asphalt, and the afternoon thunderstorm cycle (Orlando averages more than 70 thunderstorms a year) beats shingles with wind and water. When you get three estimates, expect the middle one to be the honest one — the low bidder is usually bidding on materials that won't survive Florida's building code.

Do Roofers Need a License in Florida?

Yes — Florida requires roofers to hold a state license from the Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR). That's either a Certified Roofing Contractor (statewide) or Registered Roofing Contractor (county-level) license, and you can verify both on the DBPR website. Florida is one of the strictest states in the country on roofing licensing because of the storm-claims abuse that followed past hurricanes.

Since 2022's SB 4D, Florida law requires roofers to give homeowners a written notice explaining insurance deductibles and forbids paying, waiving, or rebating deductibles as an incentive. Contractors also can't solicit roof repair door-to-door after a storm without following strict rules. A licensed Orlando roofer will walk you through the paperwork, pull the Orange County permit, and schedule the inspection — anyone who wants cash, skips permits, or promises "free roofs" is a red flag.

Orlando's Heat, Humidity, and Hurricane Season

Orlando sits in a high wind zone with 120+ mph design wind speeds under the Florida Building Code. That means impact-rated shingles, proper nail patterns (6 nails per shingle minimum, not 4), and hurricane clips on the roof deck are code requirements, not upgrades. Roofs built to the post-2007 code handle hurricanes far better than older ones — homes with roofs installed before 2001 are the ones that lose shingles when a storm comes through.

Heat and humidity do their own damage. Black algae and moss streaks on Orlando roofs aren't just cosmetic — the organisms trap moisture and degrade shingles. Ridge vents and proper attic ventilation drop attic temps 20–30°F and extend roof life by years. A wind-mitigation inspection by a licensed contractor can also cut your Florida homeowners insurance premium by 10–40% — in a state with the highest average premiums in the country, that's often $500–$2,000 a year back in your pocket.

Storm Damage and Insurance Claims in Orlando

After a hurricane or severe storm, document everything before anyone touches the roof: photos of missing shingles, water stains, and debris, plus a timeline. Cover immediate damage with tarps if it's safe, and file your insurance claim promptly — Florida law imposes strict deadlines on property insurance claims, and waiting weeks after a storm can cost you coverage. Get a licensed roofer's written estimate before the adjuster visit, and make sure your roofer walks the roof with the adjuster.

Florida's insurance market has been through major changes — many carriers have left or restricted new policies, and assignment of benefits rules now make it risky to sign over your claim to a contractor. The safe play in 2026: work with a licensed local roofer who documents everything, don't sign AOBs, and read your policy's deductible terms before repairs start. Roofers who pressure you to sign the claim over to them on the spot are the #1 source of Florida roofing fraud complaints.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a roof last in Orlando?

Architectural asphalt shingles last 15–20 years in central Florida's heat and storms; metal lasts 30–50 years; tile lasts 40–60 years. If your asphalt roof is past 15 years, have it inspected annually before hurricane season.

Shingle, metal, or tile — which is best for Orlando?

Asphalt is cheapest ($8,000–$18,000) but shortest-lived in Florida's climate. Metal ($15,000–$30,000) handles storms and heat best and lowers insurance. Tile ($20,000–$40,000) is the classic Florida look with the longest life but the heaviest structural demands.

Does homeowners insurance cover roof replacement in Florida?

Yes, for storm or sudden damage, minus your deductible. Florida law requires insurers to offer a replacement-cost endorsement. Normal wear-and-tear and age-related failure are not covered — that's why annual inspections matter.

How do I spot storm damage on my Orlando roof?

Check for missing or lifted shingles, exposed nail heads, dented or cracked tiles, and granules in the gutters after a storm. Inside, look for water stains on ceilings and in the attic. When in doubt, a licensed roofer's inspection is usually free.

Does Orange County require a permit for a roof replacement?

Yes — Orange County requires a roofing permit for any replacement and schedules a final inspection. A licensed contractor pulls the permit as part of the job; if a roofer says you don't need one, find another roofer.
