What Are Arizona's Business Tax Requirements?

Arizona businesses pay a 2.5% flat corporate income tax (one of the lowest in the US), plus Transaction Privilege Tax (TPT) — Arizona's version of a sales tax — which applies to gross receipts and runs 5.6% state rate plus city rates (Phoenix's combined TPT is 8.6%). TPT is paid monthly or quarterly depending on volume, and penalties for late filing start at 4.7% per month.

New businesses must register for an Arizona Transaction Privilege Tax license and a City of Phoenix privilege license, and LLCs pay an annual report fee of $45 to the Arizona Corporation Commission. Unlike most states, Arizona requires no separate state-level LLC franchise tax — but it does require foreign LLCs registering in Arizona to file annual reports with the ACC.

Phoenix-Specific Accounting Issues to Watch

Construction and home services — a massive Phoenix sector — face unique TPT rules: contractors must pay TPT on materials at purchase, then again on the installed labor, and Arizona requires separate contractor licenses by trade (ROC Class 39/40 for HVAC, B-1 for general contractors). Getting this classification wrong is the #1 audit trigger for Arizona contractors.

Remote and seasonal businesses have a second layer: Arizona's economic nexus rules require out-of-state companies selling into Arizona to collect TPT once they exceed $150,000 in annual sales to Arizona customers (or 200 transactions). Snowbird-heavy markets like Scottsdale and Paradise Valley also generate part-year residency questions that a local CPA handles routinely — a $500–$1,000 planning fee that routinely saves $5,000+ in tax.

When Should You Hire a Phoenix Accountant?

Hire an accountant when you cross any of these lines: revenue above $250K/year, hiring employees (payroll, unemployment, and workers' comp reporting), collecting TPT, buying real estate or major equipment, or any multi-owner structure. Before that, bookkeeping software plus a $350–$500 annual tax-prep engagement may be sufficient.

The most expensive mistake Phoenix founders make is waiting until tax season. A proactive accountant who reviews your books quarterly can shift $10,000–$25,000 in deductions (home office, vehicle, equipment Section 179, retirement plans) that a reactive preparer will miss. Ask any candidate: "How many of your clients have you saved from an Arizona TPT audit?" — the good ones have a number.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a Phoenix CPA charge per month?

Ongoing small business packages run $250–$800/month for bookkeeping, monthly financials, and tax filing support. Full-service engagements with payroll and TPT filing run $800–$1,500/month.

What taxes do Arizona small businesses pay?

Corporate income tax at 2.5% (or pass-through rates on your personal return), Transaction Privilege Tax on gross receipts (Phoenix combined rate 8.6%), plus payroll taxes if you have employees. LLCs also pay a $45 annual report fee.

Do I need a CPA or a bookkeeper in Phoenix?

Most businesses need both: a bookkeeper ($250–$600/month) for day-to-day books and a CPA for tax strategy, filings, and compliance. Many Phoenix firms bundle both — one flat fee, one point of contact.

What is Arizona TPT and who has to pay it?

TPT (Transaction Privilege Tax) is Arizona's gross-receipts tax. Any business selling goods or services in Arizona must register, collect, and file TPT — including remote sellers above Arizona's economic nexus threshold of $150K in annual sales.

When is the Arizona corporate tax deadline?

Corporate and partnership returns are due April 15 (extensions to October 15). TPT returns are due monthly or quarterly depending on volume — a good accountant files these for you automatically.
