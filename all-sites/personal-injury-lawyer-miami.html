How Much Does a Miami Personal Injury Lawyer Cost?

Miami personal injury lawyers work on contingency — you pay nothing upfront, and the attorney takes a percentage of the recovery only if you win. Florida rules cap contingency fees in personal injury cases at 33.3% of the recovery before trial and 40% after a lawsuit is filed, plus approved costs. That means the initial consultation is free, and if the case doesn't settle or win, you owe no attorney's fees.

What you should watch: costs. Litigation costs — medical records, expert witnesses, filing fees — can run $2,000–$15,000 on contested cases, and the fee agreement determines whether those come out of your settlement or are billed separately. A standard Miami car accident case settles between $15,000 and $150,000 depending on injuries and coverage; a strong case with clear liability and good policy limits is worth far more.

Florida's 2-Year Deadline: The Statute of Limitations Changed in 2023

Florida's statute of limitations for personal injury is now 2 years — down from 4 years — for any accident that occurred on or after March 24, 2023. That change came from the 2023 tort reform (HB 837) and it's the single most important deadline in a Miami injury case. Miss it, and your claim is dead: Florida courts will dismiss the suit no matter how strong the facts.

The 2023 reform also changed Florida from pure comparative fault to modified comparative fault — you can't recover if you're found 50% or more at fault, and your recovery is reduced by your percentage of fault if you're under that bar. HB 837 also shortened the window for claims against the government (still a tight 3-year notice rule for Miami-Dade County claims) and changed how medical bills are proven at trial. This is why hiring a Miami lawyer who knows the post-2023 rules matters — the law changed dramatically, and general practice attorneys often miss it.

Miami Car Accidents: The Numbers and What They Mean

Miami-Dade County leads Florida in car crashes year after year — over 60,000 crashes annually, with I-95, the Palmetto Expressway (SR 826), and US-1 among the state's most dangerous corridors. Heavy tourist traffic, aggressive driving, and uninsured motorists (Florida's uninsured rate is among the nation's highest, around 20%) make Miami a uniquely tough accident market.

Because Florida is a no-fault state, your own PIP (Personal Injury Protection) covers the first $10,000 of medical bills regardless of fault — but PIP is often exhausted quickly after a serious crash, and it does NOT cover pain and suffering. That's when a liability claim against the at-fault driver matters. If the at-fault driver is uninsured or underinsured, your own UM/UIM (uninsured/underinsured motorist) coverage becomes the recovery source — which is why Miami attorneys push clients to carry UM/UIM limits even though Florida doesn't require them.

What to Do After a Miami Accident (And What Not to Do)

Right after a crash: call 911, get medical attention even if you feel fine (adrenaline masks injuries — whiplash and herniated discs often surface days later), exchange information, take photos, and report the crash. Don't admit fault, don't sign anything from the other driver's insurer, and don't give a recorded statement — the other carrier is building its defense, not helping you.

The insurance company's first offer is almost always low, and Miami's flood of "accident attorneys" advertising on billboards means adjusters lowball aggressively. A real-world example: a client rear-ended on I-95 was offered $8,000 by the at-fault insurer before treatment was complete; after treatment documented a herniated disc and policy limits were pursued, the case settled at $85,000. Document everything — medical records, lost wages, photos, and a journal of how the injury affects daily life — and let your lawyer handle the adjuster.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to file a personal injury lawsuit in Florida?

Two years from the date of the accident for accidents on or after March 24, 2023 (reduced from 4 years by HB 837). Claims against Miami-Dade County or other government entities have even shorter notice deadlines — act fast.

What is my Miami personal injury case worth?

Value depends on medical bills, lost wages, permanency of injury, pain and suffering, and available insurance coverage. Miami car accident cases typically range from $15,000 to $150,000+, with severe or permanent injuries worth substantially more.

Do I really need a lawyer for a car accident in Miami?

For any injury beyond minor scrapes, yes. Insurers in Miami settle unrepresented claims for a fraction of their value. A lawyer handles the adjuster, documents the claim properly, and typically increases the net recovery even after the contingency fee.

What if the at-fault driver has no insurance?

Your own uninsured motorist (UM) coverage steps in if you have it. Florida doesn't require UM coverage, but it's the only protection against hit-and-runs and uninsured drivers — one of the main reasons Miami attorneys recommend carrying it.

How long does a Miami injury case take?

Most cases settle in 6–18 months. Cases that go to trial or involve disputed liability can take 2–3 years. Delaying treatment or missing the 2-year deadline are the two ways to destroy a claim.
