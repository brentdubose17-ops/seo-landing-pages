How Much Does a Virtual Bookkeeper Cost in San Francisco?

Virtual bookkeeping for a San Francisco small business runs $350–$2,000 per month depending on transaction volume, account complexity, and whether payroll is bundled. Most virtual firms charge a flat monthly fee that includes monthly bank and credit card reconciliations, income statements, and balance sheets. Catch-up bookkeeping — cleaning up months of neglected books — runs $1,000–$4,000 as a one-time project.

Compared to hiring in-house, the math is compelling: a full-time bookkeeper in San Francisco costs $45,000–$70,000 a year plus benefits and payroll taxes, while virtual bookkeeping lands at $4,200–$24,000 a year. For a startup or solo consultant, that's a 60–70% saving for the same monthly financials, with a CPA on the other end for tax time.

What Taxes Is a San Francisco Business Responsible For?

San Francisco layers city taxes on top of California's already-high state taxes. Every business pays an annual business registration fee, and businesses with over $2.19 million in gross receipts (2026 threshold) owe the city's gross receipts tax, which varies by industry from 0.16% to 1.5%. Larger employers also face San Francisco's payroll expense tax.

Add the $800 California minimum franchise tax to the FTB and the picture gets complicated fast. A bookkeeper who understands San Francisco's receipts and payroll rules ensures your monthly books capture the right categories so your CPA can file accurately — and so you don't underpay the city and get hit with penalties.

Virtual vs. In-House Bookkeeper: Which Should You Choose?

A virtual bookkeeper is the right fit for most San Francisco startups and small businesses under roughly $5 million in revenue. You get a dedicated bookkeeper working in your accounting software (QuickBooks or Xero), monthly reconciliations, and financial statements delivered by a deadline — without the overhead of a hire. The trade-off is that they're not sitting in your office for ad-hoc questions.

Go in-house when you need someone physically present for daily operations — a retail business with heavy cash handling, or a company doing 500+ transactions a day. For a SaaS startup, a consulting firm, or an e-commerce business, virtual bookkeeping handles the workload cleanly and costs a fraction of a salary plus equity.

Getting Your Books Tax-Ready Before the Deadline

The difference between a smooth tax season and a nightmare is whether your books are current year-round. A virtual bookkeeper reconciles accounts monthly, categorizes every transaction, and issues 1099s to contractors before the January 31 deadline — so when your CPA sits down to file, they're reviewing clean numbers instead of reconstructing a year of receipts.

If you're behind, don't wait. Catch-up bookkeeping is a one-time project most virtual firms can complete in 2–4 weeks, and it's cheaper than the penalties and missed deductions that come from filing late or filing wrong. For a San Francisco business, one missed deduction or a botched 1099 can easily exceed the cost of a year of bookkeeping.
