Massachusetts Auto Glass Law — Best Coverage in the US

Massachusetts has the most consumer-friendly auto glass law in the country. Under M.G.L. c. 175, § 142R, auto insurers must offer comprehensive coverage with a $0 deductible for windshield repair and replacement. This means that if you have comprehensive coverage on your Massachusetts auto policy, a windshield replacement costs you nothing — zero out of pocket, no deductible, no copay.

This law applies to all passenger vehicles registered in Massachusetts. It covers cracks, chips, and full replacement. There's no limit on the number of claims, though excessive claims may affect your renewal rates. The law also prevents insurers from raising your rates solely for filing a glass claim. Boston drivers should always file a glass claim — paying out of pocket leaves a benefit on the table that your premiums already cover.

Vehicle break-ins in downtown Boston and Allston/Brighton neighborhoods mean side window replacements are common. The combination of construction debris, road salt spray, and winter sand makes Boston one of the highest-volume auto glass markets in the Northeast.

ADAS Calibration After Windshield Replacement

If your vehicle was built in 2017 or later, it likely has Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS) — lane departure warning, adaptive cruise control, automatic emergency braking, or forward collision alert. These systems use cameras mounted on the windshield. When the windshield is replaced, the camera must be recalibrated. ADAS recalibration costs $150–$400 on top of the windshield replacement. It is mandatory for safety — driving without recalibration means these safety systems will not function correctly.

Massachusetts safety inspection (required annually) will fail a vehicle with improperly calibrated ADAS or a cracked windshield. Always ask your Boston auto glass shop whether they include ADAS calibration in their quote. Some shops include it; others charge separately. Verify before scheduling to avoid unexpected costs.
