How Much Does Windshield Replacement Cost in St. Louis?

Windshield replacement in St. Louis runs $250–$500 for most cars, with the price driven by windshield glass size, sensors, and trim level. A basic sedan windshield with no advanced features runs $250–$350 installed; SUVs and trucks run $350–$500. If your car has ADAS features — lane-departure cameras, automatic emergency braking, rain-sensing wipers — recalibration adds $150–$400, pushing the total to $500–$900. Chip repair runs $60–$120 and is usually free or waived when your insurance covers it.

Mobile service (they come to your driveway or office) costs nothing extra at most St. Louis shops. A 2022 Toyota RAV4 owner in Clayton recently paid $680 total — $480 for the glass plus $200 for ADAS recalibration — installed in the parking lot while she worked. UV-coated and acoustic windshields add $50–$150, and OEM glass runs 20–50% more than aftermarket — worth it for late-model cars under warranty.

Do St. Louis Auto Glass Shops Need a License?

Missouri doesn't license auto glass installers specifically, which is why shop reputation and warranty terms matter so much. What Missouri does require is that the work not compromise safety: the vehicle must be roadworthy, and a cracked or star-damaged windshield can fail a Missouri safety inspection — required for title transfers and rebuilt titles. Tinted windshield film must comply with Missouri's 35% light-transmission rule on the front side windows, and the windshield itself cannot be tinted below the AS-1 line.

One St. Louis-specific caution: unlicensed mobile windshield \"dealers\" have been a recurring scam in the metro — they quote $99, install junk glass with no warranty, and vanish. A legitimate shop uses ANSI/AGRSS-compliant (Auto Glass Safety Council) installation standards and provides a written warranty covering leaks and defects for at least a year. Ask for both before anyone removes your old windshield.

Why St. Louis Windshields Get Hit So Often

St. Louis is a highway city — I-270, I-64, I-70, and I-55 funnel heavy truck traffic through the metro daily, and trucks are the #1 source of rock chips and cracks from kicked-up debris. Add the region's freeze-thaw winters: a small chip from summer highway debris becomes a running crack when water freezes and expands inside it in January. Missouri road crews also use more aggregate on winter ice than many states, and that loose gravel is what peppers windshields on I-270 in February.

That's why the golden rule in St. Louis is: fix the chip early. A quarter-sized chip repaired within days costs $60–$120 (often $0 through insurance) and stays invisible; the same chip ignored through one freeze-thaw cycle becomes a 2-foot crack requiring a full $300–$500 replacement. Most metro shops repair chips in 30–45 minutes, and most insurers waive the deductible for chip repair specifically because it's cheaper than replacement for them.

Insurance: Does Your St. Louis Policy Cover Windshields?

Missouri is not a zero-deductible windshield state (unlike Kentucky, which famously mandates free windshield replacement), so your coverage depends on your policy. Comprehensive coverage includes glass, but you pay your deductible — typically $100–$500 — unless you carry a separate glass endorsement. St. Louis drivers with a $500 comprehensive deductible often skip small repairs, then regret it when a winter crack forces a $500 deductible plus hassle. A $0 or $50 glass deductible rider costs $5–$15 per month and is a smart buy in this metro.

Important: Missouri law requires insurers to let you choose your own glass shop — they cannot force you to use their preferred vendor. Watch for shops that \"pay your deductible\" as a come-on; that practice is legal but means the shop inflated the price to the insurer, and it can trigger fraud review. A clean transaction: your insurer pays for the glass, you pay your deductible, you keep the receipt.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a windshield chip be repaired in St. Louis?

Yes, if it's smaller than a dollar bill, the damage is not in the driver's line of sight, and there's no crack running from it. Repair takes 30–45 minutes and most insurers cover it with the deductible waived. Beyond that size, replacement is the safe call.

How long does windshield replacement take?

About 60–90 minutes for the glass itself; ADAS recalibration adds 30–60 minutes. Mobile installers in St. Louis will do it in your driveway. You can drive immediately after — just don't run the defroster on max heat for the first 24 hours and avoid car washes for 24–48 hours while the adhesive cures.

Does Missouri require a deductible for windshield claims?

Yes, unless your policy includes a glass endorsement with a lower or $0 deductible. Comprehensive claims apply your regular deductible to glass. Check your policy — a $50 glass rider is cheap and covers the freeze-thaw crack pattern St. Louis is famous for.

Do I need ADAS recalibration after a St. Louis windshield replacement?

If your car has a camera or sensors mounted on the windshield (most 2017+ vehicles), yes. Driving without recalibration can disable or misalign lane-keeping and automatic braking. Many St. Louis shops include recalibration in the quote — ask before you agree to a price.

Can I drive with a cracked windshield in Missouri?

Legally, yes, unless the crack obstructs the driver's view or fails a safety inspection (title transfer, rebuilt title). Practically, a crack in the driver's line of sight is a ticket risk and a safety hazard — and in winter it will grow. Fix it early.
