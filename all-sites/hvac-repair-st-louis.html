How Much Does HVAC Repair Cost in St. Louis?

St. Louis HVAC repairs run $150–$1,300 for most calls. Common jobs: AC capacitor replacement $150–$350, refrigerant recharge $200–$650, blower motor $400–$950, and gas furnace ignitor or flame sensor fixes $200–$450. Full system replacements cost far more — a new AC unit runs $4,500–$9,500, a gas furnace $3,500–$8,500, and a matched AC-plus-furnace package $8,000–$17,000 depending on SEER rating and ductwork condition.

Hourly labor in the St. Louis metro runs $80–$120/hour. A 1,900-square-foot home in Kirkwood recently paid $7,400 for a 15 SEER AC and 96% AFUE furnace replacement — right in line with the metro average. Missouri law requires HVAC contractors to provide written estimates for work over $500, so always get an itemized quote in writing before work starts.

Do HVAC Contractors Need a License in Missouri?

Yes. Missouri requires HVAC contractors to hold a state license through the Division of Professional Registration, and the City of St. Louis adds its own mechanical contractor registration. A licensed Missouri HVAC contractor must pass the state mechanical exam, carry liability insurance, and in St. Louis city pull permits through the Building Division for furnace, AC, and gas-line work.

One St. Louis-specific catch: ductwork in pre-1960 brick homes is often undersized or collapsed, and Missouri's minimum 1-year workmanship warranty on new installations only helps if the installer is actually licensed. Ask for the license number before any significant repair — unlicensed "handyman" installs are common after summer heat waves and they void most equipment warranties.

St. Louis Weather: The Humidity and Freeze Cycle That Kills HVAC

St. Louis has one of the most punishing HVAC climates in the Midwest. Summers push 95°F+ with dew points near 75, running AC units 2,200+ hours a year; winters drop to the teens with wind chills below zero. The city's century-old housing stock — much of it with original ductwork and uninsulated attics — makes systems work harder than in newer suburbs like Chesterfield or O'Fallon.

Ameren Missouri electricity rates and Spire natural gas rates are both on the high side nationally, which is why SEER efficiency matters. A 13 SEER unit costs roughly $260 more per year to run than a 16 SEER unit on a typical 2,000-square-foot home. Before summer peaks, most St. Louis HVAC companies offer a $79–$129 tune-up special that includes coil cleaning, refrigerant check, and a carbon-monoxide test — worth booking in April or May, not August.

Emergency HVAC: What Counts as a Call-Out in St. Louis?

A dead AC when it's 100°F is an emergency; a noisy fan on a mild evening is not. St. Louis emergency HVAC call-outs run $250–$600 including after-hours labor, and most companies waive the fee if you book a repair. In winter, gas furnaces that won't light are treated as priority calls because of carbon-monoxide risk — many metro companies offer 24/7 emergency service with a 2-hour response guarantee.

Before calling, check the basics: thermostat batteries, circuit breaker, and air filter. A clogged filter causes more summer "no cooling" calls in St. Louis than any mechanical failure — a $15 filter swap beats a $350 service fee. If the outdoor unit is iced over in winter, turn the system off and call — running it wastes electricity and can kill the compressor.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does an HVAC repair take in St. Louis?

Most repairs take 1–3 hours once a technician arrives. Same-day service is standard in the metro; emergency calls in winter typically get a 2-hour response window.

When should I replace instead of repair my AC or furnace?

The rule of thumb is the $5,000 rule: multiply the equipment's age by the repair estimate. If a 12-year-old system needs a $600 repair, that's $7,200 — replace it. Also replace if the unit uses R-22 refrigerant (phased out in 2020) or if repairs exceed half the cost of a new system.

Do St. Louis HVAC companies charge for estimates?

Most charge a diagnostic fee of $59–$129 for a service call, credited toward the repair if you proceed. Free estimates are common for full system replacements, but a small diagnostic fee on repairs is normal.

Why is my St. Louis AC freezing up?

Frozen coils are usually caused by a dirty filter, low refrigerant, or a blocked condensate line. Turn the AC off immediately — running it with a frozen coil can damage the compressor — and call a technician to check refrigerant levels and airflow.

What SEER rating should I buy in St. Louis?

Missouri's minimum is 14 SEER for new residential AC in 2026. Given Ameren's high electricity rates, 16–18 SEER units pay for themselves in 3–5 years on a typical St. Louis home through lower monthly bills.
