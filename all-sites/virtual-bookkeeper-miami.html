How Much Does a Virtual Bookkeeper Cost in Miami?

Miami virtual bookkeeping pricing: $250–$750 per month for a typical small business (monthly close, bank reconciliation, financial statements), $750–$1,500 per month for higher-volume operations like restaurants and multi-location retail, and one-time catch-up cleanups at $500–$2,500 depending on how far behind the books are. Payroll add-on runs $75–$150 per month, and most firms include QuickBooks Online setup and management in the base rate.

The 'virtual' part is what keeps Miami prices honest — no office overhead, no on-site visits to pay for, and cloud access means your bookkeeper reconciles while you sleep. Compare that to in-person bookkeepers who charge $60–$120 per hour and it's rare for a remote monthly plan not to be the cheaper, more consistent option.

Florida Taxes Miami Business Owners Need to Know

Florida's biggest selling point: no state income tax — for individuals, LLCs, S-corps, or C-corps. Your business income is exempt from state-level income taxation entirely, which is why so many entrepreneurs structure in Florida. The trade-offs are sales tax and compliance: Florida sales tax is 6%, and Miami-Dade County adds a 1% surtax, so Miami businesses collect 7% on taxable sales and remit monthly or quarterly through the Florida DOR.

Two Miami-specific obligations that catch newcomers: the $138.75 annual report to the Florida Division of Corporations (Sunbiz) — late filings accrue $400 reinstatement fees — and tangible personal property tax, which applies to business equipment and furniture even though there's no income tax. And for the 2026 hurricane season: when a storm hits, SBA disaster loans and EIDL grants are processed off your financial statements — the businesses with clean books get funded first.

Why Miami Businesses Need Clean Books

Miami's economy runs on cash-heavy, card-heavy businesses — restaurants, bars, retail, tourism, and trades — and that's exactly where messy books get expensive. Every credit card processor settlement needs reconciling against sales, chargebacks need tracking, and Florida's sales tax must be remitted on the right schedule. A restaurant doing $40,000 a month in card sales that never reconciles its processor fees is leaving hundreds of dollars a month unaccounted for.

The other silent audit is workers' comp. Florida insurers audit payroll annually, and if your reported payroll is low because the books are sloppy, you get hit with a back-audit bill — commonly $5,000–$20,000 for a mid-size crew — plus higher premiums going forward. Clean books are also your loan application: Miami banks and SBA lenders want 12–24 months of tidy financials, and businesses that have them close loans in weeks instead of months.

Remote vs In-Person Bookkeeping in Miami

For 90% of Miami small businesses, remote bookkeeping wins. Cloud tools — QuickBooks Online, Xero, Dext for receipts, Gusto for payroll — give your bookkeeper real-time access without anyone touching your office. Weekly or monthly cadence replaces the 'drop off the shoebox in April' model, and bank-level encryption plus read-only access means the bookkeeper can see everything but touch nothing without your approval.

The 10% that still needs in-person help: businesses running on paper-heavy systems, cash-only operations that need on-site cash counting, and owners who simply won't adopt apps. Even then, the hybrid model works — an in-person CPA for tax strategy and a virtual bookkeeper for the monthly mechanics. Miami's traffic makes the virtual option even more attractive: nobody wants to cross the Dolphin Expressway to hand over receipts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Florida have state income tax?

No — Florida has no state income tax for individuals or businesses. That's one of the strongest reasons Miami entrepreneurs structure their companies here. You still owe federal taxes and Florida sales tax (6% + 1% Miami-Dade surtax on taxable sales).

How much does Miami bookkeeping cost per month?

$250–$750 per month covers monthly reconciliation, financial statements, and QuickBooks management for most small businesses. Restaurants and higher-volume operations typically run $750–$1,500. Payroll adds $75–$150.

What software do virtual bookkeepers use?

QuickBooks Online is the standard, with Xero, Dext (receipt capture), and Gusto (payroll) as common complements. Your bookkeeper works in your account with bank-level encryption and read-only access where appropriate — your data stays yours.

Can you help with Florida sales tax filings?

Yes — most virtual bookkeepers handle Florida DOR registration, monthly or quarterly sales tax returns, and the 7% Miami-Dade rate (6% state + 1% surtax). Late filings accrue interest and penalties, so it's worth putting on autopilot.

How fast can you get my books caught up?

Most Miami businesses with 3–12 months of backlog are current within 1–2 weeks. A year or more of paper receipts can take longer, and the one-time cleanup fee reflects the depth of the catch-up. After cleanup, monthly pricing keeps you current.
