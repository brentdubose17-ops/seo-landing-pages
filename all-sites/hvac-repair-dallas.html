How Much Does HVAC Repair Cost in Dallas?

Diagnostic visits in Dallas run $89–$129 and are typically waived when you approve the repair. Common repair jobs: capacitor replacement $150–$350, blower motor $400–$850, refrigerant recharge $250–$600, and thermostat replacement $200–$450 with labor. Compressor replacements, the big-ticket repair, run $1,200–$2,800 depending on system size and refrigerant.

Full system replacements run $5,500–$14,000: a 3-ton AC-only unit lands around $5,500–$9,000 installed, while a matched heat pump system at 15–17 SEER2 runs $9,000–$14,000. Dallas's brutal July–August heat means an undersized system never recovers during peak hours — insist on a Manual J load calculation ($150–$250) before accepting any quote, especially for older homes in Lakewood, Oak Cliff, and the Park Cities with original ductwork.

What Licenses Do HVAC Contractors Need in Texas?

Texas HVAC contractors are licensed by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR). Class A contractors can work on residential and commercial systems; Class B contractors are limited to residential. Every contractor must carry liability insurance, and anyone doing work for you should be able to produce a TDLR license number on the spot — verify it on the TDLR website before work begins.

The City of Dallas requires mechanical permits for system replacements and major repairs, and the permit fee is usually bundled into the install quote. Skipping the permit is a false economy: after the February 2021 winter storm, insurers started denying claims tied to unpermitted furnace and heat pump work. A permitted install also protects you when ERCOT demand-response programs offer rebates for high-efficiency upgrades — unpermitted systems don't qualify.

Why Is Dallas So Hard on AC Systems?

Dallas averages 20–30 days above 100°F per year, and 90°F+ nights in July and August keep compressors running nearly around the clock. That sustained load is the #1 cause of capacitor and contactor failures — most Dallas AC breakdowns happen between 3pm and 8pm on the hottest days, when repair queues stretch to 2–3 days. A spring tune-up in March or April is the single cheapest insurance against a July failure.

Hail is the second big threat. North Texas sits in the southern edge of Hail Alley, and spring storms regularly pelt condenser coils and roof-mounted equipment. After a hailstorm, check your outdoor unit for bent fins and dents — a $100–$180 coil straightening and wash restores efficiency, while a badly dimpled coil can cut cooling capacity 15–25% and needs replacement at $800–$1,600.

Heat Pump or Gas Furnace: What Works in Dallas?

Dallas winters are short but real — January lows touch the mid-20s, and the 2021 winter storm proved the grid can't always cover electric heat. That's why most Dallas homes use a gas furnace for heating and straight-cool AC for summer. If you have natural gas at the street, a 96% AFUE furnace plus AC is usually the lowest long-term operating cost.

Heat pumps are catching up as A2L refrigerants (R-454B) become standard in 2026 and Oncor rebates stack with federal tax credits. A cold-climate heat pump handles Dallas's occasional hard freezes, but you'll want backup heat for the rare single-digit mornings. If your system is 10+ years old and needs a compressor, run the numbers before repairing: a $2,400 compressor repair on an aging R-22 system is usually better spent toward a new high-SEER2 system.

Frequently Asked Questions

How fast can I get AC repair in Dallas?

Same-day service is available for calls before 11am during peak summer months; no-cooling emergencies are prioritized. Off-season, most repairs book within 24 hours, and winter furnace failures are typically same-day because demand is lower.

Do I need a permit for a new AC unit in Dallas?

Yes. The City of Dallas requires a mechanical permit for system replacements and major repairs. A licensed contractor pulls it as part of the job — ask for the permit number before final payment so your insurance stays valid.

How often should I service my HVAC in Dallas?

Twice a year: a spring AC tune-up before the April–October cooling season and a fall furnace check before first freeze. Given Dallas's 100°F summers, skipping the spring service is how July breakdowns happen.

Why is my Dallas AC running but not cooling?

Most common causes: frozen evaporator coil from a dirty filter, failed run capacitor, low refrigerant from a leak, or hail-damaged condenser coils. A diagnostic visit will pinpoint it — expect $89–$129 for the service call.

How long does an AC system last in North Texas?

12–15 years with annual maintenance. Dallas's long, hot summers are harder on compressors than northern climates, and systems that skip spring tune-ups often fail at year 10–12 — right when a repair stops making financial sense.
