How Much Does a New Roof Cost in Denver?

Denver homeowners pay $7,000–$16,000 for a standard asphalt shingle reroof on a 2,000-square-foot home, including tear-off and disposal. Architectural shingles run $4.50–$7.00 per square foot, while premium impact-rated shingles — increasingly standard in hail-prone Colorado — run $6.50–$9.00. Metal roofing costs $15,000–$30,000 for the same footprint.

Emergency storm repairs are cheaper: hail damage to a section of shingles runs $500–$3,000, and gutter or flashing repair runs $300–$1,500. Most Denver roof replacements are insurance-paid after hail claims, so the out-of-pocket cost is usually just the deductible — commonly $1,000–$3,000 depending on your policy.

What Are Colorado's Roofing Contractor Requirements?

Colorado requires roofing contractors to be registered with the state's Department of Regulatory Agencies (DORA) and to carry workers' compensation insurance for all employees. The City of Denver additionally requires a roofing contractor license and a building permit for any reroof, with inspection fees of $100–$250.

Colorado law also requires insurance carriers to offer homeowners a 1% wind-and-hail deductible option — meaning on a $500,000 home, your hail deductible can be as low as $5,000 or as little as $2,500 on a $250,000 home. Always confirm your contractor's DORA registration and workers' comp coverage before signing; unregistered "storm chasers" are common after every hail event.

How Does Denver's Hail Season Affect Your Roof?

Denver sits in the heart of "Hail Alley," with the most significant hail activity of any major US metro. Hail season runs April through September, and the Denver metro averages 4–6 significant hail events per year, including storms that drop golf-ball-size (1.75-inch) and larger stones. A single severe storm can damage thousands of roofs across the Front Range.

After a hailstorm, Colorado insurers typically give homeowners 12–24 months to file a claim, but acting fast matters: documented damage, photos of shingle bruising, and a contractor's storm inspection within 30 days protect your claim. Most Denver roofers offer free storm inspections and handle the insurance adjuster process end-to-end.

Why Do Denver Roofs Age Faster?

Denver's mile-high elevation means 20–25% stronger UV radiation than sea-level cities, which accelerates shingle granule loss and cracking. Combined with 40+ freeze-thaw cycles each year and extreme temperature swings, a 30-year architectural shingle in Denver typically delivers 20–25 years of real service life.

That's why impact-rated shingles and metal roofs are the fastest-growing choices in the Denver market. Class 4 impact-rated shingles add $1,000–$2,500 to a reroof but often qualify for insurance premium discounts, and Colorado's strong sun makes reflective metal and cool-roof coatings a smart energy play — cutting summer cooling bills by 10–20%.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a roof replacement take in Denver?

Most Denver reroofs are completed in 1–2 days for asphalt shingles, weather permitting. Metal roofs typically take 3–5 days.

Do I pay the deductible out of pocket after hail damage?

Yes, unless your contractor provides financing. Colorado law caps wind-and-hail deductibles at 1% of the dwelling value if your carrier offers that option. Some contractors offer deductible-rebate programs — check the legality in your county.

Should I repair or replace my roof after hail?

If damage covers more than 30% of the roof surface, most Denver insurers approve full replacement. Smaller isolated damage is typically repaired. A licensed roofer's storm inspection determines which path your claim supports.

Are metal roofs worth it in Denver?

For many Denver homeowners, yes. Metal roofs withstand hail better, last 40–60 years, and reflect UV heat. They cost roughly double asphalt but can lower insurance premiums and cooling costs.

What should I do immediately after a hailstorm?

Photograph the damage from the ground, note the storm date and hail size, and request a free storm inspection within 30 days. Avoid signing anything with door-to-door storm chasers before verifying their DORA registration.
