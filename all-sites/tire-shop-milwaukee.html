Winter Tires vs. All-Season in Milwaukee

All-season tires harden in cold (below 45°F), losing grip. Winter tires stay soft and maintain 25–50% better traction on snow and ice. In Milwaukee, winter tires from November through March reduce accident risk significantly.

The cost difference ($30–$50 more per tire) is offset by fewer accidents and longer all-season tire life (you rotate them off in winter, preserving them).

Studded tires are permitted in Wisconsin from Nov 1–Apr 15. They offer maximum ice grip but wear pavement faster. Not necessary if you have good winter tires.

Pothole Damage and Wheel Replacement

Milwaukee's freeze-thaw cycles destroy pavement, creating potholes that bend rims, break sidewalls, and throw off alignment. A 20-mph hit into a 3-inch pothole can cause $200–$500 in damage.

If your car suddenly pulls to one side or the tire bulges, stop driving and get immediate repair. A bulged sidewall will fail suddenly at speed.

Many tire shops offer pothole damage guarantees: if a pothole damages a tire within 30 days of purchase, they'll replace it free.

Tire Rotation & Maintenance Schedule

Rotate tires every 5,000–7,000 miles to even out wear. Front tires wear faster (they steer), so regular rotation extends tire life 20–30%.

Seasonal tire swaps count as a rotation opportunity. Move winter tires on and off the rims twice a year anyway, so combine that with the rotation.

Check tire pressure monthly and before long drives. Under-inflation wastes fuel and overheats tires; over-inflation reduces grip and causes faster center wear.
