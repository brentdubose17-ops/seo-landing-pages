Rhode Island Tax Basics for Providence Business Owners

Rhode Island taxes individual income at a flat 3.75%, one of the lowest rates in the Northeast, and the corporate rate is 7.0%. C-corporations pay corporate tax on profits, then the owner pays personal tax on dividends, creating a double-tax structure that encourages pass-through entity elections. If your business is an S-corp or LLC taxed as an S-corp, income flows through to your personal return and is taxed once at 3.75%.

Rhode Island sales tax is 7%, with no city or local add-on, making it simple to program into your point-of-sale system. Services are generally not taxed, so a consulting firm or bookkeeping business does not collect sales tax. Retail, restaurants and certain services (e.g., repairs over $500) do collect tax. You must register for a sales tax permit and file quarterly.

The city of Providence has a property tax cap for businesses under $50,000 annual revenue (as of 2026), but the threshold and rules change periodically. We track Providence tax rule changes so you do not miss new filing requirements or deadlines.

Catch-Up Bookkeeping and Starting Fresh

Many Providence businesses come to us with years of disorganized records. A shoebox full of receipts, personal and business spending mixed into one bank account, and no records of 1099 contractors paid—this is fixable, but it takes work. A catch-up engagement typically costs $1,500–$5,000 per year of neglected books, depending on transaction volume and how much separation work is needed.

The first step in any catch-up is to separate personal and business transactions. Money spent on groceries, personal auto insurance or family travel should never have been in the business account. Separating these accounts can cut cleanup cost in half and makes the final books defensible.

After catch-up, we move to monthly bookkeeping to keep the books current. The cost of monthly service ($350–$700/month) is much less than the cost of future catch-up, and it keeps you compliant for tax filing.
