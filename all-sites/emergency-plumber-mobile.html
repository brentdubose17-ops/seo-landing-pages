Water Heater Replacement and Sizing for Mobile Homes

Most Mobile homes are equipped with either a 40-gallon or 50-gallon electric tank water heater, and they typically fail between 8 and 12 years. Water heater failure in summer means cold showers and laundry pileup; failure in winter can freeze the pipes if the system isn't quickly restored. Average household uses 40–50 gallons of hot water daily—enough for two showers and a load of laundry—so a 40-gallon tank works if the home has moderate use and someone isn't home during peak morning hours.

Mobile's hard water scales a water heater's heating element and tank interior, which shortens tank life by 2–3 years compared to soft-water areas. A water heater in Mobile can fail as early as 6–8 years if it is not flushed annually. When replacing, many homeowners move from electric to a gas unit (if natural gas is available), because gas heats water faster and the per-gallon energy cost is lower.

Tankless water heaters are an option in Mobile, and they eliminate the storage tank and the scaling problem. Tankless units cost 2–3x more to buy and install, and they require gas service and a venting upgrade. A tankless system works well for small households or as a supplement to a tank.

Sewer Line Damage and Roots in Mobile's Older Neighborhoods

Many homes in Mobile's older neighborhoods were built in the 1960s–1980s with clay sewer lines running under the foundation and yard. Tree roots penetrate clay pipes seeking water, and once inside the pipe they form a blockage that traps soil and toilet paper, creating a sewage backup into the home. Symptoms include slow drains, backups in the lowest drain (basement or crawlspace shower), and raw sewage smell in the yard.

Sewer line damage is confirmed via sewer camera inspection—a small camera on a flexible rod that travels through the main line and shows roots, cracks and offset joints. The cost of the inspection is $300–$500, and it is always the first step before planning any repair. If roots are the problem, a plumber can either snake out the blockage ($300–$800, temporary fix) or replace the sewer line (permanent fix, $3,500–$12,000+ depending on line length and depth).

Some Mobile neighborhoods have municipal sewer, others have septic systems, and some have older on-site sewage treatment plants. It is critical to understand what system the home uses before any work is done. Municipal sewer backups require city notification, septic backups may require tank pumping and drain field inspection, and treatment plant failures are expensive to fix.

Hard Water and Galvanized Pipe Replacement

Mobile's municipal water averages 120–180 ppm of calcium and magnesium hardness, which is considered very hard. Hard water scales fixture aerators, shower heads, water heater elements and the interior of pipes, reducing flow and pressure over time. A water softener eliminates scaling and extends the life of water heaters and fixtures by 5–10 years, and the cost is $1,500–$3,000 installed.

Homes built before 1980 in Mobile often have galvanized steel water supply lines under the foundation and in the walls. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, and by 30–40 years the interior is often pinhole-size cracks that leak water into the walls. Replacing galvanized supply lines is a whole-house job that costs $4,000–$8,000 and is often done when the home is already under renovation.

The first sign of galvanized pipe failure is reduced water pressure at the top floors or in hot-water lines (corroded faster than cold). A plumber can identify the problem via water testing and inline inspection. Replacement involves running new copper or PEX lines from the meter to every fixture, and the project usually takes 3–5 days.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a water heater last in Mobile?

6–12 years depending on maintenance. Hard water and scale buildup shorten the lifespan. Annual flushing extends life by 2–3 years.

Is my sewer line covered by my homeowner's insurance?

No. Sewer line damage is usually excluded from homeowner's insurance. Some policies offer it as an add-on rider, but check your policy.

What causes a sewer backup in a Mobile home?

Tree roots penetrating clay pipes, grease and toilet paper accumulation, or structural cracks in the line. Sewer camera inspection pinpoints the cause.

How much does an emergency plumbing call cost in Mobile?

Emergency calls after 6pm, on weekends or holidays run $150–$250 plus the cost of the repair. Day service diagnostics are usually $85–$150.

Do I need to replace galvanized pipe if it is still holding pressure?

Galvanized pipe that is holding pressure today may fail next year. When pinhole leaks start, they spread fast. Replacement is recommended once failures begin.
