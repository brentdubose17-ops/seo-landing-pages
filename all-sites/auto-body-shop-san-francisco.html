Always get the estimate in writing with a breakdown of parts, labor, and paint. In California, shops must give you a written estimate before work and get your approval if the cost rises more than 10%.

Why Is California BAR Registration Important?

California requires auto body repair shops to register with the Bureau of Automotive Repair (BAR). Registration means the shop has passed BAR's facility requirements, carries the right insurance, and is subject to inspections and complaint enforcement.

Before you hand over keys, verify the shop's BAR registration number on the BAR website. Shops that operate without it often skip proper paint-booth procedures, use uncertified parts, or fail to honor warranties. You can also check their complaint history — a shop with repeated unresolved complaints is a shop to avoid.

Door Dings and Bumper Scuffs — What's Worth Fixing?

In SF's street-parking reality, you'll collect small damage constantly. Not all of it needs a claim or a shop visit:

  • Paintless dent repair (PDR): $150–$700 per panel, no repaint, preserves factory finish. Best for dings without creased paint.
  • Bumper scuffs on plastic: $200–$500 to blend and clear-coat. Wait until you have 2–3 scuffs and do them together.
  • Anything requiring a repaint: only worth it for visible panels or before resale — a full respray runs $3,000+.

One caution: if the damage is from a hit-and-run, file a police report within 24 hours. Your uninsured-motorist property damage coverage may require it, and SF is hit-and-run central.

OEM vs. Aftermarket Parts for Insurance Claims

California law requires insurers to consider OEM (original equipment manufacturer) parts for vehicles under a certain age, but many policies default to aftermarket or 'recycled' parts to cut costs. The shop should tell you which parts are on your estimate — by law, the estimate must specify the part source.

For structural or safety-critical parts (bumpers with sensors, hoods with crumple zones, headlights), OEM is worth pushing for. Aftermarket panels often need extra fitting work that can cancel out the savings. If your policy has a 'betterment' clause, your insurer may subtract depreciation from parts costs — ask the shop's estimator to explain any deductions line by line.

Frequently Asked Questions

%

How long does collision repair take in San Francisco?

Minor repairs take 2–4 days; major collision work runs 2–4 weeks depending on parts availability. Ask for a written completion date.

Does my insurance cover a rental car?

Only if you have rental reimbursement coverage on your policy — typically $30–$50/day. Check before the shop starts so there are no surprises.

Can I choose my own body shop?

Yes. In California you can use any registered shop. Your insurer may steer you to their preferred network, but you are not required to use it.

Will my rates go up if I file a claim?

Possibly — especially for at-fault claims. For minor damage under your deductible, paying out of pocket is often cheaper than a rate increase. Ask your agent for the math.

What is PDR and is it reliable?

Paintless dent repair uses specialized tools to massage dents out from behind the panel. It's reliable, preserves factory paint, and is usually cheaper and faster than body work.
