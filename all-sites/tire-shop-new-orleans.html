Why Do Potholes Wreck Alignments Here?

New Orleans sits on soft, waterlogged soil, and the constant freeze-thaw cycles of asphalt plus storm flooding create potholes year-round. Hitting one at speed doesn't just damage the tire — it bends suspension components and knocks the wheels out of alignment.

Signs your alignment is off: the car pulls to one side, the steering wheel is off-center, or the inside edges of your tires are wearing faster than the rest. An alignment costs $80–$140 and should be done at least once a year, and immediately after any big pothole hit. Catching it early saves you from buying a $300 tire every 10,000 miles instead of every 40,000.

How Heat and Humidity Shorten Tire Life

Louisiana's combination of 90-degree summers, intense UV, and high humidity accelerates rubber degradation. Tires age from the inside out, and an 8-year-old tire is dangerous even with plenty of tread — sidewall cracks are the giveaway.

Two habits extend tire life in NOLA: check pressure monthly (pressure drops 1 PSI for every 10°F temperature swing), and park in the shade when you can. Also watch for dry rot in the tread — UV damage shows as fine cracks between tread blocks. Most shops will flag it during a free inspection.

Rotation and Balancing — How Often?

Every 5,000–7,500 miles, or with every oil change if you're at that interval. Front tires wear faster on most cars because they carry the engine weight and do the steering — rotating evens out wear and gets 15,000–20,000 extra miles out of a set.

Balancing should be done any time you mount a new tire or feel a vibration in the steering wheel at highway speed. A $20–$40 balance job beats the $150+ you'll spend replacing a cupped tire early. Most reputable NOLA shops bundle rotation and balance for $40–$80.

Frequently Asked Questions

%

How long do tires last in New Orleans?

With rotation and proper pressure, 40,000–60,000 miles is realistic for all-season tires. Heat and UV can age tires out before the tread wears — check the sidewall date code.

Do I need special tires for hurricane season?

All-season tires with good tread depth handle NOLA rain fine. Consider replacing tires under 3/32" before storm season — hydroplaning risk rises sharply below that.

What's the difference between new and used tires?

Used tires cost $40–$90 and work for temporary or budget fixes, but you're buying someone else's wear and potential damage. New tires come with warranty coverage and predictable life.

Is an alignment really necessary after new tires?

Yes. New tires on a misaligned car will wear unevenly and you'll lose thousands of miles of life. Shops usually offer a package discount when you buy tires + alignment together.

How do I know if a tire shop is reputable?

Look for ASE-certified technicians, a clean shop with modern equipment, published prices, and a warranty on the work. Check online reviews and ask about their road-hazard warranty.
