Do Bookkeepers Need a License in California?

California does not license bookkeepers as a profession — anyone can call themselves a bookkeeper. What matters is whether the person is a CPA (licensed by the California Board of Accountancy) or an enrolled agent (federally licensed by the IRS). A bookkeeper who prepares tax returns must be a CPA, EA, or attorney, or work under one. Verify any CPA license through the California Board of Accountancy's license lookup.

For California businesses, bookkeepers handling payroll must stay current on state payroll rules — the California Employment Development Department (EDD) requires quarterly payroll tax returns, and the state's minimum wage (starts at $16.50/hour in 2026 and rises with inflation) drives payroll complexity. A good San Diego virtual bookkeeper handles EDD registration, quarterly returns, and the state's new retirement program (CalSavers) auto-enrollment requirements.

What to Look For in a San Diego Virtual Bookkeeper

Start with software compatibility — QuickBooks Online is the standard, and your bookkeeper should be a ProAdvisor (for QBO) or certified partner (for Xero). Ask about their client load (a bookkeeper with 60+ clients is stretched thin), their cleanup process (do they reconcile to the penny every month?), and their reporting cadence — monthly P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow statements are the minimum.

Security matters more with remote bookkeeping: your bookkeeper should use bank-level encryption, role-based access to your financial accounts, and never store credentials in plain text. Ask about their error-handling and whether they work with your CPA at tax time. And get the fee structure in writing — flat monthly pricing beats hourly, and most San Diego virtual bookkeepers offer a free month-end cleanup or a 30-day money-back guarantee to prove their systems work.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between a bookkeeper and a CPA?

A bookkeeper records transactions, reconciles accounts, and produces financial reports — the day-to-day work. A CPA is a licensed accountant who prepares tax returns, gives tax advice, and can represent you before the IRS. Most small businesses hire both: a virtual bookkeeper monthly, a CPA at tax time.

How is virtual bookkeeping different from in-office bookkeeping?

Virtual bookkeepers work remotely using cloud software (QuickBooks Online, Xero) — you share access instead of handing over paper. The work quality is the same, but virtual rates are typically 30–50% lower than in-office San Diego bookkeepers because they don't carry office overhead.

What does my bookkeeper need from me each month?

Bank and credit card statements, invoices, receipts, and payroll records. Most San Diego virtual bookkeepers connect directly to your bank feed and need only a monthly check-in on anything unusual. The cleaner your records, the lower your monthly fee.

Can a virtual bookkeeper handle my California sales tax?

Yes — most California bookkeepers handle sales tax filing (San Diego's combined rate is 7.75%) as part of monthly service, or as an add-on. Just confirm they're current on California's marketplace facilitator rules if you sell online.

How do I know if my books are actually accurate?

Ask for a monthly reconciliation report — every bank and credit card account should tie to the penny. Your monthly P&L should match your bank activity, and your bookkeeper should flag discrepancies immediately. A good virtual bookkeeper offers a free 1-month cleanup so you can see the quality before committing.
