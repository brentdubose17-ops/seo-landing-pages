Georgia Business Taxes: What Atlanta Small Business Owners Need to Know

Georgia's tax structure is business-friendly overall but has several Atlanta-specific requirements:

Georgia income tax rate: 5.39% flat rate on corporate and personal income (2026 rate). No progressive brackets — every dollar over deductions is taxed at 5.39%. S-corps and LLCs file through the owner's personal return (Form 600 pass-through). An accountant who structures your Georgia return properly can save thousands by maximizing the pass-through deduction under Section 199A.
City of Atlanta occupational tax: All businesses operating within Atlanta city limits must pay an occupational tax based on gross receipts. Tier 1 ($0–$50K): $50/year. Tier 4 ($500K+): $500/year plus 0.15% of gross over $500K. Plus a separate $75 regulatory fee. Failure to register results in back-tax assessments with 12% annual interest.
Fulton County business license: Every business in Fulton County (including the City of Atlanta) must obtain a business license from the county. The license fee is based on the number of employees: $75 + $5/employee for 1–5 employees, up to $350 + $10/employee for 20+. These licenses must be renewed annually and require proof of liability insurance.
Georgia sales tax complexity: Georgia has 159 counties with different local option sales tax rates (SPLOST, TSPLOST, MARTA). Atlanta's rate is 8.9% (4% state + 4.9% local). For an e-commerce business shipping to all 159 counties, you need sales tax software that knows each county's rate — manual calculation guarantees errors.

Georgia's Pass-Through Entity (PTE) Tax Election

Georgia's PTE tax election (HB 1067, effective 2022) is the single biggest tax-saving opportunity for Atlanta small business owners — but most accountants don't proactively offer it. Here's why it matters:

The federal SALT cap is $10,000. If your business income flows through to your personal return (LLC, S-Corp, partnership), your state and local tax deduction is capped at $10K on your federal return — a huge problem for Atlanta business owners earning over $150K/year.
The PTE workaround: Georgia allows the business entity itself to pay state income tax at the entity level. The entity's tax payment is fully deductible on the federal return (no SALT cap — it's a business expense, not an itemized deduction). Your personal Georgia return then gives you a credit for the tax the entity paid.
Typical savings for an Atlanta business: An Atlanta S-Corp with $200K in net profit. Without PTE: personal Georgia tax ~$10,780, federal SALT deduction capped at $10K. With PTE: entity pays the $10,780, deducts it on the federal return at 35% bracket, saving $3,773 in federal tax. Your Atlanta accountant should be asking about PTE at the first meeting — if they don't, find one who does.

A CPA has passed the Uniform CPA Exam, completed 150+ credit hours, and completed 2+ years of supervised experience. Only CPAs can represent you before the IRS in an audit (non-CPA preparers have limited representation rights).

Virtual CFO certification: Not a formal license but signals the accountant provides strategic advisory beyond data entry. Important for Atlanta startups and scaling businesses.

FAQ — Small Business Accountant Atlanta, GA

Do I need a business license to operate in Atlanta? Yes — every business in Atlanta city limits must have both a City of Atlanta occupational tax certificate and a Fulton County business license. The penalty for operating without them includes fines, back-tax assessments with 12% interest, and potential criminal penalties for willful non-compliance. An Atlanta accountant can help you register or catch up on delinquent filings.
When should I hire a full-time accountant vs a freelance bookkeeper? Under $100K annual revenue: a freelance bookkeeper ($350–$600/month) + annual CPA review ($800–$1,500) is sufficient. $100K–$500K: hire a part-time accountant ($600–$1,200/month) who also does tax planning. Above $500K or 10+ employees: consider a full-time controller or CFO ($75K–$120K salary) plus an external CPA for tax strategy.
What's the difference between an LLC and S-Corp for tax purposes in Georgia? All LLCs default to sole proprietorship (single-member) or partnership (multi-member) taxation. A Georgia S-Corp election requires filing Form 2553 with the IRS and a Georgia S-Corp election form. Key difference: S-Corps reduce self-employment tax by splitting income into salary (payroll taxes) and distributions (no SE tax) — savings of $3,000–$12,000/year for profitable service businesses. You must pay yourself a "reasonable salary" — the IRS flags S-Corps with zero salary on audit.
Do I need to collect Georgia sales tax for my Atlanta online business? If you have economic nexus in Georgia (more than $100K in sales or 200+ transactions in the prior or current calendar year), YES. Georgia follows the South Dakota v. Wayfair standard. You must register with the Georgia Department of Revenue for a sales tax permit, collect 8.9% on taxable sales to Georgia customers, and file monthly/quarterly returns. Atlanta's MARTA tax (1%) applies to sales within Fulton and DeKalb counties.
What records does an Atlanta accountant need from me? Current accounting software login (QuickBooks, Xero); last 3 years of tax returns; Articles of Organization/Incorporation; EIN letter from IRS; Georgia Secretary of State registration; payroll reports; bank and credit card statements; 1099 forms for contractors; any IRS or Georgia DOR correspondence. Starting clean saves $200–$400 in catch-up fees.
