Why Does Altitude Matter for Furnaces and Boilers in Denver?

At Denver's 5,280-foot elevation, the air is about 17% thinner than at sea level. Gas furnaces and boilers pull oxygen from that thinner air, which changes combustion. Equipment that isn't altitude-adjusted burns less efficiently, produces more carbon monoxide, and can fail well before its rated lifespan.

Any reputable Denver HVAC company derates burners or installs high-altitude conversion kits when required by the manufacturer. If a contractor quotes you a furnace without asking about your elevation and gas type (natural vs. propane), that's a sign they're not used to working in Colorado.

You should also install a carbon monoxide detector on every level if you don't already have one. Furnace season in Denver runs roughly October through April, and CO calls spike every winter.

What Happens When Hail Damages Your AC Condenser?

Denver sits in one of the most hail-prone corridors in the country, with storms that produce golf-ball-size or larger stones. A direct hit can dent condenser fins, crack the coil, or shear the fan blade. The unit may still run, but efficiency drops and the compressor can overheat within weeks.

After any big storm, check your condenser for dented fins, then call a contractor for a coil inspection. Many homeowners' insurance policies cover hail damage to outdoor HVAC equipment, and a $200 inspection can turn into a $9,000 covered replacement.

Pro tip: some Denver contractors offer hail-guard fan grilles and coil guards for $300–$600. Given the region's storm frequency, they pay for themselves in a single season.

How Do You Choose a Licensed Denver HVAC Contractor?

The City and County of Denver requires HVAC contractors to hold a mechanical contractor license, and every technician who handles refrigerants must be EPA Section 608 certified. Colorado does not have a single statewide HVAC license, so local licensing is your main protection.

  • Verify the company's Denver mechanical license and liability insurance before the truck rolls.
  • Ask if the tech is EPA-certified for refrigerant handling.
  • Request references from the last 12 months in your neighborhood.
  • Confirm the quote is fixed-price for the repair, not time-and-materials.

A licensed contractor in Denver will also know the 2023 Colorado energy code requirements for new installs, which affects which equipment qualifies for Xcel Energy rebates. Rebates on high-efficiency furnaces and heat pumps can reach several hundred dollars.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an HVAC diagnostic cost in Denver?

Most Denver HVAC companies charge $89–$149 for a diagnostic visit, and reputable shops credit that fee toward the repair if you hire them.

How often should I service my furnace in Denver?

Once a year is the standard — ideally in September or early October before the first hard freeze. Skipping annual service is the #1 cause of mid-winter failures.

What size AC do I need for a 2,000 sq ft Denver home?

Typically 3–4 tons, but the correct answer depends on insulation, window area, and sun exposure. A contractor who quotes a size without a load calculation is guessing.

Does home insurance cover hail damage to my AC?

Usually yes, if the condenser was damaged by hail. Document the damage with photos and file a claim promptly — Colorado hail claims are common and routinely paid.

How long does a furnace last in Colorado?

A well-maintained furnace lasts 15–20 years in Denver. After 15 years, replacement is usually more cost-effective than a major repair.
