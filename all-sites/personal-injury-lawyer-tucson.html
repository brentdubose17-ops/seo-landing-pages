Tucson Personal Injury Attorney & Accident Claims

Arizona gives injury victims two things that matter enormously: a pure comparative negligence rule, which means you can still recover even if you were partly at fault, and a two-year deadline that closes fast after a serious crash. Tucson adds its own wrinkles — a higher-than-average uninsured driver rate, heavy I-10 commercial truck traffic, and monsoon-season collisions that spike every July through September. This guide explains how Arizona's rules actually work, what a claim is worth, and the deadlines that end cases before they start.

Arizona's Two Rules That Decide Most Cases

Arizona follows pure comparative negligence under A.R.S. §12-2505. That means if a jury finds you 20% at fault for a Tucson intersection crash, you still recover 80% of your damages — there is no 50% or 51% bar that wipes out a claim. It also means the defendant will work hard to build a fault story against you, which is why the earliest evidence — photographs, the police report, witness names, and vehicle event data — matters so much in the first weeks.

The filing deadline for a personal injury claim in Arizona is two years from the date of injury under A.R.S. §12-542. Cases against a government entity work differently and much faster: A.R.S. §12-821.01 requires a notice of claim within 180 days of the incident. Miss that notice window and a claim against a public entity or employee is barred regardless of how strong the liability is. City buses, public school vehicles, and municipal road defects all fall into that category.

Tucson-Specific Risks: I-10, Monsoon Season, and Uninsured Drivers

The I-10 corridor through Tucson carries heavy commercial freight, and crashes involving tractor-trailers are governed by federal motor carrier safety rules on top of state law. Those cases involve corporate defendants with dedicated legal teams, electronic logging data that must be preserved quickly, and insurance limits far above a personal auto policy. Spoliation letters to preserve driver logs, dashcam footage, and maintenance records are time-sensitive and rarely sent early enough.

Tucson's monsoon season runs from mid-June through September, and the first heavy rain after months of dry weather turns dust and oil into a slick film on every road surface. Collisions spike accordingly. Add Arizona's uninsured and underinsured driver rate, which is consistently above the national average, and the practical conclusion is that your own UIM coverage is often the difference between a real recovery and a judgment you cannot collect.

What to Do in the First Two Weeks

Get medical care immediately, even if you feel fine. Soft-tissue injuries routinely show up 24–72 hours later, and Arizona insurers read a delay in treatment as evidence the injury was not serious. Follow every referral and every follow-up appointment. A gap in the record is the most effective argument an adjuster has against you.

Then preserve evidence. Photograph the vehicles, the road, the weather, and your visible injuries. Get the police report number. Write down witness names before memories fade. Notify your own insurer of the crash — that is a contractual obligation separate from any claim against the other driver — and do not give a recorded statement to the other side's adjuster. A recorded statement given in the first week is nearly impossible to take back and is routinely used to cap a claim.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the statute of limitations for personal injury in Arizona?

Two years from the date of injury under A.R.S. §12-542 for most personal injury claims. Claims against a government entity or employee are far shorter — A.R.S. §12-821.01 requires a notice of claim within 180 days.

Can I recover if I was partly at fault in a Tucson crash?

Yes. Arizona follows pure comparative negligence under A.R.S. §12-2505, so your recovery is reduced by your percentage of fault but is not barred, even at 50% or more. Expect the defense to build a fault argument against you, which is why early evidence matters.

What are minimum liability limits in Arizona?

Arizona raised minimum liability coverage to 25/50/15 effective July 1, 2025. Given the state's higher-than-average uninsured driver rate, stacking uninsured and underinsured motorist coverage is the practical protection most Tucson drivers need.

How much do Tucson personal injury lawyers charge?

Almost all work on contingency — typically 33.3% of the recovery if the case settles before a lawsuit is filed and 40% if litigation is required. You pay nothing up front and nothing if the case does not recover.

Should I give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer?

No. You are not obligated to give a recorded statement to the opposing insurer, and early statements are routinely used to limit claims. Notify your own insurer as your policy requires, then refer the other side to your attorney.
