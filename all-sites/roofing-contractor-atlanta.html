How Much Does a Roof Replacement Cost in Atlanta?

Atlanta roof replacements run $8,000–$18,000 for a typical 2,000–2,500 square foot home, or $350–$450 per "square" (100 sq ft) for architectural asphalt shingles. Three-tab shingles are cheaper at $280–$350 per square but last only 15–20 years, while architectural shingles run 30–50 years and dominate the Atlanta market. Metal roofing runs $900–$1,800 per square and is growing fast thanks to storm durability and energy savings.

A standard single-story ranch in Marietta recently ran $11,600 for a full architectural-shingle replacement with tear-off and new underlayment — right at the metro average. Repairs are far cheaper: a small leak patch runs $350–$800, while storm repairs after hail or wind damage are often fully covered by insurance. Always get three itemized quotes; Atlanta pricing swings 30% between the low-end and high-end shops.

Georgia Roofing License & Insurance Requirements

Georgia requires roofers performing work over $2,500 to hold a Residential-Basic Contractor license from the State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors, and work over $25,000 requires a Residential-Light Commercial license. Verify the license at the Secretary of State's office before signing — Atlanta is a magnet for out-of-state "storm chaser" roofers who descend after every hail event and skip town after payment.

Insurance matters as much as the license. A legitimate Atlanta roofer carries general liability insurance and workers' compensation, and will show you the certificates. If a roofer can't produce both, you're personally on the hook if a worker falls off your roof. Never pay more than a small deposit (typically 10–30%) upfront, and never pay in full until the job is complete and the permit is closed.

Atlanta's Storm Season: Hail, Wind & Tornado Risks

Atlanta sits in a severe-weather corridor. Spring (March–May) is the peak hail season, and the metro has seen repeated billion-dollar hail events — including the March 2023 storm that damaged tens of thousands of roofs across Cobb, Gwinnett, and DeKalb counties. Summer brings derechos and straight-line winds, and Atlanta's humid subtropical climate means hurricane remnants can push 60–80 mph gusts inland in late summer.

Hail damage often isn't visible from the ground — it shows as bruising to the shingle's mat, which only a roof walk reveals. After any hail event, get a free storm inspection and file an insurance claim within the policy window (most policies require claims within 1 year of the storm date in Georgia). A Class 4 impact-resistant shingle upgrade often earns a 10–20% discount on homeowners insurance premiums and holds up far better to the next storm.

Architectural Shingles & Impact-Resistant Upgrades

Atlanta's hot, wet climate punishes cheap roofs. The combination of UV exposure, afternoon thunderstorms, and high attic heat (summer attic temps routinely hit 130°F+) means three-tab shingles curl, crack, and blow off years earlier than their rated life. Architectural shingles — thicker, laminated, and rated for 110–130 mph winds — are the standard choice and the reason they command the market here.

Ventilation is the invisible factor most bids ignore. Proper ridge vent plus soffit vent intake drops attic temps 20–30°F, extends shingle life by years, and cuts cooling bills by up to 15%. A $400 ventilation upgrade on a $12,000 roof is the single best dollar-for-dollar investment you can make in the job — insist it's itemized in your estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a roof replacement take in Atlanta?

Most single-family roofs are torn off and replaced in 1–2 days. Larger or steep roofs, or those needing decking repair, run 3–5 days. Weather in Atlanta's summer thunderstorm season is the main cause of delays.

Will my insurance cover hail damage?

If your policy covers wind and hail (most Georgia homeowners policies do), yes — minus your deductible. After a hail event, a storm inspection documents the damage and you file within the policy's claim window, typically 1 year from the storm date.

How do I spot a storm-chaser roofer in Atlanta?

Red flags: out-of-state plates, door-to-door pressure after a storm, no Georgia license number, no local address, and demanding full payment upfront. Always verify the license with the Secretary of State and check local references.

Repair or replace — how do I decide?

If your roof is under 10 years old and damage is isolated, repair. If it's 15+ years old with widespread granule loss, curling, or multiple leaks, replace. A roofer should walk the roof and show you photos before recommending either way.

What's the difference between 3-tab and architectural shingles?

Three-tab shingles are flat, single-layer, and last 15–20 years. Architectural shingles are laminated, thicker, rated for higher winds, and last 30–50 years — for roughly 20–30% more cost. Architectural is the standard in Atlanta.
