How Much Does a Virtual Bookkeeper Cost in Seattle?

Seattle virtual bookkeeping runs $250–$600 per month for a standard small business (up to ~200 transactions), $600–$1,200/month for higher-volume businesses, and $100–$300/month for payroll-only services. Sales tax (WA excise tax) filing is typically bundled at $50–$150/month, and year-end package pricing runs $300–$800 depending on complexity.

Virtual bookkeepers serving Seattle are typically 30–50% cheaper than in-house hires — a full-time Seattle bookkeeper costs $65,000–$85,000/year including benefits, versus $3,000–$7,200/year for a virtual service. For a 5–20 person company, that's a $50,000+ annual saving with better continuity: no sick days, no turnover, and no training ramp.

What Are Washington's Business Tax Requirements?

Washington has NO state income tax — a major advantage for business owners. Instead, businesses pay the Business & Occupation (B&O) tax on gross receipts: 1.5% for most service businesses, 0.471% for retailing, and 0.2904% for wholesaling. B&O applies to gross revenue with no deductions for expenses — it's a gross-receipts tax, so tracking revenue accurately is critical.

Retailers also collect Washington sales tax — Seattle's combined rate is 10.25% (state 6.5% + city/county 3.75%), one of the highest in the US. Businesses register through the Washington Department of Revenue (business license $19/year), and most file excise tax returns monthly or quarterly. Washington's economic nexus rules require out-of-state sellers to register once they exceed $100,000 in WA sales or 200 transactions annually.

Seattle-Specific Bookkeeping Issues to Watch

Payroll in Washington carries two unique costs your bookkeeper must handle: Paid Family & Medical Leave (PFML) premiums — about 0.8% of wages split between employer and employee, capped — and the WA Cares Fund, a long-term care payroll tax of 0.58% on employee wages that launched in 2023. A bookkeeper who doesn't track these creates compliance headaches fast, since the Employment Security Department audits PFML reporting.

Seattle's tech-heavy economy means many clients are S-corps, LLCs, and startups with R&D credits, 83(b) elections, and equity compensation — all of which complicate bookkeeping beyond QuickBooks basics. For tech founders, a virtual bookkeeper with startup experience (equity tracking, cap table basics, R&D credit documentation) is worth the premium over a generalist.

How to Choose a Virtual Bookkeeper in Seattle

Ask three questions: (1) Do you handle Washington B&O and sales tax filings, or only federal? (2) What software stack do you use — QuickBooks Online, Xero, or NetSuite — and do you integrate with my bank and merchant accounts? (3) Who is your backup when you're out — a solo operator with no redundancy is a risk for time-sensitive filings.

Look for a bookkeeper who provides monthly financial statements, not just data entry. A monthly P&L that flags margin shifts, cash-flow gaps, and tax liabilities 30 days early is worth far more than the $250–$600 fee. Most Seattle virtual bookkeepers offer a free consultation and a fixed monthly price — avoid hourly billing, which creates misaligned incentives and surprise invoices.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does virtual bookkeeping cost in Seattle?

$250–$600/month for most small businesses, $600–$1,200/month for higher-volume companies. Payroll-only runs $100–$300/month. Most providers offer a free month or a discounted first quarter to onboard.

What is Washington's B&O tax rate?

Services pay 1.5% of gross receipts, retailing pays 0.471%, and wholesaling pays 0.2904%. B&O is a gross-receipts tax — expenses are not deductible, so accurate revenue tracking is essential.

Does Washington have a state income tax?

No. Washington has no personal or corporate income tax. Business owners pay B&O tax on gross receipts and (if retail) sales tax — which is why clean books matter more than income-tax strategy here.

What payroll taxes apply in Seattle?

Federal payroll taxes plus Washington PFML (~0.8% combined employer/employee) and the WA Cares long-term care tax (0.58% employee-paid). Seattle also has a small city payroll expense tax for larger employers.

Is a virtual bookkeeper as good as an in-house one?

For most small businesses, yes — virtual bookkeepers are 30–50% cheaper, use the same software stack, and provide monthly financials with faster turnaround. In-house only wins at 30+ employees or with heavy on-site document handling.
