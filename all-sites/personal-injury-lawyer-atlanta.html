Georgia Personal Injury Law: What Atlanta Residents Must Know

Georgia uses modified comparative negligence — you can recover damages only if you are less than 50% at fault, and your award is reduced by your percentage of fault. The statute of limitations is 2 years from the date of injury for most claims (car accidents, slip and falls, medical malpractice). If you miss that deadline, your claim is barred forever.

Georgia has no cap on most personal injury damages, including pain and suffering — a major reason Atlanta firms pursue full-value claims. One exception: medical malpractice caps non-economic damages at $350,000 (and $700,000 for hospital cases) under Georgia's revised statute. Georgia is also a "choice no-fault" state with a minimum PIP coverage of $2,500 — not the full no-fault system of neighboring Florida, meaning at-fault claims are the norm.

What to Do After an Atlanta Car Accident

Call 911 for medical help and a police report, then exchange insurance info without admitting fault. Georgia's comparative negligence rule means even "I'm sorry" can reduce your recovery — say nothing about fault at the scene. Seek medical attention within 72 hours even if you feel fine; delayed whiplash and soft tissue injuries are common.

Contact an Atlanta PI lawyer before giving any recorded statement to an insurance adjuster. Georgia requires insurers to respond in good faith, but they routinely seek early recorded statements to minimize payouts. Your lawyer will handle adjuster communication, get your medical bills paid through your PIP ($2,500 minimum), and build the evidence file. Photograph the scene, get witness contacts, and keep every medical record and receipt — a complete paper trail adds 15–30% to settlement value in metro Atlanta cases.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to file a lawsuit in Georgia?

2 years from the date of injury for car accidents, slip and falls, and most personal injury claims. Medical malpractice also has a 2-year limit (from discovery, max 5 years). Act fast — evidence degrades and witnesses move.

Can I recover if I was partially at fault in Georgia?

Yes, if you are less than 50% at fault. Your recovery is reduced by your percentage. At 50% or more fault, you recover nothing under Georgia's modified comparative negligence rule.

How much is my Atlanta car accident case worth?

Average metro Atlanta settlements: $18,000–$52,000 for soft tissue, $70,000–$200,000 for surgery/permanent injury, $150,000+ for truck accidents. Value depends on medical bills, lost wages, fault, and insurance limits.

Does Georgia cap pain and suffering damages?

No — most Georgia personal injury cases have no cap on non-economic damages. The exception is medical malpractice, capped at $350,000 (or $700,000 for hospital cases).

What happens if the at-fault driver has no insurance?

Your uninsured motorist (UM) coverage pays your claim. Georgia requires insurers to offer UM coverage, and many Atlanta policies carry $25,000–$100,000 in UM limits. Your lawyer will identify all applicable policies, including umbrella coverage.
