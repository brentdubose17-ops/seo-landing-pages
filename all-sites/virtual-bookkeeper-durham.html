North Carolina Bookkeeping & Tax Requirements

North Carolina requires all businesses collecting sales tax to register with the NC Department of Revenue. The state sales tax rate is 4.75% with Durham County adding 2.25% for a combined 7.00%. Quarterly sales tax returns are required for most small businesses, with monthly filing mandatory once you exceed $10,000/month in sales.

NC has a flat 2.50% corporate income tax rate as of 2026—one of the lowest in the Southeast. However, the state's franchise tax ($1.50 per $1,000 of the greater of capital stock or net worth, capped at $150,000) catches many Durham LLCs that don't expect an annual franchise tax payment. A proper bookkeeper sets aside for this.

Durham Startup & Small Business Tax Credits

North Carolina offers several tax credits that Durham small businesses commonly miss. The state's Article 3J credit provides a 3.5% tax credit on qualifying research and development expenditures—directly applicable to Durham's biotech and tech startups. The state also offers a $2,500 per year credit for employing a disabled worker through the NCWorks program.

Durham County offers a property tax abatement of up to 50% for the first 5 years on capital investments over $5 million. For smaller businesses, the City of Durham's Small Business Microgrant Program provides $5,000–$15,000 grants, which are non-taxable and don't affect a business's P&L statement.

When to Hire a Virtual Bookkeeper vs. In-House

For Durham businesses under $500K annual revenue, virtual bookkeeping is 40–60% cheaper than an in-house employee ($250–$600/month vs. $3,500–$5,000/month salary plus benefits for a full-time staff bookkeeper). The tipping point is $1M+ revenue where dedicated in-house support starts making sense for larger operations with AP/AR staff, but a virtual CFO ($600–$1,200/month) still beats a full-time controller at $85,000–$110,000/year for most Durham companies under $5M.
