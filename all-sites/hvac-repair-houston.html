Houston HVAC Repair & Installation That Survives Texas Summers

Houston air conditioning runs more months of the year than almost anywhere else in the country, and the heat is only half the fight — humidity corrodes coils, clogs drain lines with algae, and punishes undersized systems. Texas also has its own rulebook: HVAC contractors must hold an Air Conditioning and Refrigeration (ACR) license from TDLR, and the City of Houston requires mechanical permits for most installations. This guide covers realistic Houston pricing, the licensing you should verify, and what actually breaks in Gulf Coast heat.

TDLR Licensing, Permits, and Refrigerant Rules in Texas

Texas does not let just anyone install air conditioning. HVAC contractors must hold an Air Conditioning and Refrigeration (ACR) contractor license from the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation, and the licensed company is the one responsible for the work — not a subcontractor or a handyman with a truck. Anyone who buys or handles refrigerant must also hold EPA Section 608 certification, which applies to the technician on the job, not just the company. You can verify both through the TDLR license lookup before you sign anything.

Permits are the other non-negotiable. The City of Houston requires mechanical permits for most AC installations and replacements, and the electrical work connected to the system must be done by a licensed electrician under the city's permit system. A contractor who quotes you a cash price with no permit is quoting you a job with no inspection — and no recourse when the coil leaks six months later.

Why Houston Is One of the Hardest Markets on AC Equipment

Houston air conditioners work like no other city's. The cooling season runs from April through October, with dozens of days past 95°F and humidity that makes 90 degrees feel like 105. That duty cycle alone shortens equipment life, but the Gulf Coast environment adds three specific killers. First, the humidity keeps condensate drain lines wet year-round, and algae clogs them — the number one cause of attic overflow damage in the city. Second, coastal air and heavy rain corrode condenser coils, fins, and electrical connections faster than inland systems. Third, most Houston systems sit in attics that hit 140°F in summer, baking ductwork and making even efficient units work harder.

Houston homeowners who run their systems smart get more life out of them: change the filter monthly in summer, pour a cup of bleach down the condensate line twice a year, and book a spring maintenance visit before the first 95-degree week. When ERCOT issues heat-event alerts and the grid gets tight, a well-maintained system is also the difference between riding out a rolling outage comfortably and calling every repair shop in the city at once.

Heat Pumps, Efficiency, and What to Buy in 2026

Houston's mild winters — average January highs around 62°F — make heat pumps a natural fit. A modern heat pump cools as well as a traditional AC in summer and handles winter heating without a gas line, which matters in a city where new construction and renovations are increasingly all-electric. The economics improved sharply with the federal 25C tax credit, which covers up to $2,000 for qualifying Energy Star heat pumps installed through 2032, and many Houston utility and municipal programs layer their own rebates on top.

Whatever you buy, right-size it. Houston contractors who quote by square footage alone often oversize, because the industry rule of thumb overestimates cooling load in a humid climate. A proper Manual J load calculation accounts for your insulation, windows, shade, and duct losses — and in Houston, spending the extra hour on that calculation is what separates a system that cools efficiently for 15 years from one that short-cycles and rusts out in eight.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an AC diagnostic cost in Houston?

Most Houston HVAC companies charge $89–$169 for a diagnostic visit, and reputable shops credit that fee toward the repair if you hire them.

How do I verify an HVAC contractor's license in Texas?

Texas HVAC contractors hold an Air Conditioning and Refrigeration (ACR) license from the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. You can search the TDLR license lookup by company name or license number, and technicians who handle refrigerant must also hold EPA Section 608 certification.

Do I need a permit for AC work in Houston?

The City of Houston requires mechanical permits for most AC installations and replacements. A licensed contractor pulls the permit, and the city schedules the inspection.

How long does central AC last in Houston?

10–14 years is realistic in Houston when the system is properly sized and serviced annually. Units that run six months a year in 95-degree heat, get oversized at install, or never receive maintenance die sooner.

Are heat pumps a good choice in Houston?

Yes — Houston winters are mild enough that a modern cold-climate heat pump handles heating with no gas line, and the federal 25C tax credit covers up to $2,000 for qualifying Energy Star heat pumps installed through 2032.
