How Much Does a Houston Small Business Accountant Cost?

Houston accounting fees run $150 for a simple consultation up to $5,000+ for full-service year-round support. Monthly bookkeeping typically runs $300–$800 for a small business, business tax return preparation (S-corp or partnership) $800–$2,500, and a personal return with business schedules $350–$900. New entity formation and clean-up work runs $500–$1,500.

Houston's accountant rates sit below coastal markets but above the state average, reflecting the city's concentration of energy, logistics, and professional-services businesses. Paying for a CPA or Enrolled Agent — rather than an uncredentialed "tax preparer" — usually costs more upfront but is cheaper than an IRS notice or a missed deduction down the road.

Texas Tax Reality: No Income Tax, But Franchise Tax Applies

Texas has no state personal income tax, which is a major draw for Houston business owners. But businesses still face the Texas franchise tax — a margin tax on most LLCs, corporations, and partnerships with gross receipts above the no-tax-due threshold (about $2.47 million for most businesses in recent years). Below that threshold, many entities owe nothing, but they must still file a report.

The franchise tax is calculated on a "margin" — roughly total revenue minus either cost of goods sold or compensation, whichever is lower — with rates under 1% for most businesses. Getting the margin calculation right, and choosing the right deduction, is where an accountant earns their fee. Filing errors and late reports trigger penalties even when no tax is owed.

Houston Sales Tax and Local Jurisdictions

Houston's combined sales and use tax rate is 8.25% — 6.25% state, 1% city, and 1% Metropolitan Transit Authority. But businesses that sell across the metro must also track the different local rates in surrounding jurisdictions like Harris County, and the thousands of special-purpose districts in the region. Remitting to the wrong jurisdiction is a common and costly mistake.

An accountant who understands Texas mixed-jurisdiction rules will set up your point-of-sale and accounting software to charge and remit correctly, and file your sales tax returns on time. For e-commerce and service businesses, the "economic nexus" threshold also means you may owe Texas sales tax even with no physical presence in the state.

Houston-Specific Considerations: Energy, Hurricanes, and Growth

Houston's economy runs on energy, healthcare, logistics, and aerospace, and many small businesses here serve those industries as contractors and vendors. That creates specific tax questions — 1099 contractor classification, equipment depreciation (especially for oilfield and industrial equipment), and percentage depletion for mineral interests. A generalist accountant who doesn't know energy-sector rules can leave serious money on the table.

Hurricane preparedness is another Houston reality. After events like Hurricane Harvey, businesses needed casualty-loss deductions, disaster-loss documentation, and — for many — business-interruption claims that required forensic accounting. An accountant who has guided clients through a disaster can document losses properly from day one, which is the difference between a fast recovery and a denied claim.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to file a Texas franchise tax report?

Most LLCs, corporations, and partnerships must file a franchise tax report annually, even if they owe nothing. Entities below the no-tax-due threshold file a simple "no tax due" report. Missing the filing triggers penalties, so confirm your status with an accountant.

Should my Houston business be an LLC or S-corp?

It depends on your revenue, growth plans, and how much you take as salary. An LLC is simpler; an S-corp election can reduce self-employment tax once you're profitable. An accountant can model both against your actual numbers rather than a rule of thumb.

How much does monthly bookkeeping cost in Houston?

Most Houston small businesses pay $300–$800 per month for monthly reconciliation, categorization, and financial statements. The rate depends on transaction volume and complexity. Clean, current books are also what make tax season and financing easier.

What tax deductions do Houston contractors often miss?

Vehicle mileage, home-office expenses, equipment depreciation (Section 179 and bonus depreciation), and 1099 contractor costs are commonly under-claimed. In the energy sector, depreciation and depletion rules add further opportunities a generalist may overlook.

How do I document losses after a Houston hurricane?

Photograph and inventory all damage immediately, retain receipts for repairs and temporary relocation, and work with an accountant to compute casualty-loss deductions and support any business-interruption claim. Early documentation is the key to a successful claim.
