Paintless Dent Removal vs. Traditional Body Repair

Minor dents from hail, parking lot dings or door strikes can often be removed without paint work, a process called paintless dent removal (PDR). A PDR specialist uses specialized tools to access the dent from behind the panel, gently massaging it back to shape without sanding, priming or painting. PDR works best on small dents (golf ball size or smaller) where the paint is not cracked or wrinkled.

The cost of PDR is $200–$600 per dent, which is much less than traditional repair ($800–$2,000+ for the same dent if it requires repainting). The downside is that PDR does not work on large dents, creases in the metal, or dents where the paint is already damaged. In those cases, traditional repair with sanding, filling, priming and painting is required.

Hail damage to a vehicle can mean dozens of small dents over the hood, roof and doors. PDR is perfect for hail because each dent is small and the paint is usually intact. A badly hail-damaged sedan can be restored to showroom condition with PDR in 2–3 days, and the cost is often partially covered by insurance under the comprehensive claim.

Delaware's Right-to-Choose-Shop Law

Delaware law gives vehicle owners the right to choose their own auto body shop for repairs, regardless of what the insurance company suggests. If your insurer recommends a particular shop, you can still take your car to Wilmington Auto Body, and the insurance company must pay the reasonable cost of repair. This protection exists to prevent insurers from steering all business to shops that agree to lower their labor rates.

When you file a claim, document the damage with photos before the car is towed, and provide your insurance company with your choice of shop. If the insurer questions the cost estimate, get a second opinion from another shop. The insurance company has the right to have their own adjuster inspect the vehicle, but they cannot force you into their preferred shop.

Some body shops have agreements with insurance companies that allow direct billing (the shop bills the insurer directly rather than the owner), which simplifies the process. We honor these agreements and will coordinate directly with your insurance company to avoid out-of-pocket costs.

Frame Straightening and Structural Repair

A serious collision can damage the frame or unibody structure of a vehicle, which affects handling, wheel alignment and the structural integrity of the vehicle in future accidents. Frame damage is not always visible—a car may look fine cosmetically but have bent frame rails that throw off the suspension geometry. An alignment rack or frame measurement system detects these problems before repair begins.

Frame straightening requires specialized equipment (a frame rack or jig) and is usually done before any body work or painting. The process involves pulling the frame back into alignment by measuring against factory specs, then stress-testing the repairs to ensure they hold. A serious frame repair can take 2–4 weeks and cost $3,000–$15,000+, depending on the vehicle and the extent of damage.

After frame repair, the vehicle must be re-aligned and brake-tested before it is returned to the owner. This ensures that the steering, suspension and brakes work correctly and that the vehicle is safe to drive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Delaware insurance require me to use their preferred body shop?

No. You have the legal right to choose your own shop. The insurer must pay the reasonable cost of repair at the shop you select.

Does my collision insurance cover paintless dent removal?

Yes. If the damage is from a covered collision or comprehensive event, PDR is covered under the collision or comprehensive deductible.

How long does an auto body repair take?

Minor repairs: 2–5 days. Frame damage: 2–4 weeks. Paint cure time may add 3–7 days before the vehicle is ready for pickup.

Will my car's value be affected by frame damage repair?

A properly repaired frame should not affect resale value, but the repair history is recorded. A frame damage claim will show up on a carfax report.

What happens if the insurance company's estimate is lower than our estimate?

We will work with the adjuster to reach agreement. If there is a significant gap, you can request a second appraisal.
