How Much Does a New Roof Cost in Columbus?

A new asphalt shingle roof on a typical 2,000-square-foot Columbus home runs $9,500–$18,000, including tear-off and disposal. Architectural shingles (the standard upgrade) cost $4.50–$7.00 per square foot installed; premium class-4 impact-resistant shingles run $6.50–$9.50 per square foot.

Metal roofing — increasingly popular for Ohio hail resistance — costs $18,000–$32,000 on the same footprint. Repairs are far cheaper: shingle spot repair $300–$800, flashing repair $350–$900, and leak repair $500–$1,500 depending on access. Columbus contractors add 5.75% Ohio sales tax plus a $75–$200 building permit fee from the city.

Ohio Hail & Storm Damage: The #1 Roof Killer

Central Ohio sits in a hail corridor — Columbus averages 40+ thunderstorms per year, with damaging hail (1 inch or larger) hitting Franklin County every 1–2 seasons. The May 2020 derecho alone damaged thousands of Columbus roofs with 80+ mph winds and golf-ball-size hail.

Hail damage is often invisible from the ground. Granule loss, bruised (soft) shingle spots, and cracked fiberglass mat are detectable only on a close inspection. After any storm with 1-inch-plus hail, get a free roof inspection within 30 days — Ohio homeowners insurance covers hail damage, but most policies require claims within 1 year of the storm date.

Ice Dams: Columbus Winter Roof Damage

Columbus winters cycle above and below freezing constantly — January averages a 25°F low but thaws are frequent. That freeze-thaw pattern creates ice dams: melted snow refreezes at the eaves, backing water up under shingles. Ice dam leaks are the most common winter roof repair in Franklin County, averaging $800–$2,500 per repair.

Prevention is cheaper than repair. Proper attic insulation (R-38+ in central Ohio) and ridge-to-eave ventilation stop 90% of ice dams. For homes with recurring dams, installing ice-and-water shield along the first 6 feet of eaves runs $500–$1,200 at replacement time and eliminates the problem permanently.

Asphalt Shingle vs Metal Roofing in Ohio

Architectural asphalt shingles cost $9,500–$18,000 and last 20–25 years in Ohio's climate. Class-4 impact-resistant shingles add 15–25% upfront but earn most insurers a discount (often 5–15% on the dwelling premium) and survive hail that would damage standard shingles.

Standing-seam metal costs $18,000–$32,000 — roughly 2x shingles — but lasts 40–60 years and sheds hail and snow effectively. For Columbus homeowners planning to stay 10+ years, the break-even point lands around year 18–22. Metal also reflects solar heat, cutting summer cooling costs 10–25% in Ohio's humid summers.

Insurance Claims for Roof Damage in Ohio

Ohio homeowners policies cover hail and wind damage, minus your deductible ($1,000–$2,500 typical for roofs). Ohio is a "replacement cost" state — insurers must pay to replace damaged roofing, not just depreciated value, as long as the roof was maintained and under 20 years old.

The smart Columbus play: file the claim through a contractor who does insurance work. They document damage with photos and a drone inspection, negotiate with the adjuster, and supplement if the initial scope misses items. Be wary of storm-chaser crews from out of state — verify the contractor's Ohio registration and ask for 3 local references before signing any assignment-of-benefits form.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Columbus roofing contractors need a license?

Ohio has no statewide roofing license, but Columbus requires contractors to register with the city and pull building permits for roof replacements. Permits run $75–$200 in Franklin County. Always verify registration before signing a contract.

How long does a roof replacement take in Columbus?

A typical residential re-roof takes 1–3 days in good weather. Ohio's spring storms and winter cold can delay projects, so most contractors schedule replacements March–November and book 3–6 weeks out during peak season.

Will my insurance pay for hail damage in Columbus?

Yes — hail and wind are covered perils under standard Ohio homeowners policies. File within 1 year of the storm, get a contractor-documented inspection, and expect the adjuster to match the contractor's scope for a full roof replacement if shingles are damaged.

How do I know if my Columbus roof needs replacing?

Signs: shingles curling or missing granules, 3+ layers of old roofing, daylight through the attic, sagging decking, or a roof over 20 years old. A free inspection with photos is the fastest way to know for sure.

What warranty comes with a Columbus roof replacement?

Manufacturer shingle warranties run 25–50 years (pro-rated), and reputable contractors offer 5–10 year workmanship warranties covering installation defects. Get the workmanship warranty in writing before paying.
