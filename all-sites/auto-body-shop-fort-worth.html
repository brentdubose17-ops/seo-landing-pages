How Much Does Auto Body Repair Cost in Fort Worth?

Fort Worth collision repairs range from $500 for a scuffed bumper to $8,000–$15,000 for a major structural hit. Typical jobs: bumper repair or replacement $600–$1,800, fender replacement $800–$2,200, door skin replacement $900–$2,500, and full-panel paint $500–$1,200 per panel. Hail damage is a Fort Worth specialty — paintless dent repair (PDR) runs $50–$150 per dent, and a fully hailed-out car (30+ dents) typically runs $3,000–$7,000.

Fort Worth shops bill at $55–$75/hour for body labor and $45–$65/hour for paint. A typical rear-end repair on a late-model sedan — new bumper cover, absorber, and paint — runs $2,800–$4,500. Because DFW is one of the most hail-prone metros in the country, most established Fort Worth shops have dedicated PDR technicians and hail-season capacity; expect 2–4 week wait times after a major hailstorm.

What Certifications Should a Fort Worth Body Shop Have?

Texas doesn't license auto body shops, so certifications are the quality screen. Look for I-CAR Gold Class certification, OEM certifications (Ford, GM, Toyota, etc. all certify shops for their brands), and ASE-certified technicians. An OEM-certified shop uses factory-specified repair procedures and parts — critical for modern cars with advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS) that need recalibration after any bumper or windshield work.

Ask specifically about ADAS recalibration: a shop that replaces a bumper on a 2022+ vehicle without recalibrating the front camera or radar is setting you up for a failed safety system — and that recalibration ($250–$700) should appear on the estimate. Reputable Fort Worth shops also offer a lifetime workmanship warranty on their repairs, meaning they'll fix any paint or body defect that shows up while you own the car.

Hail Alley: Fort Worth's #1 Body Shop Driver

Fort Worth sits squarely in Hail Alley. The metro sees multiple severe hail events a year — hailstones of 1–2 inches are routine, and 3-inch+ stones hit the area in major outbreaks. That's why PDR is a $3,000–$7,000 industry driver here: a car covered in small dents is often cheaper to repair with PDR than repaint, and PDR preserves the factory finish, which matters for resale value.

After a hail storm, timing matters: insurance demand spikes, and shops book out for weeks. Document the damage with photos the same day, file the claim within days, and get on a shop's schedule immediately — hail claims in Fort Worth can take 30–60 days to complete at peak volume. If your insurer totals the car on hail alone, negotiate — a PDR specialist's quote can be a third of what a traditional repair estimate shows.

Insurance Claims: How Fort Worth Body Work Gets Paid

Fort Worth shops split into two models: insurance-direct (DRP) shops that work directly with your insurer, and independent shops that write their own estimates. A DRP shop streamlines the process — no waiting on the adjuster — but some are pressured to cut corners to hit insurer price targets. An independent shop with a strong reputation will fight for OEM parts and proper procedures, which matters when the insurer's estimate comes in low.

Your rights under Texas law: you can choose any shop, and the insurer must pay the "reasonable and customary" cost of repairs — not force you to a preferred shop. If the insurer lowballs, get a supplement from the shop and push back; supplements are normal, and Texas insurers expect them. Never sign a repair authorization that waives your right to the difference, and ask whether the shop uses OEM, LKQ, or aftermarket parts before work starts.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a Fort Worth collision repair take?

Minor bumper work takes 2–4 days; major structural repairs 2–6 weeks. Hail PDR on a fully dented car runs 1–3 weeks depending on shop backlog — DFW shops get slammed after every major storm.

Should I file an insurance claim for hail damage?

If your deductible is under ~$1,000 and the damage exceeds it, file — hail claims are covered perils. But repeated hail claims can raise premiums; if the damage is only a few dents under your deductible, paying cash for PDR is often the smarter move.

Will my insurance pay for OEM parts?

Texas requires insurers to cover OEM parts if your car is under 2 years old. Older vehicles get aftermarket or LKQ parts unless you push back — ask the shop to document why OEM matters for your specific repair.

Does hail damage hurt my resale value?

Yes — unrepaired hail dents can cut resale value 10–25% on a trade-in. PDR is the cheapest way to protect value, and because it keeps the factory paint, a proper PDR job is nearly invisible to buyers.

Do I need ADAS recalibration after body work?

If your car has front cameras, radar, or sensors and the bumper or windshield was replaced, yes — most 2019+ vehicles require recalibration. Confirm the shop includes it in the estimate; skipping it can disable automatic braking and lane-keeping.
