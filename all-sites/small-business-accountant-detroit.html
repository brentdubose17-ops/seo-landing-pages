How Much Does a Small Business Accountant Cost in Detroit?

Detroit small business accounting runs $200–$2,000 per year for tax prep plus $300–$1,500/month for ongoing bookkeeping, depending on transaction volume. A solo LLC with simple books pays roughly $300–$600 for tax prep and $300–$500/month for bookkeeping. A 20-person manufacturing supplier with payroll, inventory, and sales tax runs $1,500–$3,000/month with monthly financials. Hourly rates for CPA work in the metro run $150–$350/hour; many Detroit firms now offer flat monthly plans that bundle bookkeeping and tax prep.

A Detroit restaurant owner recently paid $4,800/year for a full package — monthly bookkeeping, quarterly payroll, sales tax filings, and year-end tax prep — and the accountant found $9,200 in missed deductions (meal delivery fees, tip credits, and equipment depreciation) in year one. That's the pattern: in Detroit's margins-thin service and manufacturing economy, a good accountant pays for itself in deductions and penalties avoided.

Who Can Call Themselves an Accountant in Michigan?

Anyone can call themselves an \"accountant\" or \"bookkeeper\" in Michigan — the title isn't regulated. What IS regulated is the CPA designation: only individuals licensed by the Michigan Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA) can call themselves Certified Public Accountants. CPAs must pass the uniform CPA exam, meet 150 credit hours, and complete continuing education. For anything involving audited financials, bank covenants, or complex tax positions, you need a licensed CPA — verify their license on LARA's website.

For day-to-day bookkeeping, payroll, and sales tax, an experienced bookkeeper under a CPA's supervision is often the better value. The Michigan structure most Detroit firms use: a bookkeeper handles monthly reconciliations, a CPA reviews quarterly and signs the returns. That division keeps costs down without sacrificing the credentialed sign-off you need for your tax filings and lender relationships.

Michigan Taxes Every Detroit Business Owner Must Know

Michigan has a flat 4.25% individual income tax, and corporations pay the 6% Michigan Corporate Income Tax (CIT). Sales tax is 6%. But the one that surprises most new Detroit business owners is the city income tax: Detroit levies 2.4% on residents and 1.2% on non-residents who work in the city — the highest municipal income tax in Michigan — and the city's withholding and return requirements are enforced aggressively. If you have employees working in Detroit, you are responsible for Detroit withholding even if your business is in Troy.

Michigan-specific deductions and credits matter too: the MBT-era credits are gone, but businesses can claim the Michigan research and development credit, and manufacturers get significant property tax relief through the Industrial Facilities Exemption (IFT). The state's unemployment (UIA) rates and wage reporting have their own calendar — missing a UIA filing costs real penalties. This is where local expertise pays: a Detroit accountant who files in LARA, the CIT system, Detroit's city portal, and the UIA weekly will keep you compliant in a way a national online service often misses.

Detroit's Comeback Economy: Manufacturing, Mobility, and Tax Complexity

Detroit's business base — auto suppliers, manufacturing, logistics, construction, and a fast-growing mobility-tech sector — creates tax complexity most cities don't have. Manufacturers deal with sales tax exemptions on production equipment, personal property tax (though Michigan has eliminated the industrial personal property tax for most manufacturers), and multi-state nexus if you ship parts across state lines. A parts supplier selling to Ford, GM, and Stellantis has three separate OEM compliance portals plus Michigan's CIT apportionment — this is not DIY territory.

Then there's the workforce angle: Detroit's construction boom (Little Caesars Arena area, Hudson's site, countless rehab projects) means many small contractors operate here, and construction has the highest audit rate of any Michigan industry — prevailing wage, workers' comp classification, and 1099-vs-W-2 rules are enforced hard. A Detroit accountant who knows the construction industry will keep you out of the two most common traps: misclassified subcontractors and missed prevailing wage filings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a CPA or just a bookkeeper in Detroit?

You need a bookkeeper for monthly reconciliations and a CPA to sign your tax returns. Most Detroit firms offer both under one roof. If you only need annual tax filing, a CPA alone suffices; if you're growing and need monthly financials, add the bookkeeping layer.

Does my Detroit business pay city income tax?

Detroit levies 2.4% on residents and 1.2% on non-residents working in the city. If you or your employees work in Detroit, city withholding applies — even for non-resident owners. Your accountant should handle Detroit city registration, withholding, and annual returns.

What taxes does a Michigan LLC pay?

Michigan LLCs are pass-through for income tax (the 4.25% individual rate applies to owners), and the LLC pays the 6% Corporate Income Tax only if it elects corporate treatment. You'll also handle sales tax (6%) if you sell goods, plus Detroit city income tax if applicable.

When are quarterly estimated taxes due in Michigan?

The federal schedule applies: April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15. Michigan's estimated payment schedule matches. Missing an estimated payment triggers interest and penalties from both the IRS and the Michigan Department of Treasury.

Can a Detroit accountant help if I'm behind on filings?

Yes. Michigan has voluntary disclosure programs that can reduce or waive penalties for businesses that come forward before being contacted. A CPA who does this regularly can often settle multi-year back filings for a fraction of the assessed penalties.
