How Much Does HVAC Repair Cost in Detroit?

Diagnostic visits in Detroit run $89–$129 and are usually waived if you approve the repair. Common repair jobs: capacitor replacement $150–$350, blower motor $400–$800, igniter and flame sensor $200–$450, and refrigerant recharge $250–$600.

Full system replacements run $3,500–$12,000: a furnace-only swap lands around $3,500–$7,500, a 3-ton AC unit $4,500–$9,500 installed, and a matched cold-climate heat pump with a 16+ SEER2 rating runs $7,000–$12,000. Many Detroit homes — especially in Indian Village and Boston-Edison — still have original 1950s ductwork, so budget $500–$1,500 for duct sealing or resizing to get the rated efficiency out of a new system.

What Licenses Do HVAC Contractors Need in Michigan?

Michigan licenses HVAC contractors through LARA (Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs). A Mechanical Contractor license requires 3 years of documented experience plus a PSI exam at 70% or better. Verify the license number on the LARA website before any work starts — unlicensed installs void warranties and can fail your insurance inspection.

Detroit's BSEED department requires mechanical permits for system replacements and major repairs, running $700–$2,200 depending on scope. Insurers are checking for unpermitted work after major claims, so never accept "we don't pull permits" as a discount — it can cost you the claim later.

Why Is Detroit So Hard on Furnaces and ACs?

Detroit winters bring weeks of single-digit temperatures and lake-effect snow, so the furnace carries a brutal sustained load from November through March. That's why the #1 winter call is a failed igniter or pressure switch on a 15+ year-old unit. Summer swings the other way — humid 85–90°F stretches that push older AC condensers to failure in July and August, when wait times stretch to 2–3 days.

Road salt is the second culprit. Salt spray corrodes condenser coils and cabinet bases within 4–6 years, cutting efficiency by 15–30%. A spring coil wash ($100–$180) plus annual maintenance is the single highest-ROI dollar for Detroit homeowners — DTE Energy also offers rebates on high-efficiency furnaces and heat pumps that can knock $200–$800 off a replacement.

Heat Pump or Furnace: What Works in Detroit?

Detroit winters regularly drop below 10°F, so a straight heat pump struggles without backup heat. The winning configuration in Michigan is a cold-climate heat pump paired with a gas or electric furnace — the heat pump handles shoulder seasons efficiently and the furnace covers the deep-freeze stretches. Plan on a furnace lasting 15–20 years and a heat pump 12–15.

If your system is 10+ years old and needs a compressor, do the math before repairing: a $2,800 compressor repair on an old R-22 system is usually wasted money. In 2026 new systems switch to lower-GWP A2L refrigerants (R-454B), so a new cold-climate system is the better long-term value for Detroit homeowners — especially stacked with DTE rebates and federal energy-efficiency tax credits.

Frequently Asked Questions

How fast can I get AC repair in Detroit?

Same-day service is available for calls placed before 11am during peak season; no-heat emergencies in winter are prioritized. Off-season, most repairs are booked within 24 hours.

Do I need a permit for a new furnace or AC in Detroit?

Yes. Detroit's BSEED department requires mechanical permits for system replacements and major repairs, typically $700–$2,200 depending on scope. A licensed contractor pulls the permit as part of the job — verify the permit number before final payment.

How often should I service my HVAC in Michigan?

Twice a year: once before the heating season (September–October) and once before cooling season (April–May). Detroit's extreme swing between single-digit winters and humid 90°F summers makes both visits worth it.

Why is my furnace short-cycling in Detroit?

Most common causes: clogged filter, dirty flame sensor, oversized furnace, or a failing pressure switch — all common in older Detroit homes with original ductwork. A diagnostic visit will pinpoint it; expect $89–$129 for the service call.

How long does an AC system last in Michigan?

12–15 years with annual maintenance. Detroit's humid summers and road-salt winters are hard on outdoor condensers — units that skip annual service often fail at year 10–12.
