Kansas Personal Injury Laws: What Wichita Residents Need to Know

Kansas has several laws that directly affect personal injury claims. The statute of limitations is 2 years from the date of injury for most cases (car accidents, slip and falls, medical malpractice). Kansas follows a modified comparative fault rule — you can recover damages only if you are less than 50% at fault, and your award is reduced by your percentage of fault.

Kansas also caps non-economic damages (pain and suffering) at $350,000 per plaintiff for personal injury cases, raised from $300,000 in 2024. For medical malpractice, the cap is $350,000 per defendant and $1.25 million total. Kansas's no-fault auto insurance system means your own PIP coverage pays medical bills up to $4,500 regardless of fault, and you must meet a $2,000 threshold of medical expenses before you can sue for pain and suffering.

What to Do After a Wichita Car Accident

If you're in a Wichita-area car accident, first ensure everyone's safety and call 911 for medical help and a police report. Exchange insurance information with the other driver, but do not admit fault or apologize — Kansas's comparative fault rule means any admission can reduce your recovery. Seek medical attention within 72 hours even if you feel fine; delayed-onset injuries (whiplash, soft tissue damage) are common after collisions.

Contact a Wichita personal injury lawyer before giving a recorded statement to any insurance adjuster. Insurance companies often seek early recorded statements to minimize payouts. Your lawyer will handle all communication with the at-fault driver's insurer and ensure your medical bills are covered through Kansas's PIP system (the first $4,500 of medical expenses, regardless of fault). Document the scene with photos, gather witness contact information, and save all medical records and receipts related to the accident.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to file a personal injury lawsuit in Kansas?

2 years from the date of injury for car accidents, slip and falls, and most personal injury claims. Medical malpractice also has a 2-year statute of limitations from the date of discovery or 4 years from the date of the incident (whichever is earlier).

How much is my Wichita car accident case worth?

Average settlements in Sedgwick County: $16,000–$45,000 for soft tissue injuries, $65,000–$185,000 for injuries requiring surgery. Factors include medical bills, lost wages, fault percentage, and insurance policy limits.

Can I still recover if I was partially at fault in Kansas?

Yes, as long as you are less than 50% at fault. Your recovery is reduced by your percentage of fault. If you're 30% at fault and the damages are $100,000, you recover $70,000.

What is Kansas's cap on pain and suffering damages?

Kansas caps non-economic damages at $350,000 per plaintiff for personal injury cases. For medical malpractice, the cap is $350,000 per defendant up to $1.25 million total.

Does Kansas have no-fault insurance?

Yes. Kansas is a no-fault state, meaning your own PIP insurance pays the first $4,500 of medical bills regardless of fault. You must meet a $2,000 medical expense threshold to sue for pain and suffering.
