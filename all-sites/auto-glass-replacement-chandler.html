Arizona's Zero-Deductible Windshield Law: What It Means

Arizona is one of the few states that requires insurers to waive the comprehensive deductible for windshield repair and replacement. Under ARS §20-259.01, any policy with comprehensive coverage must pay the full cost of windshield glass replacement with no deductible. That means most Chandler drivers with full coverage pay $0 out of pocket — the insurer covers the glass, and if your car has ADAS, the recalibration is part of the claim.

The practical steps: file the claim with your insurer or let the glass shop handle it (most do). You're still entitled to choose your own shop — Arizona law prohibits insurers from requiring their preferred vendor, though some try to steer. Watch for the two common gotchas: (1) claims history — a no-deductible glass claim is still a claim and can affect renewal pricing, so a $60 chip repair might be worth paying cash; (2) some policies exclude "safe glass" after multiple claims, so don't abuse the free replacement.

Chandler's Heat, Freeway Gravel, and the Chip-to-Crack Cycle

Chandler's summer is the enemy of auto glass. Pavement temps hit 160°F+, and the 100–115°F ambient heat expands the glass while the AC blows cold air on the interior — that differential stress turns a dime-sized chip into a foot-long crack in days. That's why Arizona leads the nation in glass claims, and why local shops advise repairing chips immediately, especially before the monsoon season's temperature swings in July–September.

Freeway gravel is the other driver: the 202 and Loop 101 carry heavy construction traffic through Chandler year-round, and stone impacts at 70 mph chip windshields constantly. If a chip is smaller than a quarter and not in the driver's line of sight, it can usually be resin-repaired in 30 minutes for $50–$120 — but only if it hasn't collected dirt or started cracking. After repair, avoid car washes and hard braking for 24 hours so the resin cures. And check your wiper blades while you're at it — the desert sun destroys wiper rubber in 6–12 months, and worn blades scratch glass.

ADAS Recalibration: The Extra Step Modern Cars Require

Any car with a windshield-mounted camera — lane-keeping, automatic emergency braking, adaptive cruise control — needs recalibration after windshield replacement. In Chandler, that's most cars built after 2017. Static calibration runs $150–$350 and takes 1–2 hours with the car in a specific position relative to targets; dynamic calibration requires a test drive with a scan tool. Some shops quote "windshield only" and skip recalibration to win the price — that's dangerous, because a misaligned camera can brake or steer incorrectly at highway speed.

Ask three questions before booking: (1) Does your quote include ADAS recalibration? (2) Is it static or dynamic, and what equipment do you use? (3) Will the calibration be verified with a scan tool before I take the car? A reputable Chandler shop will show you the calibration report. OEM glass is also worth considering for ADAS cars — aftermarket glass can have slightly different optical properties that affect camera performance, and most insurers cover OEM glass when ADAS is involved if you ask.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a windshield replacement take in Chandler?

Most replacements take 60–90 minutes including adhesive curing. ADAS recalibration adds 1–2 hours. Mobile service comes to your home or office anywhere in the southeast Valley.

Is windshield replacement really free in Arizona?

Yes, if you carry comprehensive coverage. Arizona law waives the comprehensive deductible for windshield repair and replacement, so most Chandler drivers pay $0. Your insurer may still count it as a claim, which can affect renewal rates.

Can a cracked windshield be repaired instead of replaced?

Only if the damage is a chip or crack under about 6 inches, not in the driver's direct line of sight, and not at the edge of the glass. Anything larger or in the critical zone requires replacement for safety and structural integrity.

How fast should I fix a chip in Chandler's heat?

Within days. The desert heat and AC temperature differential make chips spread rapidly — a quarter-sized chip that would take months to crack in a cooler climate can become a full windshield crack in under a week in a Chandler summer.

Do I need OEM glass for my ADAS-equipped car?

It's recommended. Aftermarket glass can have slightly different optics that affect camera calibration. Ask your insurer about OEM coverage — many waive the upgrade cost when the car has windshield-mounted safety systems.
