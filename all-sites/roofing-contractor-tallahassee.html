Hurricane Season Roof Damage and Insurance Claims in Florida

Hurricane season in Tallahassee runs June 1 through November 30, and the threat of wind, hail and heavy rain keeps roofing contractors busy. A roof that has reached the end of its life (15+ years for shingles, 20–25 years for metal) is much more vulnerable to wind and hail damage than a new one. Underwriters know this, which is why they inspect the roof age before issuing a new insurance policy.

Florida law requires that insurance claims for roof damage be filed within 2–3 years of the loss (the statute of limitations), and the burden is on the homeowner to document the damage and file the claim. When a storm passes, take photos of the damage immediately—missing shingles, exposed plywood, gutters bent or torn off—and call a roofing contractor for a free inspection. The contractor can supply photos and a damage estimate that documents the loss for the insurance company.

Some insurance companies are leaving Florida, and some are non-renewing customers whose roofs are older than 20 years. The state-run Citizens Property Insurance is the insurer of last resort. If your roof is over 20 years old, expect an inspection before any new policy is issued, and budget for replacement if the inspector recommends it.

Roof Materials and Lifespan in Tallahassee's Climate

Three-tab asphalt shingles are the most common in Tallahassee, and they last 15–20 years if properly ventilated. Architectural shingles (thicker, dimensional) last 20–25 years and have better wind resistance (rated up to 130 mph). Metal roofs (standing seam) last 40–50+ years and are becoming popular in Florida because they are durable in hurricanes and reduce cooling costs by 15–20% due to their reflectivity.

Flat roofs on commercial buildings and some residential additions are more vulnerable to standing water and UV damage. A flat roof lasts 10–20 years depending on the material (tar and gravel, rolled asphalt, TPO, PVC), and annual inspection for pooling water and membrane cracks is critical. Many flat roof failures in Tallahassee are due to poor drainage and clogged gutters allowing water to sit on the roof.

Roof ventilation is crucial in Tallahassee's heat and humidity. Warm, moist air trapped in the attic during the day expands, putting pressure on the roof deck, and at night it cools and condenses, leading to mold and rot. Proper ventilation—soffit intake vents and ridge exhaust vents—extends shingle and plywood life by 5–10 years and reduces HVAC strain.

Roof Repair vs. Replacement Decision

A roof that is 10–15 years old and has isolated damage (a few missing shingles, a small leak) can usually be repaired. A repair typically costs $200–$800 and buys 5–10 more years of life. A roof that is 18+ years old or has widespread damage is usually a candidate for replacement, especially if the insurance company has expressed concern about age.

Replacement on a 2,000 sq ft home costs $8,000–$18,000 for shingles and $15,000–$35,000 for metal or premium materials. Many insurance claims will partially reimburse the replacement cost (the insurance company pays Actual Cash Value, which is replacement cost minus depreciation), and homeowners often finance the difference. Some contractors offer financing, and some homeowners use HELOC or personal loans.

After any major repair or replacement, schedule a follow-up inspection at 6 months to look for any missed leaks or flashing problems. Tallahassee's heavy rains can expose workmanship issues early, and fixing them promptly prevents water damage inside the home.

Frequently Asked Questions

How old is too old for a roof in Florida?

15 years for 3-tab shingles (end of life), 20 years for architectural shingles or metal is when insurance companies start to require inspection and replacement consideration.

Will my homeowner's insurance cover roof replacement?

If damage is from a covered event (storm, hail, wind), yes. The company pays Actual Cash Value (replacement cost minus depreciation). Age and maintenance history affect the payment.

Can I repair just part of my roof?

Yes, for isolated damage. But if the roof is older than 15 years, the new shingles will not match the aged ones, which is a cosmetic issue but visible.

How long does a roof replacement take?

1–3 days for asphalt shingles, 2–5 days for metal, depending on home size and weather. Weather delays are common in Florida during hurricane season.

How much does roof replacement cost in Tallahassee?

Asphalt shingles: $8,000–$12,000 for a 2,000 sq ft home. Metal roofs: $15,000–$25,000+. Costs vary by material, roof complexity and pitch.
