Pittsburgh Personal Injury Lawyers Who Take Your Case Personally

Pennsylvania gives you two years to file a personal injury claim — and insurance companies know most people have no idea the clock is running while they recover. Whether it is a crash on the Parkway East, a slip on an icy South Side sidewalk, or a workplace accident in the Strip District, the difference between a fair settlement and a lowball offer is usually decided in the first weeks: evidence preserved, medical records collected, and the right tort choice applied to your claim. This guide explains the Pennsylvania rules that determine what your case is worth.

Pennsylvania's 2-Year Statute of Limitations

In Pennsylvania, most personal injury claims must be filed within two years of the injury. The clock starts on the date of the accident — not when you feel better, not when the medical bills stop, and not when the insurance company finishes its investigation. Miss the deadline and the court will dismiss your case with no hearing on the merits, even if the other driver was 100% at fault.

Two years sounds like plenty until you count what has to happen in that window: treatment, maximum medical improvement, gathering records, hiring experts, negotiating, and filing before the deadline. Insurance adjusters routinely stall until the clock is dangerous because delay is their best tool. That is why the first call matters — a Pittsburgh PI lawyer can put the insurer on notice, preserve evidence, and lock in the deadline calendar while you focus on recovering.

Limited Tort vs. Full Tort: The Choice Most Drivers Never Think About

Pennsylvania is a choice no-fault state, and the choice is on your auto insurance card. Drivers who select limited tort pay a lower premium but give up the right to recover non-economic damages — pain and suffering — unless their injury meets Pennsylvania's serious injury threshold. Drivers with full tort keep the right to full compensation. It is one of the most consequential boxes on a Pennsylvania policy, and most people only learn which one they picked after a crash.

If you have limited tort, the case strategy changes completely: your lawyer has to prove serious injury — permanent disfigurement, permanent impairment, or substantially similar long-term impact — before pain and suffering is even on the table. If you are unsure what your policy says, check the declarations page now, and ask a lawyer to review it before you accept any settlement. A bad settlement signed under limited tort is permanent.

How Much Is a Pittsburgh Personal Injury Case Worth?

No lawyer can quote a number without medical records, but the ranges below reflect what Allegheny County cases actually settle for. The four biggest drivers are medical bills, lost wages, permanency, and the at-fault party's policy limits — you cannot collect more than the policy covers unless the at-fault party has personal assets.

Fault matters too. Pennsylvania uses modified comparative fault: your recovery is reduced by your percentage of fault, and you are barred entirely if you are found 51% or more at fault. That makes investigation critical — a lawyer who photographs the scene, pulls the police report, and talks to witnesses early can be the difference between 100% and 40% recovery.

Choosing a Lawyer in Pittsburgh and Allegheny County

Personal injury lawyers in Pittsburgh work on contingency — typically 33–40% of the recovery, paid only if you win. The consultation is free, and there is no upfront cost. That fee structure means the lawyer's incentive is aligned with yours, but it also means you should interview more than one firm and ask hard questions: how many cases like yours have you taken to trial, who will actually handle the file, and how often do you communicate with clients?

Ask about the venue too. Allegheny County's Court of Common Pleas is where most Pittsburgh cases resolve, and a firm that works there daily knows the judges, the mediators, and the defense lawyers — that local knowledge quietly moves settlement numbers. And in a city of bridges, tunnels, and winter ice, cases run the gamut from I-376 rear-enders to construction falls; make sure the firm you pick has actually tried your kind of case, not just advertised for it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to file a personal injury lawsuit in Pennsylvania?

Pennsylvania gives you two years from the date of injury to file most personal injury claims. Miss that deadline and your case is barred permanently, with very narrow exceptions.

What is the difference between limited tort and full tort in Pennsylvania?

Limited tort costs less on your auto premium but blocks you from recovering pain and suffering unless your injury meets the serious injury threshold. Full tort preserves your right to full non-economic damages. Check your own policy before a crash decides for you.

How much does a Pittsburgh personal injury lawyer cost?

Most Pennsylvania PI lawyers work on contingency — typically 33–40% of the recovery, paid only if you win. Initial consultations are free, and there is no upfront fee.

What if I was partly at fault for the accident?

Pennsylvania follows modified comparative fault: your recovery is reduced by your percentage of fault, and you are barred from any recovery if you are found 51% or more at fault.

What is my Pittsburgh personal injury case worth?

It depends on medical bills, lost wages, permanency of injury, insurance policy limits, and fault. Soft-tissue crash cases often settle in the $5,000–$40,000 range; cases involving surgery or permanent injury routinely reach six figures.
