Baltimore's Unique HVAC Challenges

Baltimore's humid subtropical climate with hot, humid summers and cold winters puts extreme stress on HVAC systems. The most common service calls across the metro area: frozen evaporator coils from high humidity, failed capacitors during July–August peak loads, clogged condensate drains (algae growth is worse near the harbor), cracked heat exchangers in aging gas furnaces, and blower motor failures from continuously running systems in older homes without zoning.

Maryland HVAC Licensing Requirements

Maryland requires all HVAC contractors to hold a Maryland Master HVACR license issued by the Maryland Board of Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning, and Refrigeration Contractors. Technicians must complete 4 years of apprenticeship plus pass the state exam. Baltimore City requires an additional city trade license. Always verify both licenses before authorizing work over $500.

Should You Repair or Replace in Baltimore?

If your unit is under 10 years old and the repair costs less than half of a replacement, repair it. If it's 15+ years old, needs a major component like a compressor or heat exchanger, or uses R-22 refrigerant (phased out), replacement is more cost-effective. Modern SEER2-rated systems cut monthly energy bills by 30–50% — a meaningful saving in Baltimore's long cooling season.
