Emergency Plumbing Service in Wilmington, Delaware

Wilmington's older neighborhoods—Riverside, Trolley Square, Center City—have homes built with cast iron and galvanized steel pipes from the 1950s and earlier. These pipes corrode over time and fail without warning: burst pipes, water damage, slow drains and sewage backups. Modern copper and PEX pipes are more reliable, but replumbing an entire house runs $8,000–$16,000. Our Wilmington emergency plumbing team responds 24/7 to burst pipes, no water pressure, frozen pipes and sewer line backups.

Wilmington's Aging Water and Sewer Infrastructure

Wilmington's water system is managed by Aqua Delaware, and most homes on the Aqua Delaware grid have lead service lines installed between 1890 and 1970. In 2024, the EPA lowered the lead action level to 10 ppb, and Aqua Delaware has begun replacing lead lines, but the process will take years. Homeowners on private wells must maintain their own water quality. If you live in Riverside, Trolley Square, North Star or Center City Wilmington, assume your home has a lead line and have the water tested before consuming it.

Wilmington's combined sewer system (in older parts of the city) means that stormwater and sewage share the same pipes. Heavy rainfall can overwhelm these pipes, causing sewage backups into homes or blockages in main sewer lines. The Wilmington Department of Public Works is upgrading the sewer system, but it will take a decade. If your home has repeatedly backed up after heavy rain, a sump pump or backwater valve can prevent damage.

Delaware requires all plumbing contractors to be licensed by the Division of Professional Regulation. The license is renewable every two years, and licensed plumbers must complete continuing education. When hiring a plumber in Wilmington, ask to verify their license and ask about their insurance coverage for water damage.

Why Burst Pipes Happen in Wilmington and How to Prevent Them

Wilmington winters are mild (average low 28°F), but occasional hard freezes do occur. Pipes in uninsulated attics, crawl spaces or exterior walls are at risk of freezing. If the temperature drops below 32°F for 8+ hours with no heat running, water in exposed pipes can freeze and expand, causing the pipe to burst. A burst copper pipe can pump 500+ gallons of water into a home before it is discovered.

The best prevention is insulation: wrap exposed pipes in foam pipe insulation ($2–5 per foot), seal air leaks around pipes, and keep cabinet doors open in the kitchen during cold snaps to allow warm air to reach the pipes. If you have a vacation home or rental property, leaving the heat set to at least 60°F even when vacant is critical to prevent frozen and burst pipes.

If a pipe bursts, shut off the main water valve immediately (it is usually near the meter or where the line enters the home) and call for emergency service. A burst in the basement may need to be excavated if the break is in the buried portion of the water line. Cost for a frozen-pipe repair ranges from $300 for a small repair to $2,000+ if the break is underground or in a wall.

Water Heater Replacement and Sewer Line Issues in Wilmington Homes

Most Wilmington homes have 30–50-gallon gas or electric water heaters, and they last 8–12 years before needing replacement. A standard 50-gallon gas water heater costs $600–$1,200 installed, including labor and venting. A tankless water heater (on-demand system) costs $2,000–$4,500 installed but saves money on energy over time. If your home is on the Aqua Delaware water line and has an old water heater, the replacement is a good time to check for a lead service line.

Sewer line backups happen when roots invade the clay or cast iron sewer line, or when the line settles and develops low spots that trap sewage. Symptoms are slow drains throughout the home, gurgling toilets after heavy water use, or raw sewage backing up into the basement. A camera inspection of the sewer line (called CCTV) costs $400–$800 and shows the location and severity of the problem. Cleaning costs $300–$1,200 for a jetting service, or $3,000–$10,000 for trenchless sewer line repair.

Wilmington is a historic city with many original brick and tile sewer lines that are over 100 years old. If your home is near the Christina River or in one of the oldest neighborhoods, the sewer line may be failing. Getting it inspected before a backup occurs is cheaper than emergency damage repair.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do if a pipe bursts?

Shut off the main water valve immediately (usually at the meter or where the line enters the home), then call for emergency service. Every minute of water flow causes more damage.

Do I need a licensed plumber in Wilmington?

Yes. Delaware requires plumbers to be licensed by the Division of Professional Regulation. Verify the license before hiring.

Does Aqua Delaware handle water line issues?

Aqua Delaware is responsible for the main water line from the street to your meter. From the meter into your home is your responsibility. If the problem is on Aqua's side, contact them directly.

How do I know if my home has a lead service line?

If your home was built before 1970 in Wilmington, it likely has a lead line. A plumber can scrape the line to check. Have the water tested and contact Aqua Delaware about line replacement programs.

How much does sewer line repair cost?

Camera inspection: $400–$800. Jetting: $300–$1,200. Trenchless repair: $3,000–$10,000.
