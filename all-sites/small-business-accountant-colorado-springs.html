Colorado Business Tax Requirements for Small Businesses

Colorado has several tax obligations that catch new business owners off guard. The state has a flat 4.40% corporate income tax rate and requires all businesses collecting sales tax to file monthly, quarterly, or annually depending on volume. Colorado Springs city sales tax is 3.15% (plus 1% PPRTA for regional transportation), bringing the total to 8.20% in most areas.

Colorado also requires a business personal property tax return (Form 600) for businesses with over $50,000 in personal property assets. This catches many e-commerce and service businesses who don't realize their computers, furniture, and equipment count as taxable assets. Failing to file triggers a 5% monthly penalty up to 25% of the tax due.

Colorado Business License & Registration Requirements

Every business operating in Colorado Springs must register with the Colorado Secretary of State (one-time filing of $50 for LLCs, $100 for corporations) and obtain a Colorado Springs city business license ($50–$200 annually depending on business type). Home-based businesses need a separate conditional use permit if customers visit the location.

El Paso County requires a trade name registration if your business operates under a name different from your legal entity. Professional services (accounting firms, legal, medical) also need state professional licensing through the Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies.

Tax Deductions Specific to Colorado Springs Businesses

Colorado Springs small businesses can access deductions beyond federal ones. Colorado allows a 100% deduction of health insurance premiums for S-Corp shareholders (federal allows the deduction but differently structured). The state's enterprise zone program offers up to $2,500 in tax credits per new full-time employee created in designated areas of Colorado Springs (downtown, southeast side).
