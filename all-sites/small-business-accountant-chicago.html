A Chicago Accountant Who Knows Illinois Taxes

Illinois taxes small businesses with a flat, predictable structure that is friendlier than its reputation suggests — a flat 4.95% personal income tax with no brackets, no city income tax in Chicago, and no local wage tax. But it pairs that with a brutal sales tax — Chicago's combined rate tops 10%, among the highest of any big city — and a 9.5% combined corporate rate for C-corps. A Chicago small business accountant turns that structure into real decisions: which entity to form, when to register for sales tax, and how to keep the books clean enough that tax season is an event, not an emergency. This guide explains what an Illinois accountant handles, the tax rules that matter, and what the work costs.

What a Chicago Small Business Accountant Actually Does

A good small business accountant is a year-round partner, not a March filing service. The job starts with setup: choosing between an LLC, S-corp, or C-corp and getting the entity registered with the Illinois Secretary of State and the Illinois Department of Revenue. Then comes the monthly engine — clean books in QuickBooks or Xero, reconciled bank accounts, payroll runs, sales tax filings for businesses that sell taxable goods, and 1099s for contractors at year-end.

For a Chicago business specifically, the accountant also owns the local details: Illinois has no state-level business license for most companies, but the City of Chicago requires most businesses to register for a business license, and food, retail, and professional services have their own city and state layers. Between the state, Cook County, and the city, a business that sells goods in Chicago can owe sales tax to all three jurisdictions — and the accountant is the one who makes sure every registration and filing deadline is met before the penalties start accruing.

Illinois Tax Rates Every Chicago Owner Should Know

Illinois's income tax is a flat 4.95% — locked in by the state constitution, which requires a single rate, after voters rejected a graduated-tax amendment in 2020. For LLC and S-corp owners, that flat rate applies to pass-through income on top of federal tax. C-corporations face a combined 9.5% — the 7% corporate rate plus the 2.5% personal property replacement tax — and then shareholders pay tax again on dividends. That double layer is why most small businesses choose pass-through status, and why an accountant's entity advice is worth real money at formation time.

Sales tax is where Illinois surprises people. Chicago's combined rate — state, county, city, and transit district portions — tops 10%, among the highest in the country. If you sell taxable goods or certain services in the city, you must register with the Illinois Department of Revenue, collect the tax, and file on the state's schedule. Remote sellers and marketplace sellers face the same registration duties under Illinois's marketplace rules. An accountant who knows Illinois sales tax will tell you exactly when registration is triggered — and will stop you from the classic mistake of collecting tax you never remit, which turns into personal liability fast.

Entity Choice in Illinois: LLC, S-Corp, or C-Corp

Most Chicago small businesses start as LLCs — simple to form, flexible, and the default protection for personal assets. But the LLC's tax treatment has a ceiling: all net profit flows to you as self-employment income, and once your business is profitable, the self-employment tax on that income starts to hurt. That is when the S-corp election becomes the accountant's standard move — the business pays you a reasonable salary (subject to payroll tax) and the remaining profit passes through without self-employment tax. The trade-off is payroll administration: an S-corp must run payroll and file the related returns, which is why the election only pays off above roughly $40,000–$60,000 of net profit.

C-corporations are the right answer less often than owners think. The 9.5% Illinois corporate rate plus the federal 21% rate means entity-level tax of nearly 30% before any dividend — a heavy load for a business whose owners just want the profit personally. C-corps make sense when you plan to reinvest heavily, need to attract investors, or want to offer equity incentives. In Illinois, where the pass-through rates are flat and predictable, most service businesses are better off as an LLC or S-corp — and the accountant who runs that math for you before you file the formation paperwork is worth the fee.

What a Chicago Accountant Costs — and How to Choose One

Chicago accounting rates sit near the national average for a major city. Monthly bookkeeping for a small business typically runs $300–$900 depending on transaction volume; business tax preparation runs $500–$1,800 based on entity type and complexity; and bundled bookkeeping-plus-tax packages usually come with a discount. Formation and sales tax registration are often one-time setup fees of $300–$800. The pricing model to look for is flat and written down — a monthly fee for a defined scope, with catch-up cleanup quoted separately before it starts.

Choose by fit, not just price. Ask whether the accountant works with businesses your size in your industry, whether they use cloud bookkeeping you can see in real time, and whether tax strategy — entity choice, estimated payments, sales tax planning — is included or billed hourly. A Chicago accountant who files one return a year and disappears is not a partner; the value is in the monthly discipline that makes tax season boring. And for anything requiring a CPA's credential — audited financials, certain attest work, or representing you before the IRS in serious disputes — confirm the person you hire actually holds the Illinois CPA license.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the income tax rate for small businesses in Illinois?

Illinois taxes individuals at a flat 4.95%, which applies to LLC and S-corp owners' pass-through income. C-corporations pay 9.5% combined — 7% base plus 2.5% personal property replacement tax. The state constitution requires a flat rate, so there are no brackets.

Does Chicago have a city income tax on businesses?

No. Unlike New York or Philadelphia, Chicago has no city income tax or wage tax. Chicago businesses deal with the state's flat income tax plus the city's high sales tax — over 10% combined in the city — but payroll is simpler without a local wage tax.

LLC, S-corp, or C-corp — which is right for my Chicago business?

Most small Chicago businesses start as LLCs for simplicity and liability protection. An S-corp election can reduce self-employment tax once profits are steady, and a C-corp makes sense only when you plan to reinvest heavily or raise investors — but it means paying Illinois's 9.5% corporate rate plus shareholder tax on dividends.

What is the sales tax rate in Chicago?

Chicago's combined sales tax rate tops 10% — among the highest of any major U.S. city. If you sell taxable goods in the city, you must register with the Illinois Department of Revenue, collect the tax, and file returns on the state's schedule.

How much does a small business accountant cost in Chicago?

Monthly bookkeeping for a small Chicago business runs $300–$900, and business tax preparation runs $500–$1,800 depending on entity type and complexity. Many accountants bundle bookkeeping and tax prep at a discount.
