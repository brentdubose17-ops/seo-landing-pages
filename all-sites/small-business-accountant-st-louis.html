The St. Louis Earnings Tax: What Every Business Must Know

St. Louis is one of the few U.S. cities with a local earnings tax — 1% on wages, salaries, and business net profits for anyone who lives or works in the City of St. Louis. If your business is located in the city, or you have employees who work there, you must register with the Collector of Revenue and withhold this 1% tax. It's entirely separate from state income tax and frequently missed by new businesses.

This is where a local accountant pays for themselves. The 1% earnings tax applies to net profits of businesses operating in the city, and the rules for what counts as "earned in the city" are complex for businesses with remote or hybrid workers. A St. Louis CPA ensures you're compliant — and helps you structure operations to minimize the tax legally — without the penalties and interest the city assesses for non-filing, which can run into the thousands.

Do Accountants Need a License in Missouri?

Missouri licenses Certified Public Accountants through the Missouri State Board of Accountancy. A CPA has passed the uniform CPA exam, completed 150 semester hours of education, and met experience requirements. Anyone can call themselves an "accountant" or "bookkeeper," but only a licensed CPA can sign audits, reviews, and compilations or represent you before the IRS as a credentialed preparer.

For tax work, look for a CPA or an Enrolled Agent (EA) — an EA is federally licensed by the IRS and can represent you in any tax matter. St. Louis has a competitive accounting market, so verify credentials on the Missouri Board of Accountancy's website and ask about experience with businesses your size and in your industry before engaging anyone.

LLC vs. S-Corp in Missouri: The Decision That Saves Thousands

The single most valuable decision a St. Louis small business makes is its entity election. An LLC is taxed as a pass-through by default — you pay self-employment tax (15.3%) on all profits. Electing S-corp status lets you take part of your income as a salary (subject to payroll tax) and the rest as distributions (not subject to self-employment tax). A business netting $120,000 can save $6,000–$9,000 a year with the right election.

The trade-off is compliance: S-corps require payroll, reasonable compensation, and separate tax filings. Missouri also has its own franchise tax considerations and a 4% state income tax on top of federal rates. A St. Louis accountant runs the numbers both ways — the analysis is specific to your profit level and salary — and files the election with the IRS and Missouri by the deadline so you don't leave money on the table.
