Do Accountants Need a License in Missouri?

Only CPAs are licensed in Missouri — by the Missouri State Board of Accountancy — and only CPAs can legally "certify" financial statements. Anyone can call themselves a bookkeeper or tax preparer, so credential-checking matters. For tax preparation, look for an Enrolled Agent (EA), CPA, or attorney — the three credentials with unlimited IRS representation rights.

One Missouri-specific catch: the state's business taxes are simpler than in many states (no state corporate income tax on most S-corps and LLCs, but a 4% corporate income tax on C-corps and a 4% top individual rate), but St. Louis City adds its own 1% earnings tax on businesses operating within the city limits. A local accountant who handles the city earnings tax and the state's franchise tax correctly saves you from late-filing penalties.

Missouri and St. Louis Business Taxes You Can't Skip

Missouri small businesses face a specific tax stack: federal income tax, Missouri corporate income tax (4% on C-corps; pass-through income taxed at the 4% individual rate), Missouri sales tax (4.225% state rate plus local — St. Louis City pushes the combined rate to 9.679%, among the highest in the nation), and the St. Louis City earnings tax (1% on business net income). Payroll taxes (FICA, FUTA, SUTA) add another layer.

Missouri also imposes a franchise tax on corporations — annual reporting to the Secretary of State is required, and failure to file dissolves the entity. A good St. Louis accountant keeps you compliant across federal, state, and city filings, handles sales tax nexus questions if you sell online, and structures the entity (LLC vs. S-corp) to minimize the self-employment tax hit — typically saving small business owners $3,000–$8,000/year.

Bookkeeping vs. Accounting: What St. Louis Businesses Actually Need

Bookkeeping is recording transactions — categorizing income and expenses, reconciling bank accounts, managing receivables. Accounting is the higher-level work: financial statements, tax strategy, cash-flow planning, and IRS representation. Most St. Louis firms sell both, and the line between them matters for price. A $300/month bookkeeper keeps the books current; a $900/month accountant interprets them and plans taxes.

Common St. Louis mistake: hiring a "tax preparer" for year-end only, then discovering the books are a mess — triggering a $2,000+ cleanup bill and missed deductions. The smarter play is monthly bookkeeping year-round (so tax season is a 2-hour review, not a fire drill) and a quarterly tax planning session. Most firms will do a free consultation to scope the work — ask for a fixed monthly quote in writing.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I hire a small business accountant in St. Louis?

Hire one when you incorporate, hire employees, cross $100K in revenue, or get a tax notice. Before that, a good bookkeeper plus accounting software is usually enough. The earlier you bring in an accountant, the more tax structure you save.

What's the difference between a CPA and a bookkeeper?

A CPA is state-licensed, can certify financials, and represent you before the IRS. A bookkeeper records transactions but can't certify or give formal tax advice. Many small businesses need both: monthly bookkeeping + a CPA for tax strategy.

How much does tax preparation cost for an LLC in Missouri?

Most St. Louis firms charge $400–$1,200 for an LLC tax return depending on complexity, with an additional fee if you also file S-corp payroll. Flat monthly accounting plans usually include the business return.

Do I need to pay St. Louis City earnings tax?

Yes — if your business operates inside St. Louis City limits, you owe the 1% earnings tax on net business income, even if you're registered elsewhere. A local accountant handles the city registration and quarterly payments automatically.

What records should I keep for my St. Louis business?

Keep receipts for all expenses, bank and credit card statements, payroll records, and sales tax filings for at least 7 years. Cloud-based bookkeeping (QuickBooks, Xero) makes monthly reconciliation simple and keeps you audit-ready.
