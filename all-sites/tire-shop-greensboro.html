Winter Tires vs. All-Season Tires in Greensboro

North Carolina gets 5–15 inches of snow per year on average, with the heaviest snow in January and February. Winter tires are designed to stay pliable in cold temperatures and to grip snow and ice better than all-season tires. A winter tire with the 3PMSF (Three-Peak Mountain Snowflake) rating meets severe winter performance standards and is the safest choice for anyone driving in Greensboro's winter weather.

All-season tires are a compromise: they are decent in snow but do not perform as well as winter tires, and they wear faster in hot weather. Most Greensboro drivers run all-seasons year-round because buying two sets of tires is an extra expense. If winter driving is a priority, budget for winter tires October–March and switch to summer or all-season tires April–September.

Tire pressure also matters in winter. Cold temperatures cause tire pressure to drop about 1 psi for every 10°F of temperature decrease. A tire that is at the correct pressure in summer will be 8–10 psi low in January, which reduces traction and increases rolling resistance. Checking tire pressure monthly and adjusting for seasonal temperature changes improves winter safety.

Wheel Alignment and Suspension Services

Greensboro streets and highways are lined with potholes, especially after winter, and hitting a pothole can throw wheel alignment off. Symptoms of misalignment are pulling to one side, uneven tire wear, and vibration in the steering wheel. An alignment service costs $75–$200 and takes about an hour, and it should be done whenever you notice these symptoms or every 30,000 miles.

Suspension components like struts, shocks, springs and bushings wear out over time, especially on rough roads. A worn strut lets the car bounce excessively after bumps, which is not just uncomfortable—it reduces tire contact with the road and increases stopping distance. Strut replacement costs $500–$1,200 per axle depending on the vehicle.

If your car is vibrating, pulling, or bouncing, get a suspension and alignment check. Many shops charge $50–$100 for the inspection, which gives you a detailed report of what needs repair. We offer free estimates and can prioritize repairs by safety.

Brake Service and Stopping Safety

Brake pads wear out every 25,000–50,000 miles depending on driving habits. Hard braking in traffic wears pads faster than highway cruising, and mountain driving (constant braking downhill) is especially hard on brakes. When you hear squeaking or grinding, the pads are worn and need replacement. Ignoring it damages the brake rotor, which costs more to fix.

A brake service includes replacing the pads, inspecting the rotors and cylinders, and topping up the brake fluid. Cost is $200–$600 per axle. If the rotors are damaged, they must be replaced or turned, which adds $150–$300 per axle. Doing brake work promptly prevents rotor damage and keeps your stopping power reliable.

Greensboro winters mean salt on the roads, which corrodes brake components. An annual brake inspection keeps you ahead of corrosion and catches issues before they become safety problems.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I buy winter tires or all-season tires for Greensboro?

Winter tires are safer in snow and ice. All-seasons are a compromise. Many Greensboro drivers switch to winters October–March for better traction.

How often does my car need an alignment?

Every 30,000 miles, or whenever you notice pulling, uneven tire wear, or steering wheel vibration. Potholes can knock alignment off anytime.

How long do brake pads last?

25,000–50,000 miles depending on driving habits. Hard braking and mountain driving wear them faster. Listen for squeaking; that is a warning sign.

Can I mix tire brands on my car?

Mixing brands is not ideal, but if you must, keep the same brand and tread pattern on each axle. All four matching tires are always best.

What does an alignment cost in Greensboro?

2-wheel alignment: $75–$125. 4-wheel alignment: $120–$200. Most cars need 4-wheel alignment for proper handling.
