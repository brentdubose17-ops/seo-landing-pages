Why Jacksonville Has So Many Slab Leaks

Most Jacksonville homes are built on slab-on-grade foundation (concrete directly on soil). When supply lines embedded in the slab corrode or develop a pinhole leak, the only detection is rising water bills or a wet spot in the floor.

Copper piping (common in homes built 1980–2000) can corrode in as little as 15–25 years, especially with Jacksonville's salt air and acidic soil. Galvanized steel fails faster. Modern systems use PEX tubing, which lasts 50+ years without corrosion.

If you suspect a slab leak, call a plumber for detection first ($250–$500) before digging. They can use acoustic or thermal imaging to find the exact location. Avoid drilling blind.

Water Heater Replacement in Jacksonville's Climate

Jacksonville's warm year-round temperatures mean water heaters run constantly. A gas water heater lasts 8–12 years, electric 10–15 years. Saltwater environments accelerate corrosion on outdoor equipment.

Tankless water heaters are popular in Florida because they take up less space and have no standby losses. They cost $1,500–$2,500 installed (vs. $1,000–$1,500 for a tank), but save 25–40% on energy over the lifetime.

Insulate hot water pipes to reduce waste. Jacksonville homes often have long runs from the heater to bathrooms, wasting hot water down the drain. Pipe wrap costs $20–$50 and pays for itself in 2–3 months.

Plumbing Code Updates for Jacksonville Homeowners

Duval County follows Florida Plumbing Code, which is updated every 3 years. Recent updates include stricter backflow prevention for high-hazard fixtures (irrigation, boilers) and seismic restraints on water heaters.

If you're doing plumbing work, permits are required for most repairs. The permit fee is typically $100–$300 and inspections ensure code compliance. Skipping permits may void insurance coverage and make the home harder to sell.
