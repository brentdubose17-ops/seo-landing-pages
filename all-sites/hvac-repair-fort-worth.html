Fort Worth HVAC Repair You Can Count On

Fort Worth summers push thermostats into the high 90s for weeks at a time, and when an AC dies in July the repair wait can stretch past a week if you pick the wrong company. Why TDLR Licensing Matters for Fort Worth AC Work

Texas is one of the stricter states on HVAC licensing. Anyone who contracts to install, repair, or maintain air conditioning or refrigeration equipment must hold an Air Conditioning and Refrigeration (ACR) Contractor license from TDLR, and the technicians doing the work must hold a TDLR technician registration. The same agency licenses plumbers, electricians, and other trades, so the license number is easy to verify online.

Before you hire, confirm three things: the company's TDLR ACR license is active, the tech on the truck is registered, and the job will be permitted. Fort Worth requires mechanical permits for most AC and furnace installations, and the city inspects the work. A contractor who dodges the permit is usually dodging the inspection too — walk away.

How DFW Heat and Freezes Stress Your System

Fort Worth averages more than 20 days a year at or above 100°F, and July through August your AC can run 12+ hours a day. That duty cycle is brutal on capacitors, contactors, and compressors — the classic mid-summer failure is a $15 capacitor killing a $6,000 system's cooling for days. Annual pre-summer maintenance catches those weak parts before the heat wave.

The other extreme matters too. The February 2021 freeze (Uri) left thousands of DFW homes without heat for days, and the post-freeze repair backlog took months. If your system is more than 12 years old, having a furnace inspection in October and an AC inspection in April is cheaper than any emergency call — and it keeps you near the front of the line when a storm hits and every contractor is booked solid.

Right-Sizing and Efficiency: What Actually Lowers Your Bill

Fort Worth is a cooling-dominated market, so efficiency decisions revolve around the AC. A properly sized 16 SEER unit in a well-sealed home can cut cooling costs 20–35% versus an old 10 SEER system running in a leaky house. But the size has to come from a Manual J load calculation, not a rule of thumb — an oversized unit short-cycles, dehumidifies poorly, and dies young, while an undersized unit runs nonstop on 100-degree days.

Also check the air side of the equation. Duct leaks in an attic that hits 130°F can waste 20–30% of your cooling before it reaches a room. A duct sealing job in the $500–$1,500 range often pays back faster than a premium high-SEER condenser. Ask your contractor for a static pressure test if you suspect duct problems.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an AC diagnostic cost in Fort Worth?

Most Fort Worth HVAC companies charge $89–$149 for a diagnostic visit, and reputable shops credit that fee toward the repair if you hire them.

Does Texas require HVAC contractors to be licensed?

Yes. Anyone who contracts air conditioning or refrigeration work must hold an Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractor license from the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR). Verify the number online before hiring.

What size AC do I need for a 2,000 sq ft Fort Worth home?

Typically 3.5–4 tons, but the correct answer depends on insulation, window area, and sun exposure. A contractor who quotes a size without a Manual J load calculation is guessing.

How long does an AC unit last in the DFW heat?

10–15 years with annual maintenance is realistic in Fort Worth, though the punishing summer duty cycles shorten the life of units that are undersized or never serviced.

Do I need a permit for an AC replacement in Fort Worth?

Yes — Fort Worth requires a mechanical permit for most AC and furnace installations, and the city inspects the work. A licensed contractor handles the permit and inspection as part of the job.
