How Much Does Roof Replacement Cost in Austin?

Architectural asphalt shingle roofs in Austin run $4.50–$7.00 per square foot installed — typically $9,000–$16,000 for a 2,000 sq ft home. Metal roofs cost $9–$14 per sq ft ($18,000–$28,000 for the same home) but last 40–70 years versus 20–25 for shingles. Flat and low-slope commercial roofs (TPO, EPDM) run $6–$10 per sq ft.

Austin's hail history means most roof replacements are insurance claims. Storm restoration pricing runs 10–20% higher than retail because of insurance paperwork, code upgrades, and debris removal — a standard roof that costs $11,000 retail typically invoices $13,000–$15,000 through a hail claim.

What Are Texas Roofing Licensing Requirements?

Texas requires roofing contractors to register with the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR). Registration requires proof of general liability insurance ($300,000+), workers' compensation coverage, and a criminal background check. TDLR registration numbers are publicly searchable — always verify before signing a contract.

The City of Austin requires roof permits for re-roofing jobs, with fees based on roof size ($150–$400 for most homes). Austin's 2022 building code update added requirements for impact-resistant underlayment in hail-prone zones, and all new roofs must be inspected by the city before final approval.

Why Is Austin Roofing Unique?

Austin sits in the heart of Texas Hail Alley — the I-35 corridor between San Antonio and Dallas records more large-hail events than almost anywhere in the US, with the peak season running March through June. Hailstones of 1.5–2 inches are common enough that most insurance carriers require roofers to document damage with photos and adjuster sign-off before paying claims.

Central Texas weather also swings hard: 100°F+ summers bake shingles (accelerating granule loss), while occasional winter freezes like the 2021 Winter Storm Uri cause ice-dam and wind damage. Cedar shake and tile roofs — popular in Westlake and Lakeway — need specialized installers and cost 2–3x asphalt, but handle the heat better.

Roof Repair vs Replacement: Which Do You Need?

Repairs make sense when damage is localized (a few missing shingles, a small leak around a vent pipe) and the roof is under 15 years old. Typical Austin roof repairs run $300–$1,500. If hail has bruised or cracked shingles across multiple slopes, or the roof is over 20 years old, replacement is almost always the better long-term call — patched hail roofs fail early and trigger insurance claim denials on the next storm.

Get a free inspection after every significant hail event. Austin hail damage is often invisible from the ground: granule loss, cracked tabs, and dented flashing on the north-facing slope are the telltale signs. An insurance-approved adjuster's report is your best evidence if you file a claim.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Texas require roofing contractors to be licensed?

Yes. All roofing contractors must register with the TDLR and carry liability insurance and workers' comp. Verify the registration number on the TDLR website before signing a contract.

How do I know if hail damaged my Austin roof?

Look for cracked or missing shingle tabs, granule loss in gutters, and dents on flashing, gutters, and AC units. A free inspection after any storm with 1-inch+ hail is the fastest way to find out — and the only way to document it for insurance.

Will my insurance cover a hail-damaged roof in Austin?

Most Texas homeowners policies cover hail damage to roofs, minus your deductible (typically 1% of dwelling coverage). Document damage before repairs, and have the contractor meet your adjuster on-site — that meeting is where claims get approved or denied.

Do I need a permit for a roof replacement in Austin?

Yes. The City of Austin requires a roofing permit (typically $150–$400) and a final inspection. Reputable contractors include permitting in their bid — never accept a quote that skips it.

How long does a roof replacement take in Austin?

Most residential asphalt roofs take 1–3 days to tear off and reinstall. Metal and tile roofs run 3–7 days. Rain delays are common in spring storm season, so budget a week of flexibility.
