Monthly plans typically cover bank and credit card reconciliation, categorized transactions, financial statements, and quarterly check-ins. Payroll add-ons run $50–$150 per month, and sales tax filing is usually bundled or charged at $50–$150 per filing. Compared to a full-time in-house bookkeeper in Denver ($55,000–$75,000 salary plus benefits), virtual bookkeeping delivers the same function at 20–30% of the cost.

What Does a Denver Virtual Bookkeeper Handle?

A good Denver virtual bookkeeper handles your full monthly cycle: reconciling bank and credit card accounts, categorizing expenses, tracking accounts receivable and payable, producing profit-and-loss statements, and preparing clean books for your CPA at tax time. Most use QuickBooks Online or Xero and give you real-time dashboard access.

Colorado-specific work matters too: sales tax registration and filing, tracking 1099 contractors, and managing the Colorado Retail Delivery Fee (a 27-cent fee on retail deliveries that trips up many e-commerce and delivery businesses). A local virtual bookkeeper knows these state quirks without you needing an office visit.

What Are Colorado's Business Tax Basics?

Colorado has a flat 4.4% corporate income tax rate and a 2.9% state sales tax; Denver adds its own local sales tax, bringing the combined Denver rate to 8.81%. If you sell taxable goods or services, you must register with the Colorado Department of Revenue and file sales tax returns — monthly, quarterly, or annually depending on volume.

Denver also has its own minimum wage — higher than the state's — which affects payroll calculations for businesses with Denver employees. In 2026 Denver's minimum wage is above $19.50/hour for large employers, with annual inflation adjustments. Getting payroll wrong on city-specific minimums is a common Denver audit issue.

Why Do Denver Businesses Need Catch-Up Bookkeeping?

Denver's fast-growing small-business scene means many owners run 6–18 months before hiring a bookkeeper — by which time their records are a mess of unreconciled accounts, misfiled receipts, and uncollected receivables. Catch-up cleanup is one of the most requested services from Denver bookkeepers, and the cost scales with how far behind you are.

Colorado businesses with specialized industries — cannabis (subject to IRC 280E, which disallows most business deductions), construction (contractor retainage and lien tracking), and restaurants (tip pooling, inventory) — benefit most from a bookkeeper who knows the industry's specific rules. Ask about industry experience before hiring.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a bookkeeper if I use accounting software?

Software categorizes transactions but doesn't reconcile, file taxes, or catch errors. Denver businesses using software alone typically spend 10+ hours a month on books they could outsource for $200–$700.

What's the difference between a bookkeeper and a CPA?

Bookkeepers maintain your monthly books and records. CPAs provide tax strategy, prepare and file tax returns, and give financial advice. Most Denver businesses use both — the bookkeeper keeps the books clean so the CPA's tax work is faster and cheaper.

How fast can you clean up my past-due books?

Most catch-up cleanups are completed in 1–3 weeks depending on how far behind you are. You'll receive current financial statements and a clear plan for staying current.

Is virtual bookkeeping secure?

Yes. Reputable firms use bank-level encryption, read-only bank feeds, and role-based access. You keep ownership of your QuickBooks or Xero account and can revoke access at any time.

Do you work with businesses outside Denver?

Yes — virtual bookkeeping works statewide and nationally. However, a bookkeeper familiar with Colorado sales tax, the retail delivery fee, and Denver's minimum wage is essential if you operate in Colorado.
