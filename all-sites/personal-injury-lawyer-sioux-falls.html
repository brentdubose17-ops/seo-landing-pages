South Dakota Injury Laws & Comparative Fault Rules

Under South Dakota Codified Laws (SDCL 15-2-14), injury victims generally have a 3-year statute of limitations from the date of the accident to file a lawsuit in civil court. Missing this statutory deadline permanently forfeits your legal right to financial recovery.

South Dakota follows a unique 'slight-gross' comparative negligence standard (SDCL 20-9-2). If your contribution to the accident is deemed more than 'slight' compared to the defendant's negligence, recovery may be barred. Having a skilled trial attorney build strong liability evidence is essential to protect your legal claim.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to file a personal injury claim in South Dakota?

South Dakota law mandates a 3-year statute of limitations for personal injury lawsuits from the date of the accident.

What is the 'slight-gross' comparative fault rule in South Dakota?

Under SDCL 20-9-2, you can only recover damages if your fault was 'slight' in comparison to the defendant's 'gross' negligence.

How much does a personal injury lawyer cost in Sioux Falls?

We operate on a contingency fee basis—you pay $0 upfront and no attorney fees unless we successfully recover money for you.

What damages can I recover in a South Dakota injury lawsuit?

Victims can recover past and future medical expenses, lost earnings, reduced earning capacity, physical pain, and emotional suffering.

Should I speak with the insurance adjuster after a car crash?

No, insurance adjusters aim to minimize payouts. Consult an experienced personal injury attorney before giving recorded statements.
