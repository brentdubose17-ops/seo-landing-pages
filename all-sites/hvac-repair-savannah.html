Why Savannah's Humidity Matters for HVAC Performance

Savannah averages 75–80% humidity year-round, and that humidity does two things to air conditioning systems. First, it raises the temperature at which the outdoor condenser unit can release heat, which means the system has to work harder to cool the home to the same indoor temperature. A compressor that is rated to operate down to 32°F ambient can only function efficiently when the outdoor temperature is below 85°F or so in Savannah's humid conditions. Second, high humidity accelerates corrosion of the refrigerant lines, condenser coils and connectors, especially near the coast where salt spray is a factor.

Many Savannah homes built before 1985 were equipped with window AC units or no air conditioning at all, because cooling was a luxury in that era. As people move to Savannah from cooler climates, the demand for central AC has grown, and older homes are getting retrofit systems that are sometimes undersized or poorly ductwork. A unit that worked fine in Charlotte gets overwhelmed in Savannah, and the result is a home that runs the AC 24/7 and never gets below 76–78°F.

Maintenance is the lever. An AC system in Savannah needs a filter change every 30 days (not 90), a coil cleaning every 12 months, and a full seasonal service before May and before November. Skipping maintenance accelerates the corrosion and failure cycle, and it is the reason Savannah HVAC units fail 2–3 years earlier than the national average.

Georgia Licensing and Air Conditioning Permit Requirements

Georgia requires any contractor performing HVAC work to hold a Georgia Construction Contractor License (CCL) through the Georgia Construction Industry Licensing Board (GCILB). The license comes in classifications for residential, commercial and industrial work, and the GCILB website allows you to verify a contractor's license before work starts. EPA Section 608 certification is required to handle refrigerant, and any HVAC install, replacement or refrigerant service must be performed by a 608-certified technician.

Savannah, Chatham County and other municipalities in the Savannah area require a permit for air conditioning replacement and major repairs. The permit ensures that the new equipment meets current electrical codes, that the unit is properly sized for the building, and that the installation is inspected by a city official. Contractors typically handle the permit process as part of the service, and the cost is rolled into the overall job price.

New air conditioning installations qualify for a federal 30% tax credit through the end of 2032 under the IRA (Inflation Reduction Act). A heat pump installed for primary heating and cooling, or a central air conditioning system in a home with electric resistance heating, gets the credit. Georgia does not have a state tax credit on top of the federal one, but the IRA credit is substantial enough that it often pays for a portion of the system cost.

Common AC Problems in Savannah and When to Call

The three most common AC problems we see in Savannah are refrigerant leaks, compressor failure and frozen evaporator coils. Refrigerant leaks show up as weak cooling and hissing sounds near the outdoor condenser, and they require a 608-certified tech to locate the leak, repair it, vacuum the lines and recharge the system. Most leaks take 2–3 hours to diagnose and repair, and the cost is $400–$1,200 depending on the location and size of the leak.

Compressor failure is usually caused by refrigerant loss, low oil levels, or years of running at high load without maintenance. When a compressor fails, the whole outdoor unit typically needs to be replaced, which runs $1,800–$5,500 depending on size and efficiency. A compressor that is less than 10 years old and fails due to a manufacturer defect is often covered under warranty.

Frozen coils happen when the system runs with low refrigerant or restricted airflow through the indoor coil. The symptoms are no cold air coming out of the vents and ice buildup on the copper tubing near the indoor unit. Frozen coils require immediate attention—running a frozen system causes compressor damage—so shut the system off and call for same-day service. The fix is usually a filter change, coil cleaning and refrigerant top-up, which takes 1–2 hours and costs $300–$700.

Every 12 months minimum due to humidity and salt air. Filter changes should happen every 30 days, and coils should be cleaned annually.

Yes. Savannah and Chatham County require permits for AC replacement and major repairs. The contractor pulls it and the city inspector signs off on the install.

Yes. A heat pump or central AC system installed for primary cooling, or a heat pump installed for heating and cooling, qualifies for a 30% federal tax credit through 2032.
