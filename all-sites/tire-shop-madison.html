Winter Tires — Not Optional for Madison Winters

Madison averages 42 inches of snow per year with temperatures below freezing from December through March. All-season tires lose 50% of their grip below 45°F as the rubber compound hardens. A quality winter tire (like Blizzak WS90 or X-Ice Snow) stops 25–35% shorter on snow and ice than an all-season at 30°F. The cost of a winter set ($600–$1,200) is far less than a single collision deductible. Most Madison tire shops offer free seasonal changeover storage if you buy both sets from them — a perk worth asking about.

Madison's Potholes — How to Protect Your Tires and Wheels

Wisconsin's freeze-thaw cycles create some of the worst potholes in the Midwest, and Madison's side streets are particularly bad. Pothole impacts can cause sidewall bulges (tire is destroyed, $100–$350 each), bent rims (straightening $75–$150 or replacement $150–$500), and knock alignment out of spec ($89–$129 to reset). Symptoms: steering wheel vibration, pulling to one side, or a thumping sound — all mean you need an inspection. If your tire has a sidewall bulge, do not drive on it — sidewall failure at highway speed causes sudden loss of control.
