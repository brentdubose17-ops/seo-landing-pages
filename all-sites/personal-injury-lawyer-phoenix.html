How Much Does a Personal Injury Lawyer Cost in Phoenix?

Phoenix personal injury lawyers work on contingency — you pay nothing upfront, and the lawyer takes a percentage of the settlement or verdict. The standard fee in Arizona is 33.3% (one-third) if the case settles before a lawsuit is filed, and 40% if it goes to trial or requires extended litigation. Costs (expert witnesses, medical records, filing fees) are usually advanced by the firm and deducted from the recovery.

If you win nothing, you owe nothing — that's the deal that makes a lawyer affordable after an accident. A Phoenix car accident case that settles at $50,000 typically nets the client $30,000–$33,000 after the one-third fee and costs. That same case without a lawyer often settles at $15,000–$25,000 with the insurance company's first offer, which is why most attorneys recommend a free case review before accepting anything.

What Is a Personal Injury Case Worth in Arizona?

Arizona injury cases are worth the sum of economic damages (medical bills, lost wages, future care) plus non-economic damages (pain and suffering). Arizona has no cap on non-economic damages in personal injury cases — unlike medical malpractice, which has a $750,000 cap. A moderate Phoenix car accident with $20,000 in medical bills and two months of lost work typically settles for $40,000–$90,000; a permanent injury case can run $250,000–$1,000,000+.

Phoenix-specific factors that boost value: aggressive bad drivers (Maricopa County leads Arizona in at-fault accidents), the I-10 and US-60 corridors with daily rear-end collisions, and the summer heat that makes walking injuries worse. If the other driver was uninsured, your own uninsured motorist (UM) coverage — which Arizona requires insurers to offer — becomes the funding source; a good lawyer knows how to layer UM coverage to maximize recovery.

Arizona's 2-Year Statute of Limitations: Don't Wait

Arizona gives you 2 years from the date of the injury to file a personal injury lawsuit (A.R.S. § 12-542). Miss that deadline and your claim is dead — no matter how strong the facts. There are narrow exceptions: if the injury wasn't discovered until later (the "discovery rule") or if the defendant is a government entity, which shrinks the window to just 180 days for claims against Arizona or its political subdivisions.

The practical Phoenix advice: don't talk to the other driver's insurance company before talking to a lawyer. Insurers record everything and use your statements against you. Arizona is a pure comparative fault state — if you're 30% at fault, your recovery drops 30% — so how you describe the accident matters. A free consultation with a Phoenix injury lawyer before any recorded statement is the single best-protected move.

What to Do Right After a Phoenix Car Accident

The first 24 hours decide the value of your case. Call the police and get a report — Arizona law requires a report for accidents with injury or damage over $1,000. Get medical attention even if you feel fine; soft-tissue injuries like whiplash often show up 24-48 hours later, and a gap in treatment is the #1 reason insurers lowball. Photograph the scene, the vehicles, and your injuries. Exchange insurance info but do NOT admit fault or apologize — in comparative fault states, "I'm sorry" can be used as an admission.

Then, in Phoenix specifically: report the accident to your own insurer (your policy requires it), but decline to give a recorded statement to the other party's insurer until you've consulted counsel. Keep all medical records, bills, and a pain journal. If you miss work, document the days — lost wages are recoverable damages. A lawyer typically takes over all insurer communication within 24 hours, which stops the pressure calls immediately.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a Phoenix personal injury case take?

Most car accident cases settle in 6–18 months. Cases that go to trial or involve permanent injuries can take 2–3 years. The 2-year statute of limitations runs from the accident date regardless.

What if the at-fault driver has no insurance in Arizona?

Your uninsured motorist (UM) coverage pays, and Arizona law requires insurers to offer UM coverage. An experienced lawyer can also pursue the driver personally and identify other coverage like umbrella policies.

Do I really need a lawyer for a minor accident?

If medical bills are small and fault is clear, you may not. But a free consultation costs nothing, and lawyers routinely increase settlements 2-4x over what insurers offer unrepresented victims — even on "minor" cases.

What is the average settlement for a Phoenix car accident?

Settlements vary wildly by injury: soft-tissue-only cases $8,000–$30,000, fractures $30,000–$100,000, and permanent or catastrophic injuries $250,000–$1,000,000+. Medical bills and lost wages drive the number.

Can I sue the City of Phoenix for a pothole accident?

Yes, but there's a catch: claims against Arizona government entities must be filed within 180 days, and you must prove the city had notice of the dangerous condition. Call a lawyer immediately — the short deadline is easy to miss.
