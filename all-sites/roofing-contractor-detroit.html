How Much Does a New Roof Cost in Detroit?

An asphalt shingle roof in Detroit runs $6,500–$14,000 for a typical 1,500–2,000-square-foot home, or roughly $4.50–$7.00 per square foot installed. A targeted repair — replacing a few missing or lifted shingles — runs $400–$2,000. Metal roofing runs $14,000–$28,000, and a flat roof on a city brick commercial building $8,000–$20,000.

Detroit's pricing sits slightly below the national average because of lower labor rates, but materials dominate the cost — architectural shingles add 15–25% over 3-tab. A roof nearing 20 years old in southeast Michigan has already lived through enough snow, ice, and hail that a replacement pencils out better than repeated patching.

Michigan Roofing Licensing Requirements

Michigan requires any roofing contractor doing work over $600 to hold a Residential Builder or Maintenance & Alteration license issued by LARA, the Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs. That license number must appear on the contract, and you can verify it online. Working without one is a misdemeanor in Michigan.

Roofers should also carry both general liability and workers' compensation insurance — roofing is Michigan's highest-injury construction trade, and an uninsured crew falling on your property becomes your lawsuit. Get a certificate of insurance naming you as an additional insured before any tear-off begins.

Detroit's Snow, Ice Dams, and Hail Season

Detroit averages 43 inches of snow a year, and the real roof-killer is the ice dam — snow melts at the warm roof peak, refreezes at the cold eave, and backs water up under the shingles. Ice dams are the leading cause of Detroit winter leaks, and they're a ventilation and insulation problem as much as a roofing one. Proper attic insulation and ridge venting stop them at the source.

Hail is the other big threat. Southeast Michigan sees several hail events each spring and summer, and even pea-sized hail can bruise shingles and void their lifespan. After a storm, get a free inspection within 30 days — insurance carriers tighten the claims window and an adjuster will need documented storm damage, not a 15-year-old roof that simply aged out.

Historic Detroit Homes and Their Roofing Challenges

Detroit's historic districts — Boston-Edison, Indian Village, Woodbridge — are full of steep-pitch roofs and original slate or cedar that are expensive to match. Many homeowners' associations and historic commissions require in-kind materials, and slate repair runs $800–$1,500 per square versus a full asphalt reroof.

If your home has a steep roof or unusual pitch, budget 15–30% more than a standard ranch — steeper roofs are slower and more dangerous to work, and that labor shows up in the estimate. The good news: Detroit's remaining slate and tile roofs often have another 50 years of life with proper maintenance, so restoration usually beats replacement on these older homes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a Detroit roof replacement take?

Most asphalt shingle reroofs take 1–3 working days, plus a day for a tear-off if one is needed. Flat and metal roofs take longer. Permitting and material delivery add a week or two upfront, and contractors book out further in spring and summer.

What is an ice dam and how do I stop it?

An ice dam forms when warm air from the attic melts roof snow, which refreezes at the colder eave and backs water under shingles. The fix is attic insulation and ridge venting, not just raking snow — though a roof rake is a useful winter band-aid.

Will insurance pay for my Detroit roof?

Only if it was damaged by a covered peril like hail, wind, or a fallen tree — not normal aging. Document storm damage with photos and file within the carrier's window, typically 30–60 days after the event.

How do I verify a Michigan roofer's license?

Check the license number on the LARA website (michigan.gov/lara). Michigan requires a Residential Builder or Maintenance & Alteration license for any roofing job over $600, and the number must appear on your contract.

Should I repair or replace my 18-year-old roof?

At 18 years in southeast Michigan, replacement usually wins. A roof that old has absorbed 18 winters of snow and ice dams, and patching scattered failures rarely costs less than $1,500–$2,500 anyway.
