How Much Does a Personal Injury Lawyer Cost in Chicago?

Chicago personal injury lawyers work on contingency: you pay nothing upfront and nothing unless they recover money for you. The standard fee is 33% of a pre-trial settlement and 40% if the case goes to trial or appeal. The initial consultation is always free, and most firms advance case costs (filing fees, medical records, expert witnesses) that come out of the recovery.

That structure means a lawyer only takes your case if they believe it's winnable — if they pass, it's a signal the case has weak liability or limited insurance. With Illinois's high minimum auto coverage ($25,000/$50,000 for bodily injury), most Chicago claims resolve against the policy limits plus any underinsured motorist coverage you carry.

How Long Do You Have to File a Claim in Illinois?

Illinois's statute of limitations for personal injury is 2 years from the date of injury (735 ILCS 5/13-202). If the at-fault party is a government entity — a CTA bus, a city vehicle, or a trip-and-fall on a city sidewalk — you must file a notice of claim within 1 year and the claim within the same 2-year window. Missing either deadline permanently bars your case.

There's a reason to move fast beyond deadlines: evidence disappears. Surveillance footage overwrites in 7–30 days, witnesses forget details, and injuries become harder to tie to the accident. Call a lawyer within days of an accident — not weeks — to preserve evidence and get a head start on the insurance company's own investigation.

How Much Is a Chicago Car Accident Case Worth?

Minor soft-tissue injuries (whiplash, sprains) typically settle for $10,000–$50,000. Cases involving surgery, fractures, or permanent impairment run $100,000–$500,000 and up. The key variables: total medical bills, whether treatment was invasive, lost income, and whether the injury leaves a permanent limitation that a jury can see.

Illinois is a modified comparative fault state — your recovery is reduced by your percentage of fault, and if you're more than 50% at fault you recover nothing. That's why the insurance adjuster's first move is often to pin partial blame on you. A lawyer who documents fault early (police report, photos, witness statements) protects your recovery from being unfairly reduced.

What Should You Do After an Accident in Chicago?

First: call 911, get medical attention even if you feel fine, photograph the scene and all vehicles, and exchange insurance and plate information. Illinois law requires you to report any crash involving injury, death, or more than $1,500 in property damage — file that report within 10 days. Do not admit fault or post about the accident on social media.

Second: do not give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer. Adjusters are trained to elicit statements they can use to reduce your claim. Have a lawyer handle all communication — a single poorly-worded answer to "how are you feeling today?" can cost you thousands. The consultation is free, and the sooner a lawyer is involved, the harder it is for the insurer to lowball you.
