How Much Does an Emergency Plumber Cost in St. Louis?

An emergency plumbing call in St. Louis runs $150–$350 just to get a licensed plumber to your door after hours, and that dispatch fee usually covers the first 30–60 minutes of diagnosis. A burst pipe repair runs $300–$800, a water heater replacement $1,200–$2,500, and a sewer line repair or replacement $2,500–$8,000 depending on depth and length.

The most expensive surprise is a main sewer backup, which can hit $8,000–$15,000 if the line has to be excavated and replaced. St. Louis's stock of century-old clay sewer laterals makes this common. Always get an upfront price for the dispatch and an itemized estimate before work begins — reputable emergency plumbers quote before they cut.

St. Louis Plumbing Licensing and Permits

Missouri has no statewide plumber license, which means regulation falls to each municipality. The City of St. Louis requires plumbers to be registered with the city, and St. Louis County requires a plumbing license plus a permit for any work beyond routine fixture replacement. This patchwork is exactly why you should verify credentials before an emergency hire.

For water heater replacement, sewer work, or any job that touches the main line, a permit and inspection are mandatory in most of the metro. Ask the plumber for their local license number and confirm they'll pull the permit. An unpermitted sewer repair can come back to bite you when you sell the house and the inspection flags unapproved work.

Freeze-Thaw Cycles and St. Louis's Aging Pipes

St. Louis sits on the freeze-thaw line — winter temps regularly swing from 20°F to 50°F, and every freeze-thaw cycle stresses pipes and joints. Homes built in the 1890s–1920s brick boom often still have galvanized steel supply lines, which corrode from the inside and have a 60–100 year lifespan that many have already exhausted.

That combination of cold snaps and old metal explains why burst pipes spike in January and February. If your home still has galvanized or lead supply lines, a repipe to PEX or copper runs $4,000–$10,000 and is the only permanent fix. Insulating exposed pipes in basements and crawl spaces is a $50–$150 job that prevents the most common cold-weather failures.

What to Do Before the Plumber Arrives

The 30–60 minutes before your plumber shows up can save you thousands in water damage. First, shut off the water at the main shutoff valve — usually in the basement or at the street meter — and then open the lowest faucet to drain the lines. For a water heater leak, kill the power or gas to the unit and shut its inlet valve.

Second, document everything with photos before you move anything; your homeowners insurance claim depends on it. Third, clear a path to the problem area and have your water and sewer line access points identified. These three steps turn a $8,000 water-damage event into a $400 pipe repair.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there really a plumber available at 3 AM in St. Louis?

Yes. Multiple St. Louis metro plumbers run 24/7 crews year-round, but expect a $150–$350 after-hours dispatch fee. For a genuine emergency — a burst pipe or sewer backup — paying it beats $5,000 in overnight water damage.

How do I know if a pipe burst or just a fitting leaked?

A burst pipe sprays continuously and often shows as a stain spreading fast on a ceiling or wall; a leaking fitting drips slowly. Either way, shut off the main valve first, then call — the plumber will diagnose once the water is off.

Why does my house have galvanized pipes and should I replace them?

Galvanized steel was standard through the 1950s and corrodes from the inside after 60–100 years, causing low pressure, rusty water, and eventual pinhole leaks. A full PEX repipe runs $4,000–$10,000 and is the permanent fix.

Will my insurance cover a burst pipe?

Usually yes, if the burst was sudden and accidental — not gradual neglect. Sudden frozen-pipe bursts are typically covered for the water damage, though the pipe repair itself may fall to you. Document the damage with photos and call your insurer promptly.

How much does sewer line replacement cost in St. Louis?

A full sewer line replacement typically runs $2,500–$8,000 for a standard residential lot, rising to $8,000–$15,000 for deep or long lines under driveways. Trenchless relining is often $2,000–$4,000 cheaper than excavation.
