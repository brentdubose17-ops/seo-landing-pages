San Antonio AC Repair, Replacement & Tune-Ups

San Antonio asks more of an air conditioner than almost any metro in Texas: five months of 95°F-plus afternoons with Gulf humidity that keeps systems running long, wet duty cycles. Add water at roughly 14–16 grains per gallon and you get scaled coils, clogged condensate lines, and equipment that fails years early. Texas licenses the trade through TDLR, and Bexar County requires mechanical permits, so the contractor you hire is verifiable — if you know which number to check. This guide covers real San Antonio pricing, the licensing rules, and which upgrades actually pay back in CPS Energy territory.

TDLR Licensing and San Antonio Permit Rules

Texas requires air conditioning and refrigeration contractors to be licensed by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation. Individual technicians hold ACR licenses, and the business must operate under a licensed contractor whose number appears on the estimate. Verify that number in TDLR’s online license search before you sign anything, and check that the license is active rather than expired or under a different company name.

San Antonio and Bexar County issue mechanical permits for equipment replacement, and the permit triggers an inspection. That record matters in two places: an unpermitted install can complicate a home sale when a buyer’s inspector pulls permit history, and if a claim ever traces back to the equipment, an unpermitted installation is the first thing an adjuster flags.

Why San Antonio Systems Fail Early

Humidity is the silent killer. A system that is right-sized on paper but oversized in practice cools the air faster than it can remove moisture, so it cycles off with the house still damp — and a damp house keeps the thermostat low, which keeps the system running harder. That loop is why so many San Antonio homes feel cold and clammy at the same time, and why humidity control is worth mentioning to whoever quotes your replacement.

Water is the second problem. San Antonio Water System delivers roughly 14–16 grains per gallon, which is hard enough to scale the inside of condensate pans, humidifiers, and the outdoor coil. Scale on the evaporator coil cuts capacity and raises head pressure; scale in the drain line causes the pan to overflow into a ceiling or closet. Annual coil cleaning and a pan treatment are inexpensive insurance, and a simple water test kit will tell you whether a whole-house softener is worth discussing.

Rebates, Heat Pumps, and What to Buy in 2026

CPS Energy runs efficiency rebates for qualifying high-efficiency equipment, and the federal 25C tax credit covers up to $2,000 for qualifying Energy Star heat pumps installed through 2032. Stack them: a rebate from the utility plus the federal credit can move a heat pump into the same price range as a mid-tier straight-cool replacement, and the heat pump also covers the mild San Antonio winters without a gas connection.

Whatever you choose, plan for the freeze. The 2021 winter event proved that San Antonio equipment has to survive occasional hard freezes, which means heat strips or a backup heat source, insulated and heat-traced lines in the attic, and a drain pan that will not crack. Insist on a written scope that names tonnage, SEER2 rating, refrigerant type, and the line-set treatment — not a one-line quote for a number of tons.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an AC diagnostic cost in San Antonio?

Most San Antonio HVAC companies charge $79–$149 for a diagnostic visit, and many credit the fee toward the repair if you hire them for the work. Confirm the credit policy before the technician arrives.

Are HVAC contractors licensed in Texas?

Yes. Texas requires air conditioning and refrigeration contractors to be licensed by TDLR, and the business must operate under a licensed contractor. Verify the license number in TDLR’s online search before authorizing work.

Do I need a permit to replace an AC in San Antonio?

Yes. Equipment replacement requires a mechanical permit from the city or county, and the permit triggers an inspection. Unpermitted installations can complicate a future home sale and any insurance claim tied to the system.

Why does my San Antonio house feel cold and damp?

That is usually an oversized system. An oversized unit cools the air before it can remove moisture, so the house stays humid and the thermostat keeps getting pushed lower. A Manual J load calculation is the fix for the next system.

How long should a central AC last in San Antonio?

Twelve to fifteen years with annual service is realistic. Hard water scale, humidity-driven drain problems, and oversized installs are the three biggest reasons systems here fail earlier than the sticker promises.
