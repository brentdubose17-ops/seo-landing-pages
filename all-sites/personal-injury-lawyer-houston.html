How Much Does a Houston Personal Injury Lawyer Cost?

Houston personal injury lawyers work on contingency — you pay nothing upfront and the attorney takes a percentage of the settlement, typically 33% if the case settles before filing and 40% if it goes to litigation. Case costs (expert witnesses, medical records, filing fees) are usually advanced by the firm and deducted from the settlement.

Because there are no upfront costs, hiring an attorney costs you nothing out of pocket. Studies consistently show injury victims with attorneys recover 2–3 times more than those who negotiate alone, even after fees — which is why contingency representation is the standard model in Texas.

What Is Texas's Statute of Limitations for Injury Claims?

Texas gives you 2 years from the date of the accident to file a personal injury lawsuit. Miss that deadline and your claim is barred forever, no matter how strong the case. There are exceptions — minors, fraud, and delayed-discovery cases can extend the window — but the safe assumption is 2 years.

Texas also uses modified comparative fault: if you're found 50% or less at fault, your recovery is reduced by your percentage of fault. At 51% fault or more, you recover nothing. A Houston attorney builds the liability case that keeps your fault percentage low — this single issue often decides whether a claim is worth pursuing.

Why Houston Is a Truck Accident Hub

Houston is one of the busiest freight corridors in the country — I-10, I-45, and US-59/290 converge here, and the Port of Houston moves more cargo than any Gulf Coast port. That density makes commercial truck accidents a leading category of Houston injury cases, and they carry much higher policy limits than passenger car claims.

Interstate trucking companies must carry at least $750,000 in liability coverage (federal minimum), and many carry $1M+ in umbrella coverage. Truck accident cases also involve independent contractors, logbook violations, and black-box data — an experienced Houston firm knows how to preserve driver logs, ECM data, and dashcam footage before it disappears.

What Is Your Houston Injury Case Worth?

Texas injury settlements compensate for medical bills, lost wages, future care, pain and suffering, and property damage. In Harris County, pre-litigation settlements for moderate injuries typically range from $15,000 to $100,000; cases that go to trial in Harris County juries are known for some of the largest verdicts in Texas, particularly in trucking and industrial cases.

Houston's industrial base adds another category: refinery and petrochemical accidents, offshore injuries, and construction-site falls fall under different rules (maritime law, workers' comp, or third-party liability). If your injury happened at a job site, an initial consultation determines which legal path applies — workers' comp is often not your only option.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do immediately after a Houston accident?

Call 911 if there are injuries, seek medical care, exchange information, photograph the scene, and report the accident. Do not give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer before speaking with an attorney.

How long will my case take?

Most Houston injury cases settle in 6–18 months. Cases that require litigation or trial can take 2–3 years. Your attorney should give you a timeline estimate after reviewing the case.

Do I pay anything if I lose?

With a contingency agreement, you pay no attorney fees if you recover nothing. Some firms also waive case costs in a loss. Confirm the fee structure in writing before signing.

Can I handle my own insurance claim?

You can, but insurers are in the business of settling cheap. Attorney-represented claimants recover substantially more on average, and attorneys handle the negotiations, medical liens, and paperwork at no upfront cost.

What if the accident was partly my fault?

Texas modified comparative fault allows recovery if you're 50% or less at fault, with your recovery reduced by your percentage. An attorney argues the liability evidence to minimize your share.
