Memphis Roofing Repair & Replacement After Hail

Memphis is one of the most hail-hit metros in the country, and the damage is rarely dramatic — it is bruised shingle matting, granule loss, and dented soft metal that lets water in two winters later. Because storm claims drive most of the roofing work here, the difference between a good job and a bad one usually comes down to documentation: which contractor photographs the damage properly, meets your adjuster on the roof, and installs to code the first time. This guide covers Memphis pricing, Tennessee licensing thresholds, and what your policy does and doesn't cover.

Tennessee Licensing: Thresholds That Matter

Tennessee does not license roofers as a standalone trade, but the state's contractor licensing thresholds apply. Residential and commercial construction work over $25,000 requires a license from the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors, and residential roofing in Tennessee's larger counties frequently falls under the state's Home Improvement license rules. In practice: ask for the license number, the classification (residential vs commercial), and proof of general liability and workers' compensation coverage.

Workers' compensation proof is not optional in Tennessee. State law requires construction employers with one or more employees to carry it, and on a roofing job the practical question is simple — if a crew member falls off your roof, whose policy pays? A contractor who cannot produce a certificate should not be on your property.

Memphis Hail, Wind, and What Insurance Actually Covers

Memphis sits in the heart of Dixie Alley, a corridor that produces the same large-hail events as Tornado Alley but with more frequent, higher-moisture storms. Hail bruises shingles by knocking granules loose and cracking the mat underneath; the roof may look intact from the ground while its remaining life drops by half. That is why an adjuster's decision often hinges on soft-metal evidence — dented gutters, vents, and flashing — rather than the shingles themselves.

Most Tennessee homeowner policies cover hail and wind damage on an actual-cash-value or replacement-cost basis, and replacement cost usually requires you to complete the work to collect the full amount. Two practical rules: file promptly, because most policies impose a claim deadline and there is talk of shorter statutory windows in several states; and never sign a contract that assigns your claim benefits to a contractor without reading the fine print, because that document transfers control of your claim to someone else.

Choosing a Memphis Roofer Without Getting Burned

Storm-chasing crews follow hail events across the region, which means a contractor with a magnetic sign and an out-of-state plate may be unreachable when the work leaks. Check three things: a physical local address you can visit, a license number you can verify with the state board, and a written warranty that names both the manufacturer's material coverage and the contractor's own labor coverage.

Get the work documented at the end. Photograph the finished roof, keep the permit and inspection record, and save the invoice with the warranty number on it. In Shelby County, an unpermitted re-roof can surface during a home sale inspection years later, and the buyer's lender will ask for the documentation whether you have it or not.
