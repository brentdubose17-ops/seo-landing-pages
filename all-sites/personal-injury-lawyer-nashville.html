After a serious accident in Nashville, you're facing medical bills, lost wages, and insurance adjusters whose job is to pay you as little as possible. Tennessee's personal injury laws have strict deadlines and unique rules that make working with a local attorney essential — not optional.

How Much Is a Nashville Personal Injury Case Worth?

Settlement values depend on medical expenses, lost income, pain and suffering, and liability clarity. Minor injury claims (soft tissue, no hospitalization) settle for $5,000–$25,000. Moderate injury claims (fractures, surgery required) range $30,000–$100,000. Serious injury claims (permanent disability, traumatic brain injury) reach $150,000–$1M+. Every case is unique, but the average Nashville auto accident settlement is about 3x the medical specials.

Tennessee's Statute of Limitations — Don't Wait

Tennessee gives you just 1 year from the accident date to file a personal injury lawsuit — one of the shortest deadlines in the country. Miss it and you lose your right to compensation forever. Insurance companies know this and often stall negotiations past the deadline, then refuse to settle. An attorney files a protective lawsuit well before the deadline to preserve your rights while continuing to negotiate.

Tennessee's Modified Comparative Fault Rule

Tennessee follows a modified comparative fault rule: if you're found even 1% at fault, your compensation is reduced by your percentage. If you're 50% or more at fault, you recover nothing. This makes accident reconstruction and evidence preservation critical. Don't talk to the other insurance company without an attorney — anything you say can be used to assign you partial fault.
