Austin Auto Glass That's Ready for Hail Season

Austin windshields take a beating from every direction: 100-degree summers that stress the glass, construction traffic on I-35 that kicks up gravel, and March-through-June hailstorms that roll through Texas hail alley and leave thousands of cracked windshields in their wake. One Texas fact surprises most drivers: unlike Florida and Kentucky, Texas has no zero-deductible windshield law — glass damage falls under your comprehensive coverage, so your deductible applies unless you carry a glass endorsement. This guide covers Austin auto glass pricing, how Texas insurance actually handles windshields, and why modern cars need recalibration after replacement.

Texas Insurance: There Is No Zero-Deductible Law

Here is the Texas rule that surprises people who moved from Florida or Kentucky: Texas does not require insurers to waive your deductible for windshield replacement. In Florida and Kentucky, state law forces insurers to cover glass with no deductible; in Texas, a cracked windshield is simply a comprehensive-coverage claim, and your comprehensive deductible — typically $250–$1,000 — applies. The two exceptions are voluntary: some insurers waive the deductible for chip repair (not replacement) because it saves them the cost of a full windshield, and you can buy a separate glass endorsement that lowers or eliminates the deductible for glass claims.

So when you call your insurer about a cracked windshield in Austin, ask three questions before you authorize anything: What is my comprehensive deductible? Does my policy include a glass endorsement? And does my insurer have a preferred glass shop, or can I choose my own? Under Texas law you are generally free to choose the shop — and the shop should handle the claim paperwork with your insurer either way.

Why Austin Windshields Crack — and Why You Should Fix Chips Fast

Austin's environment is engineered to turn small chips into big cracks. The heat is the primary driver: on a 100-degree day, the glass expands, and when you park in the shade or blast the AC, the sudden temperature swing makes the glass contract — flexing the chip into a spreading crack. Add vibration from driving on I-35's construction-zone surfaces and you have a recipe for a chip that was repairable on Monday becoming a full replacement by Friday. Texas hail season adds the seasonal spike: March through June storms drop hail across the Austin metro, and every significant hail event produces thousands of windshield claims in a single afternoon.

The economics are clear. Chip repair runs $50–$120 and takes under an hour; windshield replacement runs $250–$800 plus recalibration. Most insurers cover chip repair free precisely because it is a tenth of the replacement cost. The moment you see a chip — even a bullseye the size of a quarter — get it filled. If a crack has already spread past a few inches or reached the edge of the glass, replacement is the only safe option: a cracked windshield compromises the structural roof support in a rollover and can fail airbag deployment timing.

ADAS and Modern Windshields: Why Recalibration Is Not Optional

If your car was built after about 2017, the odds are high that its safety systems look through the windshield: lane-keeping assist cameras, automatic emergency braking sensors, rain-sensing wipers, and adaptive cruise control all mount at or near the glass. Those systems were calibrated at the factory to read through a specific thickness and curvature of glass — which is why automakers specify replacement glass and why the cameras must be recalibrated after any windshield replacement. Skipping recalibration can leave you with a car whose automatic braking is misaligned, and no legitimate shop will guarantee the safety systems without doing it.

When you choose an Austin glass shop, ask directly: Do you use OEM-spec glass, and do you perform ADAS recalibration in-house or send the car elsewhere? Shops that do both in-house give you one warranty and one point of contact. On newer vehicles the recalibration typically runs $150–$400 on top of the glass — and on some makes, like Subaru with EyeSight, the procedure is required every time the windshield comes out. It is not an upsell; it is how the car keeps its safety features working.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a windshield replacement cost in Austin?

A standard windshield replacement in Austin runs $250–$700 installed depending on the vehicle, with acoustic, heated, or heads-up-display glass running $350–$800. If your car has cameras or sensors mounted at the windshield, add $150–$400 for the required ADAS recalibration.

Does Texas require zero-deductible windshield coverage?

No. Texas has no zero-deductible glass law — unlike Florida and Kentucky, it does not require insurers to waive the deductible for windshield replacement. Glass damage is covered under your comprehensive coverage, so your deductible applies unless you have a separate glass endorsement.

How fast should I fix a rock chip in Austin?

As soon as possible — ideally within days. Austin's heat makes chips spread fast: the glass expands and contracts through 100-degree days, and vibration from driving turns a repairable chip into a crack that requires full replacement. Chip repair runs $50–$120, and many insurers cover it free because it prevents a costlier replacement.

Do I need ADAS recalibration after a windshield replacement?

Yes, if your vehicle has cameras or sensors mounted at the windshield — which includes lane-keeping assist, automatic emergency braking, and rain-sensing wipers on most cars built after 2017. Those systems read through the glass, so the windshield must be replaced to OEM spec and the cameras recalibrated to factory procedure, typically $150–$400.

Does hail damage count as a windshield claim in Austin?

Yes. Hail damage to glass is covered under comprehensive coverage, the same as any other glass break. Austin sits in Texas hail alley, and March–June storms produce the biggest spike in windshield claims every year.
