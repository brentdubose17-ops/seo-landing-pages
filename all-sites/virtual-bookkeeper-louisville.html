Kentucky Tax Changes Affecting Louisville Business Owners

Kentucky individual income tax was 3.5% as of 2026, down from 4% previously. That rate applies to pass-through business income on your personal return.

Kentucky does not have a corporate income tax for C-corps (they pay federal tax only). S-corps and LLCs are pass-through entities, so income flows to personal returns at the 3.5% rate.

Louisville and Jefferson County add occupational taxes on top of state income tax. The combined rate can hit 5–6% depending on the county area. A bookkeeper familiar with Louisville will know these rules.

Payroll Filings & Employee Records in Kentucky

Kentucky requires withholding for state income tax, federal income tax, and payroll taxes. Payroll must be filed quarterly (or more frequently if you have many employees).

Annual filings include W-2s (due Jan 31) and a Kentucky U-4 wage report (due by Feb 10). A virtual bookkeeper who misses these deadlines costs you in penalties.

Keep employment records for at least 3 years. Include I-9s, W-4s, and any pay stubs related to wage disputes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does virtual bookkeeping cost in Louisville?

Monthly fees range $200–$400 for simple businesses, $400–$800 for moderate complexity. Payroll adds $5–$12 per employee per pay period.

What does a monthly close include?

Reconciled accounts, profit & loss statement, balance sheet, and a written summary of the month's activity and any unusual items.

Does Kentucky have income tax?

Yes, 3.5% state income tax. Louisville and Jefferson County add occupational taxes, raising the total to 5–6% depending on the area.

What payroll filings are required in Kentucky?

Quarterly payroll tax filings, annual W-2s (Jan 31 deadline), and a Kentucky U-4 wage report (Feb 10 deadline).

Can a virtual bookkeeper in another state handle my Louisville business?

Yes. Bookkeeping is location-independent and cloud-based. The bookkeeper needs to know Kentucky tax rules, but doesn't need to be in Louisville.
