Every experienced electrician knows the feeling: you're halfway through a panel upgrade when the client asks for an extra circuit, or discovers they want dimmers in every room. Without a clear electrician change order template, those extras can slip through the cracks — costing you money and creating disputes. A structured change order protects both you and your client by recording exactly what changed, why, and at what price.

Why Every Electrician Needs a Change Order Template

Change orders are the backbone of profitable contracting. They convert verbal requests into written agreements, reducing misunderstandings and chargebacks. A solid electrician change order template includes fields for:

  • Original scope vs. revised scope
  • Additional materials and labor costs
  • Client approval signature and date
  • Impact on project timeline
  • Photo documentation of the change

By using a consistent template, you create a paper trail that makes disputes easy to resolve — and helps you maintain professional credibility with every job.

What to Include in Your Change Order Form

A good change order doesn't need to be complicated. Start with the job name, date, and change order number. Then describe the original work and the requested change in plain language. List all new materials, quantities, and prices. Add a section for labor hours or flat fees. Finally, include a signature block for both parties. When you electrician change order template is this straightforward, you'll use it every time — and your clients will appreciate the transparency.
